# Book Selection Criteria — Filtering Sources

> **Purpose**: A specific filter that decides which sources enter the knowledge base. Not "read more books" — read the RIGHT books.

---

## The Three-Question Filter

For every potential source (book, paper, article, podcast, blog), ask:

### Q1: Does it explain WHY, not just WHAT?

**Reject** if the source:
- Lists 10 habits of successful people without explaining the mechanism behind each
- Tells you to "think positive" without explaining the neurochemistry of expectation
- Says "Sun Tzu believed X" without explaining why Sun Tzu's era made X true
- Tells you to "wake up at 5 AM" without explaining circadian rhythm or sleep debt

**Accept** if the source:
- Traces a principle back to its root cause
- Shows you WHY a strategy works in some conditions and fails in others
- Explains the mechanism behind the rule, so you can derive new rules yourself

This is the **Sima Yi test**. Sima Yi doesn't memorize Sun Tzu; he understands why Sun Tzu's principles work, so he can apply them to situations Sun Tzu never imagined.

### Q2: Does the author have credibility?

Credibility is earned in one of three ways:

**Type A — Track record**: The author has done the thing they're writing about, with measurable outcomes.
- A contractor who built 50 projects writing about contracting ✅
- A business school professor who has never run a business writing about entrepreneurship ❌ (sometimes still valuable, but flag the lack of practice)
- A retired CEO who grew a company from $0 to $1B writing about scaling ✅

**Type B — Peer review**: The author's work has been scrutinized by other experts in the field.
- A psychologist with peer-reviewed publications on decision-making ✅
- An "influencer" with 100k Instagram followers but no peer-reviewed work ❌
- A historian cited by other historians in their field ✅

**Type C — Lived experience**: The author has personally lived through the phenomenon they describe.
- A prisoner of war writing about resilience ✅
- A founder who lost a company writing about failure ✅
- Someone who has never been poor writing about poverty ❌ (may still have data, but flag the perspective)

A source with NONE of these three is automatically rejected. A source with two of three is high-quality.

### Q3: Does it apply to the user's situation?

The user is:
- Egyptian
- Muslim (with specific commitments to halal business and spiritual growth)
- An electrical contractor
- Expanding into real estate and general contracting
- Working with limited initial capital
- Operating in 2026 Egypt (post-devaluation, post-construction sector changes)
- Multi-domain curious (physics, aqeedah, programming, etc.)

**Reject or downgrade** sources that:
- Assume Silicon Valley venture capital is available (it isn't here)
- Assume US tax structures (they don't apply)
- Assume the author's culture is universal (it isn't)
- Recommend interest-based financing (against the user's principles)
- Are written for a different stage of business (e.g., advice for Fortune 500 CEOs is useless to a one-person contractor)

**Accept and translate** sources that:
- Are written for a different context but have transferable principles (e.g., Japanese Kaizen applies to Egyptian construction; you just adapt it)
- Are written for a similar economic environment (Turkey, India, Brazil have valuable parallels)

**Accept strongly** sources that:
- Are written by Egyptians or about Egypt
- Are written by Muslims or about Islamic finance
- Are written by contractors or about construction specifically
- Cover the post-2024 economic conditions in Egypt

## The Tiered Library

When building the knowledge base, organize sources into tiers. Each tier serves a different purpose.

### Tier 1 — The Constants (foundational)
Sources whose principles have held for centuries or millennia. Read deeply, return often.

- Sun Tzu, *The Art of War*
- Clausewitz, *On War*
- Machiavelli, *The Prince* and *Discourses*
- Ibn Khaldun, *Muqaddimah*
- Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics*
- Al-Ghazali, *Ihya Ulum al-Din* (specifically the sections on earning and spending)
- The 36 Stratagems (anonymous Chinese classic)
- Robert Greene, *48 Laws of Power* and *The Laws of Human Nature*

### Tier 2 — The Modern Classics (last 100 years, stood the test of time)
Sources that synthesized older principles for the modern world.

- Kahneman, *Thinking, Fast and Slow*
- Cialdini, *Influence*
- Sunstein and Thaler, *Nudge*
- Taleb, *The Black Swan*, *Antifragile*, *Skin in the Game*
- Drucker, *The Effective Executive*
- Christensen, *The Innovator's Dilemma*
- Seligman, *Learned Optimism*
- Peterson, *12 Rules for Life* (caveat: read critically)

### Tier 3 — Practitioner Reports (last 10 years, currently active)
Sources written by people doing the work now.

- Contractor blogs and YouTube channels (filter for honesty)
- Egyptian real estate investor case studies
- Islamic finance practitioners
- Substack writers on adjacent topics (e.g., "The Diff" by Byrne Hobart for strategy)
- Industry-specific newsletters (Egypt's construction sector)

### Tier 4 — Specialized Reference (when needed)
For deep dives — academic papers, legal codes, technical manuals.

- Egyptian Civil Code (for contracts)
- Federation of Egyptian Contractors publications
- Shariah standards (AAOIFI) for Islamic finance
- Engineering specifications (NEC, IEC standards for electrical work)

## The Anti-Bibliography

These sources are explicitly REJECTED from the knowledge base:

### "Hustle culture" books
- Books that promise " millionaire in 30 days"
- Books where the author's only credential is "I made money selling this book"
- Books with titles like "Crush It!", "Hustle Harder", etc.
- Why: They are designed to sell copies, not to teach. The advice is recycled self-help with branding.

### Influencer content
- 60-second video "courses"
- "Mastermind" paid groups where the only product is the mastermind
- Courses by people whose only business is selling courses
- Why: The incentive is to keep you paying, not to make you competent.

### Pop-psychology without peer review
- "The Secret" and similar manifestation books
- Books that misinterpret Kahneman's work
- Books that quote Sun Tzu out of context to sell business consulting
- Why: They strip the depth out of real research and leave the user with slogans.

### Anti-islamic finance sources disguised as Islamic
- Books that rebrand conventional interest-based loans as "Islamic" via fee structures
- Scholars with no shariah training issuing fatwas on finance
- Why: They are not Islamic finance; they are conventional finance with halal branding. The user's spiritual integrity matters.

## How to Use This Filter

When a user asks for research in a new domain:

1. **Apply the three-question filter** to candidate sources
2. **Tier them** (Constants, Modern Classics, Practitioner, Specialized)
3. **For Tier 1-2**: read deeply, take time
4. **For Tier 3**: read selectively (practitioners repeat themselves; find the unique insights)
5. **For Tier 4**: only when a specific question requires depth

**Output rule**: Always include the tier in the source citation in the file. This tells the user how much weight to give each source.

```
[Source: Sun Tzu, *Art of War*, ch. 3 — Tier 1 (Constant). Principle: know yourself and know your enemy.]
[Source: Egyptian Civil Code, Article 651 — Tier 4 (Reference). Direct authority on contracting law.]
[Source: Personal conversation with contractor who lost EGP 2M on a 2024 project — Tier 3 (Practitioner). The specific failure was X.]
```

## A Practical Example

The user asks: "How do I scale from a one-person electrical contractor to a company with 20 employees and 5 projects running in parallel?"

**Source discovery:**

Tier 1 candidates:
- Drucker, *The Effective Executive* (✅ accept — explains WHY delegation works)
- Sun Tzu (✅ accept — but only the chapters on organization and command structure)

Tier 2 candidates:
- Christensen, *The Innovator's Dilemma* (✅ accept — for understanding when to fire yourself from operations)
- Kahneman, *Thinking Fast and Slow* (✅ accept — for the biases that hit founders during scaling)

Tier 3 candidates:
- Egyptian contractor LinkedIn posts on hiring and scaling (filter for honest ones)
- YouTube channels by Egyptian contractors who have done the scaling (filter for detail, not motivation)
- Forum threads on r/Construction about the transition from solo to team

Tier 4 candidates:
- Egyptian labor law (Chapter on employer obligations)
- Federation of Contractors classification rules (need 20 employees? need higher license tier?)
- Egypt's social insurance system (cost per employee)

**Result**: 8-12 sources, filtered to 4 tiers, synthesized into one file `09_BUSINESS/contracting/scaling_from_zero.md`. The file explains WHY each principle works (from Tier 1-2), how it applies in Egypt (from Tier 3-4), and what the user should do specifically.

## The Reading Discipline

Reading more books is NOT the goal. Reading the RIGHT books deeply is the goal. A person who has read 50 great books deeply outperforms a person who has read 500 books superficially. This is true in strategy (Sima Yi read few books, deeply), in scholarship (Ibn Taymiyyah returned repeatedly to the same sources), and in business (Buffett reads 500 pages a day but from a small set of high-quality sources).

The filter is more important than the volume.
