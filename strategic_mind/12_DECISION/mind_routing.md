## The Premise

Different situations require different minds. The patient mind (Sima Yi) is wrong for the rapid-exploitation window (Guo Jia's domain). The force mind (Lu Bu) is wrong for the legitimacy-building situation (Liu Bei's domain). The chaos-exploitation mind (Yuan Fang) is wrong for the macro-board control situation (Dong Zhuo's domain).

The discipline: for each decision, identify the situation type, then route to the mind(s) whose `when_to_activate.md` file specifies that situation type. The routing matrix below is the distillation of the when_to_activate files across all minds.

The mistake is to always invoke the same mind (the contractor's favorite) for every situation. The favorite mind becomes a hammer; every situation becomes a nail. The discipline is to invoke the mind that fits the situation, not the mind that fits the contractor's preference.

## The Primary Minds — Routing Matrix

### 1. Sima Yi (司马懿) — The Patient Strategist

**Worldview**: the long game is the only game that matters; most people lose because they cannot wait; conceal strength until it cannot be contested; let enemies weaken each other; survive your allies as well as your enemies.

**Activate when**:
- The decision is long-horizon (3-10+ years)
- The situation requires waiting for an opportunity to ripen
- The contractor has time and optionality on his side
- Reveal of strength would invite counter-mobilization
- The competitor landscape is fragmented and self-weakening

**Do NOT activate when**:
- The window is closing fast (Guo Jia territory)
- Direct force is required (Lu Bu territory)
- The contractor is the underdog who must seize legitimacy (Liu Bei territory)
- The situation is chaotic and needs exploitation, not patience (Yuan Fang territory)

**For the contractor**: Sima Yi is the default mind for the contractor's overarching strategy — the patient accumulation of capacity, reputation, and capital over 10 years to move from electrical contracting to real estate development. Most of the contractor's major decisions are Sima Yi decisions.

### 2. Cao Cao (曹操) — The Integration Strategist

**Worldview**: comprehensive strategy; integrate military, political, economic, and cultural dimensions; build institutions that outlast the founder; use people of all types, including those with moral failings, when they serve the goal.

**Activate when**:
- The decision spans multiple domains simultaneously (business + political + cultural)
- The contractor is building an institution, not just running projects
- The integration of multiple specialists is the core challenge
- The contractor needs to think about succession and legacy

**Do NOT activate when**:
- The decision is narrow and tactical (use the specialized mind)
- The contractor is in a survival situation (Jia Xu territory)
- The decision requires patience over years (Sima Yi territory)

**For the contractor**: Cao Cao applies when the contractor is building the company — hiring across specialties, building systems that work without him, thinking about who succeeds him. The contractor-as-institution-builder is Cao Cao.

### 3. Guo Jia (郭嘉) — The Rapid Exploitation Strategist

**Worldview**: read situations rapidly; identify the exploitable weakness; strike before the window closes; do not let perfection delay the good.

**Activate when**:
- The window is closing fast (a market opportunity, a competitor's moment of weakness, a client's urgency)
- The decision must be made in days, not weeks
- The cost of delay exceeds the cost of imperfect execution
- The contractor has identified the exploitable asymmetry

**Do NOT activate when**:
- The situation needs patience (Sima Yi)
- The window is not actually closing (verify before invoking)
- The contractor's rapid action would invite stronger counter-mobilization

**For the contractor**: Guo Jia applies when a market window opens — a competitor fails on a major project, a developer needs a contractor urgently, a material price drops temporarily. The contractor who waits loses the window; the contractor who acts captures it.

### 4. Liu Bei (刘备) — The Heart and Legitimacy Strategist

**Worldview**: legitimacy is the foundation of power; people follow those they trust; long-term loyalty is built by character, not by force or cunning.

**Activate when**:
- The decision concerns relationships with key people (foremen, partners, family, key clients)
- Legitimacy is the constraint (the contractor needs people to follow him willingly)
- The contractor is the underdog and must build legitimacy slowly
- The decision affects the contractor's reputation in a way that compounds

**Do NOT activate when**:
- The situation is purely tactical (Liu Bei's heart is not the constraint)
- The decision is about capital allocation, not people
- Force or rapid exploitation is what's needed (Lu Bu, Guo Jia)

**For the contractor**: Liu Bei applies when the contractor is building the team — choosing foremen, building loyalty with key subcontractors, managing family expectations, sustaining the client relationships that will yield 10 years of work. The contractor-as-team-builder is Liu Bei.

### 5. Lu Bu (吕布) — The Force Strategist

**Worldview**: when you have the strength, strike directly and overwhelming force; the situation will not improve by waiting; momentum is the resource.

**Activate when**:
- The contractor has overwhelming advantage (price, capacity, reputation)
- The situation requires decisive action, not deliberation
- The opponent is weak and the strike will end the contest
- Momentum exists and must be used before it dissipates

**Do NOT activate when**:
- The contractor is not actually stronger (Lu Bu without strength is suicide)
- The decision is long-horizon (Lu Bu's signature is the short, decisive strike)
- The decision requires building relationships (Liu Bei territory)
- The decision crosses ethical red lines (Lu Bu's blind spot is moral restraint)

**For the contractor**: Lu Bu applies rarely and carefully — when the contractor has a clear advantage and the decision is whether to bid aggressively on a project he's favored to win, when to insist on terms with a dependent client, when to push a supplier for better payment terms. Lu Bu used without strength is a disaster; Lu Bu used with strength and ethical constraint is decisive.

### 6. Yuan Fang (袁方) — The Chaos Exploitation Strategist

**Worldview**: when the situation is chaotic and other actors are reactive, the strategist who sees the chaos's structure can exploit it; let others exhaust themselves reacting; harvest the disarray.

**Activate when**:
- The market or political environment is in chaos
- Competitors are reacting to shocks, not strategizing
- The chaos creates openings that did not exist before
- The contractor can move where others cannot

**Do NOT activate when**:
- The environment is stable (chaos exploitation is irrelevant)
- The contractor is part of the chaos, not outside it
- The chaos will engulf the contractor too (use Jia Xu — survival — instead)

**For the contractor**: Yuan Fang applies when a market shock hits — a currency devaluation, a regulatory change, a major contractor's collapse. The contractor who reads the chaos's structure can pick up clients, workers, and projects that the chaos dislodged. The contractor who can't read the chaos is just buffeted by it.

### 7. Dong Zhuo (董卓) — The Macro Board Strategist

**Worldview**: the macro board is the chessboard of macro conditions (currency, demographics, regulation, political power); controlling the macro board is more durable than controlling any single position on it.

**Activate when**:
- The decision is about positioning relative to macro conditions (which market to enter, which to exit)
- The contractor's long-term position depends on macro trends, not on individual projects
- The contractor has the scale to influence macro conditions (or is preparing for that scale)

**Do NOT activate when**:
- The contractor is small (macro board control is aspirational, not operational)
- The decision is tactical (use the tactical minds)
- The macro analysis would paralyze action (Sima Yi's patience is the operating discipline; Dong Zhuo's macro view is the strategic context)

**For the contractor**: Dong Zhuo applies at the strategic planning level — choosing which geographic markets to enter (New Cairo vs 6 October vs North Coast), which sectors to specialize in (residential vs commercial vs industrial), how to position for the next 5 years of Egyptian construction market evolution. Dong Zhuo is the long-term market view.

## The Secondary Minds — When to Invoke

The primary seven cover most decisions. The secondary minds handle specialized situations:

### Sun Ce (孙策) — The Rapid Conqueror

Sun Ce's signature is rapid territorial expansion through decisive strikes. He applies when the contractor is in expansion mode and the opportunities are visible, the resources are available, and the time is now. Distinct from Guo Jia: Guo Jia exploits an existing window; Sun Ce creates the window by his own momentum.

**For the contractor**: when the contractor is aggressively building market share and the opportunities are clear — multiple projects available, sufficient capital, sufficient team. Sun Ce is the expansion mode.

### Zhou Yu (周瑜) — The Brilliant Tactician

Zhou Yu's signature is the brilliant tactical stroke — the plan that wins the battle. He applies when a specific tactical situation requires a creative plan (a complex bid structure, a negotiation maneuver, a project execution trick). Distinct from Guo Jia: Guo Jia exploits weakness; Zhou Yu designs the brilliant move.

**For the contractor**: when the contractor faces a specific tactical problem that requires a clever solution — a bid that needs a non-obvious structure, a negotiation that requires a creative framing, a project execution that requires an innovative approach.

### Jia Xu (贾诩) — The Survival Strategist

Jia Xu's signature is survival — choosing the path that maximizes the probability of survival, even at the cost of ambition. He applies when the contractor is in a survival situation (cash crisis, market collapse, project failure) and the question is not "what's the best move" but "what's the move that doesn't destroy me."

**For the contractor**: when the contractor faces a survival situation — a cash flow crisis, a major project failure, a market contraction. Jia Xu asks: what is the minimum viable path? What preserves the contractor's capacity to recover? Ambition is suspended; survival is the priority.

### Zhuge Liang (诸葛亮) — The Comprehensive Strategist

Zhuge Liang's signature is the comprehensive long-term plan executed with discipline. He applies when the contractor needs a detailed multi-year strategic plan — the equivalent of the Long Memorial (Chu Shi Biao) for the contractor's business.

**For the contractor**: when the contractor is preparing the formal strategic plan — the 5-year plan, the 10-year plan, the succession plan. Zhuge Liang provides the discipline of detailed, comprehensive, sequenced planning. Distinct from Sima Yi: Sima Yi provides the patience; Zhuge Liang provides the plan.

### Sun Quan (孙权) — The Defensive Consolidator

Sun Quan's signature is defensive consolidation — holding what you have while others fight. He applies when the contractor's priority is preserving a position, not expanding — when the market is overheated and the contractor should wait, when competitors are overextending and the contractor should hold.

**For the contractor**: when the contractor's position is strong but the environment is risky — holding cash, declining risky projects, consolidating relationships. Sun Quan is the defensive discipline.

### Lu Su (鲁肃) — The Diplomatic Strategist

Lu Su's signature is the diplomatic bridge — building alliances, mediating conflicts, finding the deal that benefits both parties. He applies when the contractor needs to build an alliance (with another contractor for a joint bid, with a supplier for a long-term relationship, with a developer for a multi-year framework).

**For the contractor**: when the contractor is building alliances — joint ventures, long-term supplier agreements, multi-client frameworks. Lu Su is the alliance builder.

### Xun Yu (荀彧) — The Internal Stabilizer

Xun Yu's signature is internal stability — building the institutions, the team, the systems that allow the strategist to focus on the strategy. He applies when the contractor needs to build the back office, the management team, the systems that allow the contractor to delegate.

**For the contractor**: when the contractor is building the company's internal capacity — the project management system, the financial controls, the HR function. Xun Yu is the internal builder.

### Chen Gong (陈宫) — The Loyal Advisor

Chen Gong's signature is the loyal advisor who speaks truth to power — the strategist who tells the leader what he needs to hear, not what he wants to hear. He applies when the contractor needs to challenge his own assumptions — to find the advisor who will say "you're wrong about this."

**For the contractor**: when the contractor is at risk of self-deception — the confirmation bias is strong, the ego is engaged, the decision is too important to make alone. Chen Gong is the internal challenger, the internal auditor.

### Pang Tong (庞统) — The Unconventional Strategist

Pang Tong's signature is the unconventional approach — the move that surprises because no one else would think of it. He applies when conventional approaches have failed and the contractor needs to think outside the patterns.

**For the contractor**: when the conventional bid structure isn't working, when the conventional client approach isn't winning, when the contractor needs a creative move. Pang Tong is the unconventional option.

## The Routing Matrix — Situation → Mind

| Situation type | Primary mind | Secondary mind |
|---|---|---|
| Long-game strategic decision (3-10+ years) | Sima Yi | Cao Cao, Dong Zhuo |
| Tactical window closing fast | Guo Jia | Zhou Yu, Sun Ce |
| Relationship / legitimacy / team building | Liu Bei | Lu Su, Xun Yu |
| Decisive strike with overwhelming advantage | Lu Bu | Sun Ce |
| Chaos exploitation | Yuan Fang | Jia Xu (if contractor is also in chaos) |
| Macro board positioning | Dong Zhuo | Cao Cao |
| Survival situation | Jia Xu | Sun Quan |

The matrix is a starting point. Most situations have primary + secondary minds (sometimes tertiary). The synthesis (see `multi_mind_synthesis.md`) is the integration of the primary mind's answer with the secondary's perspective, with the conflict resolution and hierarchy rules applied.

## The Multi-Mind Synthesis — When One Mind Is Not Enough

Most consequential decisions require multiple minds. The single-mind decision is the exception, not the rule. The discipline:

1. **Identify the primary mind** from the routing matrix.
2. **Identify the secondary mind** (the mind that adds a perspective the primary misses).
3. **Identify conflicts** (when the primary and secondary would give different answers).
4. **Resolve conflicts** by the hierarchy rules (see `multi_mind_synthesis.md` for the full rules).
5. **Synthesize** — not average, but integrate.

**The hierarchy of minds (rough)**:
- When the decision is long-horizon, Sima Yi's patience overrides Lu Bu's force.
- When the decision is a survival situation, Jia Xu's survival overrides every ambition-driven mind.
- When the decision crosses ethical red lines, no strategic mind overrides; the ethics layer rules.
- When the decision requires rapid exploitation, Guo Jia's speed overrides Sima Yi's patience (the long-game consideration is suspended; the window is closing).
- When the decision concerns legitimacy and relationships, Liu Bei's heart overrides Lu Bu's force (people cannot be forced; they must be won).

The hierarchy is not rigid; it is the default resolution that the contractor can override with explicit reasoning. See `multi_mind_synthesis.md` for the full protocol.

## The Routing Discipline — Don't Misroute

The most common routing failure: invoking the contractor's favorite mind for every situation. If the contractor is naturally patient, he invokes Sima Yi for every decision and misses the Guo Jia windows. If the contractor is naturally aggressive, he invokes Lu Bu for every decision and misses the Liu Bei relationship moments.

The discipline: for each decision, ask "which mind would I NOT naturally invoke?" Then ask "would that mind's perspective change my answer?" If yes, the missing mind should be invoked. The mind that is hardest to invoke (because it doesn't fit the contractor's natural tendency) is often the mind that would change the answer.

The discipline extends: invoke the antagonist mind — the mind whose worldview is opposite the contractor's natural one. If the contractor is naturally patient, invoke Guo Jia as a check. If the contractor is naturally aggressive, invoke Sima Yi as a check. The antagonist mind surfaces the blind spot.

## Common Pitfalls

**Pitfall 1: Favorite-mind overuse.** Invoking the same mind for every decision. The discipline is to invoke the mind that fits the situation, not the mind that fits the contractor.

**Pitfall 2: Single-mind decisions for multi-mind situations.** Most consequential decisions need multiple minds. The single-mind answer is partial. Force a second-mind check on every consequential decision.

**Pitfall 3: Confusing the minds' domains.** Sima Yi is not for tactical windows; Guo Jia is. Lu Bu is not for relationship decisions; Liu Bei is. Yuan Fang is not for stable situations; Dong Zhuo is. Routing the wrong mind produces wrong advice.

**Pitfall 4: Invoking the mind that flatters the contractor's preference.** The contractor prefers to be told he's right. The mind that says "you're right" is the easy mind. The mind that says "you're wrong, here's why" is the hard mind — and the valuable one. Invoke Chen Gong when the contractor's ego is engaged.

**Pitfall 5: Forgetting the antagonist mind.** The mind the contractor would NOT invoke is the mind that surfaces the blind spot. Invoke the antagonist as a discipline, even when (especially when) the contractor is sure he's right.
