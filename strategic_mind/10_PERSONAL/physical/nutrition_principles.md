# Nutrition Principles — The Fuel of the Contractor's Performance

> **Layer**: 10_PERSONAL/physical/
> **File**: nutrition_principles
> **Subject**: Nutrition principles for the contractor — the macronutrient framework, the micronutrient framework, the Egyptian diet, the timing, the hydration, the supplements, the Islamic dimension
> **Domain**: Physical — the fuel that powers the contractor's body and brain
> **User**: Egyptian Muslim electrical contractor
> **Why this file exists**: A contractor can train well, sleep well, and pray well — and still underperform if his nutrition is poor. The nutrition is the fuel. The contractor who fuels well has the energy, the focus, and the recovery capacity for the long days. The contractor who fuels poorly is chronically fatigued, cognitively impaired, and physically vulnerable. This file is the operator's manual.

---

## The Premise

Nutrition is the input that the contractor can most directly control. The sleep and the exercise are the other major levers, but the nutrition is the daily choice, three to five times a day, that compounds into the contractor's baseline capacity. The contractor who eats well for a year has a different body and a different brain than the contractor who eats poorly for a year. The difference is not theoretical; it is the difference between the contractor who can do the 12-hour site day and the contractor who collapses at hour 8.

The core claim: **nutrition is not a diet; it is a framework.** The contractor should not chase the latest diet (the keto, the paleo, the carnivore, the vegan). The contractor should learn the framework (the macronutrients, the micronutrients, the timing) and apply it to his situation (the Egyptian context, the site demands, the Islamic practice). The framework is stable; the diets are transient.

This file is the framework. The Islamic dimension is woven in: the body is a trust from Allah, and the fueling of it is the maintenance of the trust. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "The son of Adam does not fill a vessel worse than his stomach. A few morsels are enough for the son of Adam to keep his back straight. If he must, then one third for his food, one third for his drink, and one third for his breath." [Narrated by al-Miqdam ibn Ma'di Karib, Sunan al-Tirmidhi, no. 2380, hasan — Tier 2].

The "one third" is the moderation that the framework serves.

## The Macronutrient Framework

The macronutrients are the three nutrients the body needs in large quantities: protein, fat, and carbohydrate. Each has a function, a source, and a timing.

### Protein — The Building Block

**What it does** — protein builds and repairs the body. The muscles, the organs, the skin, the hair, the enzymes, the hormones are all made of protein. The contractor's physical work (the lifting, the climbing, the standing) requires the protein for the maintenance and the recovery of the muscle. The contractor's cognitive work (the bidding, the planning, the negotiation) requires the protein for the neurotransmitters (dopamine, serotonin, etc.) that the brain runs on.

**How much** — the standard recommendation is 1.6-2.2 grams per kilogram of body weight per day for an active adult. For a 75 kg contractor, that is 120-165 grams per day. The standard Egyptian diet (high in carbohydrates, low in protein) typically provides 60-80 grams per day — well below the optimal.

**What to eat** — the protein sources:
- Animal: meat (beef, lamb, chicken), fish, eggs, dairy (milk, yogurt, cheese).
- Plant: lentils, chickpeas, beans, nuts, seeds.
- The animal protein is "complete" (it contains all the essential amino acids in the right proportions); the plant protein is "incomplete" (it lacks one or more essential amino acids, which must be combined from different sources). The contractor who eats animal protein does not need to worry about the combination; the contractor who eats only plant protein must combine the sources.

**When to eat** — the protein should be eaten at every meal. The body cannot store protein (it stores carbohydrate as glycogen and fat as fat, but it does not store protein); the protein must be supplied regularly. The contractor who eats 30 grams of protein at each of three meals (breakfast, lunch, dinner) gets 90 grams; the contractor who eats 50 grams at each gets 150 grams. The even distribution is better than the concentrated dose.

### Fat — The Energy and the Hormones

**What it does** — fat provides the energy (the most concentrated energy source, 9 calories per gram), the satiety (the fat in a meal slows the digestion and reduces the hunger), the hormones (the cholesterol is the precursor to testosterone, the estrogen, the cortisol), and the absorption of the fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K).

**How much** — the standard recommendation is 0.8-1.2 grams per kilogram of body weight per day. For a 75 kg contractor, that is 60-90 grams per day. The standard Egyptian diet (high in refined carbohydrates, often low in healthy fats) does not optimize this.

**What to eat** — the fat sources:
- Healthy fats: olive oil (the Egyptian staple), nuts (almonds, walnuts), seeds (flax, chia), avocados, fatty fish (salmon, sardines), eggs (with the yolk).
- Less healthy fats (but acceptable in moderation): the saturated fats in meat and dairy.
- Avoid: the trans fats (in processed foods, in fried foods, in some margarines), the highly processed vegetable oils (soybean, corn, canola in excess).

**When to eat** — the fat should be at every meal, but particularly the meals that need the sustained energy (the breakfast before a site day, the dinner before the recovery sleep).

### Carbohydrate — The Energy for the Intense Work

**What it does** — carbohydrate provides the energy (4 calories per gram), particularly for the high-intensity work (the lifting, the sprinting, the sustained focus). The carbohydrate is stored as glycogen in the liver and the muscles; the glycogen is the body's quick energy reserve.

**How much** — the requirement depends on the activity. The contractor who does the moderate physical work needs 2-3 grams per kilogram per day. The contractor who does the intense physical work needs 4-6 grams per kilogram. The contractor who is sedentary (the office day) needs less (1-2 grams per kilogram). The standard Egyptian diet (high in carbohydrates) often exceeds the requirement; the issue is the type of carbohydrate, not the quantity.

**What to eat** — the carbohydrate sources:
- Complex (slow-digesting, high-fiber): vegetables (the green vegetables, the colored vegetables), the whole fruits (in moderation), the whole grains (in moderation — the Egyptian baladi bread is better than the white bread, the brown rice is better than the white rice).
- Simple (fast-digesting, low-fiber): the sugar, the sweets, the white flour, the white rice. The simple carbohydrates should be minimized — they produce the insulin spike, the energy crash, and the cravings.

**When to eat** — the carbohydrate is most useful around the physical work. The contractor who has a site day can eat the complex carbohydrate at breakfast (for the sustained energy of the day) and after the work (for the glycogen replenishment). The contractor who has an office day needs less carbohydrate and can reduce it without issue. The carbohydrate at night (within 3 hours of sleep) is less useful and can impair the sleep.

## The Micronutrient Framework

The micronutrients are the nutrients the body needs in small quantities: the vitamins, the minerals, the fiber. The micronutrients do not provide energy, but they are essential for the body's function. The contractor who is deficient in any micronutrient will underperform (the energy, the focus, the recovery, the immunity) without knowing why.

### Vitamins

The vitamins are organic compounds that the body cannot synthesize (or synthesizes in insufficient quantity). The fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) are stored in the body; the water-soluble vitamins (the B vitamins, vitamin C) are not stored and must be supplied daily.

The contractor's specific concerns:
- **Vitamin D** — the "sunshine vitamin," synthesized by the skin in the sunlight. The Egyptian contractor, despite the abundant sunlight, may be deficient (the indoor office days, the covered clothing, the sun avoidance). The deficiency produces fatigue, low testosterone, low mood. The contractor should get the morning sunlight (the fajr walk, the morning site visit) and eat the vitamin-D-rich foods (the fatty fish, the eggs, the fortified dairy).
- **Vitamin B12** — the nerve and the blood vitamin, found only in animal foods. The contractor who eats meat is fine; the contractor who is vegetarian or vegan should supplement.
- **Vitamin C** — the immune and the antioxidant vitamin, found in the citrus, the bell peppers, the tomatoes. The contractor should eat the daily vitamin C sources.

### Minerals

The minerals are inorganic elements that the body needs. The contractor's specific concerns:
- **Magnesium** — the muscle and the nerve mineral, found in the dark leafy greens, the nuts, the seeds. The standard diet is often deficient; the contractor can supplement (200-400 mg per day) or eat the magnesium-rich foods.
- **Zinc** — the immune and the testosterone mineral, found in the meat, the oysters, the pumpkin seeds. The contractor should eat the zinc-rich foods.
- **Iron** — the blood mineral, found in the red meat, the liver, the lentils. The contractor (particularly the contractor who eats little red meat) should ensure the iron intake.
- **Sodium** — the electrolyte, found in the salt. The contractor who works in the Egyptian heat (see physical/heat_tolerance_egypt.md) loses significant sodium through sweat and should ensure the adequate intake (the lightly salted water during the site work, the salted food in the meals).

### Fiber

The fiber is the indigestible carbohydrate in the plant foods. Its functions:
- **The bowel regularity** — the fiber prevents the constipation, the hemorrhoids, the diverticulitis.
- **The insulin stabilization** — the fiber slows the digestion of the carbohydrate, which reduces the insulin spike.
- **The microbiome** — the fiber feeds the beneficial bacteria in the gut, which produce the short-chain fatty acids that support the immune function and the brain function.

The contractor should eat 25-35 grams of fiber per day. The standard Egyptian diet (low in vegetables, low in whole grains) is often deficient. The fix is the increase of the vegetables, the legumes, the whole fruits, the whole grains.

## The Egyptian Diet and Its Modifications

The traditional Egyptian diet has strengths and weaknesses for the contractor's performance. The strengths should be kept; the weaknesses should be modified.

### The Strengths

- **The olive oil** — the healthy fat, the Egyptian staple. The contractor should use it generously (the salad dressing, the cooking, the dip with the bread).
- **The legumes** — the lentils, the chickpeas, the beans. The Egyptian ful (the fava beans) is a good protein and fiber source. The contractor should eat it regularly.
- **The fresh vegetables** — the tomatoes, the cucumbers, the eggplants, the peppers, the leafy greens. The Egyptian cuisine uses them generously. The contractor should eat them at every meal.
- **The moderate meat** — the Egyptian diet is not excessively meat-heavy (which is good for the budget and the cardiovascular health). The meat should be moderate, not eliminated.
- **The seafood** — the Egyptian coast (Alexandria, the Red Sea) provides the fresh fish. The contractor should eat the fish 2-3 times per week.

### The Weaknesses

- **The refined carbohydrates** — the white bread (the Egyptian bread, even the baladi, is often white), the white rice, the sugar. The contractor should reduce the refined carbohydrates (replace the white bread with the baladi bread if whole grain, replace the white rice with the brown rice or the bulgur, eliminate the sugar).
- **The sugar** — the Egyptian sweets (the basbousa, the konafa, the baklava), the sugared tea (the shai with multiple sugars), the sugared juices. The contractor should reduce the sugar to the occasional treat, not the daily habit.
- **The fried foods** — the Egyptian fried foods (the ta'miya — the fried falafel, the fried potatoes, the fried sandwiches). The contractor should reduce the fried foods (the baked versions, the grilled versions, the raw versions).
- **The over-reliance on bread** — the Egyptian meal often centers on the bread. The contractor should reduce the bread (one piece per meal instead of three), and replace it with the vegetables and the protein.
- **The sweetened drinks** — the sugared tea, the sugared coffee, the sugared juices, the sodas. The contractor should reduce these (the unsweetened tea and coffee, the water, the sparkling water).

### The Modifications

The contractor's modifications:
1. **The protein at every meal** — the breakfast (the eggs, the cheese, the yogurt, the ful), the lunch (the meat, the chicken, the fish, the legumes), the dinner (the same). The protein should be the centerpiece of the meal, not the side.
2. **The vegetables doubled** — the contractor should double the vegetable portion at every meal. The vegetables should occupy half the plate.
3. **The refined carbohydrates reduced** — the contractor should reduce the bread, the rice, the sugar to half (or less) of the standard Egyptian portion.
4. **The water as the primary drink** — the contractor should drink water (and the unsweetened tea and coffee) as the primary drink. The sugared drinks should be occasional.
5. **The fried foods reduced** — the contractor should choose the baked, the grilled, the raw, the boiled over the fried. The fried foods should be occasional, not daily.

## The Timing of Eating — Intermittent Fasting and Meal Timing

The timing of eating is as important as the content. The body's response to food depends on the timing (the circadian rhythm of the insulin sensitivity, the meal timing's effect on the sleep, the fast's effect on the insulin).

### Intermittent Fasting

Intermittent fasting (the daily 14-16 hour fast) is the practice of eating within a window of 8-10 hours and fasting for the remaining 14-16 hours. The fast produces:
- **The insulin reduction** — the fast allows the insulin to fall, which allows the body to burn the stored fat.
- **The autophagy** — the fast triggers the autophagy (the cellular cleanup, the recycling of the damaged cellular components). The autophagy is associated with the longevity and the disease prevention.
- **The cognitive benefit** — the fast produces the ketones (from the fat burning), which the brain uses efficiently. The fast is associated with the improved focus and the mental clarity.

### The Islamic Dimension

The Islamic practice supports the intermittent fasting:
- **The Ramadan fast** — the 30-day annual fast, from fajr to maghrib. The fast is the deepest intermittent fasting practice (the 13-16 hour daily fast, depending on the season).
- **The voluntary fasts** — the Prophet's ﷺ regular fast on Mondays and Thursdays, the white days fast (the 13th, 14th, 15th of the lunar month), the six days of Shawwal. These are the regular intermittent fasting practices.
- **The pre-fajr eating** — the suhoor (the pre-dawn meal) is the Sunna. The contractor who eats the suhoor (a moderate meal of protein and complex carbohydrates) has the sustained energy for the fasting day.

The contractor can adopt the daily 14-16 hour fast (e.g., eating from after Maghrib to before Fajr — the daily Ramadan-style window) or the more relaxed 12-14 hour fast (e.g., from after Isha to the late breakfast). The 12-14 hour fast is more sustainable for the long term; the 14-16 hour fast is more aggressive. The contractor should start with the 12-14 hour fast and adjust based on the energy and the focus.

### The Meal Timing

The meal timing principles:
1. **The breakfast** — the protein-rich breakfast (the eggs, the cheese, the yogurt, the ful) within an hour of waking. The protein stabilizes the insulin and the appetite for the day.
2. **The lunch** — the moderate lunch (the protein, the vegetables, the moderate complex carbohydrate). The lunch is the main meal for the contractor's energy.
3. **The dinner** — the light dinner (the protein, the vegetables, the minimal carbohydrate). The dinner should be at least 3 hours before sleep (to allow the digestion and the insulin fall before the sleep).
4. **The snacks** — the contractor should minimize the snacks (the snacking produces the constant insulin elevation). If the snack is needed, the protein snack (the nuts, the cheese) is better than the carbohydrate snack (the biscuits, the sweets).
5. **The post-workout meal** — the contractor who trains (see physical/training_basics.md) should eat the protein and the carbohydrate within 1-2 hours after the workout (for the muscle recovery and the glycogen replenishment).

## The Hydration Question

The hydration is the most under-rated nutrition principle. The contractor who is dehydrated (even mildly) is cognitively impaired (the focus, the memory, the decision quality), physically weaker (the strength, the endurance), and emotionally unstable (the mood, the patience).

### The Requirement

The standard recommendation is 2-3 liters of water per day for the sedentary adult. The contractor who works in the Egyptian heat (see physical/heat_tolerance_egypt.md) needs 4-6 liters per day. The contractor who works in the air-conditioned office needs the standard 2-3 liters.

### The Timing

The water should be drunk throughout the day, not in the concentrated doses. The morning (after waking) is the most important time — the body is dehydrated after the night's sleep, and the morning water (the "morning water" Sunna, the Prophet ﷺ drank water on waking [various sources]) restores the hydration. The contractor should drink 500 ml of water on waking, then 200-300 ml per hour through the day, with the last 500 ml before the evening meal.

### The Electrolytes

The water alone is not sufficient for the hydration; the electrolytes (the sodium, the potassium, the magnesium) are also needed. The contractor who drinks the water without the electrolytes will dilute the electrolytes and may develop the hyponatremia (the low blood sodium, which produces the fatigue, the headache, the nausea). The contractor should add a pinch of salt to the water (or eat the salted food) to maintain the electrolyte balance.

### The Egyptian Heat

The Egyptian heat (see physical/heat_tolerance_egypt.md) requires the special hydration discipline. The contractor who works in the summer heat loses 1-2 liters of water per hour through sweat. The hydration should be:
- The 500 ml before the start of the site work.
- The 200-300 ml every 20-30 minutes during the site work.
- The 500 ml after the site work, with the electrolyte replacement (the salted water, the electrolyte drink).
- The continued hydration through the rest of the day.

## The Supplement Question

The supplement industry is large, confusing, and largely fraudulent. The contractor should approach the supplements with the skepticism. The principle: the supplement supplements a deficient diet; it does not replace a good diet.

### What Works

- **Vitamin D** — for the contractor who does not get the sunlight (the office days, the covered clothing), the vitamin D supplement (2000-4000 IU per day) is the reliable intervention.
- **Magnesium** — for the contractor who does not eat the magnesium-rich foods (the dark leafy greens, the nuts, the seeds), the magnesium supplement (200-400 mg per day, preferably the magnesium glycinate or the magnesium citrate) is the reliable intervention.
- **Omega-3** — for the contractor who does not eat the fatty fish 2-3 times per week, the fish oil supplement (1-2 grams of the EPA+DHA per day) is the reliable intervention for the brain and the cardiovascular health.
- **Creatine** — for the contractor who trains (see physical/training_basics.md), the creatine supplement (3-5 grams per day) is the reliable intervention for the muscle strength and the cognitive function.
- **Protein powder** — for the contractor who cannot get the protein from the food (the busy days, the site days), the protein powder (the whey or the plant-based) is the convenient supplement.

### What Is Hype

- The multivitamins (the broad-spectrum, low-dose vitamin supplements) are largely useless if the diet is good. The contractor should eat the food, not the multivitamin.
- The "performance enhancers" (the pre-workouts, the energy drinks, the testosterone boosters) are largely hype. The contractor should optimize the lifestyle (the sleep, the exercise, the diet) before considering these.
- The "detox" supplements (the cleanses, the juice fasts, the foot baths) are entirely fraudulent. The body's detoxification is done by the liver and the kidneys, not by the supplements.

### What Is Dangerous

- The unregulated supplements (the supplements sold without the third-party testing) may contain the contaminants, the undisclosed stimulants, or the banned substances. The contractor should choose the third-party-tested supplements (the NSF, the Informed Sport certifications).
- The excessive doses of the fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) can be toxic. The contractor should not exceed the recommended doses.
- The stimulants (the high-dose caffeine, the ephedrine, the amphetamines) are dangerous and prohibited in Islam (the harm to the body exceeds the benefit).

### The Islamic Discipline

The Islamic discipline on the supplements: the supplements are permissible (mubah) if they are safe, effective, and not intoxicating. The supplements are not permissible if they harm the body (which is a trust from Allah), if they are intoxicating (which the Prophet ﷺ prohibited), or if they are fraudulent (which is a form of cheating that the Prophet ﷺ prohibited, "whoever cheats is not of us" — already cited).

The contractor should approach the supplements with the moderation: take the ones that work, avoid the ones that are hype, never take the ones that are dangerous.

## The Islamic Dimension — Fasting as Performance Optimization

The Islamic fast is not only the worship; it is the performance optimization. The Ramadan fast and the voluntary fasts produce the physiological benefits that align with the nutrition framework.

### The Ramadan Fast

The Ramadan fast (from fajr to maghrib, for 30 days) produces:
- **The insulin reduction** — the 13-16 hour daily fast allows the insulin to fall, which improves the insulin sensitivity.
- **The autophagy** — the longer fasts (in the long summer days) trigger the autophagy.
- **The discipline** — the daily fast trains the will and the appetite.
- **The spiritual benefit** — the taqwa (the consciousness of Allah) that is the stated purpose of the fast (Quran, al-Baqara 2:183).

### The Voluntary Fasts

The voluntary fasts (the Mondays and Thursdays, the white days) produce the similar benefits at a lower intensity. The contractor who maintains the regular voluntary fast has the ongoing insulin and the discipline benefits.

### The Pre-Fajr Meal (Suhoor)

The suhoor (the pre-dawn meal) is the Sunna. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "Take the suhoor, for in the suhoor there is blessing." [Narrated by Anas ibn Malik, Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 1923 — Tier 1].

The blessing is both the spiritual and the nutritional. The contractor who eats the suhoor (the moderate meal of the protein, the complex carbohydrates, the water) has the sustained energy for the fasting day.

### The Breaking of the Fast (Iftar)

The iftar (the breaking of the fast at maghrib) is the Sunna. The Prophet ﷺ broke the fast with the fresh dates (or the dried dates if fresh were not available) and the water. The contractor should follow the Sunna — break the fast with the dates and the water, then pray the maghrib prayer, then eat the main meal. The dates provide the quick carbohydrate (the immediate energy), the water restores the hydration, and the prayer provides the pause before the meal (which prevents the over-eating).
