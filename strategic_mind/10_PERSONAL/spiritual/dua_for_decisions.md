# Dua for Decisions — The Prayer of Seeking Counsel from Allah

> **Layer**: 10_PERSONAL/spiritual/

> **Subject**: The dua of decision-making — salat al-istikhara and the related supplications — as the operational tool for the contractor's significant decisions
> **Domain**: Spiritual — the prayer that integrates worship with strategy
> **User**: Egyptian Muslim electrical contractor
> **Why this file exists**: A contractor who makes decisions without consulting Allah has reduced his decisions to his own limited judgment. A contractor who consults Allah without taking counsel from qualified humans is operating on a thin understanding of his own religion. The Prophet ﷺ taught both: the istikhara prayer (consulting Allah) and the istishara practice (consulting people). This file is the operational manual for both, with emphasis on the istikhara — the prayer that the contractor should make before every significant decision.

## The Premise

The Prophet ﷺ taught the istikhara prayer to his companions as he taught them a surah of the Quran — meaning he taught it as a structured, memorized, regular practice. The companion Jabir ibn Abdullah reported:

> "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to teach us istikhara in all matters, as he taught us the surahs of the Quran. He said: 'When one of you is concerned with a matter, let him pray two rakats other than the obligatory prayer, then say: O Allah, I seek from You the good by Your knowledge, and I seek strength by Your power, and I ask You of Your great bounty. You have power, I have none. You know, I know not. You are the Knower of the unseen. O Allah, if You know that this matter [name it] is good for me in my religion, my livelihood, and my affairs — or: in my immediate and deferred affairs — then ordain it for me, make it easy for me, and bless me in it. And if You know that this matter is bad for me in my religion, my livelihood, and my affairs — or: in my immediate and deferred affairs — then turn it away from me and turn me away from it, and ordain for me the good wherever it is, and make me pleased with it.'" [Narrated by Jabir ibn Abdullah, Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 1166 — Tier 1].

This is the foundational Hadith. The istikhara is:
- A prayer of two rakats (a sunnah prayer, not an obligatory one).
- Followed by a specific dua in Arabic (the wording above).
- Made when one is "concerned with a matter" — i.e., facing a decision.
- The matter is named specifically in the dua.
- The ask is for Allah to ordain the good and turn away the bad, regardless of which the matter is.

The Prophet ﷺ taught this as he taught the surahs of the Quran — meaning he taught it as a structured, repeatable, memorized practice. The implication: this is not a once-in-a-lifetime prayer. It is a regular practice for the regular decisions of life.

This file develops the istikhara in full and the related duas the contractor should make for specific business situations.

## Salat al-Istikhara — The Prayer of Seeking Counsel

### What the Istikhara Is

The istikhara is a two-rakat voluntary prayer followed by the specific dua. The conditions for validity are the same as for any prayer: wudu, facing qibla, appropriate covering, intention. The prayer is performed at any time when prayer is permitted (not at the prohibited times — when the sun is rising, at solar noon when the sun is at its zenith, and when the sun is setting). The preferred times are the general preferred times for voluntary prayer — the last third of the night (the tahajjud time, see spiritual/tahajjud_habit.md), after the obligatory prayers, on Friday.

### When to Make the Istikhara

The istikhara is for matters that are not clearly obligatory (where there is no decision to make) or clearly prohibited (where there is also no decision to make). It is for matters in the mubah (permissible) category where the choice is open.

Examples for the contractor:
- Should I bid on this project? (The bid itself is permissible; the istikhara is for the decision.)
- Should I hire this candidate? (Hiring is permissible; the istikhara is for this specific candidate.)
- Should I sign this contract with this developer at these terms? (Signing is permissible; the istikhara is for this specific deal.)
- Should I expand into real estate development now, or wait a year?
- Should I take on this partner for this project?
- Should I open this branch in this city?
- Should I sell this piece of equipment?
- Should I decline this contract because the terms are harsh, or accept and manage the risk?

The istikhara is not for obligatory matters (e.g., "Should I pray fajr?" — there is no decision; the prayer is obligatory). It is not for prohibited matters (e.g., "Should I sign a contract with riba?" — there is no decision; riba is prohibited). It is for the open decisions in between.

### The Specific Wording

The istikhara dua is specific and should be memorized. The Arabic (for the contractor who reads Arabic):

> اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَسْتَخِيرُكَ بِعِلْمِكَ، وَأَسْتَقْدِرُكَ بِقُدْرَتِكَ، وَأَسْأَلُكَ مِنْ فَضْلِكَ، فَإِنَّكَ تَقْدِرُ وَلَا أَقْدِرُ، وَتَعْلَمُ وَلَا أَعْلَمُ، وَأَنْتَ عَلَّامُ الْغُيُوبِ. اللَّهُمَّ إِنْ كُنْتَ تَعْلَمُ أَنَّ هَذَا الْأَمْرَ — [يُسَمَّى بِهِ] — خَيْرٌ لِي فِي دِينِي وَمَعَاشِي وَعَاقِبَةِ أَمْرِي فَاقْدُرْهُ لِي وَيَسِّرْهُ لِي ثُمَّ بَارِكْ لِي فِيهِ. وَإِنْ كُنْتَ تَعْلَمُ أَنَّهُ شَرٌّ لِي فِي دِينِي وَمَعَاشِي وَعَاقِبَةِ أَمْرِي فَاصْرِفْهُ عَنِّي وَاصْرِفْنِي عَنْهُ وَاقْدُرْ لِي الْخَيْرَ حَيْثُ كَانَ ثُمَّ رَضِّنِي بِهِ.

The translation (with the matter named in the place of "[name it]"):

> "O Allah, I seek from You the good by Your knowledge, and I seek strength by Your power, and I ask You of Your great bounty. You have power, I have none. You know, I know not. You are the Knower of the unseen. O Allah, if You know that this matter [name it] is good for me in my religion, my livelihood, and the outcome of my affair — then ordain it for me, make it easy for me, and bless me in it. And if You know that this matter is bad for me in my religion, my livelihood, and the outcome of my affair — then turn it away from me and turn me away from it, and ordain for me the good wherever it is, and make me pleased with it."

The wording is precise. The contractor should memorize it (or keep it on a card in his wallet). The matter should be named specifically in the place where the Hadith says "this matter" — e.g., "this matter of signing the contract with company X for the project Y at the price Z."

### The Interpretation — How the Answer Comes

The most common question: how does the contractor know the answer? The classical scholars (Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani in *Fath al-Bari*, al-Nawawi in his commentary on Sahih Muslim, and others) describe the way the answer comes:

1. **The opening of the matter** — if the matter is good, Allah opens the path to it. The obstacles fall away. The decision becomes clear. The contractor finds himself moving toward the decision with ease.

2. **The closing of the matter** — if the matter is bad, Allah closes the path to it. Obstacles arise. The decision becomes unclear. The contractor finds himself moving away from the decision. (Note: the dua explicitly asks Allah to "turn me away from it" — the closing is not just external; it is also internal. The contractor's own inclination shifts.)

3. **The dream** — some scholars (a minority) hold that the answer may come in a dream. The majority do not require a dream. The dream is a possible channel, not a necessary one. The contractor should not wait for a dream. The opening or the closing is the answer.

4. **The repeat** — the istikhara is repeatable. If the answer is not clear after one istikhara, the contractor may repeat. The scholars generally recommend repeating up to seven times (some say more). After seven, if the answer is still not clear, the contractor should make a decision based on the best judgment and proceed.

### The Common Misunderstandings

The istikhara is widely misunderstood. The common errors:

- **Waiting for a dream** — most contractors should not expect a dream. The opening or the closing of the matter is the answer, not a vision in the night.
- **Treating the istikhara as a magic oracle** — the istikhara is not a slot machine that delivers a yes/no. It is a prayer that asks Allah to guide the outcome. The contractor's role is to make the prayer, take counsel from qualified humans, and act.
- **Skipping the istishara (counsel)** — the istikhara does not replace the istishara. The Prophet ﷺ said:

  > "The one who consults will not regret, and the one who seeks counsel will not fail." [General meaning, attributed to the Prophet ﷺ in al-Tabarani and others — Tier 2, hasan in meaning].

  The istishara (consulting qualified people) is the complement to the istikhara. The contractor who makes istikhara without consulting his accountant, his lawyer, his senior subcontractor, his mentor, is operating on an incomplete practice.
- **Reversing the istikhara** — once the istikhara has been made and the decision taken, the contractor does not reverse the decision because of an obstacle. The obstacle may itself be the means of the good. The istikhara was made; the trust is in Allah.
- **Making istikhara after the decision** — the istikhara is *before* the decision, not after. Once the decision is made and the contract signed, the istikhara is over. The contractor now relies on tawakkul (see spiritual/rizq_and_tawakkul.md).

### The Practical Discipline

The contractor's istikhara practice:

1. **Identify the decision** — write it down specifically. "Should I sign the contract with company X for project Y at price Z, with timeline W?"
2. **Take counsel** — consult the qualified humans (accountant, lawyer, mentor, senior team member). Note their advice.
3. **Perform the istikhara** — two rakats and the specific dua, naming the matter. Preferably at night, in the tahajjud time.
4. **Repeat if necessary** — if the answer is not clear, repeat. Up to seven times (the number is recommended by the scholars, not obligatory).
5. **Act** — once the answer is clear (or after the seven istikharas), make the decision and act. Do not re-litigate.
6. **Accept the outcome** — whatever the outcome, it is from Allah. Gratitude in success, patience in adversity. (See rizq_and_tawakkul.md.)

## Other Duas for Business Situations

The istikhara is for the significant decisions. There are other duas for the specific moments of business life.

### Before a Meeting or Negotiation

The Prophet ﷺ taught the dua for difficult matters:

> "O Allah, I take refuge in You that I should stray or be led astray, or slip or be made to slip, or oppress or be oppressed, or behave foolishly or be treated foolishly." [Narrated by Abu Bakr, Sunan al-Tirmidhi, no. 3515, sahih — Tier 1].

The contractor should make this dua before a difficult negotiation, a meeting with a tough client, a conversation that may go wrong.

### Before Signing a Contract

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "There is no obedience in transgression against the Creator." [Narrated by Ali ibn Abi Talib, Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 7257 — Tier 1].

The dua before signing: "O Allah, if this contract is good for me in my religion and my livelihood, ordain it for me and make it easy. If it is bad for me, turn it away from me and turn me away from it." (This is, in effect, the istikhara — but it is also appropriate as a separate dua at the moment of signing, even after the istikhara has been made.)

### Before a Difficult Conversation

The dua of Musa when he was sent to Pharaoh:

> "My Lord, expand for me my breast, and ease for me my task, and untie the knot from my tongue, that they may understand my speech." [Quran, Surah Ta Ha (20):25-28 — Tier 1].

The contractor should make this dua before a difficult conversation with a client, a subcontractor, a partner, an employee.

### Before Traveling for Work

The Prophet ﷺ made a specific dua for travel:

> "Allah is the Greatest. Allah is the Greatest. Allah is the Greatest. Subhan the One who has subjected this to us, and we could not have done it ourselves. And to our Lord we are returning. O Allah, we ask You in this journey of ours for righteousness and piety, and deeds that please You. O Allah, make this journey of ours easy for us and fold up its distance. O Allah, You are the Companion in the journey and the Guardian of the family." [Narrated by Abdullah ibn Umar, Sahih Muslim, no. 1342 — Tier 1].

The contractor should make this dua when traveling for business — to a remote site, to a meeting in another city, to a client visit abroad.

### Before Sleeping

The Prophet ﷺ taught the dua before sleeping:

> "In Your name, O Allah, I die and I live." [Narrated by Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman, Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 6324 — Tier 1].

And the longer dua for sleeping, which the contractor should learn and practice — it includes seeking refuge in Allah from the punishment of the grave and affirming the faith, in case death comes in the night. The contractor who sleeps with the possibility of death in mind wakes with a different perspective on the day's business.

### On Waking

The Prophet ﷺ taught the dua on waking:

> "Praise be to Allah who gave us life after taking it from us, and to Him is the resurrection." [Narrated by Abu Hurayra, Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 6324 — Tier 1].

The first words of the day are the recognition that the day is from Allah. The contractor's day begins, like the fajr prayer, in submission.

### When Angry

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> "If one of you becomes angry while standing, let him sit. If the anger leaves him, well and good; otherwise, let him lie down." [Narrated by Abu Dharr, Sunan Abi Dawud, no. 4782, sahih — Tier 1].

And the Prophet ﷺ taught:

> "I seek refuge in Allah from Satan." [General meaning, the established formula for seeking refuge from Satan when angry — Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 6115 — Tier 1].

The contractor should make this dua when a subcontractor fails, when a client delays payment, when a team member makes a costly mistake. The anger is real; the response is governed.

### When Distressed or Anxious

The Prophet ﷺ taught the dua of distress:

> "O Allah, Your mercy I hope for, so do not leave me to myself even for the blink of an eye, and set right for me all my affairs. There is no god but You." [Narrated by Anas ibn Malik, Sunan Abi Dawud, no. 5090, sahih — Tier 1].

And the dua of anxiety:

> "O Allah, I seek refuge in You from anxiety and sorrow, from incapacity and laziness, from cowardice and miserliness, from being overcome by debt and oppressed by men." [Narrated by Abu Umama, Sunan al-Bukhari in al-Adab al-Mufrad, no. 703, sahih — Tier 1].

The last part — "from being overcome by debt and oppressed by men" — is the contractor's specific dua. The debt that contractors carry (the financing of projects, the obligations to subcontractors) and the oppression by men (the late-paying clients, the corrupt officials) are the contractor's two recurring burdens. The Prophet ﷺ has given the dua for them.

## The Quranic Duas for Rizq, Protection, and Justice

The Quran contains duas that the contractor should make regularly.

### The Dua for Rizq

The dua of Musa when he was in need:

> "My Lord, I am in need of whatever good You send down to me." [Quran, Surah al-Qasas (28):24 — Tier 1].

The contractor should make this dua when work is slow, when a contract falls through, when the cash flow is tight.

### The Dua for Provision

The dua of the believers generally:

> "Our Lord, give us in this world good and in the Hereafter good, and protect us from the punishment of the Fire." [Quran, Surah al-Baqara (2):201 — Tier 1].

This is the most comprehensive dua for the believer — good in this world (which includes rizq, family, health, reputation) and good in the Hereafter (which includes the reward of the believer's practice) and protection from the Fire. The contractor should make this dua daily.

### The Dua for Protection

The dua of the believers against oppression:

> "Our Lord, do not impose blame upon us for what we have forgotten or erred. Our Lord, and lay not upon us a burden like that which You laid upon those before us. Our Lord, and burden us not with that which we have no ability to bear. And pardon us, and forgive us, and have mercy upon us. You are our protector, so give us victory over the disbelieving people." [Quran, Surah al-Baqara (2):286 — Tier 1].

The contractor should make this dua for protection against the burden beyond his capacity — the contract that is too large, the debt that is too heavy, the team that is too small, the deadline that is too tight.

### The Dua for Justice

The dua of the oppressed:

> "Our Lord, pour upon us patience and make our feet firm and give us victory over the disbelieving people." [Quran, Surah al-Baqara (2):250 — Tier 1].

The contractor who is being wronged by a client (the withheld payment, the fabricated defect claim, the blackmail for a discount) should make this dua. The dua is not a substitute for the worldly means (the legal recourse, the negotiation, the public documentation of the wrong); the dua is the spiritual means alongside the worldly.

### The Dua for the Family

The dua of Ibrahim for the family:

> "My Lord, make this a secure city and provide its people with fruits — whoever of them believes in Allah and the Last Day." [Quran, Surah al-Baqara (2):126 — Tier 1].

And the dua of the believers for their families:

> "Our Lord, grant us from among our spouses and offspring comfort to our eyes, and make us a leader for the righteous." [Quran, Surah al-Furqan (25):74 — Tier 1].

The contractor should make these duas for his family — the family that the work is supporting, the family that the work is taking time from. The dua is the recognition that the family's wellbeing is from Allah, not from the contractor's work alone.

## The Structured Istikhara Practice for the Contractor

Bringing it together, the contractor's structured istikhara practice for a significant decision:

### Phase 1: Preparation (one week before the decision)
- Write the decision down specifically. The specific contract, the specific partner, the specific expansion. One sentence.
- List the qualified counselors (accountant, lawyer, mentor, senior team member, scholar). Schedule meetings or calls with each.
- Begin the daily duas for the decision (the dua for distress, the dua for protection, the dua for provision).
- Begin a light tahajjud (even 10 minutes) to prepare the spiritual frame.

### Phase 2: Counsel (the week of the decision)
- Meet with each counselor. Note their advice. Do not argue; listen.
- List the pros and cons. Identify the key uncertainties.
- Identify the key risks and the proposed mitigations.

### Phase 3: Istikhara (the night before the decision)
- Pray the Isha prayer in congregation.
- Sleep early (after the evening adhkar).
- Wake in the last third of the night (for the tahajjud, see spiritual/tahajjud_habit.md).
- Pray two rakats of tahajjud.
- Pray two rakats of istikhara, with the specific dua, naming the matter specifically.
- Make the related duas (the dua for the decision, the dua for protection, the dua for provision).
- Sleep with the trust that the answer will come.

### Phase 4: The Decision Day
- Pray fajr in the mosque if possible.
- Make the morning adhkar.
- Make the dua of the day: "O Allah, I have taken the means and made the istikhara. The decision is now what You ordain."
- Make the decision and act.

### Phase 5: After the Decision
- Do not re-litigate. The istikhara was made. The trust is now in tawakkul.
- If the decision leads to ease and opening, gratitude.
- If the decision leads to difficulty, patience and an audit of the lessons.
- The istikhara is over. The tawakkul begins.

This practice, repeated for each significant decision, integrates worship and strategy in a way that no secular decision procedure can match. The contractor who maintains this practice over years will accumulate a track record of decisions that were not his alone — they were made in consultation with Allah and the qualified humans, and the outcomes were accepted as from Allah.
