# Timing Principles — When to Move, When to Wait

> **Purpose**: The right strategy at the wrong time is the wrong strategy. The wrong strategy at the right time is sometimes the right strategy. Timing is not a footnote to strategy; timing is strategy. This file teaches the system the timing question (now, soon, or later), the window recognition (when a window opens, when it closes), the momentum question (build, sustain, lose), and the patience vs action framework — the Sima Yi vs Guo Jia contrast that defines the system's timing discipline.

## The Premise

A strategy is a plan plus timing. The plan describes what to do; the timing describes when to do it. The plan without the timing is a list of intentions; the timing without the plan is a series of reactions. Strategy is the integration of plan and timing — the right action at the right moment.

Most strategic failures are not failures of plan but failures of timing. The contractor who enters the market at the right time with the wrong plan captures the opportunity and adjusts the plan. The contractor who enters the market at the wrong time with the right plan misses the opportunity and wastes the plan. The right plan is the foundation; the right timing is the multiplier.

This file is the discipline of timing: when to move, when to wait, when to sustain, when to retreat. The discipline draws on the contrast between Sima Yi (patience) and Guo Jia (action) — the two minds that define the two ends of the timing spectrum, and that the contractor must invoke at different moments.

## The Timing Question: Now, Soon, or Later?

For every strategic decision, the first question is not what to do but when to do it. The three answers — now, soon, or later — correspond to three different timing modes.

### Now: the action timing

The decision to act now is the decision that the window is open, the momentum is favorable, and the cost of waiting exceeds the cost of acting. The "now" timing is the Guo Jia timing — the rapid exploitation of the moment.

**The conditions for "now"**:
- The window is open (the opportunity is available now and may close).
- The momentum is favorable (the contractor's position is improving relative to the opportunity).
- The cost of waiting is high (the opportunity will be lost, the cost will rise, the competitor will enter).
- The contractor is ready (the resources, relationships, and capabilities are in place).

When all four conditions are met, the contractor acts now. When any one is missing, the contractor considers "soon" or "later."

**The risk of "now"**: the contractor who acts now without the readiness (the resources, relationships, capabilities) acts prematurely and fails. The discipline is to verify the readiness before acting; the readiness check is the audit before the action.

### Soon: the preparation timing

The decision to act soon is the decision that the window is not yet open or the contractor is not yet ready, but both will be ready within a defined period. The "soon" timing is the preparation timing — the deliberate work that prepares the action.

**The conditions for "soon"**:
- The window is not yet open but will open within a defined period.
- The momentum is not yet favorable but will become favorable within the period.
- The cost of waiting is moderate (the opportunity is not yet lost, the cost is not yet risen, the competitor is not yet entered).
- The contractor is not yet ready but will be ready within the period.

When all four conditions are met, the contractor prepares and acts soon. The "soon" is the timing of the 12-month plan (per `06_TIME/medium_game.md`) — the deliberate construction that prepares the action.

**The risk of "soon"**: the contractor who chooses "soon" when "now" was appropriate loses the opportunity to the competitor who acted now. The discipline is to verify that "soon" is appropriate, not just convenient; the convenient "soon" is often the disguised "later" that never becomes "now."

### Later: the patience timing

The decision to act later is the decision that the window is not open, the momentum is not favorable, the cost of waiting is low, or the contractor is not ready and will not be ready for a defined period. The "later" timing is the patience timing — the Sima Yi timing that waits for the moment of maximum leverage.

**The conditions for "later"**:
- The window is closed and will not open for a defined period.
- The momentum is unfavorable (the contractor's position is deteriorating relative to the opportunity).
- The cost of waiting is low (the opportunity is not lost, the cost is not risen, the competitor is not entered).
- The contractor is not ready and will not be ready for a defined period.

When all four conditions are met, the contractor waits and acts later. The "later" is the timing of the long game (per `06_TIME/long_game.md`) — the deliberate compounding that prepares the moment.

**The risk of "later"**: the contractor who chooses "later" when "now" or "soon" was appropriate loses the opportunity to the competitor who acted. The discipline is to verify that "later" is appropriate, not just comfortable; the comfortable "later" is often the disguised avoidance that never becomes "now."

## The Window Recognition

A window is a period during which a specific action produces disproportionate effect. Before the window, the action produces little effect; after the window, the action produces little effect. The window is the period of maximum leverage for that action.

### When a window opens

A window opens when the conditions that made the action ineffective change. The change can be external (a market shift, a regulatory change, a competitor's exit, a client's distress) or internal (the contractor's readiness, the contractor's relationship depth, the contractor's capability maturity).

**The signals of an opening window**:
- A previously unavailable opportunity becomes available (the developer who was locked with the established contractor signals openness to alternatives).
- A previously unfavorable cost structure becomes favorable (the cost of the action falls, the return on the action rises).
- A previously insufficient capability becomes sufficient (the contractor has built the capability that the action requires).
- A previously uncertain timeline becomes certain (the deadline that the action must meet becomes clear).

**The discipline**: monitor for the signals continuously. The contractor who monitors catches the window as it opens; the contractor who does not catches the window after it has been open for months, when the competitor has already entered.

### When a window closes

A window closes when the conditions that made the action effective change. The change can be external (the market shift reverses, the competitor enters, the client's distress resolves) or internal (the contractor's readiness erodes, the contractor's relationship weakens, the contractor's capability commoditizes).

**The signals of a closing window**:
- The previously available opportunity becomes unavailable (the developer signs with the competitor).
- The previously favorable cost structure becomes unfavorable (the cost of the action rises, the return on the action falls).
- The previously sufficient capability becomes insufficient (the market requires a higher capability than the contractor has built).
- The previously certain timeline becomes uncertain or compressed (the deadline moves earlier, the action cannot be completed in time).

**The discipline**: monitor for the signals continuously. The contractor who monitors catches the window as it closes and acts before it closes; the contractor who does not catches the window after it has closed, when the action produces little effect.

### The window's three phases

A window has three phases: opening, peak, closing.

**The opening phase**: the window is opening, the conditions are becoming favorable, the action is becoming effective. The contractor who acts in the opening phase captures the early-mover advantage; the contractor who waits for the peak phase captures less advantage.

**The peak phase**: the window is fully open, the conditions are most favorable, the action is most effective. The contractor who acts in the peak phase captures the maximum advantage; the contractor who waits for the closing phase captures less.

**The closing phase**: the window is closing, the conditions are becoming unfavorable, the action is becoming less effective. The contractor who acts in the closing phase captures the late-mover's residual advantage; the contractor who waits for the closed window captures no advantage.

**The discipline**: identify the window's phase and act accordingly. The contractor who acts in the opening phase captures the early-mover advantage; the contractor who acts in the peak phase captures the maximum advantage; the contractor who acts in the closing phase captures the residual advantage. The contractor who waits for the closed window captures nothing.

## The Momentum Question

Momentum is the direction and speed of the contractor's position relative to the opportunity. Positive momentum (the contractor's position is improving) makes the action easier; negative momentum (the contractor's position is deteriorating) makes the action harder. The discipline is to recognize the momentum and to act with it, not against it.

### Build momentum

The contractor builds momentum by the deliberate construction (per `06_TIME/medium_game.md`) — the 12-month plan that builds the capabilities, relationships, and capital. The momentum is built by the compounding investments (per `06_TIME/long_game.md`) that compound the resources over time.

**The signals of building momentum**:
- The contractor's client list grows (new clients, deeper relationships).
- The contractor's capability list grows (new specializations, deeper capabilities).
- The contractor's capital grows (retained earnings, deployable capital).
- The contractor's reputation grows (market recognition, premium pricing).

When the momentum is building, the contractor can act with the momentum — the action that the momentum supports is easier and produces more effect.

### Sustain momentum

The contractor sustains momentum by the maintenance (per `06_TIME/immediate_game.md` — the 30-day cycle that maintains the priorities, and per `05_POSITION/leverage_points.md` — the leverage point protection). The momentum is sustained by the maintenance that prevents the atrophy of the compounding resources.

**The signals of sustained momentum**:
- The contractor's client list is stable (no significant losses, no significant gains).
- The contractor's capability list is stable (no significant additions, no significant erosion).
- The contractor's capital is stable (no significant growth, no significant depletion).
- The contractor's reputation is stable (no significant gains, no significant losses).

When the momentum is sustained, the contractor can act with the momentum — the action that the sustained momentum supports is reliable and predictable.

### Lose momentum

The contractor loses momentum by the neglect (the failure to maintain the relationships, capabilities, capital, and reputation). The momentum is lost by the erosion of the compounding resources that were not maintained.

**The signals of losing momentum**:
- The contractor's client list shrinks (lost clients, weakened relationships).
- The contractor's capability list shrinks (commoditized capabilities, outdated techniques).
- The contractor's capital shrinks (consumed earnings, deployed capital that did not return).
- The contractor's reputation shrinks (negative market chatter, quality problems).

When the momentum is lost, the contractor cannot act with the momentum; the action against the lost momentum is harder and produces less effect. The discipline is to restore the momentum before acting, or to act with the awareness that the action is against the momentum.

### The momentum decision

The momentum decision is the decision to act with the momentum or to wait for the momentum to build. The contractor with positive momentum acts; the contractor with negative momentum waits (or acts to reverse the momentum before the strategic action). The contractor who acts against the negative momentum spends the action; the contractor who waits for the positive momentum captures the action's full effect.

## The Patience vs Action Framework: Sima Yi vs Guo Jia

The two minds that define the timing spectrum are Sima Yi (patience) and Guo Jia (action). The contractor must invoke each at the appropriate moment; the contractor who invokes the wrong mind at the wrong moment makes the wrong timing decision.

### Sima Yi: the patience mind

Sima Yi's mind is the mind that waits for the moment of maximum leverage before acting. The manga shows him waiting for years — through Cao Cao's suspicion, through Cao Pi's reign, through Cao Rui's reign, through Cao Shuang's regency — before the moment of maximum leverage at the Gaoping Tombs Incident of 249 AD. The patience is not passive waiting; it is the active compounding (the relationships, the reputation, the capabilities, the capital) that prepares the moment.

**The conditions for invoking Sima Yi**:
- The window is not yet open (the moment of maximum leverage has not arrived).
- The momentum is building but not yet sufficient (the compounding has not yet produced the decisive position).
- The cost of waiting is low (the opportunity is not lost; the competitor is not yet entered).
- The contractor is not yet ready (the resources, relationships, and capabilities are not yet sufficient for the decisive action).

When all four conditions are met, the contractor invokes Sima Yi and waits — actively compounding, deliberately preparing, patiently monitoring. The contractor who invokes Sima Yi at the wrong moment (when the window is open and the momentum is favorable) loses the opportunity to the competitor who acts.

### Guo Jia: the action mind

Guo Jia's mind is the mind that acts at the moment of maximum leverage without delay. The manga shows him acting rapidly — at Cao Cao's side, in the campaigns against Yuan Shao, in the rapid strikes that exploited Yuan Shao's structural weaknesses. The action is not impulsive; it is the rapid exploitation of the moment that the patient preparation has prepared.

**The conditions for invoking Guo Jia**:
- The window is open (the moment of maximum leverage has arrived).
- The momentum is favorable (the contractor's position is improving relative to the opportunity).
- The cost of waiting is high (the opportunity will be lost; the competitor will enter).
- The contractor is ready (the resources, relationships, and capabilities are sufficient for the action).

When all four conditions are met, the contractor invokes Guo Jia and acts — rapidly, decisively, with the full commitment of the resources that the patience has prepared. The contractor who invokes Guo Jia at the wrong moment (when the window is not yet open and the momentum is not yet favorable) acts prematurely and fails.

### The Sima Yi to Guo Jia transition

The contractor's career moves through phases of Sima Yi (patience) and Guo Jia (action) in sequence. The contractor who is in a Sima Yi phase (compounding, preparing, waiting for the window) should not act prematurely; the contractor who is in a Guo Jia phase (the window is open, the momentum is favorable) should not wait passively.

**The transition signals**:
- The Sima Yi to Guo Jia transition: the window opens, the momentum becomes favorable, the cost of waiting rises, the contractor becomes ready. The contractor transitions from patience to action.
- The Guo Jia to Sima Yi transition: the window closes, the momentum becomes less favorable, the cost of waiting falls, the contractor becomes less ready (the action consumed the readiness). The contractor transitions from action to patience.

**The discipline**: recognize the phase and invoke the appropriate mind. The contractor who is in a Sima Yi phase but invokes Guo Jia acts prematurely; the contractor who is in a Guo Jia phase but invokes Sima Yi waits too long. The two minds are not in competition; they are in sequence. The discipline is to know which phase the contractor is in and to invoke the corresponding mind.

### The combined mind: Sima Yi and Guo Jia together

The contractor who masters both minds uses them together. The Sima Yi mind prepares the moment; the Guo Jia mind exploits the moment. The patience and the action are not opposed; they are the two halves of the same strategic cycle. The contractor who is patient without acting fails to exploit the moment; the contractor who acts without being patient fails to prepare the moment. The contractor who is patient and then acts captures both the preparation and the exploitation.

**The discipline**: invoke Sima Yi during the preparation phases and Guo Jia during the exploitation phases. The two minds are complementary; the contractor who masters both captures the full timing discipline.

## The Application to the Egyptian Contractor's Domain

### Phase 1 (electrical subcontractor) — the timing

**The Sima Yi phase**: years 1-2 of Phase 1. The contractor builds the foundation (the specialization, the relationships, the capital). The window for the Phase 2 transition is not yet open. The contractor waits — actively compounding, deliberately preparing, patiently monitoring for the opening.

**The Guo Jia phase**: year 3 of Phase 1. The window for the Phase 2 transition opens (the contractor has the specialization, the relationships, and the capital for the transition). The contractor acts — enters the general contracting market, bids on the first general contracting project, executes the transition.

**The transition signal**: the contractor's specialization is deep enough to provide the asymmetric advantage in the general contracting market; the contractor's relationships with developers are deep enough to support the broader scope; the contractor's capital is sufficient for the larger projects.

### Phase 2 (general contractor) — the timing

**The Sima Yi phase**: years 4-5 of the trajectory. The contractor builds the general contracting position (the capabilities, the relationships, the team, the projects). The window for the Phase 3 transition is not yet open. The contractor waits — actively compounding, deliberately preparing, patiently monitoring for the opening.

**The Guo Jia phase**: year 6 of the trajectory. The window for the Phase 3 transition opens (the contractor has the general contracting track record, the relationships with developers and lenders, and the capital for the development phase). The contractor acts — enters the real estate development market, acquires the first land, executes the first development project.

**The transition signal**: the contractor's general contracting track record is sufficient to support the development track record; the contractor's relationships with lenders are deep enough to support the development financing; the contractor's capital is sufficient for the first development project.

### Phase 3 (real estate developer) — the timing

**The Sima Yi phase**: years 7-9 of the trajectory. The contractor builds the developer position (the development capabilities, the lender and landowner relationships, the development track record). The window for the developer phase's first project's success is not yet open. The contractor waits — actively compounding, deliberately preparing, patiently monitoring for the opening.

**The Guo Jia phase**: year 10 of the trajectory. The window for the developer phase's first project's success opens (the project is delivered, the buyers are satisfied, the lenders are repaid, the track record is established). The contractor acts — executes the next development project, expands the developer position, captures the long-game compounding.

**The transition signal**: the first development project's success is sufficient to support the second development project's financing; the contractor's development track record is sufficient to support the larger projects; the contractor's development capabilities are sufficient to manage multiple projects.

## Common Errors in Timing

### Error 1: Acting prematurely

The contractor who acts before the window opens acts prematurely and fails. The discipline is to verify the window is open before acting; the verification is the audit before the action.

### Error 2: Waiting too long

The contractor who waits after the window opens waits too long and loses the opportunity. The discipline is to act when the window opens, not to wait for the "perfect" moment (which does not exist).

### Error 3: Acting against the momentum

The contractor who acts when the momentum is negative acts against the momentum and spends the action. The discipline is to restore the momentum before acting, or to act with the awareness that the action is against the momentum.

### Error 4: Invoking Sima Yi when Guo Jia is appropriate

The contractor who waits when the window is open and the momentum is favorable loses the opportunity. The discipline is to recognize the Guo Jia phase and to act.

### Error 5: Invoking Guo Jia when Sima Yi is appropriate

The contractor who acts when the window is not open and the momentum is not favorable acts prematurely. The discipline is to recognize the Sima Yi phase and to wait — actively, with deliberate compounding.

### Error 6: Treating patience as passivity

The Sima Yi patience is not passive waiting; it is active compounding. The contractor who waits passively (without compounding) for the window to open arrives at the window unprepared. The discipline is to wait actively — to compound the resources, deepen the relationships, build the capabilities, accumulate the capital — so that when the window opens, the contractor is ready to act with the Guo Jia decisiveness.

### Error 7: Treating action as impulsiveness

The Guo Jia action is not impulsive; it is the rapid exploitation of the moment that the patience has prepared. The contractor who acts impulsively (without preparation) acts without the readiness that the action requires. The discipline is to act after the preparation — the action that follows the patience is the action that captures the moment.
