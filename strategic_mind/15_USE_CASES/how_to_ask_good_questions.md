## Why this file exists

This is the most leveraged file in the system for the user to read. Every other file in the system — the 7 minds, the 16 layers, the 200+ knowledge files — is a reservoir that the user draws from by asking questions. A question is the valve. A good question opens the valve wide; a bad question opens it a trickle. The system can hold ten thousand pages of strategic knowledge and still return a trivial answer to a trivial question. The system can hold the same ten thousand pages and return a life-changing answer to a well-formed question. The variable is not the system. The variable is the question.

This file exists because the user will spend more time asking questions than reading answers. The cost of a bad question is not just a weak answer — it is the false confidence of having "consulted the system" when in fact the system was not properly consulted. The bad question is the user's most expensive habit. The good question is the user's most leveraged skill.

This file is the user's discipline. It is not the system's discipline. The system has its own discipline (the 7-step pipeline, the self-audit, the multi-hypothesis engine). But the system can only do its work on the question the user gives it. Garbage in, garbage out. The user's responsibility is the input. The system's responsibility is the processing. This file is about the input.

## The core premise

**The quality of the answer is bounded above by the quality of the question.** A brilliant system cannot produce a brilliant answer to a stupid question. A stupid system can sometimes produce a brilliant answer to a brilliant question — because the brilliant question carries most of the strategic content inside itself. The question is the larger half of the answer.

This is the opposite of how most people think about advisors. Most people think the advisor's intelligence is the variable. They are wrong. The advisor's intelligence is a multiplier. The question is the base. A multiplier of 10x applied to a base of 1 produces 10. A multiplier of 5x applied to a base of 100 produces 500. The base matters more than the multiplier.

The user's job is to maximize the base. The system's job is to provide a multiplier worth applying. This file is about the base.

## The seven characteristics of a good question

A good question has seven characteristics. A question does not need all seven to be useful, but the more characteristics it has, the deeper the answer. The characteristics are listed in roughly decreasing importance, but the first three are co-equal — without any of them, the question collapses.

### Characteristic 1: Specific

A specific question names the actual entities, the actual numbers, the actual people, the actual constraints. A vague question names categories.

**Vague**: "Should I take this contract?"
**Specific**: "Should I take a 14-month, EGP 4.2M electrical fit-out for a New Cairo residential tower from a private developer (Talaat Moustafa Group sub-developer), with 25% advance, 60% progress, 15% on completion, and 10% retention for 6 months, given my current pipeline has 2 active projects and EGP 800K working capital?"

The specific question gives the system real numbers to model. The vague question forces the system to either guess the numbers (which it should refuse to do) or ask the user to specify them (which costs a round-trip). The specific question lets the system apply `09_BUSINESS/contracting/cash_flow_zero_capital.md`, `09_BUSINESS/contracting/project_bidding.md`, and `09_BUSINESS/contracting/client_psychology_egypt.md` directly. The vague question makes those files useless — they cannot be applied to numbers that do not exist.

The rule: **if the system has to guess a number, the question is not specific enough.**

### Characteristic 2: Contextual

A contextual question supplies the situation around the question — not just the question itself, but what produced it, what surrounds it, what the user has tried, what the user fears.

**Context-free**: "Should I hire a project manager?"
**Contextual**: "I am at EGP 12M annual revenue with 3 active projects, running operations myself with one site engineer. I am losing roughly one day a week to firefighting on the smallest project, and the largest project has slipped 18 days because I could not be on site during a critical concrete pour. I am considering hiring a project manager at EGP 18-25K/month. My fear is that I will hire someone, lose control of quality, and have to redo work — which I have seen happen to two contractors I know. Should I hire a project manager, and if so, when, and at what scope?"

The contextual question gives the system the actual problem (not the symptom), the actual constraint (one site engineer, firefighting pattern), the actual fear (quality loss, observed in peers), and the actual decision space (when, at what scope). The context-free question gets a generic answer about delegation. The contextual question gets a specific answer about whether the bottleneck is really a project manager, or whether the bottleneck is the user's refusal to delegate decisions, or whether the bottleneck is the smallest project (which should perhaps be declined next time).

The rule: **the context is half the question. A question without context is half a question.**

### Characteristic 3: With stakes

A question with stakes states what the user stands to gain or lose, and over what time horizon. Stakes are not optional — they determine whether the question deserves the full pipeline or a quick answer.

**No stakes**: "What do you think about partnerships?"
**With stakes**: "I have been offered a 30% equity stake in a 50-unit residential development in 6th of October City, in exchange for my electrical work contribution (valued at roughly EGP 3M) over 18 months. If the project succeeds, my share is worth EGP 15-25M at completion. If the project fails (the developer has had one stalled project before), I lose 18 months of cash flow and possibly EGP 1.5M in materials I fronted. I have a wife and three children; this is more than 60% of my working capital. The decision window is 3 weeks."

The stakes-bearing question tells the system: this is a high-stakes, irreversible, multi-year decision affecting family security and working capital. The system will invoke the full pipeline — diagnosis, multi-mind routing (Sima Yi for long-game, Cao Cao for multi-stakeholder, Liu Bei for relationship), multi-hypothesis generation, self-audit, and a structured answer with confidence scoring. The no-stakes question gets a short educational answer about partnership structures.

The rule: **if the user does not state the stakes, the system cannot calibrate the response.** A high-stakes question deserves the full pipeline; a low-stakes question does not. Stated stakes are the user's contract with the system about how much processing the question deserves.

### Characteristic 4: With constraints

A question with constraints states what the user cannot do, will not do, or is unable to spend. Constraints make the answer actionable; without them, the answer is a wish-list.

**Without constraints**: "How do I scale my business?"
**With constraints**: "How do I scale from EGP 8M to EGP 20M annual revenue in 24 months, without taking bank financing (riba), without diluting equity below 75%, without hiring more than 2 additional engineers, and without expanding beyond electrical work plus small-scale residential real estate development?"

The constrained question gives the system a real optimization problem. The unconstrained question gives the system a fantasy. The system's answer to the unconstrained question is "raise capital, hire, expand geographically, add services" — every one of which the user has implicitly rejected. The constrained question forces the system to find the path that fits the user's actual life, not the path that fits a generic growth model.

Constraints include: financial (working capital, cash flow, financing restrictions), ethical (halal, no deception, no harm), time (deadline, decision window, project duration), relational (family commitments, partnership obligations, employee loyalty), regulatory (license class, tax exposure, labor law), geographic (Cairo-only, New Cairo-only, one governorate), and personal (health, energy, age, family stage).

The rule: **the system is most useful when the user tells it what the user cannot do.** The constraints are the walls of the room the system must furnish. Without walls, the system furnishes an open field.

### Characteristic 5: With time horizon

A question with a time horizon tells the system whether to invoke long-game minds (Sima Yi, Liu Bei), medium-game minds (Cao Cao, Guo Jia), or immediate-game minds (Lu Bu, Sun Ce). Time horizon is the single most important routing signal after stakes.

**No time horizon**: "Should I enter real estate development?"
**With time horizon**: "Should I enter real estate development as my primary business by year-end 2027 (i.e., 18 months from now), with the goal of completing my first self-developed 8-unit building by mid-2029, while keeping electrical contracting as a cash-flow engine for the next 5 years?"

The time-horizon question routes the system to a multi-stage strategy: short-term (preserve electrical cash flow), medium-term (build the legal and capital base for development), long-term (development as primary business). The system can apply `06_TIME/long_game.md`, `06_TIME/medium_game.md`, and `06_TIME/immediate_game.md` in sequence. The no-horizon question gets a generic answer about whether real estate is a good business.

The rule: **time horizon is the user's most explicit signal to the routing engine.** Stating it eliminates a major source of misrouting.

### Characteristic 6: With what's been considered

A question that states what the user has already considered prevents the system from re-stating the obvious, and signals to the system the user's level of strategic sophistication. This raises the floor of the answer.

**Without**: "How should I bid on a government tender?"
**With what's been considered**: "I have already considered: (a) the bid decision framework in `09_BUSINESS/contracting/project_bidding.md` — I have applied all six filters and the bid passes; (b) the pre-bid investigation — the ministry's budget is funded, the project is a re-tender of a default, the consultant is acceptable; (c) the competitive landscape — three competitors likely to bid, one is desperate and may underprice. What I have NOT decided: whether to bid at a 12% margin (competitive) or 18% margin (with risk premium for the re-tender risk). The decision is in 5 days."

The "with what's been considered" question gets the system to focus on the actual undecided sub-question — the margin decision — and to apply `09_BUSINESS/contracting/pricing_methodology.md` directly to the margin question. The "without" question gets a full bid framework recital that wastes the user's time and the system's processing.

The rule: **the user who tells the system what they have already considered saves a round-trip and raises the answer's depth.** The system can spend its cycles on the open question, not on the closed ones.

### Characteristic 7: With what's been ruled out

A question that states what the user has ruled out — and ideally why — gives the system the user's prior strategic moves. This is the deepest of the seven characteristics because it shows the user's reasoning, not just the user's situation.

**Without**: "Should I partner with this developer?"
**With what's been ruled out**: "I have ruled out: (a) financing the development myself with bank financing — riba is forbidden; (b) taking a 50%+ equity stake — the developer will not accept it and I do not want operational control of a 50-unit building with no prior development experience; (c) walking away — the developer is the brother of a close friend, and the relationship has long-game value beyond this deal. The remaining options are 30% equity or a fixed-fee contract for the electrical work with a small success bonus. I am leaning toward 30% equity. Is that the right call?"

The "with what's been ruled out" question lets the system see the user's reasoning (riba-averse, equity-averse for now, relationship-preserving), the actual decision space (30% equity vs fixed fee), and the user's lean. The system can now audit the lean (is 30% equity really the right call, given the developer's one stalled project?), explore the alternative (fixed fee with bonus), and check the ruled-out options (was bank financing really ruled out correctly, or was it ruled out on a false assumption?). The "without" question gets a generic partnership answer.

The rule: **what the user has ruled out reveals the user's reasoning. Reasoning is more informative than situation.**

## The bad question patterns

There are four bad question patterns the user must learn to recognize in himself. All four produce weak answers because they withhold information the system needs.

### Pattern 1: The too-vague question

**Form**: "What should I do about [category]?"

**Example**: "What should I do about marketing?"

**Why it fails**: The system does not know what the user's marketing situation is, what the user's marketing goal is, what the user's marketing budget is, or what the user has tried. Any answer is a guess.

**Fix**: Specify the entity, the goal, the budget, the time horizon, and what's been tried. "I have EGP 100K to spend on acquiring one new private-developer client in the next 6 months. I have tried: Federation of Contractors events (low conversion), LinkedIn outreach (zero conversion), and word-of-mouth through existing clients (the source of my last 3 clients). What should I do with the EGP 100K?"

### Pattern 2: The too-generic question

**Form**: "Is [X] a good idea?"

**Example**: "Is real estate a good idea?"

**Why it fails**: "Good" is undefined. Good for whom? Under what conditions? At what price? With what capital? Generic questions are unanswerable because the truth of any strategic proposition is conditional.

**Fix**: Replace "good idea" with a specific decision. "Is entering real estate development in 6th of October City, in partnership with developer X, with EGP 3M of my own working capital at risk, a good idea given my current EGP 8M revenue base and 2 active electrical projects?"

### Pattern 3: The no-context question

**Form**: "Should I do X?" — without any of the surrounding situation.

**Example**: "Should I take this bid?"

**Why it fails**: The system cannot answer without knowing the bid, the alternative, the pipeline, the capital, the relationship, the time, the ethical constraints. The no-context question forces the system to either refuse to answer (the honest response) or to fabricate context (the dishonest response, which the system is forbidden from doing).

**Fix**: Provide the situation around the question. See Characteristic 2.

### Pattern 4: The no-stakes question

**Form**: "What do you think about X?" — without specifying what the user stands to lose.

**Example**: "What do you think about expanding into general contracting?"

**Why it fails**: The system cannot calibrate the response. If the user is asking idly over coffee, a short answer suffices. If the user is asking before signing a lease on a new office and hiring 4 staff, the answer deserves a full pipeline. Without stakes, the system guesses — and usually guesses low, which under-serves the user.

**Fix**: State what is at stake, in money, time, and irreversible commitment. See Characteristic 3.

## The Egyptian contractor's five signature question types

The user's domain produces five recurring question types. Each type has a specific structure that the system recognizes and processes well. The user should learn to recognize which type he is asking, and to include the type-specific elements.

### Type 1: The bidding question

**Structure**: A specific bid opportunity, with a client, a scope, a value, a payment schedule, a competitive landscape, a deadline, and a strategic-fit question.

**Template**: "I am considering bidding on [project description] for [client] valued at [EGP amount] over [duration] with [advance/progress/retention schedule]. The competitors likely to bid are [list]. The bid deadline is [date]. My current pipeline is [state]. Should I bid, and if so, at what margin and with what technical emphasis?"

**Why it works**: This invokes `09_BUSINESS/contracting/project_bidding.md`, `09_BUSINESS/contracting/pricing_methodology.md`, `09_BUSINESS/contracting/client_psychology_egypt.md`, and the minds of Guo Jia (for rapid read of competitors) and Sima Yi (for the strategic-fit question). The system can apply the bid decision framework's six filters directly.

### Type 2: The partnership question

**Structure**: A specific partnership offer, with a partner, a structure (equity, profit-share, fixed fee), a contribution (cash, work, both), a duration, a governance question, and an exit question.

**Template**: "I have been offered a [X]% equity stake / [Y]% profit share in [project] by [partner], in exchange for [my contribution]. The project is [description] over [duration]. The partner's track record is [state]. The governance arrangement is [state]. The exit mechanism is [state]. My working capital exposure is [EGP]. Should I accept, reject, or counter, and on what terms?"

**Why it works**: This invokes `09_BUSINESS/halal_business/mudarabah_partnership.md`, `09_BUSINESS/real_estate/construction_to_development.md`, and the minds of Sima Yi (long-game), Cao Cao (multi-stakeholder), and Liu Bei (relationship). The system can apply the partnership audit framework directly.

### Type 3: The scaling question

**Structure**: A current state (revenue, crew, capital, license), a target state (revenue, geography, service mix), a time horizon, a set of constraints (halal, no dilution, family), and a path question.

**Template**: "I am currently at [state], with [constraints]. I want to be at [target state] in [time horizon], without violating [constraints]. What is the path, and what are the stages?"

**Why it works**: This invokes `09_BUSINESS/entrepreneurship/scaling_from_zero.md`, `09_BUSINESS/entrepreneurship/market_domination.md`, and the minds of Cao Cao (comprehensive strategy), Sima Yi (long-game), and Sun Ce (kinetic expansion). The system can produce a staged plan with milestones.

### Type 4: The spiritual question

**Structure**: A specific decision with a spiritual dimension (riba, gharar, halal income, intention), a factual situation, a question about permissibility or about the better of two permissible options.

**Template**: "I am considering [decision], which involves [factual situation]. The relevant Islamic considerations appear to be [list]. Is this permissible, and if so, is it the better of the permissible options, or should I choose [alternative]?"

**Why it works**: This invokes `09_BUSINESS/halal_business/islamic_commercial_fiqh.md`, `09_BUSINESS/halal_business/riba_free_finance.md`, `10_PERSONAL/spiritual/practical_faith.md`, and `10_PERSONAL/spiritual/rizq_and_tawakkul.md`. The system will be explicit about the limits of its fiqh authority (it is not a scholar) and route borderline cases to scholarly consultation.

### Type 5: The personal question

**Structure**: A specific personal decision (marriage, family, health, time allocation, fear, ambition), with a factual situation, a stakes statement, and a question about the choice or about the reasoning.

**Template**: "I am facing [personal decision]. The facts are [situation]. The stakes are [emotional, relational, time]. My fear is [stated fear]. My ambition is [stated ambition]. What is the right call, and what does the right reasoning look like?"

**Why it works**: This invokes `10_PERSONAL/psychology_self/self_knowledge.md`, `10_PERSONAL/psychology_self/ambition_management.md`, `10_PERSONAL/psychology_self/fear_of_success.md`, and the minds of Liu Bei (heart), Chen Gong (honest counsel), and Sima Yi (long-game on the life arc).

## The meta-question

The single most powerful question the user can ask the system is:

**"What should I be asking that I am not asking?"**

This is the meta-question. It asks the system to step back from the user's stated question and examine what the user has failed to ask. The meta-question is powerful because the user's blind spots are, by definition, invisible to the user. The system can see them because the system has the full architecture (the 16 layers, the 7 minds, the 200+ files) and can check which layers the user has not touched.

The system, asked the meta-question, will:
1. Examine the user's stated question and the context provided.
2. Identify which layers and minds have been implicitly invoked.
3. Identify which layers and minds have NOT been invoked — and ask why.
4. Generate 2-5 questions the user has not asked but should.
5. Present those questions, with the reason each is strategically important.

**Example output**:

> User asked: "Should I bid on the EGP 5M New Cairo tower bid?"
>
> Meta-question response: "You have asked about the bid. You have not asked about: (a) the post-bid cash flow — if you win, how will you finance the working capital gap between the 25% advance and the progress payments, given your current pipeline? (b) the crew — if you win, who runs the project, given that your senior site engineer is already committed to your two existing projects? (c) the relationship — if you win, what is the plan to convert this one-off project into a 3-project relationship with this developer over the next 5 years? (d) the alternative — what is the next-best use of the working capital you will commit to this bid, and is that alternative more attractive than this bid? (e) the exit — if the project goes bad at month 8, what is your exit, and what does the contract say about termination? These five questions are likely more important than the bid/no-bid question you asked, because the bid is a single decision and these are the structural decisions that determine whether winning the bid is actually good for you."

The meta-question is the user's single most leveraged prompt. The user should ask it after every major question, before acting on the system's answer. The cost of asking is one extra round; the cost of not asking is acting on an incomplete picture.

## Worked examples of question re-framing

The following four re-framings show the user the difference a good question makes. Each is a real pattern (anonymized and adapted to the Egyptian contractor context).

### Re-framing 1: From vague to specific

**Bad**: "How do I grow my business?"

**Re-framed**: "I am an Egyptian electrical contractor at EGP 9M annual revenue, 4 active projects, 1 senior site engineer, 2 junior engineers, 12 electricians, EGP 600K working capital, no debt, no equity partners. My strategic goal is EGP 25M annual revenue by year-end 2028 (3 years). My constraints: halal only (no bank financing), no equity dilution below 80%, no geographic expansion beyond Greater Cairo, no service expansion beyond electrical + small residential real estate development. What is the staged path, and what is the binding constraint at each stage?"

**Why the re-frame works**: The re-framed question gives the system a current state, a target state, a time horizon, four explicit constraints, and asks for both the path and the binding constraints. The system can produce a specific 3-year plan with stage gates. The bad question would have produced a generic growth discussion.

### Re-framing 2: From no-context to contextual

**Bad**: "Should I hire my cousin?"

**Re-framed**: "My cousin (father's brother's son, age 28, electrical engineering graduate, 3 years site experience with another contractor) wants to join my firm. I am at EGP 9M annual revenue with 4 active projects and 1 senior site engineer who is overloaded. The cousin would be a second senior site engineer, paid at EGP 22K/month (slightly above market for his experience). The relational stakes: my father and his father are close; refusing the cousin will create family tension that my father will absorb; hiring the cousin and he fails will create family tension that I will absorb; the cousin's prior employer is a competitor I respect and do not want to alienate. Should I hire him, and if so, under what conditions?"

**Why the re-frame works**: The re-framed question gives the system the actual relational geometry (cousin's family relationship, the fathers' bond, the prior employer), the actual stakes (family tension either way), the actual decision (hire / not hire / conditional hire), and the actual constraints (the prior employer relationship). The system can apply `10_PERSONAL/psychology_self/relationships.md`, `04_PSYCHOLOGY/group_dynamics.md`, `09_BUSINESS/contracting/subcontractor_management.md`, and the mind of Liu Bei (relational legitimacy). The bad question would have produced a generic discussion of nepotism.

### Re-framing 3: From no-stakes to with-stakes

**Bad**: "Is real estate development a good idea?"

**Re-framed**: "I am deciding whether to enter real estate development as my primary business by year-end 2027. The stakes: I would commit roughly EGP 2.5M of working capital (50% of my liquid reserves) to a first 8-unit residential building in New Cairo, in partnership with a capital provider under a mudarabah structure (per `09_BUSINESS/halal_business/mudarabah_partnership.md`). If I do not enter, I remain a pure electrical contractor at EGP 9M revenue with a 12-15% net margin, which is comfortable but capped. If I enter and fail, I lose 2.5 years and possibly the EGP 2.5M. If I enter and succeed, the path is open to EGP 50M+ revenue by 2032. I have a wife and three children; the family reserves outside this commitment are EGP 2.5M. Should I enter, and if so, on what timeline?"

**Why the re-frame works**: The re-framed question gives the system the stakes (50% of liquid reserves, family impact, three outcomes with consequences), the time horizon (2027, 2032), the structure (mudarabah), the alternatives, and the personal situation. The system can apply `06_TIME/long_game.md`, `05_POSITION/resource_mapping.md`, `09_BUSINESS/real_estate/construction_to_development.md`, and the minds of Sima Yi (long-game), Sun Ce (kinetic founder), and Cao Cao (comprehensive strategy). The bad question would have produced a generic market analysis.

### Re-framing 4: From open to constrained and ruled-out

**Bad**: "What's the best way to finance my business?"

**Re-framed**: "I need to raise EGP 3M to finance my working capital gap for an 18-month project. I have ruled out: (a) conventional bank financing — riba is forbidden; (b) selling equity below 80% — I want to retain control; (c) family loans — my father has offered, but accepting would shift family dynamics I do not want to shift. The options I see: (1) advance payment negotiation with the client (try to move from 25% to 35% advance); (2) supplier credit extension (90-day terms on materials); (3) a mudarabah with a halal investor (per `09_BUSINESS/halal_business/mudarabah_partnership.md`), giving up 30% of project profits. Which is the best fit, and in what combination?"

**Why the re-frame works**: The re-framed question gives the system three ruled-out options (with reasons), three open options (with structure), and asks for a fit-and-combination analysis. The system can apply `09_BUSINESS/halal_business/riba_free_finance.md`, `09_BUSINESS/contracting/cash_flow_zero_capital.md`, and the mind of Cao Cao (multi-stakeholder resource allocation). The bad question would have produced a generic finance-options list including the three the user has already rejected.

## Closing discipline

The user who masters this file will produce questions that get answers worth acting on. The user who does not will produce questions that get answers worth ignoring. The system is the same in both cases; only the question differs.

The four-discipline summary for the user:

1. **Before asking, write the question down.** A spoken question is half-formed. A written question forces the user to specify the numbers, the context, the stakes, the constraints, and the ruled-out options.
2. **Apply the seven characteristics as a checklist.** Does the question have all seven? If not, can it?
3. **State the meta-question separately.** "What should I be asking that I am not?" — written as a separate prompt, after the main question. The system will answer both.
4. **Push back on the answer if it does not fit.** A bad answer is often the symptom of a question that was not specific enough. Re-ask, do not argue with the answer.

The user's discipline is the system's most powerful input. The system is built to do hard work, but only on questions worthy of the work.
