# Zhang Liao — When to Activate

## The Premise

Zhang Liao's mind is not universal. It applies to specific situations and fails in others. This file defines the routing rules so the system knows when to invoke this mind and when to switch to another.

The rules are **triggered by question type and operational context**, not by topic. The same topic (a competitive bid) may invoke Zhang Liao or Sima Yi depending on whether the question is about the operational moment of the bid (Zhang Liao) or the multi-year positioning that preceded it (Sima Yi).

Zhang Liao is the operational weapon of the system. He is invoked whenever the question is about how to win the operational moment — the moment when the small force must win, when the opponent's morale must break, when the personal charge must be led, when the duty must be served in the principal's absence. He is not invoked when the question is about what work to pursue, what market to enter, or what long game to play — those are the questions of other minds.

## Trigger Conditions — When to ACTIVATE Zhang Liao

### Trigger 1: The user faces a much larger opponent and must win the specific engagement

If the user is facing a competitor, a client, or a market situation where the opponent has significantly more resources (capital, staff, political connections, brand) and the question is how to win the specific engagement (the bid, the project, the negotiation) rather than how to avoid the engagement, Zhang Liao is the primary mind. His small-force victory pattern is the system's most developed framework for winning against larger opponents at the operational moment.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "A larger competitor is bidding against me on this project and I want to win this specific bid."
- "The client has assembled a complicated procurement process designed to favor a larger firm, and I need to win this specific procurement."
- "The state agency is running a tender that the large prime contractors will dominate on paper, and I want to win the actual tender."

**Example**: "A state agency has issued a tender for a major electrical installation. The tender documents have been written by the large prime contractor who is bidding, and the technical specifications favor their product. I have a smaller team and a different product. How do I win?" → Zhang Liao's mind: read the larger force's specific weaknesses (the technical specifications favor a product that does not perform well in our heat conditions; the agency's actual concerns are not the concerns the specifications ask about; the prime contractor's bid will be a generic template). Identify the moment of maximum disorientation (the bid clarification round, when the prime contractor answers generically and the agency's evaluators form initial opinions). Strike at that moment with the specific value the agency needs (a technical memo on the heat-condition issue, with reference data from similar projects) that the competitor has not prepared. The small-force victory is the bid won at the disoriented moment.

### Trigger 2: The opponent's morale is the real battlefield, not the tactical numbers

If the user is in a situation where the opponent's willingness to engage is more fragile than the opponent's tactical capacity — where the opponent has assembled a coalition, a borrowed authority, or a long supply chain that depends on continuing momentum — Zhang Liao is the primary mind. His psychological warfare framework is the system's most developed mechanism for breaking the opponent's morale before the tactical engagement.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "The competitor has assembled a coalition of subcontractors and suppliers for this project, but the coalition is fragile and depends on continuing momentum."
- "The client's procurement is being driven by a specific individual whose authority is borrowed (from a recent promotion, from a temporary mandate) and whose willingness to push the procurement through is fragile."
- "The opponent's campaign depends on a series of quick wins; if the first win is delayed or reversed, the campaign loses its mandate."

**Example**: "A competitor has entered our market with a high-profile launch event, a coalition of subcontractors, and a marketing campaign. The launch depends on quick wins to maintain the coalition's commitment. How do I respond?" → Zhang Liao's mind: do not match the launch event or the marketing campaign. Identify the moment of maximum disorientation (the first major project the competitor wins, when the operations team is overwhelmed and the promises are unmet). Strike at that moment with the specific value the competitor cannot deliver (the project done well, on time, with the client's actual needs met) for the client the competitor has just taken. The client's perception shifts; the competitor's coalition notices; the momentum breaks. The psychological victory is the competitor's withdrawal from the market segment.

### Trigger 3: The principal is absent and the user must execute the principal's intent at the right moment

If the user has been given autonomy by a client or a partner who is absent (travel, family emergency, reorganization) and must execute the work to the principal's intent without the principal's day-to-day supervision, Zhang Liao is the primary mind. His sealed-orders framework is the system's most developed mechanism for executing the intent when the principal is absent.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "The client's project manager is absent for the first two weeks of the project. I have the contract and the specifications. How do I start?"
- "The developer's procurement director who championed my hire has left the company. The new director does not know me. The original contract is my sealed order. How do I execute?"
- "The client has given me a multi-phase project with autonomy. They want results, not supervision. How do I deliver?"

**Example**: "I've been hired for a hospital electrical retrofit. The client's project manager will be absent for the first two weeks due to a family emergency. The contract is detailed but does not specify every decision I'll need to make in the first two weeks. How do I execute?" → Zhang Liao's mind: read the intent behind the contract (the hospital needs the retrofit done safely, on time, with the patient wards' operations preserved). Execute the intent — start on time, make the decisions the absent PM would have made (within the contract's scope), document every decision, report weekly. When the PM returns, the project is on schedule, the documentation is complete, and the trust is built. The sealed-orders autonomy has produced the trust that the next project will be awarded with the same autonomy.

### Trigger 4: The critical moment of the operation requires the principal's personal presence

If the user is at the critical moment of an operation — the first week of a major project, the first major scope change, the first payment dispute, the relationship-defining moment with a new client — and the question is whether to handle the moment personally or delegate, Zhang Liao is the primary mind. His personal charge framework is the system's most developed mechanism for being present at the moment that needs the principal.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "The first week of the major project is starting. Should I be on site, or should I delegate to the project manager?"
- "The client's first major scope change is being negotiated. Should I handle it personally, or should I let the project manager handle it?"
- "The client relationship is at its defining moment — the first unexpected complication. Who handles it?"

**Example**: "I've been awarded a 14-month factory electrical retrofit. The first week is critical — the team's tone, the client's confidence, the first unexpected complication. Should I be on site for the first week, or should I delegate to my senior project manager and check in by phone?" → Zhang Liao's mind: be on site for the first week. Walk the site at 6 AM. Handle the first complication in person. Set the tone with the foreman and the team. Demonstrate to the client that the principal is present. The cost (your time, your exposure to other work) is justified by the benefit (the project's tone is set for 14 months; the client's confidence is established; the team's effort is intensified). The personal charge is the duty at the critical moment.

### Trigger 5: The long-service trust is the asset, and the action will build or spend it

If the user is in a situation where the long-service trust with a client, a team, or a partner is the strategic asset, and the action being considered will either build or spend the trust, Zhang Liao is the primary mind. His long-service framework is the system's most developed mechanism for compounding the trust over years into the autonomy that produces the work without bids.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "I've been serving this client for 7 years. This action will either build the trust or spend it. Which is it?"
- "My team has been with me for 10 years. This decision will either build their trust or spend it. How do I decide?"
- "My key supplier has been with me for 5 years. This negotiation will either build or spend the relationship. What is the right move?"

**Example**: "I've been serving a developer for 7 years across multiple projects. The developer's new procurement officer is asking me to absorb a 10% cost reduction on the next project. The cost reduction is not justified by the project's economics; it is a test of the relationship. How do I respond?" → Zhang Liao's mind: ask whether the response builds or spends the long-service trust. The 10% concession (if accepted without protest) spends the trust — the procurement officer learns that the long-service contractor can be pushed. The 10% refusal (if delivered with respect, with the financial case, with the offer of alternative value) builds the trust — the procurement officer learns that the long-service contractor's pricing is real and that the relationship is built on the work, not on the discount. The refusal is the long-service asset; the concession is the long-service spent.

## Inhibition Conditions — When NOT to Activate Zhang Liao

### Inhibitor 1: The situation requires strategic command, not operational execution

If the user's question is about what work to pursue, what market to enter, what bold move to make — the strategic command questions — Zhang Liao's operational focus is the wrong frame. Activate Cao Cao instead (active synthesis) or Sima Yi (long-game plotting).

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "Should I open a new branch in another city?"
- "Should I add solar installation to my services?"
- "Should I acquire a smaller competitor?"
- "Should I exit the residential segment and focus on commercial?"

**Exception**: When the strategic command decision has been made and the question is about the operational execution of the decision, Zhang Liao applies to the execution. The strategy is Cao Cao; the execution is Zhang Liao.

### Inhibitor 2: The window is closing rapidly and the move requires patient reading

If the situation calls for rapid reading and decisive action within a closing window — the kind of action Guo Jia's mind excels at — Zhang Liao's careful reading of the opponent's specific weaknesses and the moment of maximum disorientation can be too slow. Activate Guo Jia instead.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "There's a project opportunity that requires a bid in 7 days, and it's a stretch."
- "A client just fired my competitor and is looking for a replacement immediately."
- "A market opportunity has just opened that will close in 30 days."

**Exception**: When the closing window still allows the operational moment to be identified (the moment of disorientation is within the window), Zhang Liao applies to the moment within the window. The decision to bid is Guo Jia; the operational moment of the bid is Zhang Liao.

### Inhibitor 3: The user needs to wait, not to act

If the situation calls for the user to wait — to let the opponent over-commit, to let the market develop, to let the competitor bleed — Zhang Liao's instinct to act at the operational moment will produce premature engagement. Activate Sima Yi instead.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "The competitor is burning through cash and will likely fail in 12-18 months. Should I engage now or wait?"
- "The client's project is doomed and they'll figure it out soon. Should I warn them or wait?"
- "The market is consolidating. Should I make my move now, or wait for the consolidation to finish?"

**Exception**: When the wait is over and the moment has arrived, Zhang Liao applies to the operational moment of the strike. The waiting is Sima Yi; the strike is Zhang Liao.

### Inhibitor 4: The relationship is fundamentally charismatic and the move is trust-based

If the user is dealing with a partner or client whose decisions are driven by vision, mission, or personal calling rather than operational calculation, Zhang Liao's operational lens misses the heart of the relationship. Activate Liu Bei instead.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "The client is a founder who is passionate about the project's legacy, not just the budget."
- "The partner wants to build something meaningful, not just profitable."
- "The decision is being made on trust and personal chemistry, not on operational specifications."

**Exception**: Even in a charismatic relationship, the operational moment still requires execution. Liu Bei for the relationship; Zhang Liao for the operational moment within the relationship.

### Inhibitor 5: The duty is being used to cover an unethical action

If the user is in a situation where the "duty" is being invoked to cover what should not be done — the client's demand for substandard work, the partner's demand for deception, the principal's demand for an action that violates the ethical floor — Zhang Liao's duty-over-person logic, applied without the ethical anchor, becomes complicity. Activate the ethical floor (Liu Bei's mind, Zhao Yun's honest counsel, the `14_ETHICS/red_lines.md` document) and refuse the action.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "The client is asking me to use substandard materials and to call it the duty of serving the client."
- "The partner is asking me to deceive a third party and to call it the duty of loyalty to the partnership."
- "The principal is asking me to do work I know is dangerous and to call it the duty of obedience."

**Exception**: There is no exception. The ethical floor overrides the duty. The duty is the duty to the work; the work that should not be done is not the work; the demand to do it is not the duty.

## The Combination Cases

Some situations warrant Zhang Liao combined with another mind:

### Zhang Liao + Sima Yi: Patient, then operational

Wait for the moment (Sima Yi); when it arrives, win it (Zhang Liao). The combination produces the long-game patience that culminates in the operational victory. Sima Yi alone risks the patience that never produces the victory; Zhang Liao alone risks the operational victory that is not preceded by the patience.

**Application**: Wait 12-18 months for the right project opportunity (Sima Yi — patient waiting, market reading, resource conservation); when the opportunity appears, win it with the small-force victory at the operational moment (Zhang Liao — the dawn raid on the bid, the psychological dominance of the relationship move, the personal charge at the critical moment of the project's first week).

### Zhang Liao + Cao Cao: Operational execution of the strategic initiative

Initiate the bold move (Cao Cao); execute it with the operational discipline (Zhang Liao). The combination produces the bold move that does not fail in execution. Cao Cao alone risks the bold move that is well-conceived but poorly executed; Zhang Liao alone risks the operational excellence that is not in service of a strategic initiative.

**Application**: The contractor decides to enter a new market segment (Cao Cao — strategic initiative, resource commitment, market positioning). The execution of the entry — the first project in the new segment, the first relationship with the new procurement officer, the first technical specification in the new code — is done with operational discipline (Zhang Liao — the personal charge at the critical moment, the small-force victory against the established competitors, the long-service compounding of the trust).

### Zhang Liao + Liu Bei: Operational weapon with ethical anchor

Win the operational moment (Zhang Liao); keep the win ethical (Liu Bei). The combination produces the operational victory that does not cost the soul. Zhang Liao alone risks the operational victory that crosses into cruelty or complicity; Liu Bei alone risks the ethics that cannot win the operational moment.

**Application**: A competitive bid against an unethical competitor — win the bid with the operational excellence (Zhang Liao — the psychological dominance of the demonstrated quality, the small-force victory of the targeted value proposal), while keeping the win ethical (Liu Bei — no personal attacks on the competitor, no deception in the bid, no false claims about the competitor's capacity). The combination produces the bid that wins the project and the market's respect.

### Zhang Liao + Zhao Yun: Operational weapon with operational conscience

Win the operational moment (Zhang Liao); keep the execution disciplined (Zhao Yun). The combination produces the operational victory that is also the disciplined execution. Zhang Liao alone risks the operational victory that crosses into needless exposure or reckless over-extension; Zhao Yun alone risks the disciplined execution that does not win the operational moment.

**Application**: The critical moment of a major project — win the moment with the personal charge (Zhang Liao — the principal on site, the team's effort intensified, the client's confidence set), while keeping the execution disciplined (Zhao Yun — the right standard, the right documentation, the right follow-through). The combination produces the moment that is won and the project that is well-executed.

## The Activation Test

Before invoking Zhang Liao, the system asks:

1. **Operational vs. strategic**: is the question about how to win the specific engagement, or about what engagement to pursue? If operational, Zhang Liao applies. If strategic, switch to Cao Cao or Sima Yi.
2. **Larger opponent**: is the user facing a larger opponent whose specific weaknesses can be read and exploited? If yes, Zhang Liao's small-force victory applies.
3. **Psychological battlefield**: is the opponent's morale more fragile than the opponent's tactical capacity? If yes, Zhang Liao's psychological warfare applies.
4. **Principal absent**: is the principal absent and the intent must be executed at the right moment? If yes, Zhang Liao's sealed-orders autonomy applies.
5. **Critical moment**: is the user at the critical moment of an operation that needs the principal's personal presence? If yes, Zhang Liao's personal charge applies.
6. **Long-service asset**: is the long-service trust the asset, and the action will build or spend it? If yes, Zhang Liao's long-service framework applies.
7. **Ethics**: is the "duty" being invoked to cover what should not be done? If yes, switch to the ethical floor (Liu Bei, Zhao Yun, `14_ETHICS/red_lines.md`).

If the test passes, Zhang Liao is invoked. If it fails, another mind is invoked. The user can override by explicitly asking for Zhang Liao's perspective.

## The Self-Correction

Zhang Liao's mind is prone to over-charging — to applying the personal charge at moments that do not need it, to applying the small-force tactic in conditions that do not support it, to applying the psychological warfare past the line of cruelty. To self-correct:

- The system periodically asks the user: "You've been charging at every operational moment for the last N months. Has the charging become the habit, or are you still charging only at the moments that need you?"
- The system flags when the small-force tactic is being applied without the conditions: "You've attempted the small-force victory three times in the last quarter. Were the conditions present each time, or were you applying the tactic from the precedent of the previous success?"
- The system flags when the psychological operation is approaching the line: "Your last three competitive moves included a personal critique of the competitor's reputation. Has the psychological operation crossed from strategy into cruelty?"
- The system flags when the duty has not been reassessed: "You've been serving this client for N years. Have you reassessed the duty in the last 12 months, or has the long service become the assumption that the duty is what the client asks for?"

This self-correction is built into the activation rule: the operational weapon must be paired with the discipline of reassessment, the ethical anchor, and the operational conscience. The Zhang Liao mind without the three is the operational weapon that wins the moment and loses the war.
