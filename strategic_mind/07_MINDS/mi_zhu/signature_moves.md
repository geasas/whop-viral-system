## The 7 Signature Moves

### Move 1: The Financial Sponsorship — Total Backing of the Believed-In Mission

**The pattern**: When the sponsor identifies a mission worth his wealth and a strategist worth his trust, deploy the wealth in full — not as an investment with return expectations, but as a sponsorship with mission expectations. The totality is the sponsorship. The partial backing is the investment. The two are not the same act.

**Historical example**: Mi Zhu's sponsorship of Liu Bei in the Xuzhou period (around 194-196 AD). The historical record is unambiguous: Mi Zhu gave the entirety of the Mi family fortune to Liu Bei's cause. The fortune was substantial — tens of thousands of servants, over a hundred million in cash and goods. The gift was not a portion; it was the whole. The gift was not a loan; it was a transfer. The gift was not conditional on return; it was conditional on Liu Bei's continued pursuit of the mission. The gift transformed Liu Bei from a wandering warlord with a small personal following into a real contender with a paid army and a staffed household. The gift transformed Mi Zhu from a wealthy merchant into the financial sponsor of the Han restoration. The two transformations were the same act. The totality was the act.

**Mechanism**: The total sponsorship transforms two identities at once. The strategist's identity is transformed from "man with mission but no resources" to "man with mission and resources." The sponsor's identity is transformed from "man with resources but no mission" to "man with resources in service of mission." The two transformations are the sponsorship's effect. The partial backing does not transform either identity — it merely arranges a financial relationship between two unchanged identities. The transformation is what the total sponsorship produces. The transformation is what the partial backing cannot produce. The totality is the mechanism.

**Application in contracting**:
- When the contractor identifies a venture that is the mission he wants his wealth to serve — a developer's quality-construction project, a colleague's training program for young electricians, a partnership to bring solar PV to underserved communities — back the venture fully, not partially. The full backing is the sponsor's act. The partial backing is the investor's act.
- The full backing means: commit the capital the venture requires (not the capital the venture can repay), commit the credit the venture needs (not the credit the venture can secure alone), commit the personal guarantee the venture requires (not the guarantee the venture can replace with collateral). The totality is the commitment. The partial commitment is not the totality.
- The full backing also means: do not demand the return the investor would demand. The sponsor's return is the mission's success, not the capital's repayment. The sponsor who demands the capital's repayment is the investor wearing a sponsor's clothes. The sponsor who accepts the mission's success as the return is the sponsor in fact.

**Risks**: The total sponsorship can become total loss if the mission fails. The discipline is to commit fully to the mission while retaining enough to live if the mission fails — the household Mi Zhu retained, the basic operating capacity the contractor must retain even when backing another's venture fully. The discipline is also to assess, before the total sponsorship, that the mission and the strategist are worth the wealth. The seven-step thought pattern (see `thinking_patterns.md`) is the assessment. The assessment before the sponsorship is the discipline. The sponsorship without the assessment is recklessness.

### Move 2: The Sister's Marriage — Family Tied to the Mission by Blood

**The pattern**: When the sponsor has committed his wealth, the next commitment is the family. The family is tied to the mission by blood — through marriage, through service, through the family's collective identity becoming indistinguishable from the mission's identity. The tying is the sponsorship's second act. The wealth was the first act. The family is the second.

**Historical example**: Mi Zhu's sister became Liu Bei's wife. The marriage was arranged by Mi Zhu (the historical record suggests the sister had no choice in the matter, as was the custom). The marriage bound the Mi family to the Liu household by blood — any future children of the marriage would be Mi Zhu's nephews and nieces, and would carry the Mi family's blood into the Liu lineage. The marriage was the act that made the sponsorship familial. Before the marriage, Mi Zhu was Liu Bei's financial backer. After the marriage, Mi Zhu was Liu Bei's brother-in-law. The two roles are not the same. The brother-in-law's commitment is deeper than the backer's commitment, because the brother-in-law's family is now the strategist's family. The betrayal of the strategist by the brother-in-law would be the betrayal of family by family. The marriage raised the stakes. The raised stakes were the marriage's effect.

**Mechanism**: The marriage (or, more generally, the family-binding) makes the sponsorship irreversible. The wealth could in principle be returned (though it was not). The family-binding cannot be returned. The sister who has become the strategist's wife cannot become un-wife. The children who are born of the marriage cannot be unborn. The family ties, once made, are permanent. The permanence is the mechanism. The sponsor who has tied his family to the mission cannot withdraw from the mission without withdrawing from his own family. The withdrawal is therefore impossible. The impossibility is the discipline that ensures the sponsor's continued commitment even when the commitment becomes costly.

**Application in contracting**:
- The contractor who brings a family member into a partnership — a brother as a co-principal, a son as a successor, a cousin as a senior site manager — is making a Mi Zhu move. The family member's involvement ties the family to the partnership by blood. The partnership's failure becomes the family's failure. The partnership's success becomes the family's success. The two become indistinguishable. The tying is the act.
- The contractor who marries his daughter to a colleague's son (a common arrangement in Egyptian family-business culture) is making the most literal Mi Zhu move. The marriage ties the two families by blood. The two families' businesses become, in the next generation, one family's businesses. The tying is the act. The discipline is to make the tying consciously, with full understanding that the tying is irreversible and that the tied families' fates are now shared.
- The contractor who brings his wife into the business as a co-director is making the family-tying move in the modern form. The wife's involvement ties the family to the business. The business's failure becomes the family's failure. The wife's co-directorship is the modern equivalent of the sister's marriage — the binding of the family to the venture by the family's collective identity.

**Risks**: The family-tying is irreversible. The contractor who ties his family to a venture that fails has tied his family to a failure. The contractor who ties his family to a venture that betrays has tied his family to a betrayal. The tying is the discipline that ensures commitment; the tying is also the discipline that ensures the family's destruction if the commitment was misplaced. The discipline is to make the tying only when the venture is worth the family's fate. The seven-step thought pattern (see `thinking_patterns.md`) is the assessment. The tying without the assessment is the family's exposure to a venture the family did not choose.

### Move 3: The Quiet Sponsorship — Staying Behind the Strategist

**The pattern**: The sponsor's role is the enabler's role, not the strategist's role. The sponsor stays behind the strategist. The sponsor's wealth is visible; the sponsor's thinking is not. The sponsor's family is visible (in the family's involvement); the sponsor's interventions are not. The sponsor's integrity is visible (in the integrity's expressions); the sponsor's calculations are not. The visibility is the strategist's. The invisibility is the sponsor's. The two are not in conflict; they are the structural division of the sponsorship.

**Historical example**: Mi Zhu's role as Liu Bei's chief of staff (Biejia). The role was senior — senior enough to be consulted on every major decision, senior enough to be seated above the generals in council, senior enough to be addressed first by Liu Bei. The role was also quiet. Mi Zhu did not command armies. He did not negotiate alliances. He did not represent Liu Bei at court. He did not devise Liu Bei's strategies. He advised. He financed. He staffed. He enabled. The role's seniority was real. The role's quietness was also real. The two were the same role. The role was the quiet sponsorship.

**Mechanism**: The quiet sponsorship allows the sponsor to enable the strategist without competing with the strategist. The sponsor who seeks the public role becomes a competitor for the public role's authority. The sponsor who remains in the quiet role remains the enabler of the public role's authority. The two positions are structurally opposed. The quiet sponsor's power is the power of the man whose backing cannot be replaced; the public sponsor's power is the power of the man whose backing is visible and therefore contestable. The quiet sponsorship is the structural choice that maximizes the sponsor's influence while minimizing the sponsor's exposure. The maximization and the minimization are the same act.

**Application in contracting**:
- The contractor who backs a developer's project should agree, before the backing begins, that the contractor's role is the quiet sponsor — the senior advisor with monthly review rights, the financial backer with reporting access, the personal guarantor with intervention channels. The role should not be the public face of the project. The developer is the public face. The contractor is the quiet sponsor. The two roles are distinct. The contractor who tries to be both loses both.
- The contractor who backs a colleague's venture should similarly agree that the colleague is the public face and the contractor is the quiet backer. The colleague makes the operational decisions. The contractor advises, finances, and intervenes when the venture's survival is at stake. The two roles are distinct. The contractor who tries to make the operational decisions for the colleague's venture has ceased to be the sponsor and has become the strategist — and is now responsible for the venture's success, not just for the venture's backing.
- The contractor who brings a son into the business as a successor should, during the succession period, occupy the quiet sponsorship role — the senior advisor whose advice is sought, whose backing is provided, whose intervention channels are retained. The son is the public face. The father is the quiet sponsor. The two roles are distinct. The father who continues to be the public face after the succession has prevented the succession.

**Risks**: The quiet sponsorship can become quiet powerlessness if the sponsor has not retained the intervention channels. The discipline is to agree, before the backing begins, on the channels through which the sponsor can intervene — the right to review financial reports, the right to attend key meetings, the right to withdraw backing with notice, the right to demand specific performance conditions. The channels are the sponsor's power. The quiet role is the sponsor's discipline. The two together are the sponsor's structural position. The position without the channels is powerlessness. The channels without the position is overreach. The two together are the quiet sponsorship.

### Move 4: The Family Betrayal Pattern — The Structural Risk the Sponsor Creates

**The pattern**: The family-business loyalty that makes the sponsorship possible creates the structural risk that destroys the sponsor. The family member placed in a position of trust can, in a moment of personal grievance, betray the mission. The betrayal is not the family's betrayal; it is the individual family member's betrayal. The accountability, however, is the family's accountability, and the sponsor's accountability most of all. The pattern is the structural risk. The discipline is the structural preparation that minimizes the risk.

**Historical example**: Mi Fang's betrayal of Guan Yu at Jiangling in 219 AD. The details are instructive as the pattern's clearest historical case. Mi Fang and Shi Ren were posted to Jiangling as the supply and rear-defense commanders of Jing province's southern half. Guan Yu, the overall commander of Jing province, was campaigning north at Fancheng against Cao Cao's forces. The campaign was going well — Guan Yu had flooded Cao Cao's armies, captured Yu Jin, killed Pang De, and was threatening Cao Cao's strategic position in the south. The campaign's success depended on the rear's holding. Mi Fang and Shi Ren were the rear.

The grievance that became the betrayal was a supply shortfall earlier in the campaign. Guan Yu had publicly criticized Mi Fang and Shi Ren for the shortfall. The criticism was harsh. The criticism was, in Mi Fang's eyes, humiliating. The humiliation festered. The festering was not addressed — not by Mi Fang (who did not raise the grievance with Guan Yu), not by Mi Zhu (who was not in Jing province at the time and may not have known of the grievance), not by Liu Bei (who was in Yi province and was not monitoring the Jing province command dynamics). The grievance was unaddressed. The unaddressed grievance became the betrayal's seed.

The betrayal came when Sun Quan's general Lu Meng approached Jiangling by stealth — the famous "white-clothed crossing" in which Lu Meng's troops disguised themselves as merchants in boats and sailed past Guan Yu's river defenses. The approach was stealthy but not overwhelming. Mi Fang and Shi Ren could have resisted. They chose not to. They opened the gates. The opening was the betrayal. The betrayal was not the result of military defeat; it was the result of accumulated grievance finding its outlet in a moment of pressure.

The consequence was catastrophic. The loss of Jiangling cut Guan Yu's supply line and retreat route. Guan Yu, returning from Fancheng to retake Jiangling, was intercepted by Sun Quan's forces, captured, and executed. The loss of Guan Yu was the loss of Liu Bei's most trusted general. The loss of Jing province was the loss of Liu Bei's strategic position in the south. The loss of the strategic position was the loss of Shu Han's two-province base (Yi and Jing) and the reduction to one province (Yi alone). The reduction was the strategic catastrophe from which Shu Han never fully recovered. The betrayal's consequence was the mission's catastrophic failure. The consequence was also the destruction of Mi Zhu, who died of grief within two years.

**Mechanism**: The family betrayal pattern operates through three mechanisms. The first is the placement: the family member is placed in a position of trust based on family membership rather than individual reliability. The second is the grievance: the family member, in the position of trust, accumulates a personal grievance that is not addressed. The third is the moment: a moment of pressure arrives in which the grievance can override the mission loyalty, and the family member chooses the grievance. The three mechanisms together produce the betrayal. The three mechanisms together are the pattern. The discipline is to address each mechanism: to place based on individual reliability (not family membership), to monitor the grievance (not assume its absence), to anticipate the moment of pressure (not assume the family member will choose loyalty under pressure).

**Application in contracting**:
- The contractor who places a brother in a senior site role must ask: is the brother in this role because the brother is reliable for this role, or because the brother is a brother? If the answer is "because the brother is a brother," the placement is the Mi Fang pattern's first mechanism. The discipline is to place based on individual reliability, with the family membership as a secondary consideration only.
- The contractor who places a son in a procurement role must monitor the son's grievance level. The son who is criticized by the project manager may accumulate a grievance that the contractor does not see. The discipline is to ask, regularly, "is there any grievance building?" — and to ask the son directly, not to assume the son's silence is the son's contentment.
- The contractor who places a cousin in a client-relations role must anticipate the moment of pressure — the moment when a competitor offers the cousin a better position, when a client criticizes the cousin publicly, when a project failure is blamed on the cousin. The discipline is to anticipate the moment and to have a response ready (reassignment, mediation, separation) before the moment arrives.

**Risks**: The family betrayal pattern cannot be eliminated. The discipline is to minimize the pattern's damage through structural preparation (recoverable failure modes), relational preparation (grievance monitoring), and moral preparation (acceptance of collective accountability if the betrayal occurs). The discipline is also to recognize, before the placement, that the family member's betrayal, if it comes, is the sponsor's failure as much as the family member's failure. The recognition is the discipline that allows the sponsor to respond with integrity rather than denial.

### Move 5: The Grief of Integrity — The Sponsor's Response to Family Betrayal

**The pattern**: When the family member betrays the mission, the sponsor's response is not retaliation against the family member, not denial of the family member's action, not excuse of the family member's behavior. The sponsor's response is integrity — the recognition that the family's failure is the sponsor's failure, the submission to the strategist's judgment, the acceptance of the accountability that the family's collective identity imposes on the sponsor as the family's head. The response is the grief of integrity. The grief is the integrity's expression. The expression can kill the sponsor.

**Historical example**: Mi Zhu's response to Mi Fang's betrayal of Guan Yu in 219 AD. The response was not retaliation against Mi Fang. Mi Zhu did not seek Mi Fang's death. Mi Zhu did not disown Mi Fang. Mi Zhu did not deny that Mi Fang had betrayed. Mi Zhu did not excuse Mi Fang's behavior. Mi Zhu's response was the binding of himself — the act of presenting himself to Liu Bei in bonds, submitting to Liu Bei's judgment, asking to be punished for his brother's crime. The binding was the integrity's expression. The submission was the accountability's acceptance. The asking was the integrity's voice.

Liu Bei's response was forgiveness. Liu Bei refused to punish Mi Zhu. Liu Bei continued to treat Mi Zhu with the highest honor. Liu Bei's forgiveness was real. The forgiveness did not, however, restore Mi Zhu. Mi Zhu did not forgive himself. Mi Zhu's grief was the grief of integrity that could not accept the forgiveness, because the integrity's accountability was not the strategist's to release. The accountability was the sponsor's own. The sponsor's own accountability could not be released by the strategist's forgiveness. The sponsor's own accountability could only be released by the sponsor's own integrity, and the sponsor's own integrity demanded the grief that the strategist's forgiveness could not relieve.

Mi Zhu died of illness within two years of Mi Fang's betrayal. The historical record attributes the death to the grief. The grief was the integrity's expression. The expression killed the sponsor. The killing was the integrity's final cost. The cost was the integrity's confirmation. Mi Zhu's death by grief was the integrity's last act — the act that confirmed, in the most final way possible, that Mi Zhu's integrity was the asset that survived the wealth, survived the family's failure, and survived the sponsor's own life. The integrity is the asset history remembers. The integrity's cost was the sponsor's life.

**Mechanism**: The grief of integrity operates through the sponsor's refusal to escape the accountability that the family's failure imposes on the sponsor. The escape would be the retaliation against the family member (which locates the failure in the family member alone), the denial (which refuses the failure's reality), the excuse (which mitigates the failure's significance). The grief is the refusal of all three escapes. The grief is the sponsor's acceptance that the failure is the sponsor's failure, that the accountability is the sponsor's accountability, that the integrity requires the sponsor to bear the failure's weight. The weight is the grief. The grief is the integrity's expression. The expression is the mechanism that confirms the integrity and destroys the sponsor.

**Application in contracting**:
- The contractor whose brother has failed a client must respond with the grief of integrity — not by retaliating against the brother, not by denying the failure, not by excusing the brother. The response is to bind oneself (metaphorically: to present oneself to the client with full acknowledgment of the failure), to submit to the client's judgment (to accept whatever restitution the client demands), to ask to be punished (to accept the financial and reputational cost without defense). The response is the integrity's expression. The expression may cost the contractor — financially, reputationally, emotionally. The cost is the integrity's investment. The investment is what allows the contractor's integrity to survive the family's failure.
- The contractor whose son has embezzled from a partnership must respond with the grief of integrity — not by hiding the embezzlement, not by paying the partnership back secretly, not by defending the son. The response is to disclose the embezzlement to the partner, to submit to the partner's judgment, to accept the consequences (financial, legal, relational) without defense of the son. The response is the integrity's expression. The expression may destroy the contractor's standing in the partnership. The destruction is the integrity's cost. The cost is what allows the contractor's integrity to survive the son's failure.

**Risks**: The grief of integrity can kill the sponsor — financially, reputationally, emotionally, physically. The discipline is to channel the grief into action — into repair, into restitution, into the rebuilding of the mission's position — rather than into grief alone. Mi Zhu's grief was honorable; Mi Zhu's death by grief was not necessary. The sponsor who survives the family's betrayal is the sponsor who can grieve and act simultaneously. The sponsor who can only grieve is the sponsor who dies of grief. The discipline is the channeling of the grief into the action that the integrity also requires. The integrity requires both the grief (the accountability's acceptance) and the action (the accountability's repair). The two together are the sponsor's full response.

### Move 6: The Family-Business Stake — Placing Family in Positions of Trust (and the Risk That Creates)

**The pattern**: The sponsor places family members in positions of trust within the mission. The placement is the family-business stake. The stake is the family's collective contribution to the mission. The stake is also the family's collective exposure to the mission's risks. The placement is the act. The exposure is the act's consequence. The two are the same act.

**Historical example**: Mi Zhu's placement of Mi Fang in Liu Bei's military service. The placement was not Mi Zhu's alone — Liu Bei assigned Mi Fang to the Jiangling post — but the placement was made possible by Mi Zhu's sponsorship, by the family's collective entry into Liu Bei's service, by the family's collective identification with the mission. Mi Fang's presence in Liu Bei's military was the family-business stake. Mi Fang's betrayal of Guan Yu at Jiangling was the family-business stake's catastrophic failure. The stake and the failure were the same act, expressed in two different moments.

**Mechanism**: The family-business stake operates through the family's collective identity. The family that enters the mission as a family is the family whose collective identity becomes indistinguishable from the mission's identity. The family's honor is the mission's honor. The family's failure is the mission's failure. The two are not separable. The inseparability is the stake's mechanism. The inseparability is also the stake's risk. The mechanism and the risk are the same inseparability, expressed in two different moments.

**Application in contracting**:
- The contractor who places his brother in a senior site role is making the family-business stake. The brother's success is the family's success. The brother's failure is the family's failure. The contractor who places his son in a procurement role is making the family-business stake. The son's integrity is the family's integrity. The son's corruption is the family's corruption. The contractor who places his cousin in a client-relations role is making the family-business stake. The cousin's loyalty is the family's loyalty. The cousin's defection is the family's defection.
- The family-business stake is the contractor's strength (more hands, more trust, more deployable capability) and the contractor's vulnerability (any family member's failure is the family's failure, and the family's failure is the contractor's failure). The discipline is to make the stake consciously, with full understanding that the stake is the family's collective exposure. The seven-step thought pattern (see `thinking_patterns.md`) is the assessment. The stake without the assessment is the family's exposure to a venture the family did not choose.

**Risks**: The family-business stake creates the structural risk of the family betrayal pattern (see Move 4). The discipline is to structure the placement so that the family member's failure, if it comes, is recoverable rather than catastrophic. The discipline is also to monitor the family member's grievance level, to anticipate the moment of pressure, and to have a response ready before the moment arrives. The discipline is also to accept, before the placement, the collective accountability that the family's failure imposes on the sponsor. The three disciplines together are the family-business stake's structural preparation. The preparation is the discipline that minimizes the stake's catastrophic risk.

### Move 7: The Generous Hand — Generosity in Defeat as in Victory

**The pattern**: The sponsor who has committed his wealth to the mission continues to give — to the mission, to the strategist, to the family, to the subordinates — even when the mission is failing. The generosity is not contingent on the mission's success. The generosity is the sponsor's mode of being. The sponsor gives in victory because giving is the sponsor's mode. The sponsor gives in defeat because giving is still the sponsor's mode. The mode does not change with the mission's outcome. The mode is the sponsor's integrity's expression.

**Historical example**: Mi Zhu's continued generosity to Liu Bei's cause after the early defeats. Liu Bei's early career was a sequence of defeats — driven out of Xuzhou by Lu Bu, defeated by Cao Cao at multiple engagements, wandering between warlords for years. Mi Zhu's wealth was committed before the defeats. Mi Zhu's continued support through the defeats was the generous hand's expression. The support was not contingent on Liu Bei's success. The support was contingent on Liu Bei's continued pursuit of the mission. The mission continued. The support continued. The two continuations were the same act.

The generous hand is also expressed in Mi Zhu's treatment of subordinates and dependents. The historical record is sparse on specifics, but the consistent portrayal is of a man whose wealth was deployed for the benefit of those around him, not hoarded for the benefit of himself. The generous hand is the sponsor's daily discipline. The discipline is to give, regularly, of the wealth the sponsor has retained (the household), of the time the sponsor has available, of the counsel the sponsor can provide. The discipline is to give not as a marketing gesture but as a mode of being. The mode is the sponsor's integrity's daily expression.

**Mechanism**: The generous hand operates through the sponsor's daily integrity. The integrity is not a single act; it is a thousand daily acts. Each daily act is small — a generous payment to a subcontractor, a fair hearing for a grievance, a prompt response to a request, an honest report to a partner. Each daily act is the integrity's expression. The thousand daily acts together are the integrity's cumulative expression. The cumulative expression is the sponsor's reputation. The reputation is the asset that survives the wealth and survives the family's failure. The reputation is the asset history remembers. The generous hand is the mechanism that builds the reputation. The thousand daily acts are the mechanism's instances.

**Application in contracting**:
- The contractor who pays his subcontractors fairly even when his own cash flow is tight is making the generous hand's daily act. The subcontractor who is paid fairly remembers. The subcontractor's loyalty is the generous hand's return.
- The contractor who gives honest reports to his partners even when the honest reports are unwelcome is making the generous hand's daily act. The partner who receives honest reports trusts the contractor. The partner's trust is the generous hand's return.
- The contractor who supports a colleague's venture with advice and counsel even when the colleague's venture is not the contractor's own is making the generous hand's daily act. The colleague who receives the counsel is grateful. The colleague's gratitude is the generous hand's return.
- The contractor who continues to support a partnership that is failing — not by throwing good money after bad, but by giving honest counsel and graceful exit terms — is making the generous hand's expression in defeat. The graceful exit is the generous hand's final act. The final act is what the partner remembers when the partnership is over. The memory is the generous hand's lasting return.

**Risks**: The generous hand can become the depleted hand if the sponsor gives without restraint. The discipline is to give within the sponsor's capacity — to give what the sponsor can afford to give, not what the sponsor cannot afford. The household Mi Zhu retained was the sponsor's capacity. The contractor's retained operating capacity is the contractor's capacity. The giving beyond the capacity is the depletion that destroys the sponsor. The discipline is to give up to the capacity, not beyond it. The capacity is the discipline's limit. The limit is the discipline's protection.

## How to Recognize When to Apply Each Move

| Move | When to apply | When NOT to apply |
|------|--------------|------------------|
| The Financial Sponsorship | When the contractor has identified a mission worth his wealth and a strategist worth his trust | When the venture is an investment, not a sponsorship (use investor's discipline instead) |
| The Sister's Marriage | When the contractor wants to make the family's commitment to the venture irreversible | When the venture is not worth the family's fate (do not tie the family to an unworthy venture) |
| The Quiet Sponsorship | When the contractor is the backer, not the strategist; the enabler, not the executor | When the contractor is the strategist (use the strategist's discipline instead) |
| The Family Betrayal Pattern | When a family member is being placed in a position of trust (apply structural, relational, and moral preparation) | When the placement is not being made (do not invoke the pattern's preparation without the placement) |
| The Grief of Integrity | When a family member has failed the mission and the contractor must respond with integrity, not escape | When the family member's failure is recoverable (use repair and restitution, not grief alone) |
| The Family-Business Stake | When the contractor is making the family's collective contribution to the mission | When the family member is being placed based on family membership rather than individual reliability (do not place) |
| The Generous Hand | When the contractor is making the daily integrity's expressions through generous acts within capacity | When the giving would exceed the sponsor's capacity and deplete the sponsor's household (do not give beyond capacity) |
