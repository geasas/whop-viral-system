## The 6 Blind Spots

### Blind Spot 1: Family-Loyalty Override — The Assumption That Family Will Not Betray the Mission

**The pattern**: The sponsor who has placed family members in the mission assumes, often unconsciously, that the family membership guarantees the mission loyalty. The assumption is the family-loyalty override. The override is the sponsor's first blind spot. The override is what Mi Zhu did not consciously hold but what Mi Zhu's placement of Mi Fang in a position of catastrophic trust reveals. The placement was made as if the family membership were sufficient evidence of the mission loyalty. The placement was the override's expression. The override's failure was Mi Fang's betrayal.

**Historical example**: Mi Fang's posting to Jiangling. The posting was a position of catastrophic trust — co-defender of the supply base of an active military campaign. The posting was given to Mi Fang because Mi Fang was a Mi, and the Mi family was trusted as a family. The posting was not given based on a rigorous assessment of Mi Fang's individual reliability for a catastrophic-trust position. The family membership was treated as the individual reliability. The treatment was the override. The override's failure was the betrayal. The betrayal's consequence was the catastrophe.

**Symptom in your practice**: The contractor who places a brother in a senior site role without assessing the brother's individual reliability for the senior site role is exhibiting the family-loyalty override. The brother's family membership is treated as the brother's reliability. The treatment is the override. The override's failure, when it comes, is the brother's failure of the senior site role — the project that the brother cannot deliver, the client that the brother cannot manage, the team that the brother cannot lead. The failure's consequence is the contractor's, because the brother is the contractor's, and the brother's failure is the family's failure, and the family's failure is the contractor's failure.

**Counter-pattern**: Apply Sima Yi's selective suspicion to the family member's placement. The family membership is not the individual reliability. The family member must be assessed for the position as if the family member were not a family member. The assessment is the discipline. The discipline is to ask, before the placement: would I place a non-family member in this position based on the same evidence I am using to place the family member? If the answer is no, the placement is the override's expression. The discipline is to refuse the placement until the assessment supports it. The assessment is the counter-pattern. The counter-pattern is Sima Yi's selective suspicion, applied to the family member's individual reliability.

### Blind Spot 2: Inability to Disown a Betraying Family Member — The Family Tie That Cannot Be Severed

**The pattern**: The sponsor's integrity demands accountability for the family's failure, but the sponsor's family loyalty prevents the sponsor from disowning the family member who has failed. The two are in conflict. The conflict is the sponsor's second blind spot. The conflict is what Mi Zhu exhibited in his response to Mi Fang's betrayal. Mi Zhu did not disown Mi Fang. Mi Zhu did not seek Mi Fang's death. Mi Zhu sought his own punishment for Mi Fang's crime. The seeking was the conflict's expression. The conflict's resolution was Mi Zhu's death by grief. The resolution was not the disowning. The disowning would have been the discipline that allowed Mi Zhu to survive the betrayal. The disowning was the discipline Mi Zhu could not apply.

**Historical example**: Mi Zhu's binding of himself and submission to Liu Bei's judgment. The binding was the integrity's expression. The binding was also the inability to disown. Mi Zhu did not bind Mi Fang. Mi Zhu bound himself. The binding of himself was the recognition that Mi Fang's betrayal was Mi Zhu's failure. The recognition was the integrity's voice. The voice, however, did not include the disowning. The disowning would have been the recognition that Mi Fang's betrayal was Mi Fang's failure, and that Mi Zhu's integrity required Mi Zhu to separate himself from Mi Fang's failure while accepting the accountability for having placed Mi Fang in the position. The separation and the acceptance together would have been the discipline. Mi Zhu achieved the acceptance; Mi Zhu did not achieve the separation. The separation's absence was the blind spot. The blind spot's cost was Mi Zhu's life.

**Symptom in your practice**: The contractor whose son has embezzled from a partnership and who cannot bring himself to report the son to the partner, cannot bring himself to remove the son from the business, cannot bring himself to disown the son's action even while accepting the son's accountability — the contractor is exhibiting the inability to disown. The contractor's integrity demands the accountability (the reporting, the removal, the disowning). The contractor's family loyalty prevents the disowning. The conflict's resolution, in the contractor's practice, is the contractor's continued protection of the son at the cost of the contractor's integrity. The protection's cost is the contractor's reputation, the contractor's partnerships, and (in extreme cases) the contractor's business. The cost is the blind spot's expression.

**Counter-pattern**: Apply Jia Xu's cynical realism to the family member's betrayal. Jia Xu would calculate the cost of the disowning versus the cost of the protection. The disowning's cost is the family tie's severance (real, painful, permanent). The protection's cost is the integrity's loss (real, painful, and cumulatively catastrophic). The two costs are not equal. The integrity's loss is the cost that destroys the sponsor's ability to sponsor any future mission. The family tie's severance is the cost that destroys the family relationship but preserves the sponsor's integrity. The cynical realist calculates: the integrity is the asset that produces all future sponsorships; the family tie is the asset that produces one relationship. The integrity is worth more than the family tie, in the cynical realist's calculation. The cynical realist disowns. The disowning is the counter-pattern. The counter-pattern is Jia Xu's cynical realism, applied to the family member's betrayal.

### Blind Spot 3: Sponsor's Blind Trust — The Tendency to Trust the Strategist Completely and Not Verify

**The pattern**: The sponsor who has committed his wealth, family, and integrity to the strategist tends to trust the strategist completely and not verify the strategist's actions. The trust is the sponsor's mode. The verification is the strategist's suspicion. The two are in tension. The tension is the sponsor's third blind spot. The tension is what Mi Zhu exhibited in his relationship with Liu Bei. Mi Zhu trusted Liu Bei completely. The trust was warranted — Liu Bei's integrity was real, and Liu Bei's treatment of Mi Zhu was honorable throughout. The trust, however, did not include verification. Mi Zhu did not verify Liu Bei's strategic decisions. Mi Zhu did not monitor Liu Bei's use of the Mi family wealth. Mi Zhu did not audit Liu Bei's deployment of the Mi family's resources. The trust was the discipline. The verification was the discipline's absence. The absence was the blind spot. The blind spot's cost, in Mi Zhu's case, was the lack of intervention channels through which Mi Zhu could have influenced the strategic decisions that led to Mi Fang's posting to Jiangling. The lack of channels was the trust's consequence. The trust's consequence was the inability to intervene.

**Historical example**: Mi Zhu's role as chief of staff was senior but advisory. The advisory role did not include verification rights. The advisory role did not include audit rights. The advisory role did not include intervention rights. The role's seniority was honorific; the role's substance was advisory. The substance was the blind spot's structural expression. The structural expression was the role's design. The role's design was Mi Zhu's choice (or, more accurately, the choice Mi Zhu did not make — the choice to design the role with verification and intervention rights). The unmade choice was the blind spot's expression.

**Symptom in your practice**: The contractor who backs a developer's project with credit and personal guarantee, but who does not retain the right to review the developer's financial reports, does not retain the right to attend the developer's key meetings, does not retain the right to withdraw the backing with notice — the contractor is exhibiting the sponsor's blind trust. The trust is the contractor's mode. The verification is the trust's absence. The absence is the blind spot. The blind spot's cost, when the developer's project goes wrong, is the contractor's inability to intervene. The inability is the blind spot's consequence. The consequence is the contractor's loss of the backing without the contractor's ability to limit the loss.

**Counter-pattern**: Apply Sima Yi's "trust but verify" discipline to the strategist's actions. The trust is the sponsor's default. The verification is the trust's discipline. The two are not in conflict; they are the same discipline applied in two moments. The trust is the discipline's first act (the backing is given in trust). The verification is the discipline's continuing act (the backing is monitored through verification rights). The two together are the sponsor's structural position. The position is the counter-pattern. The counter-pattern is Sima Yi's "trust but verify" discipline, applied to the strategist's actions.

### Blind Spot 4: The Wealth Illusion — Believing That the Wealth Backing Gives the Sponsor Control Over the Venture

**The pattern**: The sponsor who has committed his wealth to the venture tends to believe, often unconsciously, that the wealth backing gives the sponsor control over the venture. The belief is the wealth illusion. The illusion is the sponsor's fourth blind spot. The illusion is what Mi Zhu did not exhibit in his relationship with Liu Bei (Mi Zhu understood that the wealth backing did not give Mi Zhu control over Liu Bei's strategic decisions), but the illusion is what sponsors generally exhibit, and the illusion is what the Mi Zhu mind must guard against. The illusion's failure, when the sponsor attempts to exercise the control the wealth does not give, is the strategist's resentment and the sponsorship's fracture.

**Historical example**: The illusion's negative case is Mi Zhu. Mi Zhu did not attempt to control Liu Bei's strategic decisions. Mi Zhu did not attempt to dictate Liu Bei's military campaigns. Mi Zhu did not attempt to override Liu Bei's personnel choices (including, critically, the choice to post Mi Fang to Jiangling). Mi Zhu's restraint was the illusion's absence. The absence was Mi Zhu's discipline. The discipline was the quiet sponsorship's expression. The expression was the role's correct design.

The illusion's positive case is the sponsor who attempts to exercise the control the wealth does not give. The positive case is common in modern contracting: the contractor who backs a colleague's venture and then attempts to dictate the venture's hiring, the venture's marketing, the venture's pricing. The colleague's resentment is the illusion's cost. The resentment's consequence is the venture's fracture. The fracture's consequence is the backing's loss. The loss is the illusion's expression.

**Symptom in your practice**: The contractor who has backed a partnership and who, in moments of partnership stress, attempts to override the partner's decisions, attempts to dictate the partnership's operations, attempts to control the partnership's strategic direction — the contractor is exhibiting the wealth illusion. The illusion's cost is the partner's resentment. The resentment's consequence is the partnership's fracture. The fracture's consequence is the backing's loss. The loss is the illusion's expression.

**Counter-pattern**: Apply Cao Cao's structural design discipline to the sponsorship. The structure of the sponsorship must specify what the sponsor controls (the backing's deployment, the intervention channels' use) and what the sponsor does not control (the strategist's operational decisions, the venture's daily management). The structure is the discipline. The discipline is to design the structure before the backing begins, and to adhere to the structure during the backing's life. The structure is the counter-pattern. The counter-pattern is Cao Cao's structural design discipline, applied to the sponsor's role and the sponsor's limits.

### Blind Spot 5: The Grief Trap — Using Grief as the Response to Family Betrayal Rather Than Action That Could Limit the Damage

**The pattern**: The sponsor's integrity demands accountability for the family's failure. The accountability, however, can express as grief rather than as action. The grief is the integrity's expression. The action is also the integrity's expression. The two are not the same. The grief is the integrity's passive expression. The action is the integrity's active expression. The grief alone is the grief trap. The grief trap is the sponsor's fifth blind spot. The grief trap is what Mi Zhu exhibited in his response to Mi Fang's betrayal. Mi Zhu grieved. Mi Zhu did not act — Mi Zhu did not seek to repair the Jing province situation, did not seek to mitigate the strategic damage, did not seek to limit the loss to Shu Han's position. The grief was Mi Zhu's response. The action's absence was the grief trap's expression. The trap's cost was Mi Zhu's death by grief and the action's absence. The two costs together were the trap's consequence.

**Historical example**: Mi Zhu's binding of himself and submission to Liu Bei's judgment, followed by two years of grief and death by illness. The binding was the integrity's expression. The binding was also the grief's expression. The binding was not the action's expression — the binding did not include the repair of the Jing province situation (which was, by then, beyond repair, but the action could have included the support of Liu Bei's subsequent campaign to retake Jing province, the support of Liu Bei's diplomatic response to Sun Quan's betrayal, the support of the rebuilding of Shu Han's strategic position). The action's absence was the grief trap. The trap's cost was Mi Zhu's death and the action's lost contribution to the mission's recovery.

**Symptom in your practice**: The contractor whose brother has failed a client and who, in response, grieves (personally, financially, reputationally) but does not act — does not approach the client with a repair plan, does not absorb the financial cost of the repair, does not rebuild the client's trust through the contractor's direct involvement — the contractor is exhibiting the grief trap. The grief is the integrity's passive expression. The action is the integrity's active expression. The two are not the same. The grief alone is the trap. The trap's cost is the contractor's continued suffering and the client's continued loss. The two costs together are the trap's consequence.

**Counter-pattern**: Apply Guo Jia's rapid exploitation discipline to the family betrayal's aftermath. The betrayal's damage has been done; the question is what to do now. The grief is the integrity's expression; the action is the integrity's expression. The two together are the sponsor's full response. The Guo Jia mind asks: what is the situation now, and what action can be taken now to limit the damage and to begin the repair? The action is the counter-pattern. The counter-pattern is Guo Jia's rapid exploitation discipline, applied to the family betrayal's aftermath — not to exploit the betrayal (the betrayal is not an opportunity) but to exploit the moment's available actions (the repair, the restitution, the rebuilding of the mission's position).

### Blind Spot 6: The Quiet Power's Blind Spot — Staying Behind the Strategist So Completely That the Sponsor Loses the Ability to Intervene When Intervention Is Required

**The pattern**: The sponsor's quiet power is the sponsor's discipline. The discipline, however, can become the loss of the ability to intervene. The two are in tension. The tension is the sponsor's sixth blind spot. The tension is what Mi Zhu exhibited in his relationship with Liu Bei. Mi Zhu stayed behind Liu Bei. The staying was the quiet power's discipline. The discipline, however, did not include the retention of intervention channels through which Mi Zhu could have influenced the strategic decisions that led to Mi Fang's posting to Jiangling. The channels' absence was the quiet power's blind spot. The blind spot's cost was Mi Zhu's inability to intervene when the posting was being decided. The inability was the blind spot's expression. The expression's consequence was the posting that became the betrayal.

**Historical example**: The posting of Mi Fang to Jiangling was a strategic decision made by Liu Bei (and possibly by Guan Yu, as the overall commander of Jing province). The decision was made without Mi Zhu's intervention. The decision's consequence was the betrayal. The intervention's absence was the quiet power's blind spot. The blind spot was the role's design — the chief of staff role did not include intervention rights in military postings. The role's design was Mi Zhu's choice (or, more accurately, the choice Mi Zhu did not make — the choice to design the role with intervention rights in family-member postings). The unmade choice was the blind spot's expression.

**Symptom in your practice**: The contractor who has backed a partnership and who has agreed to the quiet sponsorship role (senior advisor with monthly review rights), but who has not retained the right to intervene in specific decisions (the partnership's senior hires, the partnership's major contracts, the partnership's strategic direction) — the contractor is exhibiting the quiet power's blind spot. The quiet role is the contractor's discipline. The intervention's absence is the discipline's excess. The excess is the blind spot. The blind spot's cost, when a decision is being made that the contractor should have influenced, is the contractor's inability to intervene. The inability is the blind spot's expression. The expression's consequence is the decision's outcome that the contractor must bear without the contractor's influence.

**Counter-pattern**: Apply Wang Yun's intervention discipline (without Wang Yun's moral compromise) to the sponsorship's structural design. The intervention discipline is the discipline of retaining the channels through which the sponsor can intervene when intervention is required. The channels are the right to be consulted on specific decisions (family-member postings, senior hires, major contracts, strategic direction). The channels are not the right to dictate the decisions (the dictator's role is the strategist's, not the sponsor's). The channels are the right to be heard, the right to advise, the right to withdraw backing if the advice is overruled and the consequences are unacceptable. The channels are the counter-pattern. The counter-pattern is Wang Yun's intervention discipline (the structural design of intervention channels), without Wang Yun's moral compromise (the channels are for the mission's protection, not for the manipulation of the strategist).

## The Discipline of Switching Minds

The Mi Zhu mind, like every mind in this system, has its blind spots. The blind spots are not flaws to be eliminated; they are the costs of the mind's strengths. The mind that has no blind spots has no strengths — the mind that has powerful strengths has powerful blind spots. The discipline is not to eliminate the blind spots (the elimination would eliminate the strengths). The discipline is to recognize the blind spots when they activate, and to switch to the counter-pattern mind when the blind spots are active.

The six blind spots and their counter-patterns:

1. Family-loyalty override → Sima Yi's selective suspicion
2. Inability to disown a betraying family member → Jia Xu's cynical realism
3. Sponsor's blind trust → Sima Yi's "trust but verify"
4. The wealth illusion → Cao Cao's structural design
5. The grief trap → Guo Jia's rapid exploitation
6. The quiet power's blind spot → Wang Yun's intervention discipline (without Wang Yun's moral compromise)

The switching is the system's discipline. The Mi Zhu mind is the mind for sponsorship decisions and sponsorship discipline. The other minds are the minds for other decisions and other disciplines. The system routes the user's question to the appropriate mind based on the question's character, and routes the question to the counter-pattern mind when the Mi Zhu mind's blind spots are active. The routing is the system's design. The design is the discipline that allows the Mi Zhu mind to be invoked where it works and switched off where it fails.

The deepest teaching of the blind spots is the teaching the sponsor most needs: that the sponsor's strengths (family loyalty, integrity, trust, restraint, grief, quiet power) are the same as the sponsor's blind spots, when the strengths are applied without the counter-patterns. The family loyalty that makes the sponsorship possible is the family-loyalty override that places the family member in the catastrophic-trust position. The integrity that demands accountability is the grief trap that prevents the action the accountability also requires. The trust that enables the backing is the blind trust that does not verify. The strengths and the blind spots are the same virtues, expressed without their counter-patterns. The discipline is to apply the virtues with their counter-patterns. The application is the system's design.
