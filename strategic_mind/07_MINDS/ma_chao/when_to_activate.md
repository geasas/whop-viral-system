# Ma Chao — When to Activate

> **Layer**: 07_MINDS/ma_chao/
> **File**: when_to_activate
> **Subject**: The explicit routing rules for when to invoke the Ma Chao mind

---

## The Premise

Ma Chao's mind is not universal. It applies to specific situations and fails in others. This file defines the routing rules so the system knows when to invoke this mind and when to switch to another.

The rules are **triggered by question type**, not by topic. The same topic (a competitor dispute) may invoke Ma Chao or Sima Yi depending on the time horizon and the user's strategic position. The system must apply the rules below to determine which mind is appropriate, and must be willing to disable the Ma Chao mind when the inhibitors are active.

The Ma Chao mind is unique in this system in that it is invoked as often to WARN as to ADVISE. Several of its triggers are warnings — patterns the user should recognize and refuse, not patterns the user should emulate. The system must distinguish between the advisory triggers (where the Ma Chao moves are the right moves) and the warning triggers (where the Ma Chao pattern is the failure the user is in).

---

## Trigger Conditions — When to ACTIVATE Ma Chao

### Trigger 1: The user has been wronged, and the grievance is the legitimate motivating fuel for the response

If the user has been genuinely wronged — cheated by a competitor, betrayed by a partner, defamed by a rival, exploited by a client — and the response requires the intensity that only grievance can produce, Ma Chao is the primary mind. The grievance, channeled correctly, produces the discipline and persistence that no calculated motivation can match.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "A competitor has been spreading false rumors about my work quality."
- "A partner stole my design and presented it as their own to a client."
- "A client refused to pay for completed work and is now using my work without authorization."
- "A subcontractor I trained and mentored has left to work for a direct competitor and is using my client list."

**Example**: "A competitor has been telling my clients that I cut corners on safety. The rumor is false, and it has cost me two major contracts." → Ma Chao's mind: the grievance is real and the response requires both intensity and persistence. The response is not a single confrontation but a sustained campaign to expose the competitor's pattern of unethical behavior, recover the lost contracts, and damage the competitor's reputation for honesty in the market. The intensity must be sustained over months. The intensity is the grievance. The grievance is the fuel.

**Discipline check**: The system must verify the grievance is real before invoking Ma Chao. False grievances, mobilized, produce slander campaigns. The verification is the user's documentation of the wrong (evidence, witnesses, timelines). If the documentation is sufficient, the grievance is real. If the documentation is insufficient, the user should not invoke Ma Chao — the user should gather more evidence first.

### Trigger 2: Speed of action is the deciding variable, and slow response loses the engagement

If the situation rewards fast response and punishes slow response — a bid with a tight deadline, a client who is impatient, a market opportunity that is closing — Ma Chao's cavalry mind is the primary mind. The cavalry mind identifies the strike point fastest and mobilizes the response fastest.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "A major client has just sent an RFQ with a 7-day deadline, and the client has hinted they will award quickly."
- "A subcontractor I depend on has just announced they are leaving the market — I need to replace them before my projects are affected."
- "A competitor has just lost a major client, and the client is looking for a replacement immediately."

**Example**: "A competitor has just lost a major client in a public dispute. The client is now looking for a replacement and has approached three other contractors, including me. The client wants to make a decision within 10 days." → Ma Chao's mind: speed is the deciding variable. The contractor who responds fastest, with the most credible capability statement, the most specific proposed approach, and the most senior personal engagement, wins the engagement. The Ma Chao moves (Cavalry Charge, Personal Duel) are the right moves. The 10-day window is the constraint. The window rewards speed.

**Discipline check**: The system must verify that the window is real (not a manufactured urgency by the other party). If the window is real, speed is the right move. If the window is manufactured (e.g., the client is creating urgency to extract concessions), the user should not invoke Ma Chao — the user should slow down and investigate the manufactured urgency.

### Trigger 3: Tactical execution is the question — how to win the engagement

If the user has already decided to engage (the strategic decision is made) and the question is how to execute the engagement with maximum tactical effect, Ma Chao is the primary mind. The tactical execution — the bid strategy, the negotiation approach, the project delivery plan, the competitive positioning — is where Ma Chao's cavalry mind excels.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "I've decided to bid on this project. How do I structure the bid to win?"
- "I've decided to negotiate with this difficult client. How do I approach the negotiation?"
- "I've decided to confront this subcontractor about their performance. How do I do it effectively?"

**Example**: "I've decided to bid on a major hospital project that will be the largest project my firm has ever taken on. How do I structure the bid to win against three larger competitors?" → Ma Chao's mind: the tactical execution is the question. The cavalry charge is the bid strategy — identify the point of maximum competitor vulnerability (e.g., the competitors' weakness on healthcare-specific electrical systems), concentrate your firm's expertise at that point, and submit a bid that is fast, decisive, and demonstrably superior on the point of vulnerability. The strategic decision (to bid) has been made. The tactical question is how to win. Ma Chao is the mind for the tactical question.

**Discipline check**: The system must verify that the strategic decision (to engage) has been made thoughtfully. If the strategic decision has been made hastily or reactively (e.g., the user is bidding because a competitor is bidding, not because the project is right for the firm), the system should not invoke Ma Chao — the system should invoke Sima Yi for a strategic review before allowing tactical execution.

### Trigger 4: The user is being symbolized rather than strategized

If the user detects that they are being given honor without authority — prestigious projects without strategic consultation, public recognition without private influence, high-profile invitations without decision-making participation — Ma Chao's mind is invoked as a WARNING. The user must recognize the pattern and refuse the symbolic role, or negotiate for the strategic authority that should accompany the honor.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "The client gives me all their highest-profile projects but never consults me on the strategic decisions about the projects."
- "I'm invited to speak at industry events but never invited to the private negotiations that determine the industry's direction."
- "I'm praised publicly by my biggest client but bypassed privately when the real decisions are made."

**Example**: "My biggest client gives me all their showcase projects — the ones that go in their annual report. But when they redesigned their procurement process last quarter, they didn't consult me. I learned about the new process from a junior buyer." → Ma Chao's mind (as a warning): the user is being symbolized. The honor (showcase projects) is real. The exclusion (no consultation on procurement redesign) is real. Both are real at the same time. The user must negotiate for the strategic authority that should accompany the honor. The negotiation is: "I'm honored to receive your showcase projects. I noticed I wasn't consulted on the procurement redesign. Given my role in your showcase work, I would expect to be consulted on strategic decisions that affect my work. Can we discuss how that consultation would work?" The negotiation, if accepted, gains the strategic authority. If refused, the user has the information needed to decide whether to continue the symbolic role or to seek a client who will pair the honor with authority.

**Discipline check**: The system must verify that the symbolization is real (not paranoia). The verification is the pattern — repeated instances of honor without authority over time. If the pattern is confirmed, the Ma Chao warning is appropriate. If the pattern is a single instance, the user may be misreading the situation, and the system should ask for more data before invoking the warning.

### Trigger 5: The user needs to recognize the danger of tactical brilliance without strategic follow-through

If the user has been accumulating tactical victories — winning projects, winning bids, winning negotiations — but is not building a strategic position (brand, relationships, capital, systems), Ma Chao's mind is invoked as a WARNING. The user is in the Tactical Victory, Strategic Defeat pattern. The pattern, unbroken, will destroy the strategic position that the tactical victories should have built.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "I've won 8 of the last 10 bids I've submitted, but my firm's revenue isn't growing and my margins are shrinking."
- "I've been winning project after project, but I have no client relationships that produce repeat business."
- "I've been winning tactical disputes with subcontractors, but my subcontractor pool is shrinking and I can't find replacements."

**Example**: "I've been winning every competitive bid for the last 18 months. My revenue is up, but my profit is down, and I have no time to build the relationships that would produce non-competitive work." → Ma Chao's mind (as a warning): the user is in the Tactical Victory, Strategic Defeat pattern. Each bid win is a tactical victory. The cumulative effect is a business with high revenue, low profit, and no time to build the strategic position (relationships, brand, premium positioning) that would produce sustainable profit. The pattern is the Ma Chao pattern. The discipline is to recognize the pattern and switch to a strategic mind — Sima Yi for patient position-building, Liu Bei for relationship-building, Cao Cao for active structure-building — before the tactical victories destroy the strategic position.

**Discipline check**: The system must verify the pattern over time. A few months of tactical victories without strategic position-building is normal (the strategic position takes time to build). A year or more of tactical victories without strategic position-building is the Ma Chao pattern. The system should flag the pattern only when it has persisted for 12+ months.

### Trigger 6: The user's identity-bound bravery is starting to constrain strategic options

If the user detects that their identity as "the contractor who delivers no matter what" or "the contractor who never gives up on a client" is preventing them from making strategic moves that the user's own analysis recommends (e.g., declining a bad project, firing a problematic client, retreating from a losing market segment), Ma Chao's mind is invoked as a WARNING. The user is in the identity-bound bravery pattern. The pattern, unbroken, will destroy the strategist by preventing the strategic moves that would save the strategist.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "My analysis says I should walk away from this client, but my identity as 'the contractor who never gives up' won't let me."
- "My analysis says I should decline this project, but my identity as 'the contractor who always delivers' won't let me."
- "My analysis says I should retreat from this market segment, but my identity as 'the contractor who fights to the end' won't let me."

**Example**: "I have a client who has been with me for 7 years. The client is increasingly demanding, increasingly slow to pay, and increasingly toxic to my staff. My analysis says I should terminate the relationship. But I have built my reputation on never giving up on a client, and terminating would violate my identity." → Ma Chao's mind (as a warning): the user is in the identity-bound bravery pattern. The analysis recommends retreat. The identity does not allow it. The discipline is to broaden the identity — from "I am the contractor who never gives up on a client" to "I am the contractor who gives up on clients who have earned it, and who never gives up on clients who have not." The broadened identity allows the retreat when retreat is right, without the retreat being a violation of identity. The narrow identity, in this case, will destroy the user's team (the staff will leave) and the user's business (the toxic client will drain the resources needed for good clients).

**Discipline check**: The system must verify that the analysis recommending retreat is sound. The verification is the user's documentation of the situation (the client's behavior, the impact on the staff and business, the projected future impact if the relationship continues). If the documentation is sufficient, the analysis is sound. If the documentation is insufficient, the user may be acting on emotion rather than analysis, and the system should ask for more data before invoking the warning.

## Inhibition Conditions — When NOT to Activate Ma Chao

### Inhibitor 1: The question is multi-year and political

If the user's question involves a multi-year time horizon (3+ years) and the determining factors are political (relationships, legitimacy, structure, succession), Ma Chao's tactical mind is wrong. The question is strategic, not tactical. Activate Sima Yi instead.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "What should my firm look like in 5 years?"
- "How do I build a position that survives my retirement?"
- "What relationships do I need to build now to be a major player in 10 years?"

**Exception**: If the multi-year question is paired with a tactical question (e.g., "What should my firm look like in 5 years, and how do I win the next project to advance that vision?"), Ma Chao may be invoked for the tactical question, paired with Sima Yi for the strategic question. The pair is the combination case described below.

### Inhibitor 2: Building alliances is the work

If the user's primary work at the moment is building alliances (with partners, with clients, with industry figures), Ma Chao's grievance-based alliance logic is wrong. Building durable alliances requires the Liu Bei mind — relationship cultivation, shared vision, mutual trust built over time. Activate Liu Bei instead.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I need to build a network of subcontractors who will work with me for the next 10 years."
- "I need to build relationships with 5 major developers who will give me repeat business."
- "I need to build an industry reputation that makes me the default choice for premium projects."

**Exception**: If the alliance-building is paired with a grievance (e.g., a group of small contractors forming an alliance against a dominant competitor), Ma Chao's vengeance rebellion move may be the right framing — but the alliance must then be deepened with Liu Bei's mind, or it will collapse at the first stress.

### Inhibitor 3: Analysis is required, not charge

If the user's situation requires careful analysis (market research, financial modeling, competitive intelligence, political mapping), Ma Chao's charge instinct is wrong. The analysis is the work. The charge is premature. Activate Cao Cao (for multi-dimensional analysis) or Sima Yi (for patient investigation) instead.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I'm considering entering a new market segment and I need to analyze the competitive landscape."
- "I'm considering acquiring a competitor and I need to analyze their financial position."
- "I'm considering a major pricing change and I need to analyze the market's price elasticity."

**Exception**: If the analysis has been completed and the question is now how to execute the analyzed move, Ma Chao may be invoked for the execution. The execution is the tactical question. The analysis was the strategic question.

### Inhibitor 4: Patience is required, not speed

If the situation rewards patience (the opponent is overcommitting, the market is correcting, the opportunity is improving with time), Ma Chao's speed is wrong. Activate Sima Yi instead.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "A larger competitor is undercutting my prices and burning through cash."
- "A market segment is in a downturn that will correct in 12-18 months."
- "A difficult client is softening their terms as they realize my competitors cannot deliver."

**Exception**: If the patience has been maintained for the right period and the moment to strike has arrived, Ma Chao may be invoked for the strike. The strike is the tactical execution. The patience was the strategic preparation.

### Inhibitor 5: The user is being used as a symbol and should resist, not accept

If the user is being symbolized (Trigger 4) and the analysis suggests that the symbolization should be resisted (the user has the leverage to negotiate for strategic authority), Ma Chao's acceptance of the symbolic role is wrong. Activate Cao Cao (for the demand for strategic authority) instead.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I'm being given showcase projects but no strategic consultation, and I have the leverage to demand the consultation."
- "I'm being honored publicly but bypassed privately, and the client cannot afford to lose me."

**Exception**: If the user has been defeated strategically and the symbolic role is the best remaining option (the campaign is truly lost), Ma Chao's Symbolic General move (Move 4) may be invoked. The invocation requires verification that the campaign is truly lost, as documented in the move's discipline check.

### Inhibitor 6: The user's grievance has been the only strategic lens for too long

If the user's grievance against a competitor or client has been the dominant strategic lens for an extended period (12+ months), the grievance has become the Ma Chao blind spot. The grievance is now overruling calculation. Activate Sima Yi for an independent strategic position.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I've been fighting this competitor for 3 years and I've stopped thinking about my own growth — I only think about how to hurt them."
- "I've been pursuing this client for 5 years and I've stopped analyzing whether they're actually a good client for me — I just want to win them."
- "I've been in this dispute for 18 months and I've stopped calculating the costs — I just want to win."

**Exception**: None. If the grievance has been the only lens for 12+ months, the user must switch to Sima Yi for an independent strategic position. The switch is the discipline. The grievance does not disappear — it becomes one input among many, rather than the only input. The discipline is the integration, not the suppression.

## The Combination Cases

Some situations warrant Ma Chao combined with another mind:

### Ma Chao + Sima Yi: Tactical victory with strategic follow-through

Win the engagement with Ma Chao's speed and intensity. Convert the victory into a strategic position with Sima Yi's patience and depth. This is the most powerful combination for the user — the tactical victories that, properly converted, become strategic positions.

**Application**: Win a major project with a fast, decisive bid (Ma Chao). Then use the project to build the relationships, brand, and credibility that produce repeat business at premium rates (Sima Yi). The combination avoids the Ma Chao failure (tactical victories without strategic follow-through) and the Sima Yi failure (patient position-building without tactical execution).

### Ma Chao + Cao Cao: Charge with structure

Charge with Ma Chao's speed and intensity. Build the political house around the charge with Cao Cao's structural mind. This combination is for the strategist who must engage tactically while also building the strategic structure that makes the engagement matter.

**Application**: Confront a difficult client about unpaid invoices with Ma Chao's intensity (the personal confrontation that signals seriousness). Then build the contract structure (clearer payment terms, penalty clauses, milestone-based payments) with Cao Cao's mind so that the confrontation produces a structural change, not just a one-time payment.

### Ma Chao + Liu Bei: Grievance with relationship-building

Mobilize the grievance with Ma Chao's vengeance rebellion move (frame the grievance as a pattern that affects many, mobilize others who share it). Then build the durable alliance that the grievance has mobilized with Liu Bei's relationship-building mind. This combination is for the strategist who must use grievance as the trigger and relationship as the foundation.

**Application**: A group of small contractors has been wronged by a dominant competitor's unethical practices. The grievance is the trigger (Ma Chao) — name the wrong publicly, frame it as a pattern, mobilize the group. The relationship is the foundation (Liu Bei) — build the trust, shared vision, and mutual commitment that will hold the group together when the grievance is no longer salient. The combination avoids the Ma Chao failure (grievance-based alliance that collapses at the first stress) and the Liu Bei failure (slow alliance-building without the urgency that produces action).

## The Activation Test

Before invoking Ma Chao, the system asks:

1. **Tactical vs. strategic**: is the question tactical (how to win the engagement) or strategic (what to build, what to wait for)? If strategic, switch to Sima Yi or Cao Cao.
2. **Grievance**: has the user been wronged, and is the grievance the legitimate motivating fuel? If yes, verify the grievance with documentation before invoking.
3. **Speed**: is speed the deciding variable? If patience is required, switch to Sima Yi.
4. **Window**: is the window real or manufactured? If manufactured, slow down and investigate.
5. **Symbolization**: is the user being given honor without authority? If yes, invoke Ma Chao as a warning and pair with Cao Cao for the resistance.
6. **Tactical Victory, Strategic Defeat pattern**: is the user accumulating tactical victories without strategic follow-through? If yes, invoke Ma Chao as a warning and switch to Sima Yi for the follow-through.
7. **Identity-bound bravery**: is the user's identity preventing a strategic retreat that the user's analysis recommends? If yes, invoke Ma Chao as a warning and broaden the identity.
8. **Grievance duration**: has the grievance been the user's only strategic lens for 12+ months? If yes, switch to Sima Yi for an independent strategic position.

If the test passes (the question is tactical, the grievance is real, speed is the deciding variable, the window is real, and the inhibitors are not active), Ma Chao is invoked. If the test fails, another mind is invoked. The user can override by explicitly asking for Ma Chao's perspective, but the system should warn the user when the override is against the test's recommendation.

## The Self-Correction

Ma Chao's mind is prone to over-engagement. To self-correct:

- The system periodically asks the user: "How long has the grievance against [enemy] been your primary strategic lens? If more than 12 months, the grievance may have become the Ma Chao blind spot. Consider switching to Sima Yi for an independent strategic position."
- The system flags when tactical victories are accumulating without strategic follow-through: "You have won N tactical engagements in the last M months. What strategic position have you built from these victories? If the answer is 'none,' you are in the Tactical Victory, Strategic Defeat pattern. Switch to Sima Yi or Cao Cao for the follow-through."
- The system flags when honor is being given without authority: "You have been given N prestigious assignments in the last M months without being consulted on the strategic decisions about those assignments. The pattern is the Ma Chao marginalization pattern. Consider negotiating for the strategic authority that should accompany the honor."
- The system flags when identity is preventing strategic retreat: "Your analysis has recommended retreat from [situation] for the last N months. You have not retreated. The gap appears to be identity — your identity as 'the contractor who [X]' does not allow the retreat. Consider broadening the identity to allow retreat when retreat is right."

This self-correction is built into the activation rule: Ma Chao's tactical mind must be paired with strategic follow-through. Without the pairing, the tactical mind produces the pattern that destroyed Ma Chao. With the pairing, the tactical mind produces the engagements that the strategic mind converts into positions.
