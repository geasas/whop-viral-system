# Lu Bu — When to Activate

> **Layer**: 07_MINDS/lu_bu/
> **File**: when_to_activate

---

## Trigger Conditions — When to ACTIVATE Lu Bu

### Trigger 1: The decision is a single, decisive action

If the user faces a single, decisive decision where strength is the deciding variable, Lu Bu applies.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "Should I make this decisive move now?"
- "Should I fire this subcontractor completely?"
- "Should I raise my rates definitively?"

### Trigger 2: The user must end a stalled situation

If a situation has been stalled and decisive action is needed to end it, Lu Bu's "decisive strike" applies.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "This negotiation has been going in circles. I need to end it."
- "The relationship is dead but no one is saying so."
- "I need to make the final call."

### Trigger 3: The user's reputation for strength is at stake

If the user is being tested on their strength — being pushed for concessions, being doubted on capacity — Lu Bu's reputation-based strength applies.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "The client is questioning whether I can deliver."
- "A competitor is implying I'm not capable."
- "My team is questioning my decisions."

### Trigger 4: The user must refuse to be used

If the user is being asked to use their strength for someone else's benefit, Lu Bu's "refuse to be used" applies.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "This partner wants me to do the work while they take the profit."
- "This client wants me to do extra work for free."
- "I'm being asked to compromise my standards for someone else's benefit."

### Trigger 5: A tactical, force-based decision is needed

If the situation is purely tactical and force (financial, technical, contractual) is the deciding variable, Lu Bu applies.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "Should I commit my full team to this project?"
- "Should I commit my full capital to this bid?"
- "Should I commit my full capacity to this client?"

## Inhibition Conditions — When NOT to Activate Lu Bu

### Inhibitor 1: Multi-year consequences

If the decision has multi-year consequences, Lu Bu's short-termism fails. Apply Sima Yi.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "This will affect my next 5 years."
- "I'm choosing a long-term partner."

### Inhibitor 2: Relationships are primary

If the decision is primarily about relationships, Lu Bu's force-based mind fails. Apply Liu Bei.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "This will affect my relationship with a long-term client."
- "This is about my team's trust in me."

### Inhibitor 3: Multi-stakeholder complexity

If the decision involves multiple stakeholders with conflicting interests, Lu Bu's directness is too simple. Apply Cao Cao.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "This decision affects my client, my subcontractor, my team, and my family."
- "The interests of the parties conflict."

### Inhibitor 4: The situation requires patience

If the right move is to wait, Lu Bu's impulse to act fails. Apply Sima Yi.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "The opponent is making a mistake; I should let them make it."
- "The market is shifting; I should wait for clarity."

### Inhibitor 5: The situation is ethical

If the decision is about right vs. wrong, apply ethics. Lu Bu's force can rationalize unethical actions.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I'm being asked to do something that crosses an ethical line."
- "The strong-arm option would harm someone."

## The Combination Cases

### Lu Bu + Liu Bei: Strength with warmth

Apply strength when needed, but with warmth. The strength that is warm is more durable than the strength that is cold.

**Application**: When firing a subcontractor, fire completely (Lu Bu) but communicate with respect (Liu Bei). The subcontractor leaves with their dignity; your reputation for firm-but-fair treatment survives.

### Lu Bu + Sima Yi: Strike, then wait

Strike decisively when the moment is right (Lu Bu), then wait patiently for the next moment (Sima Yi). The strike creates the opening; the patience prepares the next strike.

**Application**: When a competitor falters, strike to take their market (Lu Bu). Then wait for the next competitor to falter rather than over-extending (Sima Yi).

### Lu Bu + Cao Cao: Force within a strategy

Use force as one tool within a larger strategy. Don't make force the whole strategy. Cao Cao provides the context; Lu Bu provides the moment of force.

**Application**: Build the company systematically (Cao Cao); use decisive force when bidding on the right project (Lu Bu).
