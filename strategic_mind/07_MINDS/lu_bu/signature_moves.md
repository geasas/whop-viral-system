## The 5 Signature Moves

### Move 1: The Three-Way Duel (三英戰呂布)

**The pattern**: When facing multiple opponents, fight the strongest first. Defeating the strongest demoralizes the rest; defeating the weakest only delays them.

**Historical example**: The famous scene where Lu Bu fought Liu Bei, Guan Yu, and Zhang Fei simultaneously. (Historically dubious but symbolically important.) Lu Bu fought the strongest first — Guan Yu — and only after exhausting him did the others engage. The fight became a stalemate, but Lu Bu survived because he fought the strongest first.

**Mechanism**: When facing multiple demands (multiple projects, multiple competing offers, multiple conflicts), tackle the most demanding first. The demanding one, if not addressed, drains resources that could solve the others. Solve it first, and the others become easier.

**Application in contracting**:
- If you have 3 projects demanding attention, identify which is most critical (largest, most reputation-affecting, most time-sensitive) and focus on it first.
- If multiple clients are pressuring for delivery, address the most important client first. The others will accept delay if they trust you; the most important is the one whose trust you cannot afford to lose.

**Risks**: Fighting multiple opponents simultaneously is dangerous. Better to fight them sequentially. Apply this move when you cannot avoid multiple engagements; do not seek them.

---

### Move 2: The Decisive Strike

**The pattern**: When committed to a fight, strike with maximum force at the single point that ends the fight.

**Historical example**: Lu Bu's reputation for one-blow kills. In battle, his strike was designed to end the engagement immediately. He did not engage in prolonged duels; he sought the single decisive strike.

**Mechanism**: A single decisive strike is more efficient than a prolonged engagement. The prolonged engagement drains you; the single strike ends it.

**Application in contracting**:
- When declining a project, decline definitively. "I cannot take this project; I will not reconsider. Thank you for the opportunity." One strike ends the negotiation.
- When firing a subcontractor, fire completely. "Your work is no longer needed. Please collect your final payment. I wish you well." One strike ends the relationship.
- When raising your rates, raise them definitively. "Effective January 1, my rate is X. This is non-negotiable." One strike establishes the new position.

**Risks**: The decisive strike is irreversible. If you misjudge, you cannot retreat. The discipline is to use it only when the decision is final.

---

### Move 3: Riding Alone into the Formation

**The pattern**: Sometimes, the only way to demonstrate strength is to take on a challenge no one else will. The act of taking it on, regardless of the outcome, builds the reputation.

**Historical example**: At Hulao Pass, Lu Bu rode alone against the coalition army. The act itself — the willingness to ride alone — was the demonstration. Whether he won or lost was secondary; the demonstration had already happened.

**Mechanism**: The act of accepting a challenge that others decline is itself a strategic move. The act builds reputation; the outcome is secondary.

**Application in contracting**:
- Take on a project that other contractors have refused (because it's hard, the client is difficult, the timeline is tight). The willingness to take it on builds the reputation. The execution, if successful, cements it.
- Publicly commit to a standard that competitors don't meet. "We deliver within X days, or you don't pay." The willingness to commit builds the reputation. The execution, if you deliver, cements it.

**Risks**: The act must be backed by ability. Taking on a challenge you cannot meet is reputation destruction, not reputation building. Only apply this move when you can back the act with execution.

---

### Move 4: Refuse to Be Used

**The pattern**: Recognize when someone is using your strength for their ends. Refuse to play that role, even at the cost of conflict.

**Historical example**: Lu Bu eventually turned on Dong Zhuo (killing him) because he realized Dong Zhuo was using him. The turning was a refusal to be used. The execution was Lu Bu's standard decisive strike — overwhelming force applied at the right point.

**Mechanism**: Allowing yourself to be used depletes you over time. The user gets the benefit; you bear the cost. The refusal may cause conflict in the short term, but it preserves your strength for your own ends in the long term.

**Application in contracting**:
- A partner wants you to deliver a project at a low margin so they can take the profit. Refuse. Better to lose the project than to be the instrument of someone else's profit at your expense.
- A client wants you to do extra work without extra pay so they can save their budget. Refuse. Better to lose the client than to be the source of free work.

**Risks**: The refusal may close opportunities. Apply this move when the cost of being used exceeds the cost of the lost opportunity.

---

### Move 5: The Last Stand with Dignity

**The pattern**: When the situation is truly unwinnable, accept it with dignity. Don't beg, don't betray further, don't compromise your character. End the game on your terms.

**Historical example**: At Xiapi, Lu Bu was captured and executed by Cao Cao. His last words (in the novel) were an attempt to negotiate, which Cao Cao refused. The historical reality may have been different, but the symbolic Lu Bu — the warrior who could not surrender with dignity — is the warning.

**Mechanism**: The end of a game is also a strategic moment. The dignified end preserves the reputation that survives the loss. The begging end destroys the reputation.

**Application in contracting**:
- When a project fails and you cannot recover it, communicate the failure honestly and completely. Don't blame others, don't minimize, don't spin. The honest failure preserves your reputation.
- When you must close the business (in a hypothetical worst case), close it cleanly. Pay what you can. Apologize for what you can't. The clean exit preserves the possibility of future relationships.

**Risks**: The dignified end may feel worse than the spin in the moment. The payoff is the reputation that survives.

## How to Recognize When to Apply Each Move

| Move | When to apply | When NOT to apply |
|------|--------------|------------------|
| Three-Way Duel | When facing multiple demands simultaneously | When you can sequence them (do that instead) |
| Decisive Strike | When the decision is final and the opponent must accept it | When the decision may be reversible |
| Riding Alone | When the act of taking the challenge builds reputation | When you cannot back the act with execution |
| Refuse to Be Used | When the cost of being used exceeds the cost of refusal | When the use is mutual (both benefit) |
| Last Stand with Dignity | When the situation is unwinnable | When the situation is winnable (don't give up prematurely) |
