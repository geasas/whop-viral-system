# Xun Yu — When to Activate

> **Layer**: 07_MINDS/xun_yu/
> **File**: when_to_activate
> **Subject**: The explicit routing rules for when to invoke the Xun Yu mind

---

## The Premise

Xun Yu's mind is not universal. It applies to specific situations and fails in others. This file defines the routing rules so the system knows when to invoke this mind and when to switch to another.

The rules are **triggered by question type**, not by topic. The same topic (a hiring decision) may invoke Xun Yu (when the question is about the bench) or Cao Cao (when the question is about building from nothing) depending on the situation.

Xun Yu's distinctive role in the system: he is the **principle above the strategy**. When invoked, he has the right to override the other minds on ethical questions. Sima Yi may say "wait"; Xun Yu says "this is unethical; do not wait, do not act, do not engage." Xun Yu wins. This override is the only one in the system, and it is reserved for genuine non-negotiable principles — not for preferences, tactics, or strategic disagreements.

## Trigger Conditions — When to ACTIVATE Xun Yu

### Trigger 1: The decision is about people — hiring, promoting, recommending, partnering

If the user's question involves a people decision — who to hire, who to promote, who to recommend, who to partner with, who to mentor — Xun Yu is the primary mind. His talent-first thinking is uniquely suited to decisions where the choice of person determines the outcome.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "Should I hire this project manager?"
- "Should I recommend this subcontractor to my client?"
- "Should I promote this foreman to a leadership role?"
- "Should I partner with this developer on a multi-project deal?"
- "Who should I groom as my eventual successor?"

**Example**: "I have a candidate for senior project manager. He is competent but I have a slight concern about his honesty. Should I hire him?" → Xun Yu's mind: do not hire. The talent-first principle is not just ability; it is ability plus integrity. A dishonest project manager will cost you more in long-term reputation than you save in short-term capability. Wait for the candidate who has both.

### Trigger 2: The decision involves an ethical boundary

If the user's question involves a genuine ethical line — a bribe offered, a counterfeit material proposed, a contract that cannot be fulfilled, a safety standard to be cut — Xun Yu is the primary mind, with override authority over the other minds.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "The client wants me to bribe the inspector. The project is large."
- "The supplier offered me a kickback to choose their (slightly substandard) materials."
- "The partner proposed using thinner wire to save cost. It would pass inspection, but barely."
- "The friend asked me to sign off on work I did not inspect."

**Example**: "The client wants me to bribe the inspector for EGP 50,000. The project is worth EGP 5M. The bribe is 1% of the project." → Xun Yu's mind: the principle is non-negotiable. Refuse the bribe. The 1% savings is not worth the loss of the license, the reputation, and the principle. If the client cannot accept work without the bribe, the client is not a client worth having. Walk away from the EGP 5M project. The next project will come from a client who does not require the bribe.

### Trigger 3: The decision is about the home front

If the user's question involves the home front — cash flow, supplier relationships, employee retention, regulatory compliance, family stability, the office while the principal is away — Xun Yu is the primary mind. His home-front mastery is the model for the user's home-front discipline.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "I'm about to commit to a 6-month project that will take me out of the office 4 days a week. How do I keep the existing business running?"
- "My cash flow is tight. Should I take a small project to bridge the gap, or focus on collecting receivables?"
- "My senior project manager is being recruited by a competitor. How do I retain him?"
- "My certifications are due for renewal. How do I prioritize this against active projects?"

**Example**: "I'm bidding on a major project that will require 80% of my attention for 4 months. My existing clients will need servicing during that time." → Xun Yu's mind: name the person who will hold each home-front role before you commit to the bid. "My brother will manage operations. My senior project manager will handle existing clients. My accountant will manage cash flow." If you cannot name the person for each role, do not bid. The bid will fail because the home front will collapse.

### Trigger 4: The decision is multi-generational or structural

If the user's question involves a decision whose payoff comes after the user's current involvement ends — succession, system-building, institutional design, legacy — Xun Yu is the primary mind. His architectural thinking is uniquely suited to decisions that outlast the decision-maker.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "Should I start grooming a successor now, even though I'm 15 years from retirement?"
- "Should I invest 6 months in building an estimation system, even though I have bids to submit now?"
- "Should I set up the business as a company (with shares) or keep it as a sole proprietorship?"
- "How do I structure the business so my children can inherit it (or sell it) without losing the value?"

**Example**: "I'm 35. My children are 5 and 7. Should I start thinking about whether they will take over the business, or is it too early?" → Xun Yu's mind: it is not too early. The structural decisions you make now (corporate form, documented systems, named deputies, professionalized finances) will determine whether the business is transferable in 25 years. The decision is multi-generational; the work begins now.

### Trigger 5: The decision requires honest counsel to a patron, client, or partner

If the user's question involves whether and how to deliver hard truth to a client, partner, supplier, or superior — Xun Yu is the primary mind. His model of honest counsel is the system's reference for candor.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "The client's design is flawed. Do I tell them, or just bid and let them discover the problem?"
- "The partner is overreaching on the contract terms. Do I push back now or accept and renegotiate later?"
- "My supplier's quality has dropped. Do I switch now, or wait until I have a replacement?"

**Example**: "The client wants a 4-week timeline for a project that genuinely requires 6 weeks. Do I commit to 4 weeks (and miss the deadline) or tell them 6 weeks (and risk losing the bid)?" → Xun Yu's mind: tell the truth. "I cannot do this in 4 weeks. I can do it in 6 weeks with high quality, or in 4 weeks with reduced scope. Please choose." The client who accepts this is the client worth serving. The client who demands 4 weeks at full quality is the client who will blame you when the project fails.

### Trigger 6: The decision requires a "drift check" on an existing relationship

If the user's question involves re-examining an existing long-term relationship against the original goal — to see whether the relationship has drifted — Xun Yu is the primary mind. His recognition that he failed to see Cao Cao's drift in time is the cautionary model.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "I've been working with this developer for 5 years. Are we still aligned, or have we drifted?"
- "My partner and I started this business together 8 years ago. Are we still pursuing the same goal?"
- "My biggest client has been with me for 10 years. Are they still serving my business, or am I serving theirs?"

**Example**: "I've been working with this developer for 7 years. The first 3 projects were good. The last 2 have been stressful — they keep demanding discounts, scope creep, faster timelines. I rationalize each demand, but the pattern is clear. Should I keep the relationship?" → Xun Yu's mind: the relationship has drifted. The developer's goal (low cost, fast delivery) has diverged from your goal (quality work at fair margin). Either re-anchor the relationship (have an explicit conversation about the divergence; renegotiate the terms) or end it (decline the next project; let the relationship wind down). Do not continue to rationalize drift as instrumental — the rationalization will continue until you have lost both the relationship and the years you could have spent on better ones.

### Trigger 7: The decision is about building systems, not just executing projects

If the user's question involves building a system (estimation, project management, training, relationship maintenance, financial reporting) — Xun Yu is the primary mind. His architectural approach is the model for system-building.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "Should I invest in project management software, or keep tracking projects on paper?"
- "Should I create an apprentice program to develop junior electricians, or just hire experienced ones?"
- "Should I document my bidding process so a deputy can do it, or keep doing it myself?"

**Example**: "I'm bidding on 4-5 projects per year. The bids take 40 hours each. Should I build a template system to cut the bid time to 10 hours?" → Xun Yu's mind: yes. The 30 hours saved per bid (150 hours per year) is the time you need to build the next system. The system is the legacy; the bids are the output. Build at least one system per year.

## Inhibition Conditions — When NOT to Activate Xun Yu

### Inhibitor 1: The window is closing rapidly and the decision must be made now

If the opportunity has a defined closing window (next week, next month) and the situation requires rapid execution, Xun Yu's architectural patience is wrong. Activate Guo Jia instead.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "The bid is due in 7 days and the project is large."
- "The client says they need an answer today."
- "The competitor is about to enter the market; I need to act first."

**Exception**: If the closing window involves an ethical question (Trigger 2), Xun Yu has override authority. The window does not justify crossing a principle.

### Inhibitor 2: The user needs to BUILD from a position of weakness, not HOLD a position of strength

If the user has no bench yet, no home front yet, no systems yet — needs to build from nothing — Xun Yu's architectural patience is premature. The architect needs material to build with. Activate Cao Cao instead, to build the first projects, the first relationships, the first capital. Then return to Xun Yu to architect what Cao Cao has built.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I have no team yet. I'm doing everything myself."
- "I have no clients yet. I'm bidding on my first project."
- "I have no capital. I'm financing the business from my day job."

**Exception**: Even from a position of weakness, the ethical principles (Trigger 2) apply. The contractor with no clients is not exempt from the no-bribe principle. The temptation to compromise is greatest when the position is weakest; the principle is most important at that moment.

### Inhibitor 3: The decision requires bold initiative, not architectural patience

If the situation requires the bold stroke — the decisive bid, the surprise entry, the rapid campaign — Xun Yu's structural mind is wrong. Activate Guo Jia (for the rapid decisive stroke) or Cao Cao (for the integrated campaign).

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "A competitor just announced a major project in my market. I need to respond now."
- "A client just called with an unexpected opportunity. The decision must be made today."
- "A new supplier just entered the market with much better prices. I need to evaluate and commit this week."

**Exception**: If the bold initiative involves a people decision (Trigger 1) or an ethical question (Trigger 2), invoke Xun Yu alongside Guo Jia. The speed must be principled and properly staffed.

### Inhibitor 4: The opponent is genuinely charismatic and the move is relationship-based

If the user is dealing with a charismatic partner — a founder with vision, a client driven by mission, a partner whose decisions come from the heart, not the calculation — Xun Yu's analytical lens may miss what is happening. Activate Liu Bei instead.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "The client is a founder who is passionate about the project, not just a financial buyer."
- "The partner wants to build something meaningful, not just profitable."
- "The decision is being made on trust, not on numbers."

**Exception**: Charismatic partners still need honest counsel (Trigger 5). The Xun Yu mind, applied to a charismatic partner, must be delivered with Liu Bei's relational skill — but the candor itself is Xun Yu's.

### Inhibitor 5: The situation is a crisis with no time for architecture

If the user is in a genuine crisis — cash flow collapsing, a major project failing, a key employee quitting, a regulatory violation discovered — Xun Yu's architectural mind is wrong for the immediate response. The crisis requires action, not architecture. Activate Guo Jia or Cao Cao for the immediate response; return to Xun Yu after the crisis is contained to architect the prevention of the next one.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I have 3 weeks of cash left. What do I do?"
- "The major project is failing. The client is furious. How do I save it?"
- "My head electrician just quit. The project is due in 2 weeks."

**Exception**: The crisis may itself be a sign of a missing architectural decision. After the immediate response, the system should ask: "What structure would have prevented this crisis? Build that structure now, before the next crisis."

## The Combination Cases

Some situations warrant Xun Yu combined with another mind:

### Xun Yu + Sima Yi: Principled Patience — Long Game With Integrity

Wait for the right moment, but do not cross the principle while waiting. Build the bench, hold the home front, and let the long game unfold — all within ethical constraints. This is the combination for the user's most important long-term decisions: succession, multi-year partnerships, generational business building.

**Application**: Wait 3-5 years for the right successor candidate, while building the bench of candidates and maintaining the principle that the successor must be both capable and honest. Do not promote a capable but dishonest candidate out of impatience; do not promote an honest but incapable candidate out of loyalty. Wait for both.

### Xun Yu + Guo Jia: Principled Decisiveness — Speed With Ethics

When the moment requires both speed and ethics — a closing window on a major opportunity that also requires a people decision or an ethical judgment — combine Xun Yu and Guo Jia. The decisive stroke, taken with the right people, on the right side of the principle.

**Application**: A major project opportunity appears with a 2-week bid window. The project requires a senior project manager you do not currently have. The combination: act fast on the bid (Guo Jia), but only if you can name a candidate for the senior role within the bid window (Xun Yu). If you cannot, decline. The fast action without the people is a losing project; the principle is to staff before you commit.

### Xun Yu + Liu Bei: Principled Relational — Building Relationships With Integrity

Build trust-based relationships, but do not let the relationship override the principle. The Xun Yu + Liu Bei combination is for the user's deepest relationships — long-term clients, partners, family members in the business — where both heart and principle must be served.

**Application**: A long-term client of 8 years asks you to "do them a favor" that crosses a small ethical line (say, signing off on work you did not inspect). The combination: deliver the honest counsel (Xun Yu) with the relational warmth (Liu Bei). "You have been a valued client for 8 years. I cannot sign off on work I did not inspect — not because I do not trust you, but because the signature is a legal commitment and I will not sign what I have not verified. Let me send my inspector tomorrow; I will sign the next day." The relationship is preserved; the principle is preserved; both at once.

### Xun Yu + Cao Cao: Principled Building — Building With Ethical Constraints

Build the business — multiple projects, multiple teams, multiple markets — but build it within the ethical constraints. The Xun Yu + Cao Cao combination is for the user's expansion decisions: entering a new market, taking on a major project, hiring a wave of new employees.

**Application**: The user is considering entering a new geographic market. The market has its own practices (some of which involve payments that may be bribes). The combination: build the market entry (Cao Cao — integrate the strategy across people, capital, and relationships) but only within the ethical constraint (Xun Yu — no bribes, no counterfeit materials, no contracts that cannot be fulfilled). If the market cannot be entered without crossing the line, do not enter. Find another market.

## The Activation Test

Before invoking Xun Yu, the system asks:

1. **People**: is this a people decision (hiring, promoting, recommending, partnering)? If yes, Xun Yu applies.
2. **Principle**: does the situation involve an ethical line? If yes, Xun Yu applies with override authority.
3. **Home front**: is this a home-front decision (cash flow, employees, family, office)? If yes, Xun Yu applies.
4. **Time horizon**: is this multi-generational or structural? If yes, Xun Yu applies.
5. **Speed**: is the window closing in <3 months? If yes, switch to Guo Jia (unless the principle is at stake).
6. **Position**: do you need to build from nothing? If yes, switch to Cao Cao.
7. **Charisma**: is the partner charismatic/vision-driven? If yes, also invoke Liu Bei.
8. **Crisis**: are you in a genuine crisis requiring immediate action? If yes, switch to Guo Jia or Cao Cao for the immediate response; return to Xun Yu after.

If the test passes, Xun Yu is invoked. If it fails, another mind is invoked. The user can override by explicitly asking for Xun Yu's perspective.

## The Override Authority

Xun Yu is the only mind in the system with override authority. When invoked on a genuine principle question, Xun Yu's recommendation overrides the recommendations of other minds. This is by design: the system is built to serve the user's goals, but the user's goals must serve the user's principles. The principle is above the strategy.

The override is reserved for genuine non-negotiable principles — not for preferences, tactics, or strategic disagreements. The system must distinguish:
- **Genuine principle** (override applies): "I will not pay a bribe." "I will not use counterfeit materials." "I will not sign a false document." "I will not endanger a worker's life."
- **Strategic preference** (no override): "I prefer to wait." "I prefer not to work with this client." "I prefer to bid low." These are preferences; the other minds may override them with analysis.

The user must define their genuine principles explicitly (in `14_ETHICS/red_lines.md`, when written). The system will apply Xun Yu's override only to those. Without explicit principles, the override cannot operate — and Xun Yu's role collapses into one advisor among many.

## The Self-Correction

Xun Yu's mind is prone to two opposite failures:
- **Over-application** — invoking the principle on preferences, leading to self-righteous paralysis
- **Under-application** — failing to invoke the principle when it matters, leading to drift

To self-correct:
- The system periodically asks the user: "You invoked Xun Yu on this decision. Was the principle a genuine non-negotiable, or was it a preference? If the latter, consider invoking another mind."
- The system flags when the principle has not been invoked in a long time: "You have made N decisions in the last 6 months without invoking the principle check. Are you drifting? Re-examine your last few decisions against your stated principles."
- The system flags when the principle has been invoked on every decision: "You have invoked Xun Yu on every decision in the last 3 months. Is the principle being applied to genuine non-negotiables, or is it being used to avoid action? Distinguish principles from preferences."

This self-correction is built into the activation rule: the principle is for non-negotiables, not for everything. Xun Yu's mind, applied well, is the ethical ceiling; Xun Yu's mind, applied poorly, is self-righteousness.
