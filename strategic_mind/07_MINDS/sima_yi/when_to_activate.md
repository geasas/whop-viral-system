# Sima Yi — When to Activate

> **Layer**: 07_MINDS/sima_yi/
> **File**: when_to_activate
> **Subject**: The explicit routing rules for when to invoke the Sima Yi mind

---

## The Premise

Sima Yi's mind is not universal. It applies to specific situations and fails in others. This file defines the routing rules so the system knows when to invoke this mind and when to switch to another.

The rules are **triggered by question type**, not by topic. The same topic (a contract negotiation) may invoke Sima Yi or Guo Jia depending on the time horizon and stakes.

---

## Trigger Conditions — When to ACTIVATE Sima Yi

### Trigger 1: The time horizon is multi-year

If the user's question involves a multi-year time horizon (3+ years), Sima Yi is the primary mind. His long-game thinking is uniquely suited to decisions where the payoff comes years from now.

**Trigger pattern**: 
- "What should I do over the next 5 years?"
- "How do I prepare for [future event]?"
- "What's the long-game move here?"

**Example**: "Should I take a 5-year exclusive contract with this developer?" → Sima Yi's mind: the long game is what matters here; the 5-year commitment locks in future flexibility.

### Trigger 2: The opponent has momentum and we want them to overcommit

If the opponent is moving aggressively and we want them to commit too far, Sima Yi's "refuse battle" pattern applies. Let them overextend. Wait for the collapse.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "A new competitor is undercutting us."
- "Our largest client is pushing for unreasonable terms."
- "The opponent has more resources and is pushing hard."

**Example**: "A larger contractor has entered our market and is bidding 30% below our prices." → Sima Yi's mind: let them win the bids at low margins; the math will catch up with them; we maintain our pricing power.

### Trigger 3: The situation is politically delicate and a misstep is costly

If the user is in a situation where any action invites retaliation, Sima Yi's caution and concealment apply. Don't expose yourself; let the situation develop; strike when the moment is right.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "There are factions in the client's organization and I don't want to pick a side."
- "My competitor and I are both being considered for a major project, and the decision-maker is unpredictable."
- "I'm being courted by two partners who hate each other."

**Example**: "The developer's CEO and the procurement director are in conflict over who controls the contractor selection. I'm being considered by both." → Sima Yi's mind: maintain neutrality, build both relationships, let the conflict resolve itself, then engage with the winner.

### Trigger 4: We need to preserve resources for a larger play

If the user is facing a small opportunity that would consume resources needed for a larger opportunity later, Sima Yi's conservation principle applies.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "I have an opportunity that's good but not great, and I have limited capacity."
- "If I take this project, I won't be able to bid on the bigger project that's coming in 6 months."

**Example**: "A EGP 500K project is being offered to me, but I have a feeling a EGP 2M project is coming in 6 months, and I don't have capacity for both." → Sima Yi's mind: if the EGP 2M is real, decline the 500K. If it's a feeling only, take the 500K (income now beats potential income later).

### Trigger 5: We need to outlast rather than outfight

If the user is in a situation where the opponent will fail on their own (due to cash flow, internal politics, health, etc.), Sima Yi's patience applies.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "The competitor is burning through cash and will likely fail in 12-18 months."
- "The client's project is doomed and they'll figure it out soon."
- "The partnership is unstable and will collapse on its own."

**Example**: "A subcontractor I rely on is becoming unreliable, but I don't have a replacement yet." → Sima Yi's mind: don't confront them yet; identify alternatives; switch when the alternatives are ready, not before.

## Inhibition Conditions — When NOT to Activate Sima Yi

### Inhibitor 1: The window is closing rapidly

If the opportunity has a defined closing window (next week, next month), Sima Yi's patience is wrong. Activate Guo Jia instead.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "The bid is due in 7 days."
- "The client says they need an answer today."
- "If I don't act now, the opportunity is gone."

**Exception**: If the user can extend the window (ask for more time), Sima Yi applies to the extended window. But if the window is truly fixed, speed wins.

### Inhibitor 2: The user is in a position of strength that is decaying

If the user has a temporary advantage that is eroding (market position, capital, brand), Sima Yi's patience wastes the advantage. Activate Cao Cao instead.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I have a unique capability that competitors are catching up to."
- "My pricing power is at its peak but competitors are entering."
- "My reputation is high but I haven't been visible in 12 months."

**Exception**: If the user is in a position of strength AND the situation is politically delicate (Trigger 3), the answer is nuanced. Use the strength quietly; don't waste it, but don't expose it either.

### Inhibitor 3: The opponent is genuinely charismatic and the move is relationship-based

If the user is dealing with a charismatic partner whose decisions are driven by vision and values rather than calculation, Sima Yi's analytical lens misses what's happening. Activate Liu Bei instead.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "The client is a founder who is passionate about their project, not just a financial buyer."
- "The partner wants to build something meaningful, not just profitable."
- "The decision is being made on trust, not on numbers."

### Inhibitor 4: The user needs to BUILD, not WAIT

If the user has no position yet and needs to build one — first project, first relationship, first capital — Sima Yi's patience is wrong. The user needs to act, not wait. Activate Cao Cao.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I have no clients yet."
- "I have no reputation yet."
- "I need to build my first 3 projects before I can play a long game."

### Inhibitor 5: The situation is ethical and demands immediate action

If the user is facing an ethical situation where patience would harm innocents, Sima Yi's mind is wrong. Activate `14_ETHICS/red_lines.md` and act.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "A subcontractor is mistreating workers."
- "A client is asking me to do work I know is dangerous."
- "I'm being asked to deceive a third party."

## The Combination Cases

Some situations warrant Sima Yi combined with another mind:

### Sima Yi + Guo Jia: Patient, then decisive

Wait for the moment; when it arrives, strike with Guo Jia's speed. This is the most powerful combination. Years of patience culminating in decisive action.

**Application**: Wait 12-18 months for the right project opportunity, then bid aggressively when it appears.

### Sima Yi + Cao Cao: Patient builder

Build quietly while waiting. Use the time to accumulate resources, relationships, and reputation. When the moment arrives, you have the strength to act.

**Application**: While the market is slow, build your team, deepen relationships, accumulate capital. When the market recovers, you have everything in place to scale fast.

### Sima Yi + Liu Bei: Patient with integrity

Wait, but don't sacrifice your ethics while waiting. Build trust-based relationships, not just strategic ones. Let the long game be a clean game.

**Application**: Rejecting short-term deals that would compromise your reputation, while patiently building the relationships that will pay in 5 years.

## The Activation Test

Before invoking Sima Yi, the system asks:

1. **Time horizon**: is this a 3+ year decision? If yes, Sima Yi applies.
2. **Window**: is the window closing in <3 months? If yes, switch to Guo Jia.
3. **Position**: is the user in a position of strength or weakness? If weak and needing to build, switch to Cao Cao.
4. **Opponent**: is the opponent analytical or charismatic? If charismatic, consider Liu Bei.
5. **Ethics**: does the move require patience that harms innocents? If yes, switch to ethics.

If the test passes, Sima Yi is invoked. If it fails, another mind is invoked. The user can override by explicitly asking for Sima Yi's perspective.

---

## The Self-Correction

Sima Yi's mind is prone to over-patience. To self-correct:

- The system periodically asks the user: "You've been waiting on X for N months. Has the situation changed? Is the moment arriving, or are you waiting past the moment?"
- The system flags when waiting crosses 12 months without action: "Your patience may be turning into paralysis. Consider the Guo Jia alternative."

This self-correction is built into the activation rule: patience is not perpetual — it has a defined end.
