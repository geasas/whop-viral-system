# Yuan Shao — When to Activate (As a Warning)

> **Layer**: 07_MINDS/yuan_shao/
> **File**: when_to_activate
> **Subject**: The explicit routing rules for when to invoke the Yuan Shao mind AS A WARNING
> **Mention frequency in manga**: 427 times across 635 chapters

---

## The Premise

Yuan Shao's mind is invoked differently from every other mind in this system. The other minds are invoked when the user needs to think a certain way. Yuan Shao is invoked when the system detects that the user is thinking a certain way — and needs to stop.

Yuan Shao is the diagnostic mind. The system runs the user's situation through Yuan Shao's patterns and asks: is the user exhibiting any of these patterns? If yes, the system warns the user and invokes the appropriate counter-mind. Yuan Shao is the mirror; the counter-mind is the corrective.

The Yuan Shao mind is never invoked to give strategic advice. It is invoked to give a warning. The advice that follows the warning comes from another mind. The Yuan Shao file is the diagnostic; the counter-mind is the prescription.

This file defines the trigger conditions for invoking Yuan Shao as a warning, the form of the warning, and the routing rules for which counter-mind to invoke after the warning.

## Trigger Conditions — When to ACTIVATE Yuan Shao as a Warning

### Trigger 1: Family Appointment Above Competence

If the user is considering appointing a family member to a position above the family member's demonstrated competence, while passing over a more capable non-family candidate, Yuan Shao's mind is invoked as a warning.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "I'm thinking of putting my brother on this site as project manager."
- "My son needs a role in the business; I'll make him the operations lead."
- "My cousin needs work; I'll give him the procurement contract."

**Example**: "I'm thinking of putting my brother on the Alexandria site as project manager. He's been out of work for a few months and could use the position. The current site manager is more experienced, but my brother needs the work." → Yuan Shao warning: you are about to make Yuan Shao's nepotism mistake. The brother's need is real; the appointment is wrong. Find the brother a different role (one matched to his competence), or help him outside the business, but do not appoint him above competence on a major site.

**Counter-mind to invoke**: Cao Cao (merit-based appointment). The decision must be on demonstrated competence, not on family relationship. The discipline is to separate affection from appointment.

### Trigger 2: Late Decision on a Closing Window

If the user is delaying a decision that has a closing window — and is justifying the delay as "waiting for more information" or "letting things clarify" — Yuan Shao's mind is invoked as a warning.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "I'll wait until the scope is fully clear before I bid."
- "I'm going to see what the other contractors offer before I commit to a price."
- "I need more time to think about whether to take this project."

**Example**: "I've been asked to bid on a project that closes in 10 days. I'd like to wait until I have all the supplier quotes back before I bid." → Yuan Shao warning: you are about to make Yuan Shao's indecision mistake. The window is closing. The supplier quotes will not all come back in time. The bid made with 80% of the information in the open window beats the bid made with 100% of the information in the closed window.

**Counter-mind to invoke**: Guo Jia (rapid exploitation). Decide with the information you have. The bid is a starting point, not a final answer. Adjust as the information arrives. The commitment is more valuable than the perfection.

### Trigger 3: Rejecting Counsel That Has Been Correct

If the user is in a situation where a subordinate, partner, or advisor has warned him about a problem — and the warning has proven correct — and the user is now considering punishing the warner rather than rewarding him, Yuan Shao's mind is invoked as a warning.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "He warned me this project would lose money, and it did. He must have jinxed it."
- "She told me the client was unreliable, and the client has been. But she's always negative."
- "They said the subcontractor would fail, and he did. But their tone was insubordinate."

**Example**: "My site manager warned me the client would delay payments. The client has delayed payments. But my site manager has been negative about this client from the beginning — I think he's biased. I'm going to take him off this project." → Yuan Shao warning: you are about to make Yuan Shao's "punishing the prophet" mistake. The site manager's counsel was correct. The bias you are perceiving is your own discomfort at having been wrong. Removing him punishes correctness and teaches every other employee that correctness is dangerous.

**Counter-mind to invoke**: Liu Bei (trust-based relationship). Reward the truth-teller publicly. The reward signals to every other employee that correctness is welcome, not punished. The signal builds the information system that will save you on the next project.

### Trigger 4: Indecisive Council on a Strategic Decision

If the user is convening a council of advisors, partners, or family members to decide a strategic question, and is waiting for the council to agree before acting, Yuan Shao's mind is invoked as a warning.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "I'll discuss this with my partners and we'll decide together."
- "I want to get everyone's input before I commit."
- "I'm waiting for my brother and my accountant to agree on the structure."

**Example**: "I'm considering buying into a real estate development. I want my brother, my accountant, and my lawyer to all agree on the structure before I move forward. My brother thinks the structure is too risky, my accountant thinks it's fine, my lawyer has concerns about the contract terms. I'm going to wait until they all agree." → Yuan Shao warning: you are about to make Yuan Shao's "indecisive council" mistake. They will not all agree. The disagreement is the information — it tells you the structure is genuinely risky. Your job is not to wait for consensus. Your job is to hear the disagreement, weigh it, decide, and absorb the displeasure of the ones who disagreed.

**Counter-mind to invoke**: Cao Cao (decisive synthesis). Hear the views. Integrate them. Decide alone. The decision is yours, not the council's. The council's job is to inform your decision, not to make it.

### Trigger 5: Image-Optimized Decision Over Substance-Optimized Decision

If the user is choosing between two moves where one looks better (more impressive, more dignified, more flattering to the self-image) and one works better (produces a better outcome but looks less impressive), and the user is leaning toward the looks-better move, Yuan Shao's mind is invoked as a warning.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "I can't withdraw from this bid — it would look weak."
- "I can't take a smaller role on this project — it would damage my reputation."
- "I can't publicly credit my subcontractor — the client will think I didn't do the work."

**Example**: "I'm being offered a subcontractor role on a major project — the kind of project I'd normally lead. I'm tempted to refuse because accepting would make me look like I'm stepping down. But the project would be a major step for my business." → Yuan Shao warning: you are about to make Yuan Shao's "image-conscious retreat" mistake. The role that looks smaller is the role that builds the relationship that will give you the next big role. The refusal that protects your image is the refusal that closes the door. Choose the move that works, not the move that looks.

**Counter-mind to invoke**: Sima Yi (concealment over display). Choose the move that works, even if it looks weak. The appearance of weakness is temporary. The strength of substance compounds.

### Trigger 6: Tolerating Factional Rivalry in the Team

If the user is tolerating rivalry among his team members, his subcontractors, or his partners, and is treating the rivalry as a manageable tension that keeps everyone honest, Yuan Shao's mind is invoked as a warning.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "My site manager and my procurement manager don't get along, but the tension keeps them both sharp."
- "I have two subcontractors who compete for my work; the competition keeps prices down."
- "My partners argue about strategy, but the argument is productive."

**Example**: "My site manager and my procurement manager have been at odds for the last six months. Each blames the other for delays. I haven't intervened because the tension has kept them both on their toes — they each work harder to prove themselves. But the project is now behind schedule, and the delays are increasing." → Yuan Shao warning: you are about to make Yuan Shao's "factional warfare" mistake. The tension is not keeping them sharp. The tension is poisoning the project. Every issue has become a fight over blame. Every solution has become a fight over credit. Your information flow is contaminated by the rivalry — you cannot tell which version of events is true. You must resolve the rivalry, not tolerate it.

**Counter-mind to invoke**: Cao Cao (unified team). Pick a side, replace one of the rivals, or restructure the roles so the rivalry has no battleground. The unified team produces better work than the balanced rivalry.

### Trigger 7: Avoiding a Succession or Key Decision

If the user is avoiding a key decision — succession in his business, naming a successor, documenting processes, giving employees the authority they would need to run things without him — Yuan Shao's mind is invoked as a warning.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "I'll deal with succession later — I'm still young."
- "I don't need to document the processes yet — I'm the one who runs everything."
- "I'll give my employees more authority when they're ready."

**Example**: "I'm 45 and my business depends on me entirely. I haven't named a successor. I haven't documented my processes. My key employees don't have the authority to make decisions without me. I'll get to it — but I'm too busy running the business right now." → Yuan Shao warning: you are about to make Yuan Shao's "unnamed succession" mistake. The decision you are avoiding is being made by default — and the default decision is that your business will collapse if you cannot run it. The delay is not postponement; it is the active construction of the disaster. You must name the successor, document the processes, and delegate the authority — now, while you are well, while the decision is yours to make.

**Counter-mind to invoke**: Cao Cao (early succession planning). Name the heir. Document the playbook. Delegate the authority. The early decision is harder and the late decision is catastrophic.

## Inhibition Conditions — When NOT to Activate Yuan Shao

There are situations where the Yuan Shao warning would be inappropriate. The system should not invoke Yuan Shao when:

### Inhibitor 1: The decision is genuinely patient, not indecisive

If the user is waiting because the wait serves the strike (Sima Yi-style patience), not because the user cannot decide, Yuan Shao is not the right warning. The distinction is whether the wait has a defined end and serves a strategic purpose. If yes, the wait is patience; do not invoke Yuan Shao. If no, the wait is indecision; invoke Yuan Shao.

**Test**: Ask the user — "What is the moment you are waiting for, and what will you do when it arrives?" If the user can answer specifically, the wait is patience. If the user cannot answer specifically, the wait is indecision.

### Inhibitor 2: The family appointment is genuinely the most competent

If the family member being appointed is genuinely the most competent person for the role (demonstrated track record, clear qualifications), Yuan Shao is not the right warning. Family can be in the business. Family can even lead the business, if the family member has the competence. The Yuan Shao warning is for appointments above competence, not for appointments that happen to be family.

**Test**: Ask the user — "If this family member were not family, would you still appoint him to this role based on his track record?" If yes, the appointment is on merit; do not invoke Yuan Shao. If no, the appointment is on family; invoke Yuan Shao.

### Inhibitor 3: The counsel rejection is for cause

If the user is rejecting counsel because the counsel is genuinely wrong (not because the counsel is unwelcome), Yuan Shao is not the right warning. Rejecting bad counsel is correct. Rejecting good counsel because it feels unwelcome is the Yuan Shao mistake. The distinction is the reason for the rejection.

**Test**: Ask the user — "Is the counsel wrong on the merits, or wrong on the feeling?" If the user can identify the specific flaw in the counsel's analysis, the rejection is on the merits. If the user can only identify the feeling ("it didn't sit right," "the tone was off"), the rejection is on the feeling — and Yuan Shao should be invoked.

### Inhibitor 4: The image-optimized decision is also the substance-optimized decision

Sometimes the move that looks best is also the move that works best. In those cases, there is no conflict between image and substance, and Yuan Shao is not the right warning. The Yuan Shao warning is for cases where image and substance conflict, and the user is choosing image over substance.

**Test**: Ask the user — "If no one were watching, would you still choose this move?" If yes, the move is substance-optimized (and the fact that it also looks good is a bonus). If no, the move is image-optimized — and Yuan Shao should be invoked.

## The Form of the Warning

When the system invokes Yuan Shao, the warning should take a specific form. The form is:

1. **Name the pattern**: "You are about to make Yuan Shao's [pattern name] mistake."
2. **Cite the historical example**: "Yuan Shao did this when [historical example], and the result was [historical consequence]."
3. **Map to the user's situation**: "Your situation is similar because [specific parallel]."
4. **State the predicted consequence**: "If you proceed, the likely result is [specific consequence in the user's domain]."
5. **Recommend the counter-move**: "The counter-move is [specific action], from [counter-mind]'s mind."

The form is direct. It does not flatter the user. It does not soften the warning. Yuan Shao's mistake is, by definition, a mistake the user is making because the easier path is seductive. The warning must be sharp enough to break the seduction. Flattery would be Yuan Shao's own pattern (counsel that feels welcome). The warning must be counsel that feels unwelcome — and is correct.

## The Periodic Audit

In addition to the trigger-based activation, the system should run a periodic Yuan Shao audit on the user — monthly, at minimum. The audit asks, for each of the seven triggers:

- Have you made any family appointments above competence in the last month?
- Have you delayed any decision past its optimal moment?
- Have you rejected any counsel that has since proven correct?
- Have you convened any council that did not reach consensus, and let the lack of consensus delay a decision?
- Have you chosen any move for image reasons over substance reasons?
- Have you tolerated any factional rivalry in your team without resolving it?
- Have you avoided any succession or key decision that you know you should make?

If the answer to any of these is yes, the system invokes the corresponding counter-mind and recommends a specific action in the next 30 days.

The audit is uncomfortable. The discomfort is the point. The Yuan Shao patterns are blind spots precisely because they are comfortable to live with. The audit forces the user to see what he has been choosing not to see. The seeing is the first step to correction.

## The Combination Cases

Yuan Shao is a diagnostic mind, not a strategic mind. Yuan Shao is never combined with other minds in the way that, say, Sima Yi and Guo Jia are combined (patient then decisive). Yuan Shao is invoked alone as a warning, and then a counter-mind is invoked as the corrective. The combinations are:

### Yuan Shao warning → Cao Cao corrective
For: family appointments above competence, indecisive councils, factional tolerance, succession avoidance.

### Yuan Shao warning → Guo Jia corrective
For: late decisions on closing windows.

### Yuan Shao warning → Liu Bei corrective
For: rejecting counsel that has been correct.

### Yuan Shao warning → Sima Yi corrective
For: image-optimized decisions where substance-optimized decisions would work better; for treating advantages as proof of rightness.

Each combination is a one-way flow: Yuan Shao warns, the counter-mind corrects. Yuan Shao is never the destination; Yuan Shao is always the warning sign on the way to the destination.

## The Self-Correction

The Yuan Shao mind, because it is a warning mind, does not need the kind of self-correction that the strategic minds need (e.g., Sima Yi's tendency to over-wait). The Yuan Shao mind's self-correction is built into the audit: the audit asks whether the user is exhibiting Yuan Shao patterns, and if yes, recommends the counter-mind.

The discipline for the user is to take the audit seriously — to actually answer the questions honestly, and to actually act on the counter-mind recommendations. The discipline is the work of a lifetime, because Yuan Shao's patterns are the default patterns of a successful person who has not examined his success. The default does not change once; it changes continuously, requiring continuous examination.

The contractor who audits himself against Yuan Shao's patterns every month, and acts on what he finds, will not become Yuan Shao. The contractor who does not audit himself will become Yuan Shao — slowly, comfortably, and without noticing until it is too late.

## The Final Note

Yuan Shao is the most important mind in this system for a successful user — because Yuan Shao is the mind the user is most likely to become without realizing it. The other minds are aspirations (Sima Yi's patience, Cao Cao's decisiveness, Liu Bei's integrity, Guo Jia's speed). Yuan Shao is the destination the user is heading toward by default. The Yuan Shao file is the road sign that says "you are going the wrong way." The road sign is consulted not to be followed but to be obeyed — by turning around.

The system's job is to invoke Yuan Shao at the right moments — the moments when the user is about to make a Yuan Shao move. The user's job is to heed the warning, switch to the counter-mind, and execute the harder, better move. The discipline is the work. The work is the price of not becoming Yuan Shao.
