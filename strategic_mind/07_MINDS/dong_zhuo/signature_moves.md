## The 6 Signature Moves

### Move 1: Capture the Symbol

**The pattern**: Identify the symbol of legitimacy in your domain. Capture it. Use it to multiply your power.

**Historical example**: Dong Zhuo's capture of Emperor Xian. By holding the Emperor, Dong Zhuo's actions were framed as "the Emperor's commands." Opponents were "rebels." Allies were "loyalists." The symbol multiplied his power more than any army could.

**Mechanism**: The symbol of legitimacy, once captured, transforms the perception of your actions. Actions that would otherwise be seen as aggression are seen as legitimate exercise of authority. Opponents must oppose the symbol itself (which is hard) or accept your framing (which gives you the advantage).

**Application in contracting**:
- The symbol in Egypt's construction sector is the Federation of Contractors classification. Capturing a higher classification multiplies your legitimacy for major projects. The work you can do is the same; the classification changes what you're allowed to bid on.
- A particular certification (e.g., ISO 9001 quality management, or a specific electrical safety certification) is a symbol that multiplies your legitimacy. Without it, clients ask more questions. With it, the certification answers the questions in advance.

**Risks**: The symbol without substance is fragile. Dong Zhuo held the Emperor but could not govern effectively. The symbol bought time; the substance (actual governance) was what he needed and lacked.

### Move 2: Burn the Map

**The pattern**: When the existing map (market structure, competitive landscape, regulatory environment) is unfavorable, destroy it. Force the creation of a new map where you have advantages.

**Historical example**: Dong Zhuo's burning of Luoyang. The capital, the political structure, the existing map — all destroyed. The coalition that opposed him came to liberate the city; there was no city to liberate. The coalition's purpose was destroyed; the coalition followed.

**Mechanism**: The existing map encodes the advantages of the existing players. Destroying the map removes those advantages. The new map (which emerges from the destruction) can be shaped by whoever moves first into the new space.

**Application in contracting**:
- If the existing market structure favors large entrenched contractors, introduce a new model that makes the old structure obsolete. Example: if the market is built around prime contractors and subcontractors, introduce a "concierge contractor" model where you manage the entire project for the client, including the subcontractors. The old model becomes obsolete; the new map is one where you have first-mover advantage.

**Risks**: The destruction is real. Old relationships, old capabilities, old investments may be destroyed along with the map. The new map may not favor you as much as you expected.

### Move 3: The Capital Move

**The pattern**: When your current base is unfavorable, move the capital — relocate to a new base where you have advantages.

**Historical example**: Dong Zhuo moved the capital from Luoyang to Chang'an. Luoyang was exposed to the coalition; Chang'an was defensible. The move was unpopular and disruptive, but strategically it placed Dong Zhuo in a position where his enemies had to come to him on unfavorable terms.

**Mechanism**: The base you operate from determines your vulnerabilities. A base that exposes you is a liability. A base that protects you is an asset. The relocation is costly in the short term but protective in the long term.

**Application in contracting**:
- If your current office location is expensive and exposes you to high overhead, relocate to a lower-cost area where your margins improve. The relocation is disruptive but the lower cost base improves your competitiveness.
- If your current market segment is dominated by aggressive competitors, move to a different segment (different client type, different project size, different geography) where you have advantages.

**Risks**: The move is costly and may not pay off. The new base may have its own unforeseen disadvantages.

---

### Move 4: Co-opt Through Exchange

**The pattern**: Convert opponents or neutrals into supporters by giving them something they want. The exchange must be real (both sides receive value) or it fails.

**Historical example**: Dong Zhuo co-opted scholars by offering them positions in his administration. Some accepted (the exchange was real — they wanted the positions, he wanted their legitimacy). Some refused (the exchange was not enough — they valued their independence more than the position).

**Mechanism**: Co-option works when the exchange is genuinely valuable to both sides. It fails when the exchange is one-sided (one side gives without receiving) or when the exchange is deceptive (one side promises without delivering).

**Application in contracting**:
- Co-opt potential competitors by partnering on projects where your capabilities are complementary. The exchange: you get their credibility in a segment where you're weak; they get your capability in a segment where they're weak.
- Co-opt gatekeepers (engineering consultants, project managers) by being the easiest contractor to work with. The exchange: they get a contractor who makes their job easier; you get recommended for future projects.

**Risks**: Co-option that is one-sided (you give without receiving) becomes exploitation of you. Co-option that is deceptive (you promise without delivering) destroys your reputation.

---

### Move 5: The Hollow Victory

**The pattern**: When facing a likely defeat, shape the situation so that the opponent's victory is hollow. The opponent wins the immediate game but cannot capitalize on the win.

**Historical example**: Dong Zhuo's retreat from the coalition. He retreated, but in retreating, he destroyed the coalition's purpose. The coalition "won" by forcing the retreat, but the victory fractured the coalition. The coalition's inability to capitalize on the victory was Dong Zhuo's strategic success, even in retreat.

**Mechanism**: A victory that cannot be capitalized on is not a victory. The hollow victory frustrates the winner and demoralizes their supporters. The winner's coalition often fractures because the members of the coalition came for the spoils, and there are no spoils.

**Application in contracting**:
- If a competitor underbids you to win a project, the competitor wins the bid but may lose money on the project. Their "victory" is hollow — they have a project they cannot execute profitably. Your "loss" preserves your capital for a better project.

**Risks**: This move depends on the opponent's victory actually being hollow. If the opponent can capitalize (e.g., they win the bid at a loss but make it up on the next project), your strategy fails.

---

### Move 6: Design the Next Game While the Current One Burns

**The pattern**: While the existing game is being destroyed, design the next game. The next game should be one where you have advantages the old game denied you.

**Historical example**: While Luoyang burned, Dong Zhuo was designing the new capital at Chang'an. He was positioning his loyalists in the new administration. He was planning the political structure that would emerge from the chaos. The next game was being designed before the current one ended.

**Mechanism**: The destruction of the old game creates a vacuum. The vacuum will be filled by whoever designs the next game first. The designing must happen during the destruction, not after — because after the destruction, others will also be designing.

**Application in contracting**:
- While a market segment is collapsing (a regulatory change is destroying an old way of working), design the new way of working that will replace it. Be first to market with the new model. The first mover in the new game has advantages that late movers cannot match.

**Risks**: Designing the next game requires predicting what the next game will look like. Predictions can be wrong. If you design a game that doesn't match the emerging reality, your design is wasted.

## How to Recognize When to Apply Each Move

| Move | When to apply | When NOT to apply |
|------|--------------|------------------|
| Capture the Symbol | When the symbol exists and can be captured | When the symbol is purely honorific (no real power) |
| Burn the Map | When the existing map is unfavorable AND you can survive the destruction | When the existing map is favorable |
| Capital Move | When your current base is unfavorable | When your current base is fine |
| Co-opt Through Exchange | When genuine exchange is possible | When the exchange would be one-sided or deceptive |
| Hollow Victory | When facing likely defeat | When victory is possible |
| Design the Next Game | When the current game is being destroyed | When the current game is stable |
