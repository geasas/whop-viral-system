# Anti-Pattern Villains — Thinking Patterns (The Common Negative Thought Patterns Across Villains)

> **Layer**: 07_MINDS/anti_pattern_villains/
> **File**: thinking_patterns
> **Subject**: The common negative thought patterns shared across the anti-pattern characters in Ravages of Time — read as a diagnostic, not a template
> **Character class**: COLLECTIVE ANTI-PATTERN — read alongside the individual anti-pattern minds (yuan_shao/thinking_patterns.md, yuan_shu/thinking_patterns.md, dong_zhuo/thinking_patterns.md, lu_bu/thinking_patterns.md, liu_biao/thinking_patterns.md, liu_zhang/thinking_patterns.md), which provide the specific cognitive patterns of each villain. This file extracts the patterns that recur across multiple villains.

---

## The Premise

This file is the inverse of every other `thinking_patterns.md` in this system. The other files describe how a strong mind moves through information to produce good decisions. This file describes how a failing mind moves through information to produce bad decisions — specifically, the patterns that recur across multiple anti-pattern villains in the manga.

The patterns below are not exotic. They are the natural cognitive habits of a person in a position of power who has not examined how power affects thinking. Every one of these patterns is something you, the reader, have probably done in the last month. The point is not to recognize them as foreign. The point is to recognize them as yours — and to stop.

Read each pattern as a diagnostic question: "Is this how I am thinking about my current situation?" If the answer is yes, the system should warn you, identify which anti-pattern villain exemplifies the pattern most strongly, and route you to the counter-mind named at the end of each pattern.

The patterns are organized in eight clusters. The first four are the patterns of the man who is rising (or has recently risen). The last four are the patterns of the man who is falling (or about to fall). Most anti-pattern villains exhibit both — the rising patterns produce the position that the falling patterns destroy.

## Cluster 1: Patterns of Premature Rising

The first cluster is the cognitive signature of the man who is about to act before his base is ready. These patterns produce the disease of Premature Action (Pillar 1 of the worldview).

### Pattern 1: The Symbol-Substitution Move — "I have the symbol; therefore I have the substance"

The mind reasons: I have the seal (the credential, the certification, the award, the contract, the title). The seal represents the substance. Therefore I have the substance. The reasoning collapses the symbol into the substance. The collapse is invisible to the mind that performs it — the mind believes it is reasoning about reality, when it is reasoning about a representation of reality.

Yuan Shu's reasoning when he declared himself emperor in 197 AD is the purest example. He held the imperial seal. The seal was the symbol of the mandate of heaven. The mandate of heaven was the substance of imperial authority. Yuan Shu reasoned: I have the seal, the seal is the symbol of the mandate, therefore I have the mandate. The reasoning was wrong. The seal was a piece of carved jade. The mandate was a military, political, and economic structure that Yuan Shu had not built. The seal did not give him the structure. The seal gave him the *belief* that he had the structure. The belief stopped him from building the structure. The building was the only path to the substance. Yuan Shu skipped the building. The seal, unsupported by substance, was exposed within two years. He died.

**Diagnostic**: When you receive a credential, an award, a certification, or a major contract, what is your first thought? If your first thought is "I have arrived," you are doing the symbol-substitution move. The correct first thought is "this certifies that I have built enough to receive this; the work of building continues." The credential is a checkpoint, not a destination. The destination is the substance the credential describes — and the substance must be built continuously, including after the credential is received.

**Anti-pattern villain exemplified**: Yuan Shu (the imperial seal), the Yellow Turbans (the religious following as a substitute for the military structure), Liu Biao (the scholar's reputation as a substitute for the strategic capability).

**Counter-mind**: Cao Cao — substance over symbol. Build the capability; let the symbol follow. The symbol that follows is grounded. The symbol that leads is ungrounded.

### Pattern 2: The Moment-Trapping Move — "The moment will not return; I must act now"

The mind reasons: This opportunity is unique. The opportunity will not return. I must act on it now, even if the base is not ready, because the next opportunity will not be this opportunity. The reasoning is partly correct (moments are singular; the same moment will not return). The reasoning is wrong in its conclusion (the singular moment that requires an unprepared action is not an opportunity — it is a trap). The mind that acts on the trap is destroyed by the action. The mind that waits for the base to be ready and acts on the next moment (which will come, because moments are not singular in kind) is the mind that wins.

The Yellow Turbans' reasoning in 184 AD is the founding example. Zhang Jiao had built a religious movement over decades. The movement was ready in the sense of having followers. The movement was not ready in the sense of having coordinated command, secured supply, or strategic plan. The Han government was weak. The moment seemed right. The brothers rose — and were suppressed within a year. The rising, however, gave the regional governors (Dong Zhuo, Sun Jian, Cao Cao, Liu Bei) their first military commands. The rising created the warlords who would destroy the Han. The moment that the Yellow Turbans trapped was the moment that destroyed them and gave the warlords their start.

**Diagnostic**: When you are offered an opportunity that seems singular, ask: is the singularity in the opportunity, or in my desire to act? If the singularity is in the opportunity (the contract will not be offered again, the client will not return, the supplier will not extend the offer), the opportunity may justify a calculated risk. If the singularity is in your desire (you want the breakthrough, you are tired of waiting, you fear the path is closing), the opportunity is a trap — and the trap is set by your impatience, not by the situation. The discipline is to distinguish the two.

**Anti-pattern villain exemplified**: The Yellow Turbans (the 184 AD uprising), Yuan Shu (the 197 AD declaration), the minor warlords who took territories they could not hold.

**Counter-mind**: Sima Yi — patient counterpunching. Wait for the base. Refuse the false opportunity. The next opportunity will come. The base that the next opportunity requires is the base you are building now. The base that is sacrificed to the false opportunity is the base that the next opportunity requires.

### Pattern 3: The Step-Skipping Move — "I will jump to the destination by declaring it"

The mind reasons: The destination is the title (general contractor, developer, market leader). The path to the destination is long. The path can be skipped by declaring the destination. The declaration makes the destination true. The reasoning is wrong. The declaration does not make the destination true. The declaration makes the destination a target. The path was the only way to make the destination true. The declaration that skips the path destroys the path. The path is the substance. The destination is the symbol of the substance. The symbol without the substance is a target.

Yuan Shu's declaration of emperor in 197 AD is the purest example. He had a regional power. The path to empire required consolidation of his territory, securing his flanks, building his army, cultivating his allies. The path would have taken five to ten years. Yuan Shu skipped the path by declaring the empire. The declaration did not give him the empire. The declaration gave his peers a reason to destroy him. The peers who would have tolerated him as a regional power destroyed him as a self-declared emperor — because tolerating a self-declared emperor is itself a form of submission.

**Diagnostic**: When you are tempted to declare a destination (register your business as a general contractor before you have the general contractor's capabilities, lease the larger office on the strength of one major project, claim the market leadership on the strength of one year's revenue), ask: what is the path I am skipping? The path is the substance. The declaration is the symbol. The declaration without the path is the target. The path is the work. The work cannot be skipped.

**Anti-pattern villain exemplified**: Yuan Shu (the imperial declaration), the minor warlords who declared themselves governors without the governor's capacity.

**Counter-mind**: Cao Cao — institutional building. The destination is the institution that the title describes. Build the institution. The title will come when the institution is ready. The title that comes before the institution is the title that destroys the institution's builder.

## Cluster 2: Patterns of Vain Self-Image

The second cluster is the cognitive signature of the man who has had enough success to develop vanity, and who has not examined how vanity affects his decisions. These patterns produce the disease of Vanity Without Substance (Pillar 2 of the worldview).

### Pattern 4: The Prestige Filter — "I will evaluate counsel by the standing of the counselor"

The mind reasons: The counselor's standing (his title, his lineage, his prior successes, his relationship to me) is evidence of the counselor's competence. Counsel from a high-standing counselor is more likely to be correct. Counsel from a low-standing counselor is more likely to be incorrect. The reasoning is wrong. The standing of the counselor is evidence of his past competence, not of his current counsel's correctness. The correctness of the counsel depends on the situation, not on the counselor's standing. The mind that filters by standing systematically discards correct counsel from low-standing counselors and accepts incorrect counsel from high-standing counselors.

Yuan Shao's filter is the canonical example. He evaluated counsel partly by its content and partly by the counselor's standing. Counsel from men of lower standing that contradicted counsel from men of higher standing tended to lose, regardless of merit. Counsel that would require Yuan Shao to publicly acknowledge a mistake tended to lose, regardless of merit. Counsel that flattered Yuan Shao's self-image tended to win, regardless of merit. The filter discarded the counsel of Tian Feng and Ju Shou (low-standing relative to Yuan Shao's vanity, despite their actual competence) and retained the counsel of Guo Tu and Pang Ji (high-standing in Yuan Shao's vanity, despite their actual incompetence). The filter produced the Guandu defeat.

**Diagnostic**: When you receive counsel from someone, what is your first evaluation? If your first evaluation is "who is this person, and what is their standing?", you are doing the prestige filter. The correct first evaluation is "what is the counsel, and is it correct on the merits?" The standing of the counselor is a second-order consideration (relevant to whether the counsel can be implemented, not to whether the counsel is correct). The correctness is the first-order consideration.

**Anti-pattern villain exemplified**: Yuan Shao (the prestige filter on his advisors), Liu Biao (the prestige filter on his subordinates, preferring the scholar-officials to the military men).

**Counter-mind**: Guo Jia — cold reading. Evaluate the counsel on its content, not on its source. The source is relevant to the implementation; the content is relevant to the decision.

### Pattern 5: The Flattery Filter — "I will evaluate counsel by how it makes me feel"

The mind reasons: Counsel that makes me feel wise is more likely correct. Counsel that makes me feel foolish is more likely incorrect. The reasoning is wrong. Counsel that makes you feel wise is counsel that confirms your self-image. Counsel that makes you feel foolish is counsel that contradicts your self-image. The counsel that contradicts your self-image is the counsel that sees what you do not see. The counsel that confirms your self-image is the counsel that protects the counselor's position. The first is the counsel that would save you. The second is the counsel that would destroy you. The mind that filters by feeling accepts the destroying counsel and rejects the saving counsel.

Yuan Shao's flattery filter is the canonical example. Counsel from Guo Tu and Pang Ji (which flattered Yuan Shao's self-image) was accepted. Counsel from Tian Feng and Ju Shou (which contradicted Yuan Shao's self-image) was rejected. The filter was the destruction of his information system. The information system that flatters is the information system that destroys.

**Diagnostic**: When you receive counsel, observe your body. Does the counsel produce a feeling of warmth (it confirms you) or a feeling of resistance (it contradicts you)? The feeling of warmth is the seduction. The feeling of resistance is the signal. The counsel that produces resistance is the counsel that is doing the work you need. The counsel that produces warmth is the counsel that is doing the work of protecting the counselor's position. The discipline is to suspect, in yourself, the tendency to accept the warm counsel and reject the resisting counsel.

**Anti-pattern villain exemplified**: Yuan Shao (the flattery filter), Dong Zhuo (in his late years, surrounded by flatterers who could not warn him of the assassination plot), Liu Biao (the flattery of the scholar-officials over the hard counsel of the military men).

**Counter-mind**: Liu Bei — relational trust. Build the relationship where hard counsel is welcome. The relationship takes years to build. The relationship is the most valuable asset a leader can have.

### Pattern 6: The Image-Optimization Move — "The decision must look right, not be right"

The mind reasons: The appearance of the decision is part of the decision's effect. The appearance affects the morale of allies, the perception of enemies, the reputation of the leader. Therefore the appearance must be optimized. The reasoning has some truth (appearance matters). The reasoning fails when appearance is prioritized over substance. The substance is the decision's actual effect. The appearance is the decision's perceived effect. The two can diverge. When they diverge, the leader who optimizes appearance loses both — the appearance collapses when the substance is exposed, and the substance was never built.

Yuan Shao's image-optimization at Guandu is the canonical example. When Cao Cao raided his supply depot at Wuchao (200 AD), Yuan Shao refused counsel to send reinforcements to Wuchao and instead attacked Cao Cao's main camp. The reasoning was that attacking the main camp would be a more dignified response than reinforcing a supply depot. The result was that Wuchao fell (Cao Cao burned the supplies), the attack on the main camp failed, and Yuan Shao's army collapsed. The dignified decision was the losing decision.

**Diagnostic**: When you imagine making a decision, do you first imagine how it will look to others? Do you choose the version of the decision that photographs better — the one that produces a better story, a better impression, a better press release? If yes, you are doing the image-optimization move. The discipline is to ask: if no one were watching, what decision would I make? The answer is the substance-optimized decision. The substance-optimized decision is the one to make. The appearance will follow the substance if the substance is real. The appearance that leads the substance is the appearance that collapses when the substance is exposed.

**Anti-pattern villain exemplified**: Yuan Shao (the image-optimization at Guandu), Yuan Shu (the imperial display at Shouchun), Liu Biao (the literary gatherings as a substitute for strategic action).

**Counter-mind**: Sima Yi — concealment over display. Choose the move that works, not the move that looks. The appearance of weakness is temporary. The strength of substance compounds.

## Cluster 3: Patterns of Indecision

The third cluster is the cognitive signature of the man who has a position to defend and cannot decide how to defend it. These patterns produce the disease of Indecision in Crisis (Pillar 3 of the worldview).

### Pattern 7: The Council-Paralysis Move — "I will decide once my advisors agree"

The mind reasons: Hearing many views prevents bias. The reasoning is correct in principle. The reasoning fails in practice because the consensus requirement gives every advisor a veto. The veto is used to protect the advisor's position, not to protect the strategy. The strategy that emerges from the consensus is the strategy that offended the fewest factions — which is rarely the strategy that would have worked. The strategy that would have worked usually offended some faction, because good strategies are partial (they allocate scarce resources, which means they choose some interests over others).

Yuan Shao's council paralysis is the canonical example. He convened councils of his advisors before every major decision. The councils argued. The arguments were factional as often as strategic. Yuan Shao listened to all sides and required consensus before acting. The consensus never came. The decisions were made late, or not at all, or were made by circumstances rather than by Yuan Shao.

He Jin's council paralysis is the parallel case. He had the legal authority and the military force to remove the eunuch faction. He convened councils. The councils argued. Yuan Shao argued for immediate action. The Empress Dowager (his sister) argued for delay. Other advisors argued for caution. He Jin delayed. The eunuchs, knowing their destruction was planned, assassinated him. The assassination triggered the massacre of the eunuchs and the entry of Dong Zhuo. The council paralysis produced the warlord era.

**Diagnostic**: Are you requiring your team, your family, your partners to all agree before you can make a decision? Are you treating disagreement as a signal to wait rather than as information to weigh? If yes, you are doing the council-paralysis move. The discipline is to hear the disagreement, weigh it, decide, and absorb the displeasure of the ones who disagreed. The decision is yours, not the council's. The council's job is to inform your decision, not to make it.

**Anti-pattern villain exemplified**: Yuan Shao (the councils before Guandu), He Jin (the councils before his assassination), Liu Zhang (the councils during Liu Bei's invasion).

**Counter-mind**: Cao Cao — decisive synthesis. Hear the views. Integrate. Decide alone. The decision is faster than the council, and the action is faster than the decision.

### Pattern 8: The Option-Preservation Move — "I will keep my options open"

The mind reasons: No option foreclosed means no option lost. The reasoning is correct in a static world. The reasoning fails in a dynamic world. While the options are preserved, the situation changes. The option that was good today is bad in a month. The option that was bad today is worse in a month. The mind that preserves its options is the mind that eventually has only one option left — the worst one.

Yuan Shao's option-preservation is the canonical example. He avoided committing to any specific plan until forced. He avoided committing to attack Cao Cao, to a single heir, to a single faction at court, to a single strategy for the coalition. The instinct felt like flexibility. The instinct was paralysis. Each month he preserved his options, the options decayed. By the time he acted, all the options were closed. He acted into the worst option — the prepared defense at Guandu.

Liu Zhang's option-preservation is the parallel case. He could not decide whether to trust Liu Bei, to resist Liu Bei, or to negotiate with Liu Bei. He kept all three options open. The options decayed. Liu Bei consolidated inside Yi Province. By the time Liu Zhang decided to resist, the resistance was too late.

**Diagnostic**: Are you describing your refusal to commit as "keeping options open"? Are you waiting for more information that will not actually change the decision (because the decision is about values, not facts)? If yes, you are doing the option-preservation move. The discipline is to ask: what is the cost of waiting, and what is the benefit? If the cost of waiting exceeds the benefit of the additional information, the wait is the wrong move. Decide with the information you have, on the schedule the situation demands.

**Anti-pattern villain exemplified**: Yuan Shao (option-preservation on the Guandu campaign, on the succession), Liu Zhang (option-preservation on Liu Bei), Liu Biao (option-preservation on the succession and on Cao Cao).

**Counter-mind**: Guo Jia — rapid exploitation. Commit with the information you have. Speed beats perfect information. The commitment is more valuable than the perfection.

### Pattern 9: The Avoidance-of-Displeasing-Decision Move — "I will find a way to make the decision that pleases everyone"

The mind reasons: A decision that displeases no one is better than a decision that displeases someone. The reasoning is correct in the moment (less conflict) and wrong in the long run (the decision is eventually made by circumstances, and circumstances do not optimize for the dynasty's survival). The mind prefers the comfort of no decision to the discomfort of a necessary one. The discomfort is delayed. The cost is paid later, at interest.

Yuan Shao's avoidance of the succession decision is the canonical example. He never named an heir because naming would displease the sons not chosen and their mothers and their factions. He avoided the decision until death made it for him. The avoidance destroyed his dynasty.

Liu Biao's avoidance of the succession decision is the parallel case. He favored his younger son Liu Cong (by his second wife, the Cai family) but did not formally designate him. The avoidance created the faction that, after Liu Biao's death, surrendered Jing Province to Cao Cao without a fight.

**Diagnostic**: Are you delaying a decision (succession in your business, a key hire, a key fire, a strategic pivot) because the decision will displease someone you care about? If yes, you are doing the avoidance-of-displeasing-decision move. The discipline is to make the necessary decision now. The displeasure is the cost of leadership. The cost of not leading is much higher.

**Anti-pattern villain exemplified**: Yuan Shao (the succession), Liu Biao (the succession), Liu Zhang (the subordination decisions during Liu Bei's invasion).

**Counter-mind**: Cao Cao — make the necessary decision now. Absorb the displeasure as the price of leadership. The displeasure is finite. The cost of avoidance compounds.

## Cluster 4: Patterns of Betrayal

The fourth cluster is the cognitive signature of the man who treats alliances as instrumental and who switches them whenever the switch serves him. These patterns produce the disease of Betrayal as Pattern (Pillar 4 of the worldview).

### Pattern 10: The Instrumental-Alliance Move — "This alliance serves me; I will switch when it stops serving me"

The mind reasons: Alliance is a means to an end. The end is the self. When the alliance stops serving the self, the alliance should be switched. The reasoning has some validity (alliances are instrumental). The reasoning is wrong when it is total (the man who treats every alliance as instrumental is the man who is treated instrumentally by every potential ally). The pattern produces a reputation. The reputation destroys the man's ability to form the alliances he will need in the next crisis.

Lu Bu's pattern is the canonical example. He betrayed Ding Yuan, Dong Zhuo, Yuan Shao, and Liu Bei. Each betrayal produced a short-term gain (the new patron's reward). Each betrayal produced a long-term cost (the reputation that no patron could trust him). The reputation culminated in Cao Cao's decision to execute him at Xiapi in 199 AD — despite Cao Cao's interest in employing him — because Liu Bei reminded Cao Cao of Lu Bu's pattern of betraying his patrons. The pattern that had produced the short-term gains produced the long-term result that no one trusted the man, and the man no one trusted was executed.

**Diagnostic**: When you are in an alliance (with a subcontractor, a supplier, a client, a partner), what is your evaluation of the alliance? If your evaluation is purely instrumental (does this alliance serve me, today?), you are doing the instrumental-alliance move. The discipline is to evaluate the alliance on two axes: the instrumental axis (does this alliance serve me, today?) and the relational axis (does this alliance build the network that will serve me, in the next crisis?). The instrumental axis optimizes the present. The relational axis optimizes the future. The man who optimizes only the instrumental axis is the man whose network narrows year by year, until the network is too narrow to support him in the next crisis.

**Anti-pattern villain exemplified**: Lu Bu (the canonical case), the minor warlords who switched sides during the civil wars.

**Counter-mind**: Liu Bei — loyalty as the foundation. The alliance is the asset, not the cost to be optimized. The alliance that lasts decades produces more than the alliance that lasts months.

### Pattern 11: The Short-Horizon Reward Move — "I will take the reward now and deal with the reputation later"

The mind reasons: The reward of the switch (the new patron's money, the new client's contract, the new supplier's terms) is concrete and immediate. The cost of the switch (the damaged reputation, the narrowed network) is abstract and delayed. The concrete and immediate outweighs the abstract and delayed. The reasoning is wrong. The concrete and immediate is small. The abstract and delayed is large. The small reward is consumed quickly. The large cost compounds over years.

Lu Bu's pattern is the canonical example. Each switch produced an immediate reward (Dong Zhuo's gifts when Lu Bu switched from Ding Yuan; Wang Yun's favor when Lu Bu switched from Dong Zhuo; Yuan Shao's tolerance when Lu Bu arrived after the Chang'an defeat). Each switch produced a delayed cost (the reputation that narrowed the field of potential patrons). The delayed cost was abstract and hard to see in the moment. The delayed cost was real and was eventually paid in the execution at Xiapi.

**Diagnostic**: When you are offered a switch (a slightly cheaper subcontractor, a slightly faster supplier, a slightly larger client), do you calculate only the immediate gain? If yes, you are doing the short-horizon reward move. The discipline is to calculate the long-horizon cost. The long-horizon cost is the damage to the relationship you are leaving. The damage is not zero. The damage is the cost of the relationship you would have built if you had stayed — a cost that compounds over years. The switch that saves 5% today may cost 50% over the next decade, as the network that would have supported you narrows.

**Anti-pattern villain exemplified**: Lu Bu (the canonical case), Li Jue and Guo Si (the switch from alliance to civil war after Dong Zhuo's death).

**Counter-mind**: Sima Yi — patient alliance. The alliance that lasts decades produces more than the alliance that lasts months. The discipline is to evaluate the alliance on the long horizon, not the short.

## Cluster 5: Patterns of Post-Tyrant Civil War

The fifth cluster is the cognitive signature of the man who has removed (or is about to remove) a tyrant and who has not prepared the successor order. These patterns produce the disease of Civil War After Tyranny (Pillar 5 of the worldview).

### Pattern 12: The Liberation-Without-Structure Move — "The removal of the tyrant is the liberation; the structure will sort itself out"

The mind reasons: The tyrant was the problem. The removal of the tyrant is the solution. The solution will produce the order the tyrant prevented. The reasoning is wrong. The tyrant, however tyrannically, was holding a structure together. The structure was the tyrant's structure (built for the tyrant's benefit, not for the territory's benefit). The removal of the tyrant removes the structure. The structure is the only order that existed. The removal produces a vacuum. The vacuum is filled by the tyrant's subordinates, who fight each other. The fight destroys the territory. The liberation that felt like the start of a better order was the start of a worse chaos.

Wang Yun's refusal to amnesty Li Jue, Guo Si, Fan Chou, and Zhang Ji after Dong Zhuo's assassination is the canonical example. Wang Yun reasoned that the four subordinates were criminals who should be punished, not amnestied. The reasoning was moral. The reasoning was strategically wrong. The four subordinates, facing punishment, gathered the remnants of Dong Zhuo's army and marched on Chang'an. They captured the city, captured the Emperor, and proceeded to fight each other. Their civil war destroyed the Guanzhong region. The liberation that Wang Yun had engineered produced the civil war that ended the Han's last claim to central authority.

**Diagnostic**: When you are contemplating the removal of a partner, a senior employee, a difficult client, or a structural element of your business, do you have a documented successor structure ready? If no, you are doing the liberation-without-structure move. The removal will produce a vacuum. The vacuum will be filled by the partner's clients, subordinates, and suppliers — each of whom has an interest in the post-removal order and no mechanism for resolving their competing interests. The fight will destroy the business. The removal without the preparation is the start of the civil war. The removal with the preparation is the start of the next phase.

**Anti-pattern villain exemplified**: Wang Yun (the canonical case), Yuan Shao's sons (the civil war after Yuan Shao's death), Liu Biao's succession (the surrender after Liu Biao's death).

**Counter-mind**: Cao Cao — institutional building. The structure that survives the leader is the leader's true legacy. The structure must be built before the removal. The removal without the structure is the civil war.

### Pattern 13: The Successor-Avoidance Move — "I will name the successor later"

The mind reasons: The succession decision can be deferred because the leader will be alive to make it later. The reasoning fails because the leader does not always have the chance to make it later — illness, accident, and death do not announce themselves. The deferral also has a cost in the present: the ambiguity creates factions among the potential heirs and their supporters. The factions become entrenched. When the leader finally decides (or dies), the factions fight. The fight destroys what the leader built.

Yuan Shao's failure to name Yuan Tan or Yuan Shang is the canonical example. The ambiguity created two factions. The factions spent years preparing for the conflict they knew was coming. When Yuan Shao died in 202 AD, the factions fought. The fight destroyed the Yuan empire.

Liu Biao's failure to name Liu Qi or Liu Cong is the parallel case. The ambiguity created the Cai family faction that, after Liu Biao's death, surrendered Jing Province to Cao Cao without a fight.

**Diagnostic**: Have you named a successor in your business? Have you documented your processes so someone else could run the business? Have you given your key employees the authority they would need to run things if you were not there? If no, you are doing the successor-avoidance move. The discipline is to name the successor, document the processes, and delegate the authority — now, while you are well, while the decision displeases fewer people than it will later.

**Anti-pattern villain exemplified**: Yuan Shao (the succession), Liu Biao (the succession), Liu Zhang (the implicit succession that Liu Bei exploited).

**Counter-mind**: Cao Cao — early succession planning. Name the heir. Document the playbook. Delegate the authority. The early decision is harder and the late decision is catastrophic.

## The Cognitive Style of the Anti-Pattern Villains

The cognitive style of the anti-pattern villains, across all five diseases and all thirteen patterns, is **unexamined default**. The villains do not choose their patterns deliberately. The villains fall into their patterns because the patterns are the default cognitive habits of a person in their position. The man who has had some success defaults to the symbol-substitution move. The man who has a position to defend defaults to the council-paralysis move. The man who has allies defaults to the instrumental-alliance move. The man who has a partner to remove defaults to the liberation-without-structure move.

The default is the disease. The discipline of self-examination is the cure. The discipline of self-examination is the discipline of NOT defaulting — of asking, in each decision, "am I doing the default thing, or am I doing the right thing?" The asking is the work. The work is the price of not becoming one of the men in this catalog.

This is distinct from the cognitive styles of the strategic minds:
- **Sima Yi's style**: patient counterpunching — delay as chosen preparation, not as avoidance
- **Cao Cao's style**: active synthesis — engagement with the situation, not avoidance
- **Guo Jia's style**: rapid exploitation — commitment with available information, not avoidance
- **Liu Bei's style**: relational accumulation — engagement through relationships, not avoidance
- **Zhuge Liang's style**: vision-driven synthesis — preparation in service of the vision, not avoidance
- **Jia Xu's style**: cynical realism — calculation of survival, not avoidance
- **Sun Quan's style**: defensive consolidation — holding what is held, not avoidance

The anti-pattern villains' cognitive style is the only style in the system that is *unexamined default*. The strategic minds have examined their defaults and chosen differently. The anti-pattern villains have not examined their defaults. The examination is the work. The work is the discipline.

## The Anti-Patterns (When the Anti-Pattern Patterns Are Partially Right)

In the interest of fairness: not every anti-pattern pattern is wrong in every situation. Some patterns have legitimate versions that the system should recognize:

- **Premature action has a legitimate version**: when the base is genuinely ready and the moment is genuinely singular, acting now is correct. The error is acting when the base is not ready, not acting when it is.
- **Vanity has a legitimate version**: reputation is a strategic asset. The error is substituting reputation for substance, not having reputation.
- **Indecision has a legitimate version**: when the situation is genuinely unclear and the cost of waiting is low, waiting is correct. The error is waiting when the cost of waiting exceeds the benefit of additional information.
- **Betrayal has a legitimate version**: when the alliance has been betrayed by the other side, switching is self-defense, not betrayal. The error is switching when the alliance is sound, not switching when it has been compromised.
- **Civil war after tyranny has a legitimate version**: when the tyrant is genuinely destroying the territory, removal is correct. The error is removing without preparing the successor structure, not removing the tyrant.

The system must distinguish the legitimate version from the anti-pattern version. The distinction is the time horizon and the substitution. The legitimate version is time-bounded and complementary to the substance. The anti-pattern version is permanent and substitute for the substance. When the user's pattern is time-bounded and substantive, the system should not invoke the anti-pattern. When the user's pattern is permanent and substitutive, the system should.
