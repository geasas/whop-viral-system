# Wang Yun — When to Activate

> **Layer**: 07_MINDS/wang_yun/
> **File**: when_to_activate
> **Subject**: The explicit routing rules for when to invoke the Wang Yun mind
> **Mention frequency in manga**: 72 times across 635 chapters

---

## The Premise

Wang Yun's mind is not universal. It applies to specific situations and fails in others. This file defines the routing rules so the system knows when to invoke this mind and when to switch to another.

The rules are **triggered by situation type**, not by topic. The same topic (a difficult negotiation, a competitive bid, a partnership decision) may invoke Wang Yun or any other mind depending on the strategic situation — specifically, on whether the target can be reached by other means, whether the moral cost is justified, and whether the day-after plan is ready.

Wang Yun is the most powerful mind in the system for breaking an opponent through personal leverage, and the most dangerous mind to the strategist who uses it. The activation rules are deliberately conservative — Wang Yun should be invoked only when other minds cannot reach the target, when the goal justifies the moral cost, and when the consolidation plan is ready. In all other situations, other minds should be tried first.

---

## Trigger Conditions — When to ACTIVATE Wang Yun

### Trigger 1: The target cannot be reached by other strategic minds

The first and most fundamental trigger: Wang Yun's mind is invoked only when other minds have been tried, or when it is clear that other minds cannot reach the target. The personal-leverage trap is the last-resort weapon, not the first.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "I have tried to win this project on price, quality, and relationship, and I have lost. The competitor has the inside track. The decision-maker is committed to the competitor for reasons I cannot override commercially."
- "I have tried to negotiate this contract through honest persuasion, and the supplier will not move. They are committed to their pricing for reasons I cannot override commercially."
- "I have tried to recruit this foreman through honest offers, and they will not move. They are committed to their current employer for personal reasons I cannot override commercially."

**Example**: A contractor has been trying to win a major project from a developer for two years. The developer keeps giving the projects to a competitor. The contractor has tried better pricing, better quality references, better timelines. None have worked. The developer's chief engineer, who makes the recommendations, is a personal friend of the competitor's owner. The contractor cannot win on commercial merit; the personal bond between the engineer and the competitor is too strong. → Wang Yun's mind: the personal bond is the lever; identify the engineer's other personal bonds (the engineer's spouse, the engineer's children's school, the engineer's religious community) and identify whether any of them can be placed as a human asset to create a conflict between the engineer and the competitor's owner.

**Caution**: This trigger should be checked carefully. Often the contractor believes other minds have failed when in fact they have not been fully applied. Before invoking Wang Yun, the system should ask: has Sima Yi been tried (multi-year patience)? Has Cao Cao been tried (institutional building — becoming the developer's preferred contractor through 5 small projects before bidding on the major one)? Has Guo Jia been tried (rapid strike on a different project that changes the developer's view of you)? Has Liu Bei been tried (genuine bond-building with the engineer, not as a trap but as a relationship)?

Wang Yun is invoked only when all four have been genuinely tried, or when the situation is so urgent that there is no time for them.

### Trigger 2: A personal bond in the target's camp has been identified and can be turned

The second trigger: the strategist has identified a specific personal bond in the target's camp that can be turned. The identification of the bond is the precondition for the personal-leverage trap. Without the bond, there is no trap.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "I have learned that the competitor's lead foreman is unhappy with the competitor's recent decisions. The foreman's loyalty is conditional — he joined for the salary, not for the relationship."
- "I have learned that the supplier's key account manager is being pressured by the supplier's owner to give preferential terms to a competitor. The account manager is uncomfortable with the pressure."
- "I have learned that the client's procurement director is in conflict with the client's project director. The two have a personal rivalry that goes back years."

**Example**: A contractor has learned that a competitor's lead foreman has been complaining to colleagues about the competitor's recent decision to delay a bonus payment. The foreman's loyalty to the competitor is conditional — it depends on the competitor honoring the bonus. → Wang Yun's mind: the personal bond (the foreman's loyalty based on the bonus expectation) is the lever. The bonus delay has already converted the bond from loyalty to grievance. The trap-building can begin — build a relationship with the foreman, identify what the foreman wants (the bonus, recognition, a career path), and prepare the kill strike (a job offer that includes the bonus and the recognition).

**Caution**: The bond must be genuine. The strategist cannot invent a bond; they can only identify and exploit one that exists. If the strategist believes there is a bond where there is not (e.g., the foreman is actually loyal despite the bonus delay), the trap will fail. Before invoking Wang Yun, verify the bond through multiple sources.

### Trigger 3: The goal is genuinely worth the moral cost

The third trigger: the goal is sufficiently important to justify the moral cost of the personal-leverage trap. Not every commercial goal is worth the moral cost. The activation requires the strategist to assess, honestly, whether the goal justifies the cost.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "The goal is the survival of the firm. If I do not win this project, the firm will fail within 6 months. The personal-leverage trap will save the firm. The moral cost is justified by the survival of the firm, the employees, the family."
- "The goal is the prevention of a greater harm. The competitor is about to win a project that will allow them to dominate the market and abuse their position. The personal-leverage trap will prevent the domination. The moral cost is justified by the prevention of the greater harm."
- "The goal is the correction of a serious injustice. A supplier has been overcharging a client for years, exploiting the client's trust. The personal-leverage trap will end the overcharging. The moral cost is justified by the correction of the injustice."

**Example**: A contractor has been the loyal subcontractor to a developer for ten years. The developer has been gradually squeezing the contractor's margins, demanding more work for less money. The contractor has tried to negotiate honestly, and the developer has refused. The contractor is now losing money on every project and is facing bankruptcy. The contractor has learned that the developer's chief engineer is unhappy with the developer's recent decisions and is considering leaving. → Wang Yun's mind: the personal bond (the engineer's grievance) is the lever. The goal (the survival of the firm) justifies the moral cost (using the engineer's grievance as a lever to renegotiate the developer's terms, or to bring the engineer to your firm and force the developer to negotiate without their key technical person).

**Caution**: The "goal justifies the moral cost" test must be honest. The contractor must not deceive themselves. If the goal is merely commercial advantage (winning a project at the competitor's expense), the moral cost is not justified. If the goal is survival, justice, or the prevention of greater harm, the moral cost may be justified. The test is: would I be willing to explain this move to my father, my wife, my imam, my priest? If yes, the move may be justified. If no, the move is not justified.

### Trigger 4: The day-after plan is ready

The fourth trigger: the consolidation plan is ready. The personal-leverage trap wins the immediate battle; the day-after plan must hold the victory. Without the day-after plan, the win becomes a loss.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "I have identified the personal bond. I have designed the trap. I have prepared the kill strike. AND I have a plan for the day after — who will deliver the project, how I will manage the relationship with the displaced competitor, how I will legitimize the win to the market, how I will prevent the same trap from being set against me."
- "I have the operational capacity to deliver what I am winning. The personal-leverage move is matched by an institutional capacity to execute."

**Example**: A contractor has identified a personal bond (the competitor's foreman's grievance over a delayed bonus), has designed the trap (a job offer to the foreman), and has prepared the kill strike (the offer is ready, the role is defined, the salary is set). The contractor has also planned the day after: the foreman's role in the contractor's firm (a specific project, a specific team, a specific career path), the management of the relationship with the competitor (a quiet exit for the foreman, no public criticism of the competitor, no disparagement in the market), the legitimization of the win to the market (the foreman's recruitment is announced as a normal hire, not as a poach), and the defensive measures (a stronger retention plan for the contractor's own foremen, to prevent the same trap from being set against them).

→ Wang Yun's mind: the day-after plan is ready. The trap can be set.

**Caution**: If the day-after plan is not ready, do not set the trap. The trap without the day-after plan is the Wang Yun pattern that ends in destruction. The contractor who sets the trap and has no day-after plan will win the immediate battle and lose the larger war.

### Trigger 5: Defensive recognition — someone else's personal-leverage trap is being set against you

The fifth trigger is defensive, not offensive. Wang Yun's mind is invoked not only to set personal-leverage traps, but also to recognize and counter them when they are being set against the contractor.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "A competitor has been building a relationship with my lead foreman. I have noticed the relationship but I am not sure what it means. Is the competitor preparing a personal-leverage trap?"
- "A supplier has been giving my competitor better terms than me. The supplier's account manager has been friendly with my competitor's owner. Is the supplier being positioned as a human asset in a personal-leverage trap?"
- "A client's procurement director has been unusually critical of my recent work. The criticism does not match the actual quality. Is the procurement director being positioned to remove me from the client's preferred list?"

**Example**: A contractor has noticed that a competitor's owner has been attending the same industry events as the contractor's lead foreman. The competitor's owner has been friendly, asking about the foreman's career, family, and ambitions. The foreman has mentioned the conversations to the contractor, but not in a worried way. → Wang Yun's mind: the competitor may be setting a personal-leverage trap. The recognition triggers defensive measures: (1) check the foreman's current satisfaction with the firm — are there grievances that could be exploited? (2) strengthen the foreman's retention — career path, compensation, recognition; (3) monitor the competitor's further approaches; (4) consider a pre-emptive conversation with the foreman in which the contractor offers the foreman what the competitor is likely to offer.

**Caution**: The defensive invocation of Wang Yun does not require the goal-justifies-the-cost test. The contractor is defending, not attacking. The moral cost of defense is lower than the moral cost of offense. The day-after plan is also simpler — the contractor is preserving the status quo, not changing it.

## Inhibition Conditions — When NOT to Activate Wang Yun

### Inhibitor 1: Other minds can reach the target

The first and most important inhibitor: if other minds (Sima Yi, Cao Cao, Guo Jia, Liu Bei) can reach the target, Wang Yun should not be invoked. Wang Yun is the last-resort weapon; it should not be the first.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I have not yet tried Sima Yi's patience (waiting 12-24 months for the situation to develop)."
- "I have not yet tried Cao Cao's institutional building (becoming the obvious choice through 5 small projects before bidding on the major one)."
- "I have not yet tried Guo Jia's rapid strike (a different project that changes the developer's view of me)."
- "I have not yet tried Liu Bei's genuine bond-building (a real relationship with the engineer, not as a trap but as a friendship)."

**Exception**: If the situation is so urgent that there is no time for the other minds (the bid is due in 30 days, the foreman is being courted actively, the supplier is about to sign an exclusive with the competitor), Wang Yun may be invoked even if other minds could in principle reach the target. The urgency overrides the preference for the other minds.

### Inhibitor 2: The goal does not justify the moral cost

The second inhibitor: the goal is not important enough to justify the moral cost. Not every commercial advantage is worth the soul-corrosion of personal leverage.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "The goal is to win a single project, but I have other projects. The win is not necessary for survival."
- "The goal is to damage a competitor, but the competitor is not threatening me. The damage is for ego, not for necessity."
- "The goal is to convert a foreman, but I have other foremen. The conversion is for vanity, not for capacity."

**Exception**: The "goal justifies the moral cost" test is the strategist's own judgment, not a mechanical rule. The contractor must be honest with themselves. The system can ask the question; only the contractor can answer it.

### Inhibitor 3: The day-after plan is not ready

The third inhibitor: the consolidation plan has not been prepared. The personal-leverage trap without the day-after plan is the Wang Yun pattern that ends in destruction.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I have not yet identified who will deliver the project I am trying to win."
- "I have not yet identified how I will manage the relationship with the competitor after the trap is set."
- "I have not yet identified how I will legitimize the win to the market."
- "I have not yet identified how I will prevent the same trap from being set against me."

**Exception**: If the trap is defensive (Trigger 5), the day-after plan is simpler — the contractor is preserving the status quo, not changing it. The inhibitor is weaker for defensive invocations.

### Inhibitor 4: The strategist has used personal leverage recently

The fourth inhibitor: the strategist has used personal leverage recently, and the cumulative moral cost is high. The inhibition prevents the personal-leverage mind from becoming the contractor's only mind.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I used personal leverage three months ago to win the last project. The market is still adjusting. Another personal-leverage move now would consolidate my reputation as a manipulator."
- "I used personal leverage six months ago to recruit the last foreman. The team is still adjusting. Another personal-leverage move now would consolidate my reputation as a poacher."
- "I have used personal leverage four times in the past year. The cumulative effect is visible. The next move should be a different mind."

**Exception**: If the goal is genuinely survival (the firm will fail without the move), the recent-use inhibitor is overridden. Survival justifies the cumulative moral cost.

### Inhibitor 5: The situation is ethical and demands honest action

The fifth inhibitor: the situation is fundamentally ethical — the target is being honest, the relationship is genuine, the issue is a real disagreement that should be resolved through honest persuasion, not through manipulation. The personal-leverage trap in an ethical situation corrupts both the strategist and the relationship.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "The client is being honest with me about their budget constraints. The constraint is real, not a negotiating tactic. Manipulating the relationship to extract a higher price would be dishonest."
- "The supplier is being honest with me about their cost pressures. The pressure is real, not a tactic. Manipulating the relationship to extract better terms would be dishonest."
- "The foreman is being honest with me about their career concerns. The concerns are real, not a negotiating position. Manipulating the relationship to extract more work for less pay would be dishonest."

**Exception**: When the other party is being honest and the relationship is genuine, the personal-leverage trap is unethical and should not be invoked. The contractor should respond with Liu Bei's mind — honest bond-building, mutual benefit, long-term trust. The personal-leverage trap is for situations where the other party is not being honest, or where the relationship is not genuine. Using it on honest parties is corruption.

## The Combination Cases

Some situations warrant Wang Yun combined with another mind:

### Wang Yun + Sima Yi: Personal leverage, then long-game consolidation

The most important combination. Use Wang Yun to win the immediate battle that other minds cannot reach; use Sima Yi to consolidate the victory over the multi-year horizon. The two minds are complementary — Wang Yun for the strike, Sima Yi for the holding.

**Application**: Use Wang Yun to win the major project that has been blocked for years by a personal bond in the competitor's camp. Then switch to Sima Yi for the multi-year delivery of the project, the deepening of the client relationship, and the prevention of the same trap being set against you.

### Wang Yun + Liu Bei: Personal leverage, then genuine bond

Use Wang Yun to break the competitor's hold on the client; use Liu Bei to build a genuine bond with the client that will outlast the personal-leverage move. The combination is delicate — the personal-leverage move must be followed by genuine bond-building, or the client will see through the relationship and trust will not be built.

**Application**: Use Wang Yun to win the major project from the competitor. Then switch to Liu Bei for the genuine bond with the client — over-deliver on the project, communicate honestly, build the trust that will bring the next project without needing another personal-leverage move. The combination is the difference between a one-time win and a long-term relationship.

### Wang Yun + Cao Cao: Personal leverage, then institutional building

Use Wang Yun to break the competitor's hold; use Cao Cao to build the institutional capacity that will hold what Wang Yun won. The combination is structural — the personal-leverage move wins the moment; the institutional building holds the years.

**Application**: Use Wang Yun to win the major project. Then switch to Cao Cao for the operational systems that will deliver the project well, the financial systems that will manage the cash flow, the management systems that will coordinate the team, and the institutional capacity that will make the next project easier to win on commercial merit alone.

### Wang Yun + Guo Jia: Personal leverage with speed

Use Wang Yun's personal-leverage trap with Guo Jia's speed. The combination is for situations where the personal-leverage move must be executed quickly — the window is closing, the trigger is live, the kill strike must be performed now. Wang Yun designs the trap; Guo Jia executes it at speed.

**Application**: A competitor's key foreman has just been fired (not resigned — fired). The foreman is on the market now, available for recruitment. The window for recruiting the foreman is short — within 30 days, the foreman will have signed with another competitor. → Combine Wang Yun (the foreman's grievance against the competitor is the lever; the personal bond has already broken) with Guo Jia (the kill strike — the job offer — must be executed within the 30-day window).

## The Activation Test

Before invoking Wang Yun, the system asks:

1. **Has the target been reached by other minds?** If yes (Sima Yi's patience, Cao Cao's institutional building, Guo Jia's rapid strike, Liu Bei's bond-building have all been tried or considered), Wang Yun may be invoked. If no, the other minds should be tried first.
2. **Is there a personal bond in the target's camp that can be turned?** If yes, Wang Yun may be invoked. If no, there is no trap to set; Wang Yun is not applicable.
3. **Does the goal justify the moral cost?** If yes (survival, justice, prevention of greater harm), Wang Yun may be invoked. If no (mere commercial advantage, ego, vanity), Wang Yun should not be invoked.
4. **Is the day-after plan ready?** If yes (operational capacity, relationship management, legitimization, defensive measures), Wang Yun may be invoked. If no, the trap should not be set; the day-after plan must be prepared first.
5. **Has personal leverage been used recently?** If yes (within the past 6 months), Wang Yun should not be invoked unless the goal is survival. The cumulative moral cost is too high.
6. **Is the situation ethical (the other party is being honest)?** If yes, Wang Yun should not be invoked; the situation calls for honest persuasion (Liu Bei), not manipulation.

If all six tests pass (or the failed tests are appropriately overridden by survival urgency), Wang Yun is invoked. If any test fails, another mind is invoked. The user can override by explicitly asking for Wang Yun's perspective — but the system should warn the user of the failed test and the expected cost.

## The Self-Correction

Wang Yun's mind is prone to over-use. The personal-leverage move is powerful, dramatic, and effective in the short term. The strategist who has used it once is tempted to use it again. The system must self-correct.

The self-correction rules:

- **The system flags each personal-leverage move in a log.** Each move is recorded, with the date, the target, the lever used, and the goal. The log is reviewed quarterly.
- **The system warns the user when the log shows more than 2 personal-leverage moves in 12 months.** The warning is: "You have used Wang Yun's mind twice in the past year. The cumulative moral cost is real. Consider whether the next move could be made by another mind."
- **The system warns the user when a personal-leverage move has been made without a day-after plan.** The warning is: "You won the immediate move but the consolidation is not visible. Three months have passed; the win has not been consolidated. Switch to Sima Yi or Cao Cao for the consolidation, or accept that the win was a one-time gain."
- **The system warns the user when a personal-leverage move has triggered backlash.** The warning is: "The personal-leverage move against [target] has generated backlash. The target's former allies are acting. Defensive measures are required. Invoke Sima Yi for the multi-year defensive planning."

The self-correction is built into the activation rule: personal leverage is not a default; it is a last resort that should be invoked rarely, consolidated quickly, and audited honestly.

## The Periodic Audit

Once a quarter, the contractor should conduct a personal-leverage audit:

1. **List the personal-leverage moves made in the past quarter.** For each: the target, the lever, the goal, the outcome, the moral cost.
2. **Assess each move.** Was the goal justified? Was the moral cost paid worth the outcome? Would the move be made again?
3. **Identify patterns.** Are the moves becoming more frequent? Are the goals becoming less serious? Are the moral costs becoming more visible?
4. **Adjust.** If the audit shows the moves are becoming more frequent or less justified, reduce the frequency. If the audit shows the moves are well-justified and the moral cost is being managed, continue. If the audit shows the moves are corrupting the contractor's relationships, stop.

The audit is the discipline that prevents the Wang Yun mind from becoming the contractor's only mind. The contractor who audits honestly will use personal leverage less over time, and will reserve it for the rare cases where the goal justifies the cost. The contractor who does not audit will use personal leverage more over time, and will become — like Wang Yun — a man who has only one weapon, and who loses the war after winning the battle.
