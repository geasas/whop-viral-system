# Wang Yun — Signature Moves

## The 7 Signature Moves

Each of these moves is a tactical pattern Wang Yun repeatedly uses, or that his historical example makes available to the strategist who invokes his mind. They are not metaphors — they are concrete patterns that can be applied to specific situations.

### Move 1: The Diao Chan Stratagem — The Personal-Leverage Trap

**The pattern**: Identify a personal bond between two opponents (or between an opponent and a powerful ally). Place a single human asset between them, personally attractive to both, under your control. Feed the jealousy, the wounded pride, the possessive claim. Allow the personal bond to convert itself into hostility. Stand back while the two opponents destroy each other.

**Historical example**: Wang Yun identified Lu Bu (Dong Zhuo's adopted son and bodyguard) as the only man in the empire with the power and proximity to kill Dong Zhuo. He identified Diao Chan (his adopted daughter, a woman of legendary beauty and intelligence) as the human asset who could be placed between them. He promised Diao Chan to Lu Bu as a fiancée. Then he offered her to Dong Zhuo as a concubine. Lu Bu, believing his promised wife had been stolen, was wounded in his pride. Dong Zhuo, possessing the woman, was possessive and jealous. The conflict between them was created by Diao Chan's presence. Wang Yun had only to feed Lu Bu's jealousy (through Diao Chan's tears and confessions) and to wait. Within weeks, Lu Bu assassinated Dong Zhuo at the palace gate.

**Mechanism**: The personal bond between Lu Bu and Dong Zhuo was strong (father-son adoption) but instrumental (Lu Bu had already betrayed his first patron, Ding Yuan, for Dong Zhuo — the bond was conditional). The personal bond between Lu Bu and Diao Chan was new and intense (the promise of marriage, the wound of betrayal). The personal bond between Dong Zhuo and Diao Chan was possessive (the concubine's body as property). Three bonds, two of them irrational, one of them instrumental. Wang Yun aimed the two irrational bonds at the instrumental one. The instrumental bond broke. The irrational bonds detonated.

**Application in contracting**:
- Two of your competitors have a personal alliance that has held for years. You identify a shared supplier they both depend on. You place a piece of information with one competitor — that the supplier has been giving the other competitor better terms. The alliance breaks. You win the next project while they are distracted by their conflict.
- A client's chief engineer has a personal bond with one of your competitors — they were university classmates, they socialize, the engineer recommends the competitor for every project. You identify the engineer's spouse as the human asset (a person of influence in the engineer's personal life). You build a relationship with the spouse through a community event, a shared charity, a family connection. The spouse, having met you and respected you, mentions you to the engineer in passing. The engineer, who trusts the spouse, mentions you to the client. The competitor's monopoly is broken — not by attacking it directly, but by placing an asset (the spouse's respect) into the personal bond that held the monopoly.
- A foreman's loyalty to a senior partner in your firm is blocking your promotion of the foreman to a project manager role. You identify the foreman's wife as the human asset. You invite the wife to a company family event, where she meets the partner's wife. The two women do not get along. The foreman's wife returns home and complains about the partner's wife. The foreman's view of the partner shifts — not because of anything the partner did, but because of the personal bond between the two wives. Your opening to promote the foreman arrives.

**Risks**: The Diao Chan stratagem is the most powerful and most dangerous move in Wang Yun's repertoire. The risks are four:
1. **The asset turns on the strategist**: Diao Chan could have revealed the stratagem to Dong Zhuo, and Wang Yun would have been executed. The asset must be either loyal to the strategist (Wang Yun's adopted daughter) or self-interested in the stratagem's success (Diao Chan's survival depended on it).
2. **The opponents reconcile and turn on the strategist**: Lu Bu and Dong Zhuo could have compared notes, realized the manipulation, and turned on Wang Yun together. The opponents must be kept apart, the communication between them controlled, the reconciliation made impossible by the nature of the trigger (sexual jealousy cannot be reasoned with).
3. **The market sees the manipulation and punishes the strategist**: Wang Yun's reputation after Dong Zhuo's death was mixed — he was the liberator, but he was also the manipulator. The market (the imperial court) adjusted its trust in him accordingly. The contractor who uses Diao Chan moves must expect the same adjustment.
4. **The kill strike is not performed, and the trap creates chaos without resolution**: If Lu Bu had not killed Dong Zhuo, the jealousy would have continued, the court would have been divided, and the chaos would have helped no one. The kill strike must be ready when the trigger fires.

**Ethics check**: The Diao Chan stratagem is the most ethically loaded move in the system. It uses human beings as instruments. It weaponizes personal bonds. It creates real wounds (Lu Bu's jealousy was real to him, even if it was manufactured). The contractor should invoke this move only when:
- The target cannot be reached by other means (price, quality, scale, alliance have all failed or are inapplicable).
- The goal is worth the moral cost (the goal must be a serious one — survival, justice, the prevention of greater harm — not merely commercial advantage).
- The human asset is willing and not a victim (the asset must consent to the role, must understand what they are doing, must not be coerced).
- The day-after plan is ready (the consolidation must be planned before the trap is set, not after).

If any of these four conditions is not met, the Diao Chan stratagem should not be invoked. The cost is too high; the risk of the trap backfiring is too great; the moral cost is too corrosive.

### Move 2: The Trap-Building — Patient Relationship Construction

**The pattern**: A personal-leverage trap is not set in a day. It is built over months or years of patient relationship construction — building the trust of the target, building the bond with the human asset, building the credibility of the strategist's cover. The trap-building is the long preparation that makes the trap possible.

**Historical example**: Wang Yun served Dong Zhuo for years. He appeared loyal. He accepted Dong Zhuo's promotions. He attended Dong Zhuo's banquets. He was, to all external observation, a faithful minister of the new regime. During those years, he was not idle — he was building the relationships that would make the Diao Chan stratagem possible. He was building trust with Lu Bu (so that Lu Bu would confide in him about the woman he wanted). He was building Diao Chan's role as his adopted daughter (so that her placement would be plausible). He was building his own credibility as a loyalist (so that no one would suspect him when the kill happened).

The trap-building is the part of the stratagem that is invisible. The kill is visible. The years of preparation are not. Wang Yun's apparent loyalty to Dong Zhuo was, in retrospect, the trap-building phase — and the loyalty was real enough to fool Dong Zhuo for years, and fake enough to enable the kill when the moment came.

**Mechanism**: The trap requires three relationships to be built before the trigger can be pulled:
1. **The strategist's relationship with the target**: must be of sufficient trust that the target does not suspect the strategist. Wang Yun's relationship with Dong Zhuo was of this kind — Dong Zhuo trusted him enough to promote him to high office.
2. **The strategist's relationship with the human asset**: must be of sufficient trust that the asset will play the role. Wang Yun's relationship with Diao Chan was as her adoptive father — the trust was complete.
3. **The strategist's relationship with the instrument (the one who will perform the kill)**: must be of sufficient trust that the instrument will confide in the strategist and act on the strategist's suggestions. Wang Yun's relationship with Lu Bu was of this kind — Lu Bu confided in him about Diao Chan, and acted on his suggestions about how to handle the jealousy.

Each of these relationships takes time to build. The Diao Chan stratagem was not conceived in a week. It was conceived over years, with the trap-building phase being the longest and least visible part.

**Application in contracting**:
- A contractor who has identified a competitor as a long-term threat does not attack the competitor immediately. They build relationships with the competitor's key suppliers, key clients, key employees, over a period of years. Each relationship is built honestly — the contractor does not deceive the supplier or the client about being the competitor's competitor. The relationships are built as commercial relationships, useful in themselves. But over time, the relationships form a network that can be activated when the moment comes — the supplier who trusts the contractor is the supplier who will share information about the competitor's cash flow problems; the client who respects the contractor is the client who will listen when the competitor's quality issues are mentioned; the employee who values the contractor's opinion is the employee who will consider an offer when the time is right.
- A contractor who has identified a key foreman as a future project manager does not promote the foreman immediately. They build the relationship over years — through trust, through respect, through visible investment in the foreman's career. When the moment comes to promote (or to ask the foreman to take a difficult project, or to ask the foreman to lead a difficult team), the relationship is already in place. The promotion is not a surprise; it is the natural next step in a relationship built over time.

**Risks**: The trap-building has two risks:
1. **The trap-builder becomes the trap**: the contractor who builds relationships over years for instrumental reasons may find that the relationships themselves become more important than the trap. The relationship with the supplier becomes a genuine friendship. The trap is never pulled, because pulling it would damage the relationship. This is not always a failure — sometimes the friendship is worth more than the trap. But the strategist should know, before the building begins, whether the trap will be pulled or not. If not, the building is friendship, not strategy.
2. **The trap-building is discovered mid-construction**: if the competitor sees the contractor building relationships with their suppliers and clients, the competitor may interpret the building as a trap and respond defensively. The trap-building must be slow enough to be invisible, or commercial enough to be unremarkable. A contractor who builds relationships with every supplier in the market is a networker; a contractor who builds relationships only with the suppliers of one competitor is a strategist.

**Ethics check**: Trap-building is generally ethical when the relationships are built honestly — the contractor does not lie about their interest in the relationship, does not deceive the other party about their commercial motives. Trap-building becomes unethical when it depends on deception — pretending to be a friend when you are a strategist, pretending to be disinterested when you are collecting information, pretending to be supporting when you are positioning.

### Move 3: The Manipulation of Loyalty — Converting Bond into Betrayal

**The pattern**: The personal-leverage trap's most powerful form is the conversion of a loyal bond into a betrayal. The strategist does not create new disloyalty; they convert existing loyalty into its opposite by exploiting the conditions of the loyalty.

**Historical example**: Lu Bu was loyal to Dong Zhuo. He had been adopted by Dong Zhuo as a son. He had been given Red Hare. He had no rational reason to betray. But Wang Yun saw that the loyalty was instrumental — Lu Bu had betrayed his first patron, Ding Yuan, for Dong Zhuo. The loyalty that can be bought can be sold. Wang Yun offered Lu Bu a higher loyalty (the dynasty, the Emperor, the cause of restoring the Han) and a personal reason (Diao Chan). Lu Bu's loyalty was converted — not broken, but redirected. He remained loyal to the idea of loyalty; he just changed its object.

**Mechanism**: The manipulation of loyalty works because loyalty is rarely pure. Most loyalty is conditional — loyal to a person because the person provides something (rank, gold, security, belonging). The strategist who offers a better provider can convert the loyalty. The conversion is not betrayal from the loyalist's perspective; it is a higher loyalty, a deeper loyalty, a more authentic loyalty. The loyalist tells themselves they are not betraying; they are choosing better.

This self-justification is what makes the manipulation of loyalty so effective. The converted loyalist does not feel they have betrayed; they feel they have been enlightened. They become more loyal to the new cause than they were to the old, because the new cause has been framed as the higher cause.

**Application in contracting**:
- A subcontractor who has worked with one general contractor for years can be converted to working with you if you offer a higher cause (more stable work, better treatment, longer-term partnership). The conversion is not betrayal — the subcontractor tells themselves they are choosing better for their family. The loyalty is redirected, not destroyed.
- A foreman who has been loyal to a partner in your firm can be converted to loyalty to you if you offer a higher cause (a clearer career path, more respect, better projects). The conversion is not betrayal — the foreman tells themselves they are choosing better for their career.
- A client who has been loyal to a competitor can be converted to working with you if you offer a higher cause (better quality, better service, better value). The conversion is not betrayal — the client tells themselves they are choosing better for their project.

The manipulation of loyalty works because the converted loyalist becomes more loyal to you than they were to their previous patron. The new loyalty is deeper because it has been justified by the conversion — the loyalist has invested their self-image in the new loyalty, and to question it would be to question their own judgment.

**Risks**: The manipulation of loyalty has two risks:
1. **The converted loyalist converts again**: a foreman who has been converted from one partner to you can be converted from you to a competitor. The pattern is the same; the loyalty that can be bought can be sold. The contractor who uses this move must accept that the converted loyalist may convert again.
2. **The conversion is visible, and the market interprets the contractor as a disloyal employer/partner**: if a contractor repeatedly converts the loyalists of others, the market interprets the contractor as a poacher. The contractor's own loyalists begin to wonder when they will be poached. The contractor's own loyalty culture is undermined by the contractor's own behavior.

**Ethics check**: The manipulation of loyalty is ethically complex. It is not always wrong — sometimes a foreman or subcontractor should change employers because the current employer is mistreating them. But the manipulation of loyalty for purely instrumental reasons (to weaken a competitor, to extract a worker from a rival) is ethically questionable. The contractor should ask: am I converting this person because the conversion is good for them, or because the conversion is good for me and I am using the person's welfare as the justification?

### Move 4: The Immediate Kill Strike — Capitalizing on the Triggered Moment

**The pattern**: The trigger creates the hostility; the kill strike capitalizes on it. The kill strike is the move that converts the manipulated moment into the permanent gain. It must be ready before the trigger is pulled, executed immediately when the trigger fires, and irrevocable once executed.

**Historical example**: Wang Yun's kill strike was the assassination of Dong Zhuo at the palace. The strike was performed by Lu Bu, but it was planned by Wang Yun. The plan included:
1. The Edict: a forged or genuine imperial edict calling for Dong Zhuo's death, providing the legal basis for the assassination.
2. The Trap: the summoning of Dong Zhuo to the palace under the pretense of an imperial audience, putting him in a position where he could be attacked.
3. The Executioner: Lu Bu, who had been primed by the Diao Chan trigger and was now ready to act.
4. The Aftermath: the immediate proclamation of Dong Zhuo's death, the declaration of amnesty for most of his followers (but not Li Jue and Guo Si — the fatal error), the assumption of imperial authority by Wang Yun.

The kill strike was a masterpiece of timing. It depended on the trigger being live, on the executioner being ready, on the legal basis being in place, on the aftermath being prepared. Wang Yun had each of these elements ready when the trigger fired.

**Mechanism**: The kill strike works because it is a single, decisive, irrevocable act. It cannot be undone. Once Dong Zhuo was dead, he could not be un-killed. The killing settled the personal-leverage trap — the trap had been set for the killing, and the killing was performed. The trap was now over. The next phase (consolidation) began.

**Application in contracting**:
- After months of building a relationship with a client's chief engineer (the trap-building), and after the engineer has confided in you about a competitor's quality issues (the trigger), the kill strike is your bid to replace the competitor on the next project. The bid must be ready when the trigger fires — drafted, priced, reviewed, ready to submit. If the bid is not ready, the trigger is wasted.
- After months of cultivating a senior foreman at a competitor's firm (the trap-building), and after the foreman has confided that they are unhappy with their current pay (the trigger), the kill strike is your job offer. The offer must be ready — the role, the salary, the start date, the terms. If the offer is not ready, the foreman's complaint is vented, not acted on, and the trigger is wasted.
- After months of building a relationship with a supplier who serves a competitor (the trap-building), and after the supplier has confided that the competitor has been late on payments (the trigger), the kill strike is your offer to take over the supplier's relationship with the competitor — by becoming the supplier's primary customer, by buying out the supplier's inventory, by signing an exclusive supply agreement. The offer must be ready when the trigger fires.

**Risks**: The kill strike has three risks:
1. **The kill strike is mis-timed**: executed too early, the strike is visible before the trigger has fired; the target has time to defend. Executed too late, the strike is wasted; the trigger has cooled, the hostility has dissipated.
2. **The kill strike is reversible**: if the strike can be undone (the client can cancel the bid award, the foreman can decline the offer, the supplier can return to the competitor), the strike has not killed the target. The strike must be such that, once executed, the target cannot recover.
3. **The kill strike creates a backlash**: the strike itself may create enemies — the competitor who has been displaced, the engineer who feels used, the foreman who feels bought. The backlash may not come immediately, but it comes.

**Ethics check**: The kill strike, when it is a commercial bid to replace a competitor who has been honestly outperformed, is ethical. When it is a job offer to a foreman who is genuinely seeking better work, it is ethical. When it is an exclusive supply agreement with a supplier who is genuinely better served by you, it is ethical. The kill strike becomes unethical when it depends on deception — when the bid is won by manipulated information, the foreman is recruited by false promises, the supplier is locked in by predatory terms.

### Move 5: The Refusal to Amnesty — The Post-Victory Error

**The pattern**: After the kill, the strategist must decide what to do with the target's former allies. The refusal to amnesty is the post-victory error that turned Wang Yun's triumph into his destruction. The pattern is presented here as an ANTI-PATTERN — what Wang Yun did that the strategist invoking his mind must NOT do.

**Historical example**: After Dong Zhuo's assassination in 192 AD, the western generals Li Jue, Guo Si, Fan Chou, and Zhang Ji — all former Dong Zhuo subordinates — controlled the armies in the Guanzhong region. They petitioned Wang Yun for amnesty. Wang Yun refused. His reasoning was principled: Dong Zhuo's crimes were so great that his collaborators should not be forgiven. His policy was catastrophic: the four generals, with nothing to lose, marched on Chang'an. Within three months, they had taken the city. Wang Yun was executed, along with his family.

The refusal to amnesty was the post-victory error. Wang Yun had won the kill; he lost the hold. The refusal was the policy that turned the win into the loss.

**Mechanism (anti-pattern)**: The refusal to amnesty fails because it gives the target's former allies nothing to lose. A defeated army with a path to surrender will surrender. A defeated army with no path to surrender will fight. The fight is more dangerous than the surrender — the army that surrenders is no longer a threat; the army that fights is a continued threat, and a desperate one.

Wang Yun's error was to apply the logic of justice (collaborators should be punished) without the logic of strategy (defeated enemies with no path to surrender become undefeated enemies). The two logics conflicted. Wang Yun chose justice. Strategy demanded mercy.

**Application in contracting** (as anti-pattern):
- A contractor who wins a major project from a competitor must decide what to do with the competitor's former sub-contractors, suppliers, and clients. The refusal to engage them — the "I won the project, I don't need them" approach — leaves them with nothing to lose. They will compete with you on the next project, undermine your reputation in the market, and look for ways to make your win cost you.
- The alternative: the contractor who wins the major project reaches out to the competitor's former sub-contractors and suppliers, offers them work on the new project, integrates them into the contractor's own network. The former allies of the competitor become the contractor's allies. The competitor is isolated. The win is consolidated.
- A contractor who has recruited a key foreman from a competitor must decide what to do with the foreman's former colleagues at the competitor. The refusal to engage them — the "I have the foreman, I don't need them" approach — leaves them hostile to both the foreman (who they see as a traitor) and the contractor (who they see as a poacher). They will spread negative stories, they will resist any future approach, they will make the foreman's transition harder.
- The alternative: the contractor who recruits the foreman also reaches out to the foreman's closest former colleagues, builds relationships with them, offers them opportunities if they ever want to move. They are not poached immediately, but the relationship is built. Over time, the competitor's team erodes, while the contractor's team strengthens.

**Risks**: The refusal to amnesty, as an anti-pattern, has the risk that the strategist who refuses will lose everything. Wang Yun lost his life and his family. The contractor who refuses will lose the consolidation, the reputation, and the long-term position.

**Ethics check**: The refusal to amnesty is ethical in principle (collaborators with wrongdoing should be held accountable) but unethical in practice (when the accountability creates more harm than the wrongdoing it punishes). The strategist must balance justice and mercy — and when in doubt, in the immediate aftermath of a personal-leverage victory, mercy is the safer choice.

### Move 6: The Self-Sacrifice Exit — Dying with the Cause

**The pattern**: When the consolidation fails and the counter-strike arrives, the strategist has a choice: flee (and live) or stay (and die with the cause they served). Wang Yun's choice was to stay. He threw himself from the city gate, was captured by Li Jue and Guo Si, and was executed along with his family. He chose reputation over survival. The cause he served (the Han dynasty) was already lost, but the cause he represented (loyalty to the dynasty) was preserved by his death.

**Historical example**: In 192 AD, when Li Jue and Guo Si approached Chang'an, Wang Yun had the option to flee. He could have left the city, joined the coalition of regional warlords, and lived to fight another day. He refused. He chose to stay with the Emperor, to share the Emperor's fate, to demonstrate by his death that the Han dynasty still had loyal ministers.

His death was the final act of his strategy. He had spent his life in service of the Han; he spent his death in service of the Han. The Han dynasty did not survive him by many years, but the memory of his loyalty survived. He is remembered in the history as a patriot — a man who died for a cause that was already lost, and whose dying gave the cause its last legitimacy.

**Mechanism**: The self-sacrifice exit works as a reputation-preserving move. The strategist who flees survives as a person but loses the reputation of being committed to the cause. The strategist who stays dies as a person but preserves the reputation. The reputation, in some cases, is worth more than the person — particularly when the cause the strategist served is one that will be remembered, and the strategist's death will be a part of the memory.

**Application in contracting**:
- A contractor who has been a long-time supplier to a developer for years, and the developer goes bankrupt, has a choice: pursue every legal avenue to recover the debt (and be remembered as the contractor who sued the developer when they were down), or accept the loss and continue to support the developer's family, the developer's former employees, the developer's reputation (and be remembered as the contractor who stood by the developer in their hardest time). The first option recovers some money. The second option preserves the reputation that will bring new clients for years.
- A contractor who has been a partner in a firm, and the firm breaks up due to a partner's misconduct, has a choice: publicly blame the partner (and recover the moral high ground at the cost of the firm's reputation), or quietly take the loss, accept the public's suspicion that all partners were complicit, and rebuild elsewhere (and preserve the firm's legacy while building a new one). The first option recovers the moral position. The second option preserves the option of future partnership with the firm's former clients.

**Risks**: The self-sacrifice exit has the obvious risk that the strategist dies, commercially or reputationally. The move is appropriate only when:
1. The cause the strategist served is more important than the strategist's individual survival.
2. The reputation preserved by the death is more valuable than the resources lost by the death.
3. The death is the final act, not a prelude to a future comeback (the strategist who dies and then comes back is not a martyr; they are a manipulator).

**Ethics check**: The self-sacrifice exit is ethical when the cause is genuine (the contractor truly believed in the developer, the partner truly believed in the firm). It is unethical when the cause is instrumental (the contractor dies for the developer because the death serves the contractor's family, or for some other ulterior motive). The genuine self-sacrifice is honorable; the instrumental self-sacrifice is manipulation to the last.

### Move 7: The False Innocence — Operating Under Cover of Loyalty

**The pattern**: Wang Yun served Dong Zhuo for years without appearing to oppose him. He was promoted to high office. He attended the tyrant's banquets. He was, to external observation, a model minister. During those years, he was planning the assassination. The false innocence was the cover that allowed the planning to proceed undetected.

**Historical example**: Wang Yun's years of service to Dong Zhuo are a puzzle. Was he genuinely loyal at first, and turned later? Was he plotting from the beginning? The history is ambiguous. The Ravages of Time manga portrays him as a long-term loyalist to the Han who served Dong Zhuo only as cover for the eventual restoration. Either way, the operational pattern is the same: Wang Yun appeared loyal while he was planning the kill.

The false innocence is the cover that makes the personal-leverage trap possible. Without it, the trap-builder is visible; the trap is detected; the target defends. With it, the trap-builder is invisible; the trap is undetected; the target is exposed.

**Mechanism**: The false innocence works because the target's defenses are calibrated to detect opposition, not loyal service. The target sees the strategist as a member of their own team. The strategist's suggestions are taken as loyal advice. The strategist's questions are taken as curiosity, not investigation. The strategist's presence is taken as support, not surveillance.

The cover must be maintained consistently. Any break — a moment of visible doubt, a careless criticism, a too-eager question — and the target recalibrates. The cover is broken, the trap is exposed, the target defends.

**Application in contracting**:
- A contractor who has identified a long-term competitor as a target may join industry associations the competitor leads, attend conferences the competitor speaks at, participate in working groups the competitor chairs. The participation is genuine (the contractor does contribute to the industry), but it also provides cover for the relationship-building that the personal-leverage trap requires.
- A contractor who has identified a difficult client as a target (a client who has been refusing to pay, or who has been unfair in negotiations) may continue to deliver excellent work for that client, even at a loss, while building relationships with the client's other contractors, suppliers, and decision-makers. The excellent work is the cover; the relationship-building is the trap.
- A contractor who has identified a regulatory obstacle as a target may join the regulatory body's industry advisory group, contribute to the drafting of new regulations, and use the position to learn the regulatory body's internal dynamics. The contribution is genuine; the learning is the trap.

**Risks**: The false innocence has two risks:
1. **The cover becomes real**: the strategist who plays loyal for years may find that they have become loyal. The trap is never pulled, because the strategist no longer wants to pull it. This is not always a failure — sometimes the conversion is genuine and the relationship is more valuable than the trap. But the strategist should know, before the cover begins, whether the trap will be pulled.
2. **The cover is detected**: the target sees through the cover and the strategist is exposed. The target defends; the strategist is marked as an enemy; the trap is impossible. The cover must be played consistently, without breaks, for the duration of the trap-building phase.

**Ethics check**: The false innocence is ethical when the strategist's loyalty is genuine (the contractor really does contribute to the industry association, really does deliver excellent work for the difficult client, really does contribute to the regulatory drafting). It is unethical when the loyalty is fake (the contractor pretends to contribute while actually only collecting information). The genuine false innocence — where the service is real but the purpose is instrumental — is morally complex. The contractor should ask: am I serving because the service is worth doing, or am I serving only as cover for the trap?

## How to Recognize When to Apply Each Move

| Move | When to apply | When NOT to apply |
|------|---------------|-------------------|
| Diao Chan Stratagem | When the target cannot be reached by other means and the moral cost is justified | When other minds (Sima Yi, Guo Jia, Cao Cao) can reach the target; when the moral cost is not justified |
| Trap-Building | When the personal-leverage trap is the chosen strategy and the relationships need time to build | When the trap must be set quickly (use Guo Jia instead); when the relationships should be genuine rather than instrumental (use Liu Bei) |
| Manipulation of Loyalty | When the target's key people are conditionally loyal and can be converted by a higher cause | When the target's key people are genuinely loyal (cannot be converted; manipulation will be exposed) |
| Immediate Kill Strike | When the trigger has fired and the kill is ready to execute | When the trigger has not fired (kill is wasted); when the kill is reversible (target recovers) |
| Refusal to Amnesty (anti-pattern) | NEVER — this is what NOT to do | Always — apply mercy instead, integrate the target's former allies |
| Self-Sacrifice Exit | When the cause is genuinely more important than survival and the reputation preserved is worth the loss | When the cause is instrumental; when the strategist can rebuild elsewhere |
| False Innocence | When the trap-building requires years of cover and the strategist can maintain the cover consistently | When the cover cannot be maintained (will be detected); when the cover becomes real (the strategist becomes what they were pretending to be) |
