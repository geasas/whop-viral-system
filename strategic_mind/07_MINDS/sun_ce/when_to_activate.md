# Sun Ce — When to Activate

> **Subject**: The explicit routing rules for when to invoke the Sun Ce mind

## The Premise

Sun Ce's mind is not universal. It applies to specific situations and fails in others. This file defines the routing rules so the system knows when to invoke this mind and when to switch to another.

The rules are **triggered by the phase of the firm and the structure of the opportunity**, not by topic. The same topic (a bid on a project) may invoke Sun Ce or Sima Yi depending on whether the firm is in a founding phase and whether the opportunity is a window of unsettlement or a settled competitive bid.

Sun Ce's mind is the **founding mind**. It is right for the move from one phase of the firm to the next — from contractor to developer, from regional to national, from one segment to another. It is wrong for the consolidation within a phase — for the patient building of a portfolio after the first move.

---

## Trigger Conditions — When to ACTIVATE Sun Ce

### Trigger 1: The firm is about to make a phase transition

If the user's question involves a phase transition — moving from electrical contracting to general contracting, from general contracting to real estate development, from one segment (residential) to another (commercial), from one region to another — Sun Ce is the primary mind. Phase transitions are Sun Ce moments because they require the founding pattern: borrowed force, recruited multipliers, decisive first move, succession plan from day one.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "I'm thinking about taking on my first development project."
- "I'm considering opening an office in a new city."
- "I want to move from residential to commercial work."
- "I want to add a new service line — solar installation, smart-building systems, modular construction."

**Example**: "I've been an electrical contractor for ten years and I want to make the move into real estate development." → Sun Ce's mind: this is a phase transition; the borrowed-army pattern applies (joint venture, supplier credit, bank loan against presale), the childhood-brother pattern applies (the pivotal hires must already be relationships), the vanguard-speed pattern applies (move while the established developers in the target segment are slow), the succession pattern applies (write the succession plan for the new firm from project one).

### Trigger 2: A market window is opening and the user can move before it closes

If a window of unsettlement has opened in the user's market — a new regulatory regime, a new regional opening, a competitor's distress, a technology transition — and the window will close in 6-18 months if it is not entered, Sun Ce's decisive speed is the right mind. The window is the Jiangdong moment.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "The new building code creates an opening for contractors who can certify solar-ready buildings."
- "The new mortgage law makes it easier for first-time buyers to finance mid-tier housing."
- "A major developer in our region has been hit by a corruption investigation and his projects are stalled."
- "A new coastal development zone has been announced but no developer has moved in yet."

**Example**: "The government just announced a new financing program for mid-tier housing that subsidizes 15% of the unit cost for first-time buyers, but the program is capped at the first 50,000 units nationwide." → Sun Ce's mind: this is a 6-12 month window; the established developers will take 6 months to design the right product and another 6 months to launch; if you can move in 3 months with a smaller, faster project, you capture the early-subsidy wave before the program caps out.

### Trigger 3: A pivotal hire is available and the relationship is already built

If a pivotal multiplier — a senior engineer, a financial partner, a land scout, a sales leader — has just become available, and the contractor has a genuine personal relationship with them from before they became available, Sun Ce's childhood-brother pattern is the right mind. The recruitment is the conversion of a years-long relationship into a partnership.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "The senior engineer I've been friendly with at the developer's office just resigned and is looking for his next move."
- "The financial analyst I've been meeting for coffee for two years is ready to leave her bank and join a small firm."
- "A subcontractor I've always respected just had a falling-out with his general contractor and is open to a different model."

**Example**: "The senior project manager who has been my friend's boss at the major developer for years is leaving the company and has hinted he would consider working with me." → Sun Ce's mind: this is the childhood-brother moment; the relationship is already built; the recruitment is the conversion; move now, on personal terms, not on commercial terms.

### Trigger 4: A confirmed obstacle must be removed and is still small

If a confirmed active plotter or blocker — a subcontractor who is actively undermining the firm, a competitor who is actively spreading false rumors, a supplier who is colluding to block materials access — is still small enough to be removed cleanly, Sun Ce's decisive-removal pattern is the right mind. The removal must be confirmed (the plot must be verified, not assumed) and small (the plotter must not have entrenched allies who will retaliate at scale).

**Trigger pattern**:
- "A subcontractor I've been working with is actively telling other subcontractors to refuse to work with me."
- "A competitor is spreading a false rumor that my firm is in financial trouble."
- "A supplier I relied on has signed an exclusive deal with my competitor."

**Example**: "A subcontractor I terminated last month is telling every supplier in the market that my firm doesn't pay on time, and I've confirmed he is the source of three refusals I've received from suppliers in the last week." → Sun Ce's mind: this is a confirmed obstacle, still small enough to be addressed through formal channels (a cease-and-desist letter, a defamation complaint, a public clarification to the suppliers); move now, before the obstacle grows large enough to require a war.

### Trigger 5: The user is ready to write the succession plan for the first time

If the user has reached a milestone — the first project, the first hire, the first major deal — and has not yet written a succession plan, Sun Ce's last-words pattern is the right mind. The succession plan is the founder's discipline; it must be written early and updated often.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "I've just signed my first development deal and I want to set up the firm properly."
- "I've just hired my first project manager and I want to think about the firm structure."
- "I'm thinking about what happens to the firm if I can't run it for a year."

**Example**: "I've signed the contract for my first real estate development project and I want to make sure the firm is structured properly from day one." → Sun Ce's mind: this is the last-words moment; write the succession plan now, share it with the named successors (the project manager, the financial partner, the family member), and update it at every milestone; the firm built with a succession plan is a firm, the firm built without one is a personal practice.

## Inhibition Conditions — When NOT to Activate Sun Ce

### Inhibitor 1: The user is in a consolidation phase, not a founding phase

If the user has already made the phase transition (the first development deal is done, the new region is entered, the new segment is established) and is now consolidating — building the portfolio, deepening the relationships, scaling the operations — Sun Ce's founding mind is wrong. Activate Sima Yi instead.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I've done my first development project and now I want to build a portfolio over the next 10 years."
- "I've entered the new region and now I want to deepen my position there."
- "I've launched the new service line and now I want to make it a stable part of the firm."

**Exception**: If a new window opens within the consolidation phase (a new regulatory change creates a sub-window within the existing market), Sun Ce applies for that sub-window only. The default mind in the consolidation phase is Sima Yi; Sun Ce is invoked for sub-windows that require fast moves.

### Inhibitor 2: The window has closed or does not exist

If the window the user is considering moving on is closed (the regulatory program has been capped, the competitor's distress has been resolved, the technology has not matured, the new region is fully occupied by established players), Sun Ce's move is wrong. Activating Sun Ce on a closed window produces a Sun Ce mistake — a fast move into a brick wall.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "The new financing program is already capped out."
- "The competitor's distress turned out to be overstated — he has new capital."
- "The new technology is still in pilot stage, not at commercial scale."

**Exception**: If the window is closed but expected to reopen within 12-18 months (a new phase of the same regulatory program, a new technology generation, a new competitor distress cycle), Sun Ce applies to the preparation for the reopened window — the preparation is the Sun Ce move; the actual move waits for the window.

### Inhibitor 3: The user has not yet built the relationships that Sun Ce requires

If the user is considering a Sun Ce move but the pivotal relationships — the childhood brothers, the multipliers, the inner circle — are not yet built, the Sun Ce move will fail for lack of the multipliers. Activate Liu Bei first (build the relationships patiently); then activate Sun Ce when the relationships are built.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I want to make the development move, but I haven't built the relationships with the senior engineer or the financial partner yet."
- "I want to recruit a key person, but I've only met him twice."
- "I want to take on a partner, but I don't know him well enough to trust him with the firm."

**Exception**: If a relationship is available immediately (the multiplier has just become available and the contractor's existing network provides sufficient introduction), Sun Ce can be invoked with the relationship-building compressed into the move. But the compression is risky and should be the exception, not the rule.

### Inhibitor 4: The user is in a politically delicate situation requiring neutrality

If the user is in a situation where any move invites retaliation — factions in a client's organization competing for control, two developers competing for the same site, two suppliers competing for the same exclusive — Sun Ce's decisive move is wrong because it commits the user to one side. Activate Sima Yi instead (maintain neutrality, let the factions resolve).

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "The client's CEO and the procurement director are in conflict and I'm being asked to pick a side."
- "Two developers are competing for the same site and both want me to commit to being their contractor."
- "Two suppliers are in a price war and both want me to commit to exclusivity."

**Exception**: If one of the factions is certain to win and the user has confirmed it, Sun Ce applies — commit to the winner. The exception is rare; most factional conflicts are uncertain, and the Sun Ce commitment to the wrong faction is a fatal mistake.

### Inhibitor 5: The user's health, capital, or team cannot sustain a fast move

If the user's personal capacity is depleted (illness, exhaustion, family crisis), or the firm's capital is depleted (cash flow tight, credit maxed), or the team is at capacity (every project manager is fully loaded, every project is at peak execution), Sun Ce's fast move is wrong. The fast move requires reserves — of health, capital, and team. Without the reserves, the move breaks the firm. Activate the recovery mind instead (rest, replenish, prepare).

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I'm exhausted and I'm considering a major new project."
- "Our cash flow is tight and I'm thinking about a major expansion."
- "My team is fully loaded and I'm thinking about a new project."

**Exception**: If the Sun Ce move itself produces the reserves (the new project produces cash flow that replenishes the firm, the new partnership produces capital that recapitalizes the firm), Sun Ce can be invoked with the move and the replenishment in sequence. But the sequence must be carefully designed — the move must produce the reserves before the reserves are needed, or the move consumes the firm.

## The Combination Cases

Some situations warrant Sun Ce combined with another mind:

### Sun Ce + Sima Yi: Found then Hold

Make the Sun Ce founding move (the first development project, the entry into a new region); then, once the move has produced a result, switch to Sima Yi's mind to consolidate the result over the next decade. This is the most powerful combination for the user's specific trajectory — Sun Ce for the move from contracting to development, Sima Yi for the consolidation of the development portfolio over the next 10-15 years.

**Application**: The first development project is the Sun Ce move — borrowed capital, recruited relationships, decisive bid, succession plan from day one. After the first project is delivered and the firm has a development track record, the next 10 years of building a development portfolio is the Sima Yi move — patient accumulation, careful selection of the next project, deepening of the institutional capacity.

### Sun Ce + Guo Jia: Verify Then Move at Speed

Apply Guo Jia's rapid read to verify that the window is actually open; then apply Sun Ce's decisive speed to move through the window before it closes. The combination solves Sun Ce's blind spot #5 (the window misjudgment) by adding the verification step before the Sun Ce move.

**Application**: A regulatory window has just been announced. Before moving, apply Guo Jia's verification: is the regulation actually implemented, or only announced? Is the program actually funded, or only authorized? Is the cap actually set, or only proposed? Each verification takes days. Then, with the window verified, apply Sun Ce's speed: bid, partner, hire, and structure the deal within the 3-6 month window of the regulation's first phase.

### Sun Ce + Liu Bei: Found Through Trust, Not Through Force

Apply Sun Ce's founding pattern, but use Liu Bei's relational methods to build the inner circle and to manage the grievance tails of decisive removals. The combination solves Sun Ce's blind spot #2 (charm decay) by binding the inner circle through trust, not through charisma alone, and solves Sun Ce's blind spot #6 (the grievance tail) by repairing the relationships after each removal.

**Application**: The contractor making the development move recruits the inner circle through genuine trust-building (Liu Bei) and not just through charm (Sun Ce). The contractor who removes a subcontractor for cause also manages the grievance tail — by providing a positive reference where appropriate, by clarifying the situation to the market, by maintaining the relationship where possible. The Sun Ce move is the move; the Liu Bei method is the way the move is made.

## The Activation Test

Before invoking Sun Ce, the system asks:

1. **Phase**: is the user in a founding phase (making a phase transition, entering a new region, launching a new segment)? If yes, Sun Ce applies. If the user is in a consolidation phase, switch to Sima Yi.
2. **Window**: is there a real, open window in the market that the user can move through in 6-18 months? If yes, Sun Ce applies. If the window is closed or does not exist, switch to Guo Jia for verification or to Sima Yi for waiting.
3. **Relationships**: are the pivotal relationships already built (the childhood brothers, the multipliers, the inner circle)? If yes, Sun Ce applies. If not, switch to Liu Bei to build them.
4. **Obstacle**: is there a confirmed, small obstacle that must be removed? If yes, Sun Ce's decisive-removal pattern applies. If the obstacle is large or unconfirmed, switch to Sima Yi (for unconfirmed) or to a defensive posture (for large).
5. **Capacity**: does the user have the health, capital, and team reserves to sustain a fast move? If yes, Sun Ce applies. If not, switch to recovery.
6. **Succession**: is the user at a milestone where the succession plan should be written or updated? If yes, Sun Ce's last-words pattern applies. If the plan is current, this trigger is not active but the plan should still be reviewed quarterly.

If the test passes (phase, window, or succession trigger active; relationships built; capacity available), Sun Ce is invoked. If the test fails, another mind is invoked. The user can override by explicitly asking for Sun Ce's perspective.

## The Self-Correction

Sun Ce's mind is prone to three failures that the system should detect and warn about:

**Over-extension**: The user invokes Sun Ce repeatedly across multiple windows in a short period. Each move consumes health, capital, and team; the cumulative consumption exceeds the reserves. The system should warn: "You've made N Sun Ce moves in M months. Your reserves are depleting. Consider whether the next move is a Sun Ce move or a consolidation move."

**Window misjudgment**: The user invokes Sun Ce on a window that has not been verified. The system should warn: "You're about to move on a window that has not been verified. Apply Guo Jia's verification step first — is the window actually open, or are you reading it as open because you want it to be open?"

**Decisive-removal overuse**: The user invokes Sun Ce's decisive removal repeatedly, creating cumulative grievance tails. The system should warn: "You've made N decisive removals in M months. Each removal has a grievance tail. Are you monitoring the tails? Are you repairing where possible? The cumulative grievance may be eroding your market position."

This self-correction is built into the activation rule: Sun Ce's mind is the founding mind, not the universal mind. The founding phase is short. The system's job is to help the user recognize when the founding phase has ended and the consolidation phase has begun, and to switch minds accordingly.
