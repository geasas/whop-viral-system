# Diao Chan — Blind Spots

> **Layer**: 07_MINDS/dia_chan/
> **File**: blind_spots
> **Subject**: Where the leveraged weapon's worldview fails. Every strength has a hidden weakness.
> **Mention frequency in manga**: 38 times across 635 chapters, concentrated in the Dong Zhuo arc

---

## The Premise

A strategic mind without awareness of its blind spots is dangerous. The user of this system must know where the Diao Chan mind fails, so they don't apply it where it doesn't work. The Diao Chan mind is the most precise mind in the system for executing the role of the leveraged weapon — and the most dangerous mind to the user who confuses the role with the self.

These are not flaws to "fix." They are limitations inherent to the worldview. The system must invoke other minds when these limitations are active.

Diao Chan's blind spots are unusually severe because the worldview itself is unusually constrained. The leveraged weapon can do what no other strategic mind can do — execute, with skill and judgment, the strike that the strategist designed but cannot perform. But the same constraint that makes the weapon able to execute the strike also makes the weapon vulnerable to the strike's aftermath. The blind spots catalogued below are the ways the leveraged role consumes the weapon.

## The 6 Blind Spots

### Blind Spot 1: Identification with the Strategist — Becoming the Strategist's Voice

**The pattern**: The leveraged weapon, playing the role for an extended period, begins to identify with the strategist — to think the strategist's thoughts, to want the strategist's goals, to value what the strategist values. The identification is not chosen; it is the natural drift of a mind that has been subordinated to another mind for too long. The weapon that identifies with the strategist loses the weapon's own strategic sense, and exits the leverage as an extension of the strategist rather than as a self.

**Historical example**: Diao Chan's historical record is too sparse to show the identification clearly, but the absence of any record of her own ambitions, her own plans, her own voice, suggests it. She had no recorded ambitions of her own. She had no recorded plans of her own. She had no recorded voice of her own. She was Wang Yun's instrument, and when Wang Yun's stratagem was done, she had nothing of her own. The identification was the consumption.

The identification is the leveraged weapon's most insidious failure because it is invisible to the weapon. The weapon does not feel the identification as a loss; the weapon feels it as alignment, as commitment, as the deepening of the role. The weapon says to itself: "I am committed to the developer's project. I am aligned with the developer's vision. I am the developer's instrument." Each statement sounds like integrity. Each statement is the consumption.

**Symptom in your practice**: The Egyptian contractor who has been the leveraged weapon on a developer's project for two years begins to identify with the developer. The contractor thinks the developer's thoughts (the project's priorities, the project's metrics, the project's risks). The contractor wants the developer's goals (the project's success, the developer's profit, the developer's reputation). The contractor values what the developer values (the developer's preferred suppliers, the developer's preferred methods, the developer's preferred aesthetics).

The identification is invisible to the contractor. The contractor feels it as commitment to the project, as alignment with the developer, as the deepening of the relationship. The contractor does not feel it as the loss of the contractor's own strategic sense. But the loss is real. The contractor who has identified with the developer for two years cannot remember what kind of contractor they would be if they were not the developer's instrument. The contractor's own ambitions have atrophied. The contractor's own judgments have been subordinated. The contractor's own sense of self has been consumed.

**Counter-pattern**: When the identification with the strategist is the active risk, invoke Sima Yi's mind for the long-game view from the contractor's own position. Sima Yi thinks in years, from his own interest, not from the strategist's. The contractor who invokes Sima Yi asks: "What do I want in 5 years, from my own position, not from the developer's?" The question forces the contractor back to the contractor's own strategic sense, which has been subordinated but not destroyed.

The deeper counter-pattern: invoke Liu Bei's mind for the genuine bonds. The contractor who has identified with the developer has neglected the contractor's own genuine bonds — with the contractor's own foremen, with the contractor's own suppliers, with the contractor's own family. Liu Bei's mind rebuilds these bonds. The bonds are the contractor's anchor; the identification has lifted the anchor. Liu Bei's mind sets the anchor again.

The deepest counter-pattern: the periodic private reflection on the contractor's own ambitions. Once a quarter, the contractor should ask: "What do I want, from my own position, that is not what the developer wants? What kind of contractor would I be if I were not the developer's instrument?" The question, asked honestly, prevents the identification from completing. The contractor who has forgotten the answer is already identified; the contractor who can still answer the question has preserved the self.

### Blind Spot 2: The Silence That Consumes — When the Leverage Ends and the Weapon Has Nothing

**The pattern**: The leveraged weapon's role ends; what follows the role is the silence. The weapon that has not built within the constraint exits the leverage with nothing — no references, no capacity, no relationships that the weapon can call its own. The silence, instead of being a transition, becomes a consumption. The weapon, with nothing to fall back on, is destroyed by the silence.

**Historical example**: Diao Chan's disappearance after the assassination is the visible form of the silence that consumes. She did not become a figure in her own right. She did not become a strategist. She did not become a wife of a powerful man (in most versions). She disappeared, with nothing of her own. The disappearance was the silence that consumed her, because she had built nothing during the leverage that survived the leverage.

The silence that consumes is the leveraged weapon's most catastrophic failure because it is irreversible. The weapon that has been consumed by the silence cannot recover — there is no foundation to recover from, no relationships to rebuild on, no references to leverage. The weapon that has been consumed by the silence is the weapon that exits the leverage as nothing, and the nothing cannot be undone.

**Symptom in your practice**: The Egyptian contractor who has been the leveraged weapon on a developer's project for two years, and who exits the leverage without a build, faces the silence that consumes. The developer's calls stop. The developer's other subcontractors, who were friendly during the leverage, have no reason to call. The developer's other consultants, who were collegial during the leverage, have no reason to recommend the contractor. The contractor's team, who were trained on the developer's project, have no project to apply the training to.

The contractor, in the silence, has nothing. The contractor's references are the developer's references (the developer's name, the developer's project, the developer's network) — and the developer's references are no longer the contractor's, because the developer has moved on. The contractor's capacity is the developer's capacity (the developer's systems, the developer's suppliers, the developer's processes) — and the developer's capacity is no longer the contractor's. The contractor's relationships are the developer's relationships (the developer's other subcontractors, the developer's other consultants, the developer's other contacts) — and the developer's relationships are no longer the contractor's.

The contractor, in the silence, has only the contractor's own firm. The firm that was subordinated to the developer's project for two years has been neglected. The firm's own clients have been deprioritized. The firm's own projects have been under-invested. The firm's own team has been stretched between the developer's demands and the firm's own work. The firm, in the silence, is weaker than it was before the leverage.

**Counter-pattern**: When the silence that consumes is the active risk, invoke Sima Yi's mind for the long-game recovery and Cao Cao's mind for the institutional rebuilding. Sima Yi thinks in years; the recovery is a years-long process, not a moment. Cao Cao builds systems; the recovery requires systems, not personal relationships with the developer.

The contractor who faces the silence that consumes must:
1. Recognize that the leverage has ended and the silence has arrived. The recognition is the first step; the contractor who denies the silence (continuing to wait for the developer's calls, continuing to half-perform the leveraged role) extends the consumption.
2. Switch to Sima Yi for the multi-year recovery planning. The recovery is not quick. The contractor must plan to rebuild the firm's own client base, the firm's own project pipeline, the firm's own team, over 2-3 years.
3. Switch to Cao Cao for the institutional rebuilding. The contractor must build the systems (project management, financial management, supplier management) that the leverage did not require but the recovery does. The systems are the institutional capacity that survives the next leverage — if there is one.
4. Refuse the next leverage until the recovery is complete. The contractor who has been consumed by the silence is vulnerable to the next leverage, because the next leverage offers the quick return to relevance that the recovery does not. The contractor who accepts the next leverage before the recovery is complete extends the consumption.

The deeper discipline: the build-within-constraint (signature move 5) is the prevention of the silence that consumes. The contractor who builds within the constraint — references, capacity, relationships — exits the leverage with a foundation. The contractor who does not build within the constraint exits the leverage with nothing. The silence that consumes is the price of the failure to build.

### Blind Spot 3: The Authenticity Erosion — The Slow Loss of the Ability to Be Authentic

**The pattern**: The leveraged weapon, playing an inauthentic role for an extended period, loses the ability to be authentic. The loss is not sudden; it is the slow drift of a self that has been performing for so long that the performance has become the self. The weapon that has been the concubine, the lover, the daughter for months finds that it cannot remember what it was before the performances. The weapon that has been the developer's instrument for years finds that it cannot remember what it was before the leverage.

**Historical example**: Diao Chan's historical disappearance is the visible form of the authenticity erosion. The history does not record her voice because she had no voice left to record. The role had consumed the voice. The concubine, the lover, the daughter — none of these were her. She had been all three, for months, and the three had consumed whatever she had been before. The disappearance was the absence of the self that the performances had consumed.

The authenticity erosion is the leveraged weapon's most subtle failure because it is invisible to the weapon until it is too late. The weapon does not feel the erosion as a loss; the weapon feels it as mastery, as the deepening of the skill, as the perfection of the performance. The weapon says to itself: "I am getting better at this. I can play the role without effort. I am the role." Each statement sounds like excellence. Each statement is the consumption.

**Symptom in your practice**: The Egyptian contractor who has been the leveraged weapon on a developer's project for two years begins to lose the ability to be authentic. The contractor, who once had a direct manner, a clear voice, an honest way of speaking about the work, now speaks in the developer's voice — the hedged, careful, diplomatic voice of the leveraged instrument. The contractor, who once had his own judgments about quality, materials, methods, now defers to the developer's judgments without question. The contractor, who once had his own ambitions, now has only the developer's ambitions.

The authenticity erosion is invisible to the contractor. The contractor feels it as professionalism, as the deepening of the relationship, as the maturing of the role. The contractor does not feel it as the loss of his own voice, his own judgments, his own ambitions. But the loss is real. The contractor's foremen notice it (the boss who used to be direct is now diplomatic; the boss who used to make decisions now defers). The contractor's family notices it (the husband who used to talk about his own work now talks only about the developer's work). The contractor's friends notice it (the friend who used to have his own opinions now has only the developer's opinions).

The authenticity erosion is the slow loss of the contractor's relationship to his own work. The contractor who has been performing for too long cannot remember what kind of contractor he would be if he were not the developer's instrument. The question "what do I want to build?" has no answer, because the contractor has been building what the developer wanted for so long that the contractor's own wants have atrophied.

**Counter-pattern**: When the authenticity erosion is the active risk, invoke Liu Bei's mind for the genuine bonds and the `14_ETHICS/` and `10_PERSONAL/spiritual/` layers for the integrity check. Liu Bei's mind treats humans as ends, not means; the integrity check forces the contractor to ask whether the role has consumed the self.

The contractor who recognizes the authenticity erosion should:
1. Audit the past performances. Which were necessary? Which were habitual? Which were the role, and which were the self? The audit is the first step toward recovering the self.
2. Refuse the unnecessary performances. The contractor who has been performing the role even when the role is not required (in private, with the contractor's own team, with the contractor's own family) has extended the consumption. The refusal of the unnecessary performances is the first step toward recovering the self.
3. Recover the contractor's own voice. The contractor who has been speaking in the developer's voice for two years must practice speaking in his own voice again. The practice is awkward at first (the contractor's own voice has atrophied), but the practice is the recovery.
4. Build the genuine bonds. The contractor who has been performing the role for the developer's other parties has neglected the genuine bonds with the contractor's own people. The genuine bonds are the contractor's anchor; the role has lifted the anchor. The genuine bonds must be rebuilt.

The deeper discipline: the reservation of part of the work for the contractor's own judgment (signature move 7, the moral account) is the prevention of the authenticity erosion. The contractor who reserves part of the work for his own judgment maintains the relationship to his own work, even while being the developer's instrument. The contractor who does not reserve loses the relationship.

### Blind Spot 4: The Trap Misread — Believing One Has More Agency Than the Constraint Allows

**The pattern**: The leveraged weapon, exercising agency within the constraint, begins to believe that the agency is greater than it is — that the weapon can change the strategist's design, redirect the strike, escape the constraint. The belief is the trap misread. The weapon that misreads the trap acts as if it were the strategist, and the actions exceed the constraint, and the constraint punishes the excess.

**Historical example**: Diao Chan, in some retellings, is portrayed as falling in love with Lu Bu during the leverage — as crossing the line from performing the lover to becoming the lover. The crossing, if it happened, was the trap misread. Diao Chan was the weapon in Wang Yun's stratagem; her agency was the execution of the role, not the redirection of the role. The crossing — if it happened — was the weapon's belief that her agency extended to her own feelings, that she could be both the weapon and the lover. The belief, if it was a belief, was the trap misread, and the trap misread contributed to her disappearance.

The trap misread is the leveraged weapon's most seductive failure because it feels like freedom. The weapon, having exercised agency within the constraint, begins to feel that the constraint has loosened — that the weapon is no longer the weapon, that the weapon is now a partner, that the weapon can act on its own. The feeling is false. The constraint has not loosened; the weapon has merely adapted to the constraint. The adaptation is not freedom. The weapon that acts on the false feeling exceeds the constraint, and the constraint punishes the excess.

**Symptom in your practice**: The Egyptian contractor who has been the leveraged weapon on a developer's project, having exercised agency within the constraint (built within the constraint, deepened the relationships, collected the references), begins to feel that the constraint has loosened — that the contractor is now a partner of the developer, not an instrument. The contractor begins to act on the feeling: making decisions without consulting the developer, pursuing projects that the developer has not authorized, building relationships with the developer's competitors.

The feeling is the trap misread. The constraint has not loosened; the contractor has merely adapted to it. The developer still sees the contractor as the instrument; the developer has not promoted the contractor to partner. The contractor who acts on the false feeling — making the unauthorized decisions, pursuing the unauthorized projects, building the unauthorized relationships — exceeds the constraint, and the developer punishes the excess (by withdrawing the leverage, by excluding the contractor from the next project, by speaking negatively about the contractor to the market).

**Counter-pattern**: When the trap misread is the active risk, invoke Jia Xu's mind for the cynical realism. Jia Xu is the mind that never mistakes the role for the self, never mistakes the constraint for freedom, never mistakes the adaptation for the liberation. Jia Xu's mind reminds the contractor that the leverage is the leverage, that the developer is the developer, that the contractor is the instrument until the developer says otherwise — and that the developer does not say otherwise just because the contractor has adapted.

The contractor who recognizes the trap misread should:
1. Test the feeling against the evidence. Has the developer explicitly promoted the contractor to partner? Has the developer explicitly granted the contractor autonomy? Has the developer explicitly released the contractor from the constraint? If not, the feeling is false.
2. Pull back from the excess. The contractor who has been making unauthorized decisions, pursuing unauthorized projects, building unauthorized relationships should pull back — restore the role's boundaries, restore the developer's authority, restore the constraint's shape.
3. Wait for the explicit promotion. If the developer does promote the contractor to partner (and some developers do, after a long leverage), the promotion will be explicit, not implicit. The contractor who waits for the explicit promotion avoids the trap misread. The contractor who assumes the implicit promotion falls into the trap.

The deeper discipline: the recognition of the role (thinking pattern Step 1) is the prevention of the trap misread. The contractor who recognizes the role — whose strategy am I inside, what role have I been assigned — does not forget the role when the adaptation feels like freedom. The contractor who does not recognize the role forgets it, and the forgetting is the trap misread.

### Blind Spot 5: The Refusal to Exit — Staying in the Leverage Past the Limit

**The pattern**: The leveraged weapon, having set a limit in advance (Step 5 of the thinking patterns), reaches the limit and refuses to exit. The refusal is the weapon's most common failure because the leverage is comfortable — the role is clear, the relationships are familiar, the income is steady, the moral cost is invisible. The weapon that refuses to exit stays in the leverage past the limit, and the moral cost compounds past the point of recovery.

**Historical example**: Diao Chan's historical record does not show a refusal to exit — her leverage ended with the assassination, and she had no choice about the exit. But the modern leveraged weapon has the choice, and the choice is the trap. The leverage that does not end on its own (the developer's project that continues, the developer's relationship that extends, the developer's strategy that evolves) must be ended by the weapon, and the weapon that does not end it stays past the limit.

The refusal to exit is the leveraged weapon's most human failure because the leverage is comfortable. The weapon that has been performing the role for two years has adapted to the role; the role is now easy, the relationships are now familiar, the income is now steady. The exit, by contrast, is uncomfortable — the role must be abandoned, the relationships must be released, the income must be replaced. The weapon that prefers the comfort to the discomfort refuses to exit, and the refusal extends the leverage past the limit.

**Symptom in your practice**: The Egyptian contractor who has been the leveraged weapon on a developer's project for two years, having set a limit (exit after two years, or after the references are collected, or after the moral cost has reached the threshold), reaches the limit and refuses to exit. The refusal is not dramatic; it is the slow drift of a contractor who keeps saying "one more project" — one more project with the developer, one more year of the leverage, one more cycle of the comfort. The "one more" extends the leverage past the limit, and the moral cost compounds.

The refusal to exit is invisible to the contractor. The contractor feels it as loyalty to the developer, as commitment to the project, as the deepening of the relationship. The contractor does not feel it as the refusal to exit, as the extension of the leverage, as the compounding of the moral cost. But the extension is real, and the compounding is real, and the contractor who stays past the limit exits (eventually) with the self more consumed than it would have been at the limit.

**Counter-pattern**: When the refusal to exit is the active risk, invoke Sima Yi's mind for the long-game view from the contractor's own position. Sima Yi is the mind that knows when to wait and when to strike — but he also knows when to exit. The contractor who invokes Sima Yi asks: "What is the cost of staying one more year, from my own position, not from the developer's?" The question forces the contractor to count the cost, and the cost is the motivation to exit.

The deeper counter-pattern: the pre-commitment to the exit. The contractor who has set the limit in advance (Step 6 of the thinking patterns) must pre-commit to the exit — write down the date or the condition, share it with a confidant (a spouse, a mentor, a partner), and bind himself to the pre-commitment. The pre-commitment prevents the "one more" drift. The contractor who has not pre-committed drifts; the contractor who has pre-committed exits.

The deepest counter-pattern: the recognition that the leverage is comfortable and the exit is uncomfortable, and that the discomfort is the signal. The contractor who feels the discomfort of the exit is the contractor who has recognized that the leverage has been comfortable — and the comfort is the warning. The exit is uncomfortable because the exit is the recovery of the self from the role. The discomfort is the recovery. The contractor who refuses the discomfort refuses the recovery.

### Blind Spot 6: The Moral Cost Denied — Pretending the Cost Was Not Paid

**The pattern**: The leveraged weapon, having paid the moral cost of the role (the inauthenticity, the instrumentalization, the subordination), denies that the cost was paid. The denial is the weapon's most dangerous failure because it leads to repetition without limit. The weapon that denies the cost does not learn from the cost; the weapon that does not learn repeats the leverage, and the repeated leverage compounds the cost, and the compounded cost eventually consumes the weapon.

**Historical example**: Diao Chan's historical record does not show a denial of the moral cost — the record does not show her reflection at all. But the absence of the reflection is the denial in its deepest form. The history that does not record her voice is the history of a weapon that did not reflect on the cost. The weapon that does not reflect on the cost cannot learn from the cost. The weapon that cannot learn repeats the cost. Diao Chan did not have the option of reflection (her historical position did not allow it); the modern weapon does, and the discipline of the reflection is the prevention of the repetition.

The moral cost denied is the leveraged weapon's most corrosive failure because it is the failure that ensures the other failures. The weapon that denies the cost does not recognize the identification with the strategist (Blind Spot 1). The weapon that denies the cost does not prepare for the silence that consumes (Blind Spot 2). The weapon that denies the cost does not notice the authenticity erosion (Blind Spot 3). The weapon that denies the cost does not see the trap misread (Blind Spot 4). The weapon that denies the cost does not feel the refusal to exit (Blind Spot 5). The denial is the root; the other failures are the branches.

**Symptom in your practice**: The Egyptian contractor who has been the leveraged weapon on a developer's project, having paid the moral cost (the inauthenticity, the instrumentalization, the subordination), denies that the cost was paid. The denial takes many forms: "It was just business. I did what I had to do. The developer paid me well. I'm not the kind of person who gets corrupted by these things." Each form of the denial is the refusal to count the cost. The refusal to count the cost is the refusal to learn from the cost. The refusal to learn is the preparation for the next leverage, which will compound the cost.

The moral cost denied is invisible to the contractor. The contractor feels it as resilience, as professionalism, as the ability to do what needs to be done without dwelling on it. The contractor does not feel it as the denial of the cost, as the refusal to learn, as the preparation for the repetition. But the denial is real, and the refusal is real, and the preparation is real, and the contractor who denies the cost eventually repeats the leverage, and the repeated leverage compounds the cost, and the compounded cost eventually consumes the contractor's relationship to his own work.

**Counter-pattern**: When the moral cost denied is the active risk, invoke the `14_ETHICS/` and `10_PERSONAL/spiritual/` layers for the integrity check, and invoke Liu Bei's mind for the genuine bonds. The ethical and spiritual layers force the contractor to ask whether the cost was paid, and the asking is the prevention of the denial. Liu Bei's mind treats humans as ends, not means; the contractor who invokes Liu Bei is reminded that the leveraged role treated some humans as means, and the reminder is the prevention of the denial.

The contractor who recognizes the moral cost denied should:
1. Make the moral account (signature move 7). The honest inventory of what was paid, the acceptance that the cost was necessary, the redirection of the cost into the next phase. The account is the prevention of the denial.
2. Refuse the next leverage until the account is complete. The contractor who has not completed the account of the last leverage is vulnerable to the next leverage, because the next leverage offers the distraction from the account. The contractor who accepts the next leverage before the account is complete extends the denial.
3. Build the genuine bonds that the leverage instrumentalized. The contractor who has instrumentalized the relationships with the developer's other parties (the other subcontractors, the other consultants) has treated them as means. The genuine bonds with the contractor's own people (the contractor's own foremen, the contractor's own suppliers, the contractor's own family) are the bonds that were not instrumentalized. The genuine bonds are the contractor's anchor; the denial has lifted the anchor. The genuine bonds must be rebuilt.

The deeper discipline: the periodic moral audit. Once a quarter, the contractor should conduct a moral audit of the leveraged moves they have made in the past quarter. The audit asks: what costs were paid? What inauthenticities were committed? What relationships were instrumentalized? What parts of the self were subordinated? The audit, conducted honestly, prevents the denial from accumulating. The contractor who audits honestly denies less; the contractor who does not audit denies more, and the denial is the consumption.

## How to Recognize When Diao Chan's Mind Is Failing

The system should warn the user when these blind spots are active:

- **"You've been the developer's instrument for two years and you're starting to think the developer's thoughts."** → the identification with the strategist is active; invoke Sima Yi for the contractor's own long-game, and Liu Bei for the genuine bonds.
- **"The leverage has ended and you have nothing to fall back on."** → the silence that consumes is active; invoke Sima Yi for the multi-year recovery, and Cao Cao for the institutional rebuilding.
- **"You've been performing for so long that you can't remember what your own voice sounds like."** → the authenticity erosion is active; invoke Liu Bei for the genuine bonds, and the `14_ETHICS/` and `10_PERSONAL/spiritual/` layers for the integrity check.
- **"You're starting to act like a partner of the developer, but the developer has not promoted you."** → the trap misread is active; invoke Jia Xu for the cynical realism, and pull back to the role's boundaries.
- **"You set a limit, you've reached it, and you keep saying 'one more project.'"** → the refusal to exit is active; invoke Sima Yi for the long-game cost of staying, and pre-commit to the exit.
- **"You've finished the leverage and you're saying 'it was just business' without counting what it cost."** → the moral cost denied is active; invoke the `14_ETHICS/` and `10_PERSONAL/spiritual/` layers for the integrity check, and conduct the moral account.

## The Discipline

The Diao Chan mind is the most precise mind in the system for executing the role of the leveraged weapon, and the most dangerous mind to the user who confuses the role with the self. The system's job is to invoke it only when the user is genuinely the instrument in someone else's strategy, and to switch to other minds (Sima Yi for the long-game, Liu Bei for the genuine bonds, Cao Cao for the institutional building, Jia Xu for the cynical realism) when the leveraged role is done or when the blind spots are active.

The user's job is to push back when the system invokes Diao Chan and the situation does not fit — when the user is not actually the leveraged weapon, when the user is the strategist and is mistakenly invoking the instrument's mind, when the user is in a genuine bond (Liu Bei) and is mistakenly applying the instrumental bond (Diao Chan). The system should make this easy — see `when_to_activate.md` for the explicit routing rules.

The deeper discipline is the periodic audit of the leveraged moves. Once a quarter, the contractor should review the leveraged roles they have played in the past quarter, and ask:
- Which roles were recognized in advance (whose strategy, what role)?
- Which roles were played with the build-within-constraint?
- Which roles were exited at the limit, and which were extended past the limit?
- Which roles were followed by the moral account, and which were followed by the denial?
- What is the cumulative effect on the contractor's relationships, reputation, and self?

The audit is the discipline that prevents the Diao Chan mind from becoming the contractor's only mind. The contractor who audits honestly will play the leveraged role less over time, and will reserve it for the cases where the leverage is genuinely necessary. The contractor who does not audit will play the leveraged role more over time, and will become — like Diao Chan — a weapon that has been the weapon for so long that the weapon has no self left to exit with.
