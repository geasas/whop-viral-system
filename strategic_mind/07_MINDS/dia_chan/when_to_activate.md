# Diao Chan — When to Activate

> **Layer**: 07_MINDS/dia_chan/
> **File**: when_to_activate
> **Subject**: The explicit routing rules for when to invoke the Diao Chan mind
> **Mention frequency in manga**: 38 times across 635 chapters, concentrated in the Dong Zhuo arc

---

## The Premise

Diao Chan's mind is not universal. It applies to specific situations and fails in others. This file defines the routing rules so the system knows when to invoke this mind and when to switch to another.

The rules are **triggered by situation type**, not by topic. The same topic (a difficult project, a partnership, a negotiation) may invoke Diao Chan or any other mind depending on the strategic situation — specifically, on whether the user is the strategist or the instrument, whether the user has accepted the leveraged role, whether the moral cost is recognized, and whether the build-within-constraint is in progress.

Diao Chan is the most precise mind in the system for executing the role of the leveraged weapon, and the most dangerous mind to the user who confuses the role with the self. The activation rules are deliberately specific — Diao Chan should be invoked only when the user is genuinely the instrument in someone else's strategy, has accepted the placement with awareness, and is committed to building within the constraint and exiting at the limit. In all other situations, other minds should be tried first.

## Trigger Conditions — When to ACTIVATE Diao Chan

### Trigger 1: The user has been placed in someone else's strategy and has accepted the placement

The first and most fundamental trigger: Diao Chan's mind is invoked when the user has been placed — by a developer, a bank, a larger contractor, a government agency — in someone else's strategy, and has accepted the placement. The recognition of the placement is the precondition. Without the recognition, there is no leveraged role; there is only confusion.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "I have been chosen for this project not because I am the best contractor, but because I am useful to the developer's strategy. The developer needs a reliable executor for a project that serves the developer's portfolio, not my own. I have accepted the project on terms I did not negotiate, in service of a goal that is not entirely my own."
- "I have been invited into this partnership not as an equal, but as the technical partner who will execute the bank's vision for the loan portfolio. The bank needs my credibility to secure the loan; the bank does not need my strategic judgment. I have accepted the partnership."
- "I have been asked to front for a larger contractor on a bid I would not win on my own. The larger contractor needs my local presence to qualify; the larger contractor does not need my independence. I have accepted the fronting role."

**Example**: An Egyptian electrical contractor has been awarded a major project by a developer who is building a large residential complex. The contractor did not bid competitively; the developer chose the contractor because the developer needs a contractor who will execute the developer's design without questioning it, who will accept the developer's payment terms without negotiating, who will be present on the developer's project for the next two years. The contractor recognizes that the developer has placed the contractor in the developer's strategy — the contractor is the developer's chosen instrument, not the developer's chosen partner. The contractor has accepted the placement (the project is necessary for the contractor's cash flow, and the contractor has no better option at this scale). → Diao Chan's mind: the leveraged role is recognized; the build-within-constraint can begin.

**Caution**: The recognition must be honest. The contractor who is in fact a partner of the developer (the developer has promoted the contractor to co-strategist, has shared the project's strategic decisions, has granted the contractor autonomy) is not the leveraged weapon; the contractor is the partner. The recognition of the difference is the first step. The contractor who mistakenly invokes Diao Chan (treating a partnership as a leverage) undermines the partnership. The contractor who mistakenly invokes a partnership mind (treating a leverage as a partnership) is consumed by the leverage. The distinction must be made carefully.

### Trigger 2: The user is being used as a tool in someone else's negotiation and needs to recognize the role

The second trigger is defensive, not offensive. Diao Chan's mind is invoked not only to play the leveraged role, but also to recognize and respond to the role when the user is being played as the instrument in someone else's negotiation.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "I have noticed that a developer has been courting me for a project that I would not normally accept. The terms are favorable, the timeline is generous, the payment is prompt. The developer's interest in me seems disproportionate to the project. Is the developer positioning me as the leveraged weapon in a larger strategy — perhaps to displace a competitor, perhaps to demonstrate capability to a larger client, perhaps to qualify for a larger loan?"
- "I have noticed that a bank has been offering me favorable terms on a loan that I did not aggressively seek. The bank's enthusiasm seems disproportionate. Is the bank positioning me as the credible face on a loan that the bank needs to make for the bank's own portfolio reasons?"
- "I have noticed that a larger contractor has been inviting me to participate in bids that I would not be considered for on my own. The larger contractor's invitations seem generous. Is the larger contractor positioning me as the local front for a bid that the larger contractor needs my presence to qualify?"

**Example**: An Egyptian electrical contractor has been receiving unusually favorable invitations from a larger contractor to participate in bids for government projects. The larger contractor's invitations include generous subcontracts, the larger contractor's name on the bid documents, and the larger contractor's references backing the contractor's qualifications. The contractor, who would not be considered for these government projects on his own, is flattered by the invitations. → Diao Chan's mind: the larger contractor may be positioning the contractor as the local front. The recognition triggers the response: (1) accept the role with awareness (if the role serves the contractor's build-within-constraint), (2) build within the constraint (references, capacity, relationships with the government's project managers, that survive the leverage), (3) prepare the exit (the moment when the contractor can bid on the government projects independently), (4) refuse the role (if the role does not serve the contractor's build-within-constraint, and the moral cost is not worth the gain).

**Caution**: The defensive invocation of Diao Chan does not require acceptance of the role. The recognition of the role is the precondition; the response to the role is the contractor's choice. The contractor who recognizes the role may accept it (with awareness, with build, with exit plan), may refuse it (if the role does not serve the contractor's interest), or may negotiate it (converting the role from a pure leverage to a more equal partnership). The recognition is the trigger; the response is the contractor's.

### Trigger 3: The leveraged role is in progress and the build-within-constraint is needed

The third trigger is the active-leverage trigger. Diao Chan's mind is invoked when the leveraged role is in progress — the contractor has accepted the placement, has begun the role, and is now in the middle of the leverage. The build-within-constraint is the discipline that the Diao Chan mind provides during the active leverage.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "I have been the developer's chosen instrument on this project for six months. The role is clear, the work is steady, the income is reliable. But I have not been building within the constraint — I have not collected the references, I have not built the capacity, I have not deepened the relationships with the developer's other parties. I need to begin the build now, before the leverage ends and the silence arrives."
- "I have been the bank's technical partner on this loan portfolio for a year. The role is comfortable, the income is steady, the bank is satisfied. But I have not been building within the constraint — I have not developed the relationships with the bank's other clients, I have not built the references that the bank's other clients would respect, I have not built the capacity to operate at this scale without the bank's partnership. I need to begin the build now."

**Example**: An Egyptian electrical contractor has been the developer's chosen instrument on a major residential project for six months. The work has been steady, the income has been reliable, the developer has been satisfied with the contractor's execution. But the contractor has not been building within the constraint — the references have not been collected in a form that would survive the project, the relationships with the developer's other subcontractors have not been deepened beyond the project's requirements, the contractor's own team has not been trained on the project's systems in a way that would survive the project. → Diao Chan's mind: the build-within-constraint must begin now. The contractor should: (1) collect the references deliberately — request letters of reference, document the milestones, photograph the completed work; (2) deepen the relationships deliberately — invite the developer's other subcontractors to lunches, share information that builds mutual respect, identify the subcontractors who would be the contractor's own subcontractors after the leverage; (3) train the team deliberately — document the systems, qualify the suppliers, develop the processes, in forms that survive the project.

**Caution**: The build-within-constraint must be invisible to the developer. The contractor who builds visibly (advertising the build to the developer, signaling the intention to use the developer's project to build the contractor's own foundation) loses the leverage before the build is complete. The contractor who builds invisibly (the build is consistent with the role, not visible against it) maintains the leverage while completing the build. The invisibility is the discipline.

### Trigger 4: The leveraged role is ending and the silence is approaching

The fourth trigger is the exit trigger. Diao Chan's mind is invoked when the leveraged role is ending — the project is finishing, the developer's strategy is shifting, the bank's portfolio is reorganizing — and the silence is approaching. The preparation for the silence is the discipline that the Diao Chan mind provides at the end of the leverage.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "The developer's project is finishing in three months. The developer has not offered a next project. The silence is approaching. I need to prepare for the silence — complete the build, plan the exit, prepare the next phase."
- "The bank's loan portfolio is being reorganized. My role as the technical partner is ending. The bank has not invited me into the reorganized portfolio. The silence is approaching. I need to prepare for the silence."
- "The larger contractor's bid strategy is shifting. The bids I have been fronting are being redirected to a different local partner. My role as the local front is ending. The silence is approaching."

**Example**: An Egyptian electrical contractor has been the developer's chosen instrument on a major residential project for two years. The project is finishing in three months. The developer has not offered a next project; the developer has indicated that the next project will go to a different contractor (a contractor who is a personal friend of the developer's chief engineer). The silence is approaching. → Diao Chan's mind: the preparation for the silence must begin now. The contractor should: (1) complete the build — collect the final references, document the final milestones, photograph the completed project; (2) plan the exit — set the date, set the form (transition to the contractor's own projects, retreat to recover and reflect, exit the leverage entirely), prepare the clean break; (3) prepare the next phase — identify the contractor's own next projects, rebuild the contractor's own client base, restore the contractor's own team's attention to the contractor's own work.

**Caution**: The exit must be a clean break. The contractor who lingers in the developer's camp after the project ends (continuing to perform the role, continuing to depend on the developer's network, continuing to half-perform the leveraged role) extends the leverage past the limit and compounds the moral cost. The clean break is the discipline; the lingering is the failure.

### Trigger 5: The user is considering accepting a leveraged role and needs to assess the cost

The fifth trigger is the pre-acceptance trigger. Diao Chan's mind is invoked before the leveraged role is accepted — when the contractor is considering a project, a partnership, a position that would place the contractor in someone else's strategy, and needs to assess the cost before accepting.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "A developer has offered me a major project. The terms are favorable, but the role is clear: I would be the developer's instrument, executing the developer's design without strategic input. The leverage would last two years. I need to assess the cost before accepting."
- "A bank has offered me a technical partnership on a loan portfolio. The terms are generous, but the role is clear: I would be the bank's credible face, executing the bank's vision without strategic input. The leverage would last three years. I need to assess the cost before accepting."
- "A larger contractor has offered me a subcontracting role on a series of government bids. The terms are profitable, but the role is clear: I would be the local front, executing the larger contractor's strategy without strategic input. The leverage would last as long as the bids continue. I need to assess the cost before accepting."

**Example**: An Egyptian electrical contractor has been offered a major project by a developer. The terms are favorable — a 20% margin, a 24-month timeline, prompt payment. But the role is clear: the contractor would be the developer's instrument, executing the developer's design without strategic input, present on the developer's project for the next two years, subordinated to the developer's chief engineer. → Diao Chan's mind: the pre-acceptance assessment must consider: (1) the recognition — the contractor has been placed in the developer's strategy, and the role is the instrument; (2) the build-within-constraint — can the contractor build within the constraint (references, capacity, relationships) that would survive the leverage? (3) the moral cost — what inauthenticities would the contractor commit, what relationships would be instrumentalized, what parts of the self would be subordinated? (4) the exit — what is the limit, what is the form of the exit, what is the pre-commitment? (5) the goal — does the goal (the contractor's ascent, the firm's survival, the foundation for independence) justify the cost?

**Caution**: The pre-acceptance assessment must be honest. The contractor who assesses the cost dishonestly (denying the cost, exaggerating the build, underestimating the moral cost) accepts the leverage without awareness and is consumed by it. The contractor who assesses the cost honestly may still accept the leverage (if the goal justifies the cost), but the acceptance is conscious. The honest assessment is the contractor's protection against the consumption.

### Trigger 6: The user has just finished a leveraged project and is facing the silence

The sixth trigger is the post-leverage trigger. Diao Chan's mind is invoked when the leveraged role has just ended — the project is delivered, the partnership is concluded, the bid is won or lost — and the contractor is facing the silence. The moral account is the discipline that the Diao Chan mind provides at the end of the leverage.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "The developer's project was delivered last month. The developer has paid the final invoice. The developer's calls have stopped. I am facing the silence. I need to make the moral account before the silence consumes me."
- "The bank's loan portfolio was reorganized last quarter. My role as the technical partner ended. The bank's calls have stopped. I am facing the silence. I need to make the moral account."
- "The larger contractor's bid strategy shifted last month. My role as the local front ended. The larger contractor's calls have stopped. I am facing the silence. I need to make the moral account."

**Example**: An Egyptian electrical contractor delivered the developer's major residential project last month. The developer paid the final invoice. The developer's calls have stopped — no next project, no ongoing relationship, no further leverage. The contractor is facing the silence. The contractor's team, trained on the developer's project, has no project to apply the training to. The contractor's references, in the developer's name, are no longer the contractor's. The contractor's relationships, with the developer's other subcontractors, have no reason to continue. → Diao Chan's mind: the moral account must be made now. The contractor should: (1) make the honest inventory — what inauthenticities were committed, what relationships were instrumentalized, what parts of the self were subordinated; (2) accept the cost — the cost was necessary (given the goal of the contractor's ascent), and it cannot be undone; (3) redirect the cost — the energy spent in inauthenticity is now spent in authenticity, the relationships instrumentalized are now released or rebuilt as genuine, the parts of the self subordinated are now recovered. (4) Refuse the next leverage until the account is complete — the contractor who has not completed the account is vulnerable to the next leverage; the next leverage offers the distraction from the account.

**Caution**: The moral account must be honest. The contractor who makes the account dishonestly (denying the cost, minimizing the inauthenticities, refusing to count) does not recover from the cost; the cost continues to accumulate. The contractor who makes the account honestly recovers — slowly, over months, but the recovery is real. The honest account is the contractor's protection against the consumption.

## Inhibition Conditions — When NOT to Activate Diao Chan

### Inhibitor 1: The user is the strategist, not the instrument

The first and most important inhibitor: if the user is the strategist in the situation — the one designing the strategy, placing the instruments, setting the terms — Diao Chan should not be invoked. Diao Chan is the mind of the instrument, not the mind of the strategist. The strategist should invoke Wang Yun (for the personal-leverage trap), Sima Yi (for the long-game strategy), Cao Cao (for the institutional synthesis), or another strategist mind.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I am the developer on this project. I have chosen the contractor, set the terms, designed the strategy. The contractor is my instrument, not the other way around. I should not invoke Diao Chan; I should invoke Wang Yun (if I am using personal leverage) or Sima Yi (if I am playing the long game)."
- "I am the bank on this loan portfolio. I have chosen the technical partner, set the terms, designed the strategy. The technical partner is my instrument. I should not invoke Diao Chan; I should invoke the strategist mind appropriate to my strategy."

**Exception**: If the contractor is the strategist in one situation and the instrument in another (e.g., the contractor is the strategist in his own firm's projects, but the instrument in a larger developer's project), the two roles must be kept distinct. The contractor invokes Diao Chan for the leveraged role, and the strategist mind for the strategist role. The two minds must not be confused.

### Inhibitor 2: The user is in a genuine bond, not a leveraged role

The second inhibitor: if the user is in a genuine bond — a partnership, a friendship, a client relationship that is not instrumental — Diao Chan should not be invoked. The genuine bond is the province of Liu Bei, not Diao Chan. The contractor who mistakenly applies Diao Chan's instrumental patterns to a genuine bond destroys the bond.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I have a genuine partnership with this developer. We have been working together for ten years, on terms we have negotiated honestly, on projects we have chosen together, in service of goals we share. I am not the developer's instrument; I am the developer's partner. I should not invoke Diao Chan; I should invoke Liu Bei."
- "I have a genuine friendship with this foreman. We have worked together for fifteen years, on the basis of mutual respect, on terms we have agreed openly. The foreman is not my instrument, and I am not the foreman's instrument. I should not invoke Diao Chan; I should invoke Liu Bei."

**Exception**: If the user is in a genuine bond and a leveraged role simultaneously (e.g., a genuine partnership with one developer, a leveraged role with another), the two must be kept distinct. The user invokes Liu Bei for the genuine bond, and Diao Chan for the leveraged role. The two minds must not be confused — the instrumental patterns applied to the genuine bond destroy the bond, and the genuine patterns applied to the leveraged role weaken the leverage.

### Inhibitor 3: The user has not recognized the role, and is acting without awareness

The third inhibitor: if the user has not recognized the leveraged role — has not run Step 1 of the thinking patterns (whose strategy am I inside, what role have I been assigned) — Diao Chan should not be invoked in its full form. The user who has not recognized the role is not the leveraged weapon with awareness; the user is the leveraged weapon without awareness, and the lack of awareness is the consumption.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I am not sure whose strategy I am inside. I am not sure what role I have been assigned. I am not sure whether I am the strategist or the instrument in this situation. I should not invoke Diao Chan's full patterns; I should first run the recognition step (Step 1 of the thinking patterns), and only then invoke Diao Chan if the recognition confirms the leveraged role."

**Exception**: If the user is unsure whether they are the leveraged weapon, the system should invoke Diao Chan's defensive recognition pattern (Trigger 2) to help the user identify the role. The defensive recognition does not require the full commitment to the leveraged role; it requires only the recognition of the possibility of the role, and the response (accept, refuse, negotiate) is the user's choice.

### Inhibitor 4: The leveraged role has been played recently, and the moral cost is compounding

The fourth inhibitor: if the user has played a leveraged role recently (within the past 6-12 months), and the moral cost is compounding, Diao Chan should not be invoked again until the moral account of the previous leverage has been completed. The repeated leverage without account is the consumption.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I played a leveraged role for a developer two years ago. I exited the leverage last year. I have not made the moral account. Now I am being offered another leveraged role. I should not invoke Diao Chan for the new leverage; I should first complete the moral account of the previous leverage. The new leverage without the account is the compounding of the cost."

**Exception**: If the goal of the new leverage is survival (the firm will fail without the new leverage), the recent-use inhibitor is overridden. Survival justifies the compounding of the moral cost. But the override should be conscious — the contractor who accepts the new leverage for survival should also commit to the moral account of both leverages (the previous and the new) as soon as the survival is secured.

### Inhibitor 5: The user is being asked to be the leveraged weapon in an unethical strategy

The fifth inhibitor: if the user has been asked to be the leveraged weapon in an unethical strategy — a strategy that involves deception of the user's own clients, fraud on the user's own market, harm to the user's own community — Diao Chan should not be invoked. The leveraged weapon's role in an unethical strategy corrupts the weapon more deeply than the leveraged role in an ethical (or morally neutral) strategy.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "The larger contractor has asked me to front a bid that I know to be a sham — the bid is designed to win a government contract that the larger contractor will not deliver on, and my name is being used to provide the local credibility that the bid requires. The strategy is unethical (it involves fraud on the government), and my role as the front is the leveraged weapon in the unethical strategy. I should not invoke Diao Chan; I should refuse the role."

**Exception**: There is no exception. The leveraged weapon's role in an unethical strategy is corruption, period. The contractor who is offered the role should refuse, regardless of the financial gain. The refusal is the protection of the contractor's soul, which is worth more than the financial gain.

## The Combination Cases

Some situations warrant Diao Chan combined with another mind:

### Diao Chan + Sima Yi: Leveraged role, then long-game consolidation

The most important combination. Use Diao Chan to execute the leveraged role with awareness and build within the constraint; use Sima Yi to consolidate the build into the multi-year independence that follows the leverage. The two minds are complementary — Diao Chan for the leverage, Sima Yi for the holding.

**Application**: Use Diao Chan to play the leveraged role on the developer's project — recognize the role, build within the constraint, prepare the exit. Then switch to Sima Yi for the multi-year consolidation of the build — the deepening of the references into a client base, the conversion of the capacity into institutional systems, the development of the relationships into a network that the contractor owns.

### Diao Chan + Liu Bei: Leveraged role, with genuine bonds preserved

Use Diao Chan to play the leveraged role with the developer's other parties; use Liu Bei to preserve the genuine bonds with the contractor's own people. The combination is delicate — the leveraged role's instrumental patterns must not leak into the genuine bonds, or the genuine bonds are corrupted. The two minds must be kept strictly separate.

**Application**: Use Diao Chan to play the leveraged role with the developer, the developer's chief engineer, the developer's other subcontractors. Use Liu Bei to maintain the genuine bonds with the contractor's own foremen, the contractor's own suppliers, the contractor's own family. The compartmentalization is the discipline — the leveraged role is in the developer's camp; the genuine bonds are in the contractor's own. The two do not mix.

### Diao Chan + Cao Cao: Leveraged role, with institutional capacity built

Use Diao Chan to play the leveraged role; use Cao Cao to build the institutional capacity that the leveraged role demands but does not provide. The combination is structural — the leveraged role wins the moment (the developer's project, the bank's portfolio, the larger contractor's bid); the institutional building holds the years (the systems, the processes, the suppliers that survive the leverage).

**Application**: Use Diao Chan to play the leveraged role on the developer's project. Use Cao Cao to build the institutional capacity — the project management systems, the financial management systems, the supplier management systems — that the developer's project demands but that the contractor's firm did not previously have. The institutional capacity survives the leverage; the leveraged role does not. The combination is the difference between a leverage that exits with nothing and a leverage that exits with institutional capacity.

### Diao Chan + Wang Yun: Leveraged weapon in a personal-leverage trap

The most delicate combination. Use Wang Yun to design the personal-leverage trap; use Diao Chan to execute the trap as the leveraged weapon within it. The combination is for situations where the contractor is the instrument in another contractor's personal-leverage trap (e.g., the contractor is the chosen foreman in a developer's trap against a competitor, or the contractor is the credible face in a bank's trap against another bank's client).

**Application**: A developer has chosen the contractor as the leveraged weapon in a trap against a competitor — the developer wants the contractor to be present on a project that the competitor was counting on, in order to demonstrate that the developer has an alternative to the competitor. The contractor recognizes the role (Diao Chan): the contractor is the leveraged weapon in the developer's personal-leverage trap (Wang Yun). The contractor should: (1) invoke Wang Yun to understand the trap the developer has designed (what personal bond in the competitor's camp is being targeted, what trigger is being prepared, what kill strike is being planned); (2) invoke Diao Chan to execute the contractor's role in the trap (play the credible alternative, perform the role with skill, build within the constraint, prepare the exit). The combination is the contractor's awareness of being the weapon in another's trap, and the contractor's use of the awareness to play the weapon without being consumed.

## The Activation Test

Before invoking Diao Chan, the system asks:

1. **Is the user the instrument in someone else's strategy?** If yes (the user has been placed by a developer, a bank, a larger contractor, a government agency in someone else's plan), Diao Chan may be invoked. If no (the user is the strategist, not the instrument), Diao Chan should not be invoked; the strategist mind should be invoked.
2. **Has the user recognized the role?** If yes (the user has run Step 1 of the thinking patterns and identified whose strategy they are inside and what role they have been assigned), Diao Chan may be invoked in its full form. If no, the system should first invoke Diao Chan's defensive recognition pattern to help the user identify the role.
3. **Is the user committed to the build-within-constraint?** If yes (the user is collecting references, building capacity, deepening relationships that survive the leverage), Diao Chan may be invoked. If no (the user is playing the role without building), the system should warn the user that the silence that consumes is approaching.
4. **Has the user set a limit and pre-committed to the exit?** If yes (the user has identified the limit, the form of the exit, the pre-commitment), Diao Chan may be invoked. If no, the system should warn the user that the refusal to exit is the active risk.
5. **Has the user made the moral account of previous leverages?** If yes (the user has completed the honest inventory, the acceptance, and the redirection of previous leveraged roles), Diao Chan may be invoked for the new leverage. If no, the system should warn the user that the moral cost is compounding.
6. **Is the strategy ethical?** If yes (the leveraged role does not involve deception of the user's own clients, fraud on the user's own market, harm to the user's own community), Diao Chan may be invoked. If no, the system should refuse to invoke Diao Chan; the user should refuse the role.

If all six tests pass (or the failed tests are appropriately overridden by survival urgency), Diao Chan is invoked. If any test fails, another mind is invoked. The user can override by explicitly asking for Diao Chan's perspective — but the system should warn the user of the failed test and the expected cost.

## The Self-Correction

Diao Chan's mind is prone to over-extension. The leveraged role is comfortable, the income is steady, the role is clear. The user who has played the leveraged role once is tempted to play it again. The system must self-correct.

The self-correction rules:

- **The system flags each leveraged role in a log.** Each role is recorded, with the date, the strategist, the role assigned, the build completed, the moral account made. The log is reviewed quarterly.
- **The system warns the user when the log shows more than 2 leveraged roles in 24 months.** The warning is: "You have played the leveraged role twice in the past two years. The cumulative moral cost is real. Consider whether the next role could be played with another mind, or whether the goal justifies the cost."
- **The system warns the user when a leveraged role has been played without a build-within-constraint.** The warning is: "You played the leveraged role for two years and exited without a build. The silence that consumes is the active risk. Switch to Sima Yi for the multi-year recovery."
- **The system warns the user when a leveraged role has been played past the limit.** The warning is: "You set a limit, you reached it, and you extended the leverage. The moral cost is compounding. Invoke the moral account and prepare the exit."
- **The system warns the user when the identification with the strategist is active.** The warning is: "You have been the developer's instrument for two years, and you are starting to think the developer's thoughts. Invoke Sima Yi for your own long-game, and Liu Bei for your own genuine bonds."

The self-correction is built into the activation rule: the leveraged role is not a default; it is a phase that is entered consciously, executed with awareness, and exited at the limit. The phase that does not end becomes the consumption.

## The Periodic Audit

Once a quarter, the contractor should conduct a leveraged-role audit:

1. **List the leveraged roles played in the past quarter.** For each: the strategist, the role assigned, the build completed, the moral account made, the exit completed or pending.
2. **Assess each role.** Was the role recognized in advance? Was the build real? Was the exit at the limit? Was the moral account honest? Would the role be played again?
3. **Identify patterns.** Are the roles becoming more frequent? Are the builds becoming less complete? Are the exits becoming less clean? Are the moral accounts becoming less honest?
4. **Adjust.** If the audit shows the roles are becoming more frequent or less well-executed, reduce the frequency. If the audit shows the roles are well-recognized, well-built, well-exited, and well-accounted, continue. If the audit shows the roles are corrupting the contractor's relationships or self, stop.

The audit is the discipline that prevents the Diao Chan mind from becoming the contractor's only mind. The contractor who audits honestly will play the leveraged role less over time, and will reserve it for the rare cases where the goal justifies the cost. The contractor who does not audit will play the leveraged role more over time, and will become — like Diao Chan — an instrument that has been the instrument for so long that the instrument has no self left to exit with.
