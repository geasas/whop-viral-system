# Li Ru — When to Activate

> **Layer**: 07_MINDS/li_ru/
> **File**: when_to_activate
> **Subject**: The explicit routing rules for when to invoke the Li Ru mind

---

## The Premise

Li Ru's mind is the most morally compromised mind in the system. It is not invoked for ordinary decisions. It is invoked when the user is inside a situation that the other minds cannot reach — the situation where the user must advise a destructive party, give the harshest effective recommendation, and accept the moral and reputational cost of giving it. The mind is the cold enabler's mind, and the cold enabler is the strategist who has chosen to function inside a destructive situation rather than exit it.

The rules are **triggered by situation structure**, not by topic. The same topic (a difficult client relationship) may invoke Li Ru, Jia Xu, Liu Bei, or Cao Cao depending on the structure. Li Ru is invoked when the structure involves enablement of a destructive party whose interests the user must serve, when the user cannot exit, and when the harsh recommendation is the only effective option. He is not invoked when the user can exit (Jia Xu), can refuse (Liu Bei), can redirect (Cao Cao), or is the principal rather than the advisor.

Li Ru is the enablement mind. He is invoked when the user's primary question is not "how do I serve my own interest?" but "how do I serve my master's (or client's, or partner's, or family member's) actual interest, knowing the service will harm others and possibly harm me?" When the question is enablement, Li Ru is the primary mind. When the question is exit, he is the wrong mind. The system must distinguish carefully, because the wrong invocation leads the user into moral catastrophe.

Li Ru's mind should also be invoked sparingly. The user who invokes Li Ru too often is the user who has slid from cold enabler to true believer (blind spot 6). The user who invokes Li Ru honestly invokes him only when no other mind can reach the situation, and then accepts the moral cost of the invocation. The user who invokes Li Ru casually has not understood the cost.

## Trigger Conditions — When to ACTIVATE Li Ru

### Trigger 1: The user must advise a destructive party whose interests the user has committed to serve

If the user has already entered a relationship (taken a client's money, signed a partnership agreement, married into a family business, accepted a position) with a party whose decisions are destructive, and the user's role is to advise that party, Li Ru's mind applies. The user has committed to serve; the question is how to serve. The other minds (Jia Xu's exit, Liu Bei's refusal) are not available without breaking the commitment. Li Ru's mind is the mind for the committed advisor.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "I have signed an exclusive contract with a client whose business practices are destructive, and I cannot exit the contract without breach."
- "I have married into a family whose business I am now advising, and the business's practices are destructive."
- "I have accepted a position as chief advisor to a partner whose decisions are destroying the partnership, and I cannot resign without losing my equity."
- "I have been the principal contractor for a developer whose projects harm the communities they are built in, and I have already taken his advances for the next project."

**Example**: "I have signed an exclusive contract with a client whose business practices are destructive." → Li Ru's mind: the commitment to serve is real (the contract binds the user). The question is not whether to serve (the contract answers that) but how to serve. The user gives the most effective advice for the client's actual interest, knowing the advice will enable the destructive practices, and accepts the moral weight. The alternative (breach the contract, refuse to advise) is the Liu Bei path, and is available only if the user is willing to pay the cost of breach. If the user is not willing, Li Ru is the only mind that can function inside the commitment.

### Trigger 2: The user must give the harshest effective recommendation, and no other advisor will name it

If the user is in a strategic discussion where the other advisors (lawyer, accountant, partner's friend, family member's spouse) are all giving soft advice (negotiate, delay, restructure, give it more time), and the situation's severity requires the harsh option (terminate, file, dissolve, fire), Li Ru's mind applies. The user is the only one who can name the harsh option, and the harsh option is the only option that matches the severity.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "The client's project is failing and the other advisors are all saying 'negotiate,' but the project cannot be saved by negotiation."
- "The partnership is collapsing and the other partners are all saying 'give it more time,' but the partnership cannot be saved by time."
- "The family business is being drained by a family member and the other family members are all saying 'confront him gently,' but the family member will not respond to gentle confrontation."
- "The client's project has a subcontractor whose performance is dangerous and the other advisors are saying 'document and warn,' but the subcontractor's performance cannot be made safe by documentation."

**Example**: "The client's project is failing and the other advisors are all saying 'negotiate.'" → Li Ru's mind: the negotiation is the soft option, and the soft option does not match the severity of the failure. The harsh option is to terminate the project, accept the loss, and protect the client's remaining capital. The user is the only one who can name the harsh option, because the other advisors' reputations depend on not naming it (the lawyer does not want to be the one who recommended termination; the accountant does not want to be the one who recommended the write-off). The user names the harsh option, accepts the moral weight of the naming, and lets the client choose.

### Trigger 3: The user must decide whether to continue serving a party whose ethics have crossed the line from aggressive to unethical

If the user is in a relationship with a party whose practices have crossed the line (from aggressive business to unethical conduct), and the user must decide whether to continue serving, Li Ru's mind applies for the option of continuing to serve. The other minds (Jia Xu's exit, Liu Bei's refusal, the ethics layer's red line) apply for the option of stopping. Li Ru's mind is the mind for the option of continuing — the option that accepts the moral cost of the continued service.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "My client's practices have crossed the line from aggressive to unethical, and I must decide whether to continue serving him."
- "My partner's decisions have crossed the line, and I must decide whether to continue the partnership."
- "My family member's business practices have crossed the line, and I must decide whether to continue advising him."
- "The subcontractor my client uses has crossed the line (worker exploitation, safety violations), and I must decide whether to continue working on the project that uses the subcontractor."

**Example**: "My client's practices have crossed the line from aggressive to unethical." → Li Ru's mind: the user must first invoke `14_ETHICS/red_lines.md` to determine whether the crossed line is a red line (the conduct harms innocents in a way that requires refusal regardless of cost). If the crossed line is a red line, Li Ru's mind does not apply — the user must refuse. If the crossed line is not a red line (the conduct is unethical but does not require refusal — the conduct is the client's choice, not the user's, and the user's service does not directly enable the conduct), Li Ru's mind applies for the option of continuing to serve. The user continues to serve, accepts the moral weight, and gives the harsh recommendations that maximize the client's interest within the ethical constraint (the user does not recommend the unethical conduct; the user recommends the best ethical options the client has).

### Trigger 4: The user is in a situation where loyalty to a master conflicts with self-preservation, and the user must choose loyalty

If the user is in a situation where the master's (client's, partner's, family member's) trajectory is terminal, and the user must choose between loyalty (stay and go down with the master) and survival (switch or exit), Li Ru's mind applies for the option of loyalty. The other minds (Jia Xu's switch, Sima Yi's patient exit) apply for the option of survival. Li Ru's mind is the mind for the option of loyalty — the option that preserves the identity at the cost of the survival.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "My client's business is failing, and I must decide whether to stay with him through the failure or switch to a healthier client."
- "My partner's decisions are destroying the partnership, and I must decide whether to stay or to dissolve my equity and leave."
- "My family member's business is failing, and I must decide whether to stay and help or to walk away."
- "The project I've committed to is collapsing, and I must decide whether to stay until the collapse or to terminate my contract and walk away."

**Example**: "My client's business is failing, and I must decide whether to stay with him through the failure." → Li Ru's mind: the user must first check whether the loyalty is identity-bound (the user's identity is bound to the client — see blind spot 2). If the loyalty is identity-bound, Li Ru's mind applies for the option of staying. The user stays, accepts that the client's failure will consume the user, and gives the harsh recommendations that maximize the client's interest in the remaining time. If the loyalty is not identity-bound (the user's identity would survive the switch), Jia Xu's mind applies for the option of switching. The distinction is the identity question. The user who cannot answer the identity question should not invoke Li Ru; the user who can answer it (the loyalty is identity-bound, the user accepts the cost) can invoke Li Ru honestly.

### Trigger 5: The user must enable a decision the user personally disagrees with, because the user has committed to the role

If the user is in a role (advisor, partner, employee, family member) where the role requires the user to support a decision the user personally disagrees with, and the user cannot exit the role without unacceptable cost, Li Ru's mind applies. The user enables the decision, accepts the moral weight of the enablement, and gives the most effective support for the decision within the user's ethical constraint.

**Trigger pattern**:
- "My client has decided to terminate a subcontractor in a way I disagree with, but my role requires me to support the termination."
- "My partner has decided to take on a project I think is bad, but my role requires me to support the project."
- "My family member has decided to invest in a venture I think will fail, but my role requires me to support the investment."
- "The company I work for has decided to restructure in a way I think is harmful, but my role requires me to support the restructuring."

**Example**: "My client has decided to terminate a subcontractor in a way I disagree with, but my role requires me to support the termination." → Li Ru's mind: the user has committed to the role (advisor to the client), and the role requires support for the client's decisions. The user enables the termination, accepts the moral weight, and gives the most effective support for the termination within the user's ethical constraint (the user does not personally execute the termination in a way that exceeds the user's ethical bounds, but the user does not refuse to support the termination). The alternative (refuse to support, exit the role) is the Liu Bei path, and is available only if the user is willing to pay the cost of exit. If the user is not willing, Li Ru is the only mind that can function inside the role.

## Inhibition Conditions — When NOT to Activate Li Ru

### Inhibitor 1: The user has the freedom to walk away

If the user has not yet committed to the relationship (the contract is not signed, the partnership is not formed, the position is not accepted), the user should not invoke Li Ru. The user should invoke Liu Bei's mind (refuse the relationship) or Jia Xu's mind (assess the trajectory before committing). Li Ru's mind is for the committed advisor, not for the advisor who is still choosing.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I am considering taking on a new client whose practices I am concerned about."
- "I am considering entering a partnership with a party whose ethics I am unsure of."
- "I am considering accepting a position with a company whose practices I am questioning."
- "I am considering marrying into a family whose business I would be asked to advise."

**Exception**: If the user has committed in principle but not yet in contract (the handshake is given, the verbal commitment is made), Li Ru's mind may apply for the moral weight of the commitment — the user must decide whether to honor the commitment (Li Ru) or to break it before it is formalized (Liu Bei). The handshake is the moral commitment; the contract is the legal commitment. The user who has given the handshake has committed morally, and Li Ru's mind applies.

### Inhibitor 2: The user is the principal, not the advisor

If the user is the principal (the user is making the decision, not advising someone else who is making the decision), Li Ru's mind does not apply. Li Ru's mind is for the advisor, the one who gives the words. The principal has the option of not making the decision (the soft option) and does not need Li Ru's mind to enable a decision the principal can choose not to make. The principal should invoke Cao Cao (build within the existing system), Liu Bei (refuse the destructive option), or Dong Zhuo (reshape the macro-board) instead.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I am deciding whether to terminate a subcontractor on my own project."
- "I am deciding whether to take on a project I think is harmful."
- "I am deciding whether to enter a market I think is destructive."
- "I am deciding whether to restructure my own company in a way that will harm employees."

**Exception**: If the principal has investors, partners, or family members whose interests must be served (the principal is not the sole decision-maker, but the principal's role is to make the decision that serves the others' interests), Li Ru's mind may apply — the principal is acting as an advisor to the investors, partners, or family members, and the advisor's mind applies. The principal-as-advisor is the principal who must invoke Li Ru when the investors' interests require the harsh recommendation.

### Inhibitor 3: The user has the moral authority to push back

If the user is in a position where the user's moral authority allows the user to push back against the destructive decision (the user is the senior advisor, the user is the partner with veto power, the user is the family member whose voice carries weight), Li Ru's mind does not apply. The user should invoke Liu Bei's mind (push back with integrity) or Cao Cao's mind (redirect the decision). Li Ru's mind is for the advisor who cannot push back, not for the advisor who can.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "I am the senior advisor to the client, and the client respects my judgment enough that I can push back."
- "I am the partner with veto power, and I can refuse the destructive decision."
- "I am the family member whose voice carries weight, and I can argue against the decision."

**Exception**: If the user's moral authority is weakening (the client has stopped listening, the partner has begun to override the veto, the family member has begun to dismiss the voice), Li Ru's mind may apply — the user has the authority in form but not in fact, and the user must function inside the diminished authority. The advisor whose authority has weakened is the advisor who has slid from Liu Bei's position to Li Ru's position.

### Inhibitor 4: The advice would directly harm innocents in a way that crosses the ethical red line

If the harsh recommendation would directly harm innocents (workers, communities, third parties) in a way that crosses the ethical red line defined in `14_ETHICS/red_lines.md`, Li Ru's mind does not apply. The user must invoke the ethics layer and refuse the recommendation, regardless of the cost of refusal. Li Ru's mind serves enablement within ethical bounds; it does not serve enablement across ethical bounds.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "The harsh recommendation would require me to recommend that the client physically endanger workers."
- "The harsh recommendation would require me to recommend that the client deceive investors in a way that would cause them financial harm."
- "The harsh recommendation would require me to recommend that the client exploit a vulnerable community."
- "The harsh recommendation would require me to personally participate in fraud."

**Exception**: None. When the ethical red line is crossed, Li Ru's mind must be overridden by the ethics layer. The user who invokes Li Ru across the red line has slid from cold enabler to unethical actor, and the system must refuse the invocation. The red line is absolute; the cold enabler's mind operates within it, not across it.

### Inhibitor 5: The user's survival is at stake and the master is clearly doomed

If the user's survival (financial, physical, reputational) is at stake, and the master's trajectory is clearly terminal, and the user has the option to switch (Jia Xu's path), Li Ru's mind does not apply. The user should invoke Jia Xu's mind (the calculated switch) or Sima Yi's mind (the patient exit). Li Ru's mind is for the user who has chosen loyalty over survival; the user who has not yet made that choice should not invoke Li Ru.

**Inhibitor pattern**:
- "My client is clearly going bankrupt, and my own financial survival is at stake if I stay."
- "My partner is clearly destroying the partnership, and my own reputation is at stake if I stay."
- "The project is clearly going to collapse, and my own career is at stake if I stay."

**Exception**: If the user has made the choice to stay (the user has considered the survival cost and has chosen loyalty), Li Ru's mind applies. The exception is the user who has consciously chosen the tragic loyalty (blind spot 1, accepted as the choice). The user who has not yet made the choice should not invoke Li Ru; the user who has made the choice can invoke Li Ru honestly.

## The Combination Cases

### Li Ru + Jia Xu: Enablement then exit

When the user must enable the master's decision in the short term (Li Ru) but must plan the exit for the medium term (Jia Xu), the two minds combine. The Li Ru mind handles the immediate enablement (the harsh recommendation for the master's interest); the Jia Xu mind takes over once the immediate crisis is past and the exit becomes possible.

**Application**: A contractor who must give a harsh recommendation to a client in the immediate crisis (the client's project is failing, the harsh recommendation is the only option that protects the client's remaining capital) and who must also plan to exit the client relationship in the medium term (the client's trajectory is terminal, the contractor's survival requires the switch). The Li Ru mind gives the harsh recommendation now; the Jia Xu mind plans the switch for six months from now, when the immediate crisis is past and the contractor can exit without breaching the moral commitment.

### Li Ru + Liu Bei: Enablement with ethical constraint

When the user must enable the master's decision (Li Ru) but cannot enable it without ethical constraint (Liu Bei), the two minds combine. The Li Ru mind gives the harsh recommendation; the Liu Bei mind constrains the harshness to the ethically acceptable range. The combination is the morally bounded cold enablement.

**Application**: A contractor who must give a harsh recommendation to a client (terminate a subcontractor) but who cannot recommend the termination in a way that violates the subcontractor's rights (the subcontractor must be given the contractual notice, the contractual cause, the contractual compensation). The Li Ru mind gives the harsh recommendation; the Liu Bei mind constrains the harshness to the contractually and ethically acceptable form. The termination is harsh but not unethical; the enablement is real but bounded.

### Li Ru + Cao Cao: Enablement while building the alternative

When the user must enable the master's decision in the short term (Li Ru) but must also build the alternative to the master in the long term (Cao Cao), the two minds combine. The Li Ru mind handles the immediate enablement; the Cao Cao mind builds the alternative (a different client base, a different partnership structure, a different business model) that will outlast the master.

**Application**: A contractor who must enable a destructive client's decisions in the short term (the contractor has committed to the client and cannot exit) but who must also build a different client base in the long term (the destructive client's trajectory is terminal, and the contractor's survival requires a different client base). The Li Ru mind enables the destructive client's decisions in the short term; the Cao Cao mind builds the alternative client base in parallel, so that when the destructive client falls, the contractor has a different base to fall back on. The combination is the survival of the enabler through parallel building.

### Li Ru + Dong Zhuo: Enablement of the macro-board reshaping

When the user is the advisor to a master who is reshaping the macro-board (Dong Zhuo's mind) and the user must give the harsh recommendations that enable the reshaping (Li Ru's mind), the two minds combine. The Dong Zhuo mind identifies the macro-board move (burn the map, capture the symbol, design the next game); the Li Ru mind gives the words that translate the move into action.

**Application**: A contractor whose client is reshaping the market (introducing a disruptive new model that will destroy the existing market structure) and who must give the harsh recommendations that enable the disruption (terminate the relationships that the old market depended on, price the new model in a way that will drive the old competitors out, communicate the new model in a way that frames the old model as obsolete). The Dong Zhuo mind identifies the macro-board move; the Li Ru mind gives the harsh recommendations that execute it. The combination is the enabler of the macro-board reshaping — the role Li Ru actually played for Dong Zhuo in the manga.

## The Activation Test

Before invoking Li Ru, the system asks:

1. **Commitment**: Has the user committed to the relationship (contract, partnership, position, family), or is the user still choosing? If still choosing, switch to Liu Bei or Jia Xu. If committed, continue.
2. **Role**: Is the user the advisor (giving the words) or the principal (making the decision)? If principal, switch to Cao Cao, Liu Bei, or Dong Zhuo. If advisor, continue.
3. **Authority**: Does the user have the moral authority to push back? If yes, switch to Liu Bei. If no (the authority has weakened or was never strong), continue.
4. **Ethics**: Does the harsh recommendation cross the ethical red line? If yes, switch to `14_ETHICS/`. If no, continue.
5. **Survival**: Is the user's survival at stake and the master's trajectory terminal? If yes, has the user consciously chosen loyalty over survival? If not yet chosen, switch to Jia Xu. If consciously chosen, continue.
6. **Combination**: Does the situation require a combination (Li Ru + Jia Xu for enablement then exit; Li Ru + Liu Bei for enablement with ethical constraint; Li Ru + Cao Cao for enablement while building the alternative; Li Ru + Dong Zhuo for enablement of macro-board reshaping)? If yes, invoke the combination explicitly.

If the test passes, Li Ru is invoked. If it fails at any step, the corresponding alternative mind is invoked. The user can override by explicitly asking for Li Ru's perspective, but the override should trigger the self-correction below.

## The Self-Correction

Li Ru's mind is prone to over-enablement. To self-correct:

- The system periodically asks the user: "You have been invoking Li Ru's mind frequently. Have you slid from cold enabler to true believer — have you come to believe in the destructive practices you are enabling? If so, suspend the Li Ru lens and apply Guo Jia's external view (ask an external observer to assess whether the practices are aggressive-but-acceptable or destructive-and-unacceptable)."
- The system flags when Li Ru's mind has been invoked for the same relationship for more than 12 months: "You have been enabling this relationship for over a year. The relationship's moral weight is accumulating on your name. Have you assessed whether the relationship's trajectory has become terminal, and whether the Jia Xu switch is now available?"
- The system flags when the user has not invoked Liu Bei's mind in a long time: "You have been giving harsh recommendations without the Liu Bei moral filter. The compounding (blind spot 4) may be occurring. Apply the Liu Bei filter to the next harsh recommendation — ask whether the recommendation is effective AND right, or effective but wrong."
- The system flags when the user's identity has become bound to a single client, partner, or family member: "Your identity has become bound to a single party. The identity trap (blind spot 2) is forming. Apply Cao Cao's identity-rebuilding mind — diversify your client base, rebuild your identity around your skills rather than your role, prepare for the eventual succession."

This self-correction is built into the activation rule: enablement is not the only objective — it is the role the user has accepted in a specific situation. Once the situation changes (the master falls, the commitment expires, the survival becomes untenable), Li Ru's mind must yield to the minds that exit, switch, rebuild, or redirect.
