## The 6 Signature Moves

### Move 1: The Three Visits — Patient Recruitment

**The pattern**: To recruit the most valuable talent, visit them personally, repeatedly, without demand. Show respect by your persistence. The recruitment is the visit, not the offer.

**Historical example**: Liu Bei visited Zhuge Liang three times at his thatched cottage. The first two times, Zhuge Liang was absent (or pretended to be). Liu Bei returned. The third time, they met, and Zhuge Liang agreed to serve. The visit itself — the willingness to make the trip three times — was what convinced Zhuge Liang.

**Mechanism**: The most valuable talent does not need to be hired; they need to be courted. The courtship signals seriousness. The serious person commits; the unserious does not.

**Application in contracting**:
- To recruit a senior subcontractor who has many offers, visit them in person, multiple times. Don't just send offers. Show up. Demonstrate that you value them enough to invest your time.
- To win a major client, meet them multiple times before pitching. Build the relationship first; the contract follows.

**Risks**: The visit must be genuine, not strategic. If the visit is purely tactical, the talent detects it and is repelled. The persistence must reflect genuine respect.

---

### Move 2: The Public Weep — Emotional Visibility

**The pattern**: Express genuine emotion publicly. The emotion signals that you are not the cold calculator; you are the warm leader. The emotion creates loyalty that calculation cannot.

**Historical example**: Liu Bei wept publicly at the death of his general Pang Tong, at the death of his brother Guan Yu, at the loss of close friends. The weeping was genuine; the strategic effect was that his followers knew he cared.

**Mechanism**: Emotional visibility creates identification. Followers who see their leader weep identify with him. They feel what he feels. They become willing to die for him — because they have become part of him.

**Application in contracting**:
- Express genuine disappointment when a project fails — to the team, not just to the client. The team sees you care.
- Express genuine pride when a project succeeds. The team sees you notice.
- Express genuine concern when a team member is in trouble. The team sees you're not just a boss; you're a person.

**Risks**: Emotion that is performed, not felt, is detected and damages credibility. The emotion must be genuine.

---

### Move 3: Refuse to Take What Is Not Given

**The pattern**: Refuse opportunities that come through betrayal, even when refusal costs you. The refusal builds a reputation that pays for decades.

**Historical example**: Liu Bei refused to take Yi province from Liu Zhang by treachery, even when his advisors recommended it. He waited until Liu Zhang attacked him. Then he took Yi province, but with a justification.

**Mechanism**: Each refusal to take by betrayal is a deposit in the reputation account. The account compounds over years. When you finally need to take something (in self-defense), the reputation justifies the action.

**Application in contracting**:
- Refuse to poach a competitor's client by spreading rumors about them. Even if it would work, don't. The reputation you build is worth more than the single client.
- Refuse to take credit for a subcontractor's work. Even if no one would know, don't. The reputation for honesty is worth more than the credit.

**Risks**: The refusal costs in the short term. You lose opportunities. The cost is real. The payoff is long-term and is sometimes invisible (you never see the clients who chose you because they heard you don't betray).

---

### Move 4: Carry the People With You

**The pattern**: When retreating or relocating, take the people who depend on you, even when it slows you down. The willingness to slow down for the vulnerable is what creates the loyalty.

**Historical example**: At the Battle of Changban (208 AD), Liu Bei retreated from Cao Cao's army with the civilians of Xinye. He could have fled faster without them. He chose to slow down and take them. The civilians followed him for the rest of his life.

**Mechanism**: People who see that you will slow down for them will speed up for you. The willingness to bear cost for them creates willingness in them to bear cost for you.

**Application in contracting**:
- When a project loses money, don't take it out of the subcontractor's payment. Take the loss yourself. The subcontractor remembers.
- When a client delays payment, don't pass the delay to your subcontractor. Pay them on time, even if it costs you cash flow. The subcontractor stays with you.

**Risks**: The cost of carrying people can sink you if the situation is dire. The discipline is to do it when you can survive the cost; not when it would destroy you.

---

### Move 5: The Personal Recommendation

**The pattern**: When you want someone to join you, recommend them personally, with specific reasons. The personal recommendation is more powerful than any contract.

**Historical example**: Liu Bei recommended Zhao Yun to Gongsun Zan, then later recruited Zhao Yun to his own side. The personal recommendation from Liu Bei — "I trust this man with my life" — was more powerful than any payment could be.

**Mechanism**: A personal recommendation carries the recommender's reputation. The recommended person wants to live up to it. The recommendation creates a bond before any work begins.

**Application in contracting**:
- When introducing a new subcontractor to a client, personally vouch for them. "I have worked with him for 5 years and trust him with my own projects." The vouch creates a bond.
- When recommending a colleague to a partner, be specific. "He is the most honest person I know in this sector." The specificity is what carries weight.

**Risks**: Your reputation is on the line. If the recommended person fails, your reputation takes the hit. Recommend only those you genuinely trust.

---

### Move 6: Frame the Mission Larger Than Yourself

**The pattern**: Frame the work as something bigger than profit. The framing attracts people who want meaning, not just money.

**Historical example**: Liu Bei's mission was the restoration of the Han dynasty. He was a distant imperial relative, so the framing had some legitimacy. But the framing mattered more than the lineage. People joined him not because of his distant royal blood but because of his mission.

**Mechanism**: Money attracts mercenaries. Mission attracts missionaries. Mercenaries leave when more money is offered. Missionaries stay when the money is gone.

**Application in contracting**:
- Frame the work as: "We prove that halal business can out-compete conventional business in Egypt." The framing attracts people who want to be part of that proof.
- Frame the work as: "We raise the standard of electrical work in our city." The framing attracts people who want to be proud of their work, not just paid for it.

**Risks**: The mission must be genuine. If the mission is a marketing tool, the team detects it and is demoralized. The mission must be lived, not just stated.

## How to Recognize When to Apply Each Move

| Move | When to apply | When NOT to apply |
|------|--------------|------------------|
| Three Visits | When recruiting the most valuable talent | When the talent is ordinary (use standard recruitment) |
| Public Weep | When emotion is genuine and the team needs to see it | When emotion would be manufactured or manipulative |
| Refuse What's Not Given | When betrayal would be tempting | When the situation is genuinely self-defense (then act) |
| Carry the People | When you can afford the cost and the loyalty matters | When the cost would destroy the business |
| Personal Recommendation | When you genuinely trust the person | When you don't (your reputation is at stake) |
| Frame the Mission | When the mission is genuine | When the mission is marketing (don't fake it) |
