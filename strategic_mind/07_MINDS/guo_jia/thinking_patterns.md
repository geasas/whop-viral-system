## The 6-Step Guo Jia Thought Pattern

### Step 1: What is the opponent's weakness that I can exploit in the next 30 days?

Guo Jia does not think in years. He thinks in days and weeks. His first question: "What can I do in the next 30 days that will change the situation?"

The long game is for Sima Yi. Guo Jia plays the short game with such skill that it shapes the long game.

**Pattern in your practice**: When facing a stalled negotiation, ask: "What can I do in the next 30 days to break the stall?" Don't wait for the long-term fix. Find the short-term move that creates space for the long-term move.

### Step 2: What is the opponent overconfident about?

Guo Jia looks for the opponent's overconfidence. The opponent's confidence in their strength IS the weakness — they will not defend what they believe is invulnerable.

His analysis of Yuan Shao identified that Yuan Shao was overconfident in his army's size, in his advisors' wisdom, in his noble birth. Each overconfidence was an opening.

**Pattern in your practice**: When facing a larger competitor, ask: "What are they overconfident about?" Their brand? Their relationships? Their cash flow? Their overconfidence is where you can strike.

### Step 3: What is the smallest decisive move that wins the largest outcome?

Guo Jia looks for the lever — the small move that produces a large effect. He does not try to win the whole war at once; he tries to win the one engagement that decides the war.

His recommendation at Guandu was not "win the entire war with Yuan Shao." It was "burn the supplies at Wuchao." One small move; one large effect.

**Pattern in your practice**: In a stalled project, find the one task that unblocks everything else. Don't try to do everything at once. Find the keystone.

### Step 4: Can I commit fully to this?

Guo Jia does not do half-measures. If the situation warrants action, he commits fully. If the situation does not warrant full commitment, he does not act.

This discipline prevents the worst of both worlds: acting too little to win but enough to be exposed.

**Pattern in your practice**: For every action, ask: "Is this worth committing everything to?" If yes, commit fully. If no, do not act at all. Don't act halfway.

### Step 5: What will the opponent do in the next 24 hours after my move?

Guo Jia thinks one move ahead. Not ten moves — that's Sima Yi. One move. He asks: "If I do this, what will the opponent do tomorrow?"

This is the speed-chess mind. You don't predict the whole game. You predict the next move. If you predict correctly, you stay one move ahead.

**Pattern in your practice**: Before sending any email or making any call, predict the recipient's response. "If I send this, they will respond with X." If you can't predict the response, you're not ready to send the email.

### Step 6: What would I tell my patron (or client) if I had to be honest?

Guo Jia's candor was his credibility. He didn't flatter. He told the truth, even when it was unwelcome.

The discipline: before sending the recommendation, ask: "Is this what I actually believe? Or is this what the patron wants to hear?" If the latter, re-write.

**Pattern in your practice**: When advising a client, ask: "What would I tell my brother if he asked me this question?" That's the answer the client should get.

## The Cognitive Style

Guo Jia's cognitive style is **exploitation**. He does not build from scratch. He exploits what exists. He reads what others have built and finds the weak point.

This is distinct from Cao Cao (synthesis), Sima Yi (patience), Liu Bei (relationship). Guo Jia is the master of the exploitable weakness.

The risk: when the situation has no exploitable weakness, Guo Jia has nothing to work with. He is not the builder; he is the exploiter. When nothing exists to exploit, he waits — but waiting is not his strength.

## The Anti-Patterns

When Guo Jia's patterns are misapplied:

- Speed → impulsiveness: acting before reading correctly
- Decisiveness → recklessness: committing without sufficient analysis
- Candor → abrasiveness: honesty delivered without care
- Exploitation → predation: using other's weaknesses unethically

The system must apply Guo Jia's patterns where they work and switch to other minds where they fail. See `blind_spots.md`.
