# Resource Mapping — Auditing What You Actually Have

> **Purpose**: You cannot deploy resources you have not mapped. You cannot identify your bottleneck without an honest audit. You cannot decide what to grow without knowing what is limiting. This file teaches the system to map the contractor's five resource types, to identify the bottleneck and the compounding resource, and to apply the discipline in the Egyptian market context.

**Layer**: 05_POSITION
**File**: resource_mapping.md
**Subject**: The five resource types, the audit of each, the bottleneck resource, the compounding resource, and the resource norms of the Egyptian construction market.
**Sources**: Resource-based view of the firm (Barney, 1991); Sun Tzu (the general's resources); manga Ravages of Time (Cao Cao's resource audits at Guandu, Liu Bei's resource mapping at Xinye, Sun Quan's resource consolidation at Jiangdong).
**Application domain**: Egyptian electrical contractor whose resources are the foundation of the trajectory from electrical subcontracting to general contracting to real estate development.

## The Premise

Resources are the foundation of every strategic move. The contractor who over-estimates their resources enters negotiations they cannot execute; the contractor who under-estimates their resources leaves opportunities they could capture. The honest resource map is the foundation of every other strategic file in this system.

Most contractors map only one resource (financial) and ignore the other four. The result is strategy that assumes capital is the bottleneck when capital is not the bottleneck — and the actual bottleneck (often relational or reputational) goes unaddressed while the contractor accumulates capital they cannot deploy.

The discipline is to map all five resources honestly, to identify which is the bottleneck (limiting current growth), and which is the compounding resource (growing with use and able to multiply the other resources). The map is updated quarterly; the bottleneck changes; the compounding resource changes; the strategy changes with them.

## The Five Resource Types

### Resource 1: Financial

The capital resources — cash, credit lines, receivables, equipment value, real estate holdings, and any other liquid or near-liquid asset. The financial resource is the most visible and the most over-rated. Contractors who map only this resource produce strategy that confuses capital with capability.

**The audit**:
- **Cash on hand**: How much cash is available for deployment in the next 30 days, 90 days, 12 months?
- **Credit lines**: What credit lines are available, what are their limits, what are their terms, what are their drawn and undrawn portions?
- **Receivables**: What is the value of outstanding receivables, what is the average days-to-collect, what is the doubtful portion?
- **Equipment value**: What equipment is owned, what is its market value, what is its financing status?
- **Real estate**: What real estate is owned, what is its market value, what is its encumbrance?
- **Working capital**: Cash + undrawn credit + receivables expected within 90 days − payables due within 90 days.

The financial resource audit produces a single number: the deployable working capital for the next 90 days. This number, not the total assets, is the resource that matters for strategic decisions in the next quarter.

### Resource 2: Human

The people resources — the in-house team, the regular subcontractors, the casual labor pool, the engineers and consultants on retainer, the administrative support. The human resource is the resource that does the work; without it, the financial resource is unusable.

**The audit**:
- **In-house team**: How many, what skills, what retention status, what development trajectory?
- **Regular subcontractors**: How many regular subcontractors, what skills, what retention status, what priority relationship?
- **Casual labor pool**: How many casual laborers, what skills, what reliability?
- **Engineers and consultants**: Who is on retainer, what is their availability, what is their capability?
- **Administrative support**: What administrative capacity exists, what is its quality, what is its scalability?

The human resource audit produces two numbers: the deployable capacity for the next 90 days (in qualified person-days), and the retention risk (the percentage of key people whose departure would create a meaningful capability gap within 12 months).

### Resource 3: Intellectual

The knowledge resources — technical knowledge, project management knowledge, market knowledge, regulatory knowledge, cost estimation knowledge, design knowledge. The intellectual resource is the resource that determines the quality of the work and the efficiency of the delivery; without it, the human and financial resources produce low-quality output.

**The audit**:
- **Technical knowledge**: What technical capabilities does the team have (electrical systems, solar, high-voltage, industrial, commercial, residential, low-current, fire alarm, security, BMS)?
- **Project management knowledge**: What project management methodologies does the team practice, what is the maturity of the practice, what is the documentation quality?
- **Market knowledge**: What does the team know about the sub-markets it works in (developers, projects, prices, schedules, regulators)?
- **Regulatory knowledge**: What does the team know about the regulatory environment (codes, permits, utility coordination, environmental reviews)?
- **Cost estimation knowledge**: What does the team know about the cost of materials, labor, equipment, overhead, margin — and how accurate are the estimates?
- **Design knowledge**: What design capabilities does the team have (basic design, detailed engineering, value engineering)?

The intellectual resource audit produces a capability map: what the team can do today, at what quality, with what efficiency. The map is the basis for the specialization decision (which capabilities to deepen, which to add, which to outsource).

### Resource 4: Relational

The relationship resources — clients, suppliers, subcontractors, regulators, financiers, intermediaries, competitors (yes, competitors; the relationship with competitors is a resource). The relational resource is the most durable and the slowest to build; it compounds over years and is the foundation of the long-game strategy.

**The audit**:
- **Clients**: How many active clients, how many past clients, how many potential clients? Of the past clients, how many would call the contractor first for the next project? Of the potential clients, how many have been contacted in the last 12 months?
- **Suppliers**: How many active suppliers, what is the credit arrangement with each, what is the priority status with each, what is the alternative if each becomes unavailable?
- **Subcontractors**: How many regular subcontractors, what is the priority status with each, what is the alternative if each becomes unavailable?
- **Regulators**: Which regulators and authorities does the contractor have direct relationships with, what is the quality of each relationship, what is the alternative if each becomes unavailable?
- **Financiers**: Which banks and lenders does the contractor have relationships with, what is the credit availability with each, what is the alternative if each becomes unavailable?
- **Intermediaries**: Which engineers, consultants, and brokers carry the contractor's information to the market, what is the quality of each relationship, what is the information flow from each?
- **Competitors**: Which competitors does the contractor have direct relationships with, what is the quality of each, what is the potential for collaboration or referral?

The relational resource audit produces a relationship map: who would help the contractor in a specific situation, who would not, who would be neutral. The map is the basis for the relational leverage (per `03_ANALYSIS/leverage_detection.md`).

### Resource 5: Reputational

The reputation resources — what the market says about the contractor when the contractor is not in the room. The reputational resource is the slowest to build (years), the fastest to lose (months), and the most difficult to map honestly (the contractor does not hear what the market says behind their back).

**The audit**:
- **Specialization reputation**: What is the contractor known for? (One thing, not two. The contractor known for two things is known for none.)
- **Quality reputation**: What is the market's perception of the contractor's quality? (Premium, mid-market, value — pick one.)
- **Schedule reputation**: What is the market's perception of the contractor's schedule reliability? (Always on time, usually on time, often late, unpredictable.)
- **Pricing reputation**: What is the market's perception of the contractor's pricing? (Premium, fair, value, below-market.)
- **Integrity reputation**: What is the market's perception of the contractor's integrity? (Above reproach, generally trustworthy, transactional, sometimes suspect.)
- **Relationship reputation**: What is the market's perception of the contractor's relationships? (Long-term, transactional, mixed.)

The reputational resource audit produces a reputation map: what the market says about the contractor in the contractor's absence. The map is difficult to obtain honestly; the contractor must ask intermediaries (engineers, past clients who would be honest, competitors who would be honest) what the market says, and must reconcile the answers without dismissing the unflattering ones.

## The Bottleneck Resource

In any phase of the contractor's trajectory, one resource is the bottleneck — the resource that limits the contractor's growth more than the others. The discipline is to identify the bottleneck resource and to address it directly, rather than to accumulate more of the non-bottleneck resources that cannot be deployed.

### The bottleneck identification

The bottleneck resource is identified by the following test: if this resource were doubled tomorrow, would the contractor's growth rate increase significantly? If yes, the resource is the bottleneck. If no, the resource is not the bottleneck.

**Examples of bottleneck identification**:
- If financial resources doubled tomorrow (cash, credit, receivables), would the contractor's growth rate increase significantly? Only if the contractor has the human resources to deploy the additional capital. If the human resources are saturated, the financial resource is not the bottleneck — the human resource is.
- If human resources doubled tomorrow (twice as many qualified people), would the contractor's growth rate increase significantly? Only if the contractor has the projects to deploy the additional people. If the project pipeline is saturated, the human resource is not the bottleneck — the relational resource (client relationships) is.
- If relational resources doubled tomorrow (twice as many client relationships), would the contractor's growth rate increase significantly? Only if the contractor has the resources to deliver the additional projects. If the resources are saturated, the relational resource is not the bottleneck — the financial or human resource is.

### The bottleneck cycle

Bottlenecks change. The contractor who fixes the financial bottleneck (raises capital) often finds the human bottleneck next (no qualified people to deploy the capital). The contractor who fixes the human bottleneck (hires and trains the team) often finds the relational bottleneck next (no clients to deploy the team). The contractor who fixes the relational bottleneck (acquires client relationships) often finds the financial bottleneck next (no capital to fund the projects).

The cycle is the discipline of growth: fix the current bottleneck, identify the next bottleneck, fix the next bottleneck. The contractor who fixes the same bottleneck twice (e.g., accumulates more capital when capital is no longer the bottleneck) wastes resources that would have been more productive addressing the new bottleneck.

### The Egyptian contractor's bottleneck in each phase

**Phase 1 (electrical subcontractor)**: The bottleneck is typically relational (client diversification) or financial (working capital for the next project). The intellectual resource is usually strong (the contractor's specialization is the foundation); the human resource is usually adequate; the reputational resource is usually concentrated in a narrow sub-market.

**Phase 2 (general contractor)**: The bottleneck is typically human (qualified project managers and engineers for the broader scope) or financial (working capital for the larger projects). The relational resource is usually growing; the intellectual resource is being expanded to the broader scope; the reputational resource is in transition.

**Phase 3 (real estate developer)**: The bottleneck is typically financial (development capital is larger than contracting capital) or relational (relationships with landowners, lenders, regulators, and the development-specific network). The human resource may need to be expanded with development-specific roles; the intellectual resource needs to include development-specific knowledge (land acquisition, feasibility, financing, marketing); the reputational resource needs to be built in the development market (different from the contracting market).

## The Compounding Resource

In any phase, one resource is the compounding resource — the resource that, when deployed, grows rather than depletes. The discipline is to identify the compounding resource and to deploy it aggressively, because the compounding resource is the resource that produces the exponential returns over the long game.

### The compounding identification

The compounding resource is identified by the following test: when this resource is used, does it grow or does it deplete? If it grows, it is a compounding resource. If it depletes, it is a consumable resource.

**The five resources, classified**:
- **Financial**: Consumable. Capital deployed is capital depleted (until the return arrives, and the return is uncertain). The financial resource does not compound with use.
- **Human**: Mixed. The in-house team's skills compound with use (training on the job); the team's energy depletes with use (burnout). The human resource compounds when the use builds skills, depletes when the use exhausts the team.
- **Intellectual**: Compounding. Knowledge used is knowledge reinforced. The intellectual resource compounds with use — the team that has done three solar projects knows more about solar than the team that has done one.
- **Relational**: Compounding. Relationships used (the contractor who does work for a client strengthens the relationship with that client) grow rather than deplete. The relational resource is the most powerfully compounding resource in the system.
- **Reputational**: Compounding. Reputation deployed (the contractor who delivers on schedule strengthens the schedule-reliability reputation) grows rather than depletes. The reputational resource compounds with use — but the compounding is fragile (one bad delivery can damage the reputation built by ten good deliveries).

### The compounding strategy

The contractor who identifies the compounding resources (intellectual, relational, reputational) and deploys them aggressively captures the exponential returns. The contractor who treats the compounding resources as consumable (uses relationships without maintaining them, uses reputation without protecting it, uses knowledge without expanding it) loses the compounding returns and finds the resources depleting.

The discipline: deploy the compounding resources aggressively, but maintain them at the same time. The relationship used must also be maintained (the post-project follow-up, the regular communication, the value-add between projects). The reputation deployed must also be protected (the quality control that preserves the reputation, the response to the rare problem that addresses it before it damages the reputation). The knowledge used must also be expanded (the post-project review, the lessons-learned documentation, the training of the next team member).

### The Egyptian contractor's compounding strategy

In each phase, the contractor's compounding resources are the relational and reputational resources:
- **Phase 1 (electrical subcontractor)**: The client relationships and the reputation as an electrical specialist are the compounding resources. Each project delivered strengthens both. The contractor who delivers ten electrical projects to ten developers has compounded the relational and reputational resources ten-fold.
- **Phase 2 (general contractor)**: The expanded client relationships and the reputation as a general contractor are the compounding resources. Each general contracting project delivered strengthens both — and the projects completed in Phase 1 as an electrical subcontractor provide the foundation (the relationships and reputation from Phase 1 compound into Phase 2).
- **Phase 3 (real estate developer)**: The relationships with landowners, lenders, regulators, and buyers are the compounding resources, as is the reputation as a developer. Each project delivered strengthens both. The relationships and reputation from Phases 1 and 2 (the contractor relationships, the developer relationships from Phase 2, the supplier and subcontractor relationships) compound into Phase 3.

The compounding is multi-phase. The contractor who manages the compounding resources across the phases captures the exponential returns over the 10-year trajectory. The contractor who treats the compounding resources as consumable (uses a relationship once, lets it lapse) finds the trajectory stalling because the compounding has not happened.

## The Resource Norms in the Egyptian Market Context

The Egyptian construction market has resource norms that shape the contractor's resource strategy.

### Financial resource norms

- The Egyptian construction market is capital-constrained. Bank lending to contractors is limited; payment terms are often extended (60-90 days is common, sometimes 120+); currency devaluation affects the cost of imported materials. The contractor who relies on bank lending as the primary financial resource is exposed to the lending cycle; the contractor who relies on retained earnings as the primary financial resource is more resilient.
- The norm: contractors with 3-6 months of working capital are typical; contractors with 12+ months are exceptional. The contractor who builds to 12+ months of working capital captures a financial resource advantage that few competitors can match.

### Human resource norms

- The Egyptian construction market has a strong labor pool but a limited pool of qualified engineers and project managers. The contractor who recruits and retains qualified engineers has a human resource advantage that is difficult for competitors to replicate.
- The norm: contractors with 1-2 qualified engineers per 50 workers are typical; contractors with 1 per 25 are exceptional. The contractor who builds to 1 qualified engineer per 25 workers captures a human resource advantage that produces higher quality and faster delivery.

### Intellectual resource norms

- The Egyptian construction market has uneven technical knowledge. Many contractors operate on traditional methods; few have invested in modern techniques (BIM, prefabrication, modular, energy-efficient systems). The contractor who invests in modern techniques captures an intellectual resource advantage that produces cost savings, quality improvements, and schedule reductions.
- The norm: contractors with traditional methods are typical; contractors with modern techniques are exceptional. The contractor who invests in one modern technique per year (BIM in year 1, prefabrication in year 2, modular in year 3) builds the intellectual resource advantage over 5 years.

### Relational resource norms

- The Egyptian construction market is relationship-based. The contract is the formality; the relationship is the substance. The contractor who maintains relationships across the market (clients, suppliers, subcontractors, regulators, intermediaries) captures a relational resource advantage that produces opportunity flow and information flow.
- The norm: contractors with 5-10 active client relationships are typical; contractors with 20+ are exceptional. The contractor who maintains 20+ active client relationships (not 20+ projects — 20+ relationships, with regular contact even between projects) captures a relational resource advantage that produces opportunity flow even in slow markets.

### Reputational resource norms

- The Egyptian construction market is reputation-sensitive. A single bad project can damage a contractor's reputation for years; a single excellent project can elevate a contractor's reputation for years. The contractor who protects the reputation with quality control, responsive problem-solving, and transparent communication captures a reputational resource advantage that produces premium pricing and client preference.
- The norm: contractors with a "good" reputation are typical; contractors with a "category-defining" reputation (known as the best in a specific niche) are exceptional. The contractor who builds a category-defining reputation in one niche (e.g., solar installation, high-voltage work, premium residential) captures a reputational resource advantage that produces pricing power and client preference in that niche.

## The Application: The Resource Map in Practice

The contractor completes the resource map quarterly:

1. Audit each of the five resources (financial, human, intellectual, relational, reputational).
2. Identify the current bottleneck resource (the one that limits growth most).
3. Identify the current compounding resource (the one that compounds with deployment).
4. Allocate the next 90 days' effort: 60% to addressing the bottleneck, 30% to deploying the compounding resource, 10% to maintaining the non-bottleneck, non-compounding resources.
5. Review at the end of the 90 days: has the bottleneck changed? Has the compounding resource changed? Update the map.

The map is the foundation of the contractor's strategic position. The contractor who maps the resources honestly and updates the map quarterly has a clear view of what to do in the next 90 days. The contractor who does not map the resources drifts with the market and is surprised when a bottleneck becomes a crisis.

## Common Errors in Resource Mapping

### Error 1: Mapping only the financial resource

The contractor who maps only the financial resource produces strategy that confuses capital with capability. The financial resource is one of five; the strategy that ignores the other four misallocates the capital.

### Error 2: Failing to identify the bottleneck

The contractor who accumulates resources without identifying the bottleneck accumulates the wrong resources. The bottleneck is the resource that limits growth; the strategy that does not address the bottleneck does not produce growth.

### Error 3: Treating the compounding resource as consumable

The contractor who treats the relational and reputational resources as consumable (uses them without maintaining them) depletes the resources that would have compounded. The compounding resource is the resource that produces the exponential returns; the depletion of the compounding resource is the most expensive strategic error.

### Error 4: Failing to update the map

The map is a snapshot, not a permanent state. The bottleneck changes; the compounding resource changes; the strategy changes with them. The contractor who updates the map annually is too slow; the contractor who updates it quarterly captures the changes in time.

### Error 5: Dishonest self-assessment

The map that flatters the contractor produces bad strategy. The relational resource map that lists 30 active client relationships when only 10 would call the contractor first misleads the contractor about the actual relational position. The honest map is the foundation; the flattering map is the foundation of strategic error.
