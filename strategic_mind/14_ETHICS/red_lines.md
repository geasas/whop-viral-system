## The Premise

A red line is not a guideline; it is an absolute prohibition. The contractor who treats the red line as a guideline crosses it when the situation seems to require the crossing; the contractor who treats the red line as an absolute does not cross it regardless of the situation. The difference is the difference between the strategic mind and the opportunist.

The red lines in this file are absolute for three reasons. First, the Islamic red lines are absolute because they are the *haram* that the *aqeedah* (the creed) prohibits; the *aqeedah* does not bend to the situation. Second, the professional and personal red lines are absolute because they are the foundations of the long-game trajectory (the reputation, the relationships, the integrity) that the short-game calculation cannot preserve. Third, the strategic red lines are absolute because they are the lines whose crossing destroys the strategy itself — the crossing of the line is the destruction of the strategic mind that the strategy depends on.

The five dimensions of the red lines are not independent. The Islamic red line (the *riba*, the *ghish*, the *rishwa*) is also the professional red line (the dishonesty, the breach of the contract) and the personal red line (the betrayal of the trust) and the strategic red line (the destruction of the long game) and, often, the Egyptian legal red line (the criminal violation). The crossing of one red line is the crossing of multiple. The contractor who pays the *rishwa* (the Islamic red line) is also breaching the professional duty (the dishonesty), betraying the trust (the personal), destroying the strategy (the long-game reputation), and committing the criminal act (the Egyptian Penal Code's prohibition of the bribe).

The discipline of the red lines has two parts. The first is the **recognition of the approach** — the early warning signs that the contractor is approaching a red line, before the crossing. The second is the **response to the crossing** — the repentance (*tawbah*), the restitution (*radd al-mazalim*), and the reform (*islah*) when the contractor has crossed the line. The discipline is the operationalization of the red lines in the contractor's life; the discipline is what the contractor does, not what the contractor believes.

## Dimension 1 — The Islamic Red Lines

The Islamic red lines are the *haram* actions in business that the *ijma'* (the consensus) of the *fiqh* schools (the Hanafi, the Maliki, the Shafi'i, the Hanbali, and the modern Ja'fari) prohibits. The contractor's *aqeedah* requires the abstention from the *haram* regardless of the cost.

### The *riba* prohibition

The *riba* (the interest, the usury) is the absolute prohibition in the Islamic *fiqh*. The Quran says, "Allah has permitted trade and forbidden *riba*" [Quran 2:275 — al-Baqara]; "O you who believe, fear Allah and leave what remains of *riba*, if you are believers. And if you do not, then be warned of a war from Allah and His Messenger" [Quran 2:278–279 — al-Baqara]. The verse is the most severe in the Quran — the war from Allah and His Messenger is the explicit consequence of the *riba*.

The *riba*'s scope: the prohibition applies to both the paying and the receiving of the interest; the prohibition applies regardless of the rate (the 1% interest is the *riba* as much as the 50%); the prohibition applies to the loans, the bonds, the interest-bearing bank accounts, the conventional mortgages, the supplier credit with the interest, the late-payment interest. The contractor's *riba* exposures: the bank loan with the interest, the supplier credit with the interest, the late-payment penalty that is the interest, the interest-bearing cash reserve account.

The *riba*'s alternatives: the *murabaha* (the cost-plus-financing with the Islamic bank), the *musharaka* (the profit-sharing partnership), the *ijara* (the lease), the *salam* (the forward purchase), the *istisna'* (the manufacturing contract), the *qard hasan* (the interest-free loan). The Islamic finance market in Egypt (the Faisal Islamic Bank, the Al Baraka Bank, the Abu Dhabi Islamic Bank — Egypt, the Islamic banking windows of the conventional banks) provides the alternatives. The contractor's strategic move is the transition from the *riba*-based financing to the Islamic financing, even at the 1–2 percentage points higher cost in the early years (see Case 6 in `13_CASES/personal_business_cases.md`).

### The *gharar* prohibition

The *gharar* (the excessive uncertainty) is the prohibition in the Islamic *fiqh*. The Prophet ﷺ said, in the *hadith* of the prohibition of the *muzabana* (the sale of the dates on the tree for the dates on the ground): "Do not sell what you do not have" [Sunan al-Tirmidhi 1232, narrated by Hakim ibn Hizam — sahih]. The *gharar* is the sale with the excessive uncertainty — the sale of the unspecified quantity, the sale of the unspecified quality, the sale of the unspecified time, the sale of the nonexistent.

The *gharar*'s scope: the prohibition applies to the contracts with the excessive uncertainty; the prohibition does not apply to the contracts with the normal commercial uncertainty (the price fluctuation, the demand variation, the delivery risk). The classical *fiqh* distinguishes the *gharar fahish* (the excessive uncertainty, prohibited) from the *gharar yasir* (the minor uncertainty, permitted). The contractor's *gharar* exposures: the contract with the unspecified scope (the "we will figure out the details later"), the contract with the unspecified price (the "we will settle the price after the work"), the contract with the unspecified time (the "we will complete when we can"), the contract with the nonexistent materials (the contractor sells the materials he does not have).

The *gharar*'s alternatives: the clear specification of the scope, the price, the time, and the materials in the contract; the variation order process for the changes; the *istisna'* contract for the manufacturing with the specified milestones; the *salam* contract for the forward purchase with the specified quality and quantity. The contractor's strategic move is the discipline of the clear specification, even at the cost of the longer negotiation.

### The *maysir* prohibition

The *maysir* (the gambling, the speculation) is the prohibition in the Islamic *fiqh*. The Quran says, "They ask you about wine and *maysir*; say, in them is great sin and some benefit for people, but the sin is greater than the benefit" [Quran 2:219 — al-Baqara]. The *maysir* is the contract that depends on the chance — the gambling, the lottery, the speculation that is the gambling in substance.

The *maysir*'s scope: the prohibition applies to the contracts that depend on the chance; the prohibition does not apply to the commercial risk (the normal business risk of the price fluctuation, the demand variation, the project success). The contractor's *maysir* exposures: the speculative bidding (the bid below the cost in the hope of the variation orders), the speculative procurement (the hoarding of the materials in the hope of the price increase), the speculative land purchase (the purchase of the land in the hope of the rezoning), the speculative currency trading.

The *maysir*'s alternatives: the disciplined bidding (the bid at the cost plus the margin), the disciplined procurement (the procurement for the specific project, not the speculation), the disciplined land purchase (the purchase for the specific development, not the speculation), the disciplined currency management (the hedging for the specific transaction, not the speculation).

### The fraud (*ghish*) prohibition

The *ghish* (the fraud, the deception) is the prohibition. The Prophet ﷺ said, "Whoever cheats us is not of us" [Sahih Muslim 101, narrated by Abu Hurayra — Tier 1, classical]; "The seller and the buyer have the option as long as they have not parted; if both spoke the truth and made clear the defects, their transaction would be blessed; and if they concealed and told lies, the blessing would be obliterated" [Sahih al-Bukhari 2114, narrated by Hakim ibn Hizam — Tier 1, classical].

The *ghish*'s scope: the prohibition applies to the misrepresentation of the quality, the quantity, the price, the capacity, the timeline; the prohibition applies to the concealment of the defects, the conflicts of interest, the side arrangements; the prohibition applies to the false certification, the falsified invoices, the backdated documents. The contractor's *ghish* exposures: the bid with the misrepresented capacity (the bid for the project the contractor cannot execute), the execution with the concealed defects (the defect the contractor does not disclose to the client), the invoice with the inflated quantities (the invoice for the materials not installed), the certification with the false signatures (the certification by the engineer who did not inspect).

The *ghish*'s alternatives: the honest representation (the capacity the contractor can deliver, the quality the contractor can guarantee, the timeline the contractor can meet), the honest disclosure (the defects the contractor informs the client of, the conflicts of interest the contractor discloses, the side arrangements the contractor avoids), the honest documentation (the accurate invoices, the accurate certifications, the accurate signatures).

### The theft (*sariqa*) and the *ghasb* prohibitions

The *sariqa* (the theft) and the *ghasb* (the usurpation) are the prohibitions. The Quran says, "[As for] the thief, the male and the female, amputate their hands as a penalty for what they earned, an exemplary punishment from Allah" [Quran 5:38 — al-Ma'ida]. The *ghasb* (the usurpation of the property by the force or the deception) is the prohibition; the Prophet ﷺ said, "Whoever wrongfully takes a hand-span of land, Allah will make him carry seven earths on the Day of Resurrection" [Sahih al-Bukhari 2453, narrated by Sa'id ibn Zaid — Tier 1, classical].

The *sariqa*'s and the *ghasb*'s scope: the prohibition applies to the theft of the materials, the tools, the equipment; the prohibition applies to the usurpation of the land, the property, the rights. The contractor's *sariqa* and *ghasb* exposures: the materials the contractor takes from one project for another, the tools the contractor takes from the supplier without the consent, the equipment the contractor uses beyond the agreed time, the land the contractor uses beyond the agreed scope, the rights the contractor infringes (the patent, the trademark, the copyright).

## Dimension 2 — The Professional Red Lines

The professional red lines are the lines that the contractor's professional identity as a contractor requires. The professional red lines overlap with the Islamic red lines (the *ghish* is both the Islamic and the professional red line), but they include the additional lines that the professional standards (the engineering ethics, the construction industry's codes of conduct) require.

### The dishonesty red line

The dishonesty is the professional red line. The contractor who lies to the client (the false claim of the certification, the false statement of the capacity, the false promise of the timeline) has crossed the red line. The dishonesty is the *ghish* in the Islamic frame; the dishonesty is the breach of the engineering ethics in the professional frame.

The contractor's dishonesty exposures: the false claim of the certification (the certification the contractor does not have, the certification the contractor's engineer does not have), the false statement of the capacity (the capacity the contractor does not have, the team the contractor does not have), the false promise of the timeline (the timeline the contractor cannot meet, the milestone the contractor cannot deliver), the false claim of the past work (the project the contractor did not execute, the role the contractor did not have).

### The breach-of-contract red line

The breach of the contract is the professional red line. The contractor who breaches the contract (the scope the contractor does not deliver, the quality the contractor does not achieve, the timeline the contractor does not meet) has crossed the red line. The breach is the *ghish* in the Islamic frame; the breach is the violation of the contract law in the professional frame.

The contractor's breach exposures: the scope reduction without the agreement (the contractor delivers less than contracted), the quality reduction without the disclosure (the contractor delivers the lower quality than contracted), the timeline extension without the justification (the contractor delivers later than contracted without the force majeure or the variation order), the abandonment of the project (the contractor leaves the project before the completion).

### The harm-to-others red line

The harm to the others is the professional red line. The contractor who harms the others (the workers, the subcontractors, the suppliers, the public, the environment) has crossed the red line. The harm is the violation of the *la darar wa la dirar* principle in the Islamic frame; the harm is the violation of the safety, the labor, the environmental standards in the professional frame.

The contractor's harm exposures: the unsafe work conditions (the workers without the protective equipment, the scaffolding without the safety, the electrical work without the lockout-tagout), the underpaid workers (the wages below the market, the late payments, the unpaid overtime), the unpaid subcontractors (the invoices delayed, the partial payments, the disputed invoices), the public harm (the noise, the dust, the traffic, the disruption without the mitigation), the environmental harm (the waste dumped without the disposal, the materials spilled without the cleanup, the energy wasted without the conservation).

## Dimension 3 — The Personal Red Lines

The personal red lines are the lines that the contractor's personal identity as a Muslim, a husband, a father, a son, a friend requires. The personal red lines overlap with the Islamic and the professional red lines, but they include the additional lines that the personal integrity (the *amanah*, the *'ird*, the *rahma*) requires.

### The betrayal-of-trust red line

The betrayal of the trust (*khayanat al-amanah*) is the personal red line. The Prophet ﷺ said, "There is no faith for the one who is not trustworthy, and no religion for the one who does not keep his covenant" [Musnad Ahmad 12312, narrated by Anas — sahih per al-Albani]. The contractor who betrays the trust (the client's confidential information, the partner's financial information, the family's personal information) has crossed the red line.

The contractor's trust-betrayal exposures: the client's confidential information disclosed to the competitor (the bid strategy, the pricing methodology, the project details), the partner's financial information disclosed to the third party (the partner's bank details, the partner's tax information, the partner's business plans), the family's personal information disclosed to the public (the family's conflicts, the family's finances, the family's health).

### The abandonment-of-family red line

The abandonment of the family (*taqshur al-'a'ilah*) is the personal red line. The Quran says, "And treat your parents with kindness" [Quran 17:23 — al-Isra']; "And live with them [your wives] in kindness" [Quran 4:19 — al-Nisa]. The contractor who abandons the family (the wife without the support, the children without the presence, the parents without the care) has crossed the red line.

The contractor's abandonment exposures: the wife without the financial support (the contractor's reinvestment at the expense of the family's needs), the children without the father's presence (the contractor's work at the expense of the children's upbringing), the parents without the son's care (the contractor's distance at the expense of the parents' needs in their old age), the extended family without the member's role (the contractor's withdrawal from the family's collective obligations).

### The harm-to-self red line

The harm to the self is the personal red line. The Quran says, "And do not throw yourselves into destruction with your own hands" [Quran 2:195 — al-Baqara]; "And do not kill yourselves; indeed Allah is to you ever Merciful" [Quran 4:29 — al-Nisa]. The contractor who harms himself (the overwork that destroys the health, the stress that destroys the mind, the neglect of the spiritual practice that destroys the soul) has crossed the red line.

The contractor's self-harm exposures: the overwork (the 80-hour weeks that destroy the physical health, the marriage, the children's relationship), the chronic stress (the high-cortisol state that destroys the hormonal balance, the cardiovascular health, the mental clarity), the spiritual neglect (the missed prayers, the missed fasts, the missed Quran reading, the missed *tahajjud*), the moral compromises (the *haram* the contractor accepts for the business success, the moral injury that the compromises produce).

## Dimension 4 — The Strategic Red Lines

The strategic red lines are the lines whose crossing destroys the strategy itself. The strategic red lines overlap with the Islamic, the professional, and the personal red lines, but they include the additional lines that the strategic logic (the long-game discipline, the reputation compound, the network effect) requires.

### The overreach red line

The overreach is the strategic red line. The contractor who accepts the project beyond his capacity (the project management gap, the cash flow gap, the team quality gap — see Case 5 in `13_CASES/personal_business_cases.md`) has crossed the red line. The overreach destroys the strategy because the overreach destroys the firm; the firm is the strategic vehicle.

### The concentration red line

The concentration is the strategic red line. The contractor who depends on a single client for more than 30–40% of the revenue has crossed the red line. The concentration destroys the strategy because the concentration makes the firm the hostage of the single client; the client's doom becomes the firm's doom (see Case 4 in `13_CASES/relationship_cases.md` — the Dong Zhuo-Lu Bu pattern).

### The *riba*-financed expansion red line

The *riba*-financed expansion is the strategic red line. The contractor who finances the expansion with the *riba* has crossed the Islamic red line (the *riba* prohibition) and the strategic red line (the *riba* amplifies the cash flow risk — the interest payment is the obligation regardless of the project's success). The *riba*-financed expansion destroys the strategy because the *riba* amplifies the downturns and the over-reaches into the catastrophes.

### The reputation-destroying shortcut red line

The reputation-destroying shortcut is the strategic red line. The contractor who takes the shortcut that destroys the reputation (the *ghish*, the *rishwa*, the late payment, the underpayment, the abandonment of the project, the breach of the contract) has crossed the red line. The shortcut destroys the strategy because the reputation is the long-game asset; the shortcut is the short-game gain that destroys the long-game asset (see Case 6 in `13_CASES/personal_business_cases.md`).

### The opportunist-compromise red line

The opportunist compromise is the strategic red line. The contractor who compromises the *aqeedah* for the opportunity (the acceptance of the *haram* for the contract, the acceptance of the *ghish* for the bid, the acceptance of the *rishwa* for the award) has crossed the red line. The compromise destroys the strategy because the *aqeedah* is the foundation beneath the strategy; the compromise removes the foundation; the strategy collapses.

## Dimension 5 — The Egyptian Legal Red Lines

The Egyptian legal red lines are the criminal acts in business that the Egyptian law (the Penal Code, the Commercial Code, the Competition Law, the Anti-Money-Laundering Law, the Public Procurement Law) prohibits. The legal red lines overlap with the Islamic and the professional red lines, but they include the additional lines that the Egyptian statutory framework requires.

### The bribery red line

The bribery is the Egyptian legal red line. The Egyptian Penal Code (Law 58 of 1937, as amended) prohibits the bribery in the public sector (Articles 103–111) and the private sector (the 2018 amendment). The penalty is the imprisonment and the fine; the contractor who pays the bribe is the criminal liability; the contractor who receives the bribe is the criminal liability.

### The fraud red line

The fraud is the Egyptian legal red line. The Egyptian Penal Code (Articles 336–352) prohibits the fraud (*nashz*), the embezzlement (*khiyanat amana*), the breach of trust, the falsification of the documents. The penalty is the imprisonment and the fine; the contractor who commits the fraud is the criminal liability.

### The money-laundering red line

The money laundering is the Egyptian legal red line. The Anti-Money-Laundering Law (Law 80 of 2002, as amended) prohibits the laundering of the proceeds of the crime. The contractor who launders the proceeds (the cash payments to the different accounts, the invoicing through the different companies, the false documentation) is the criminal liability (see Dilemma 5 in `13_CASES/ethical_dilemmas.md`).

### The tax-evasion red line

The tax evasion is the Egyptian legal red line. The Egyptian Tax Law (Law 91 of 2005) and the Value-Added Tax Law (Law 67 of 2016) require the payment of the taxes and the accurate reporting. The contractor who evades the taxes (the cash transactions without the invoices, the underreported revenue, the inflated deductions) is the criminal liability.

### The unfair-competition red line

The unfair competition is the Egyptian legal red line. The Competition Law (Law 3 of 2005, as amended) prohibits the anti-competitive practices (the cartels, the price-fixing, the bid-rigging, the abuse of the dominant position). The contractor who engages in the unfair competition is the civil and the criminal liability.

### The labor-law red line

The labor-law violations are the Egyptian legal red line. The Egyptian Labor Law (Law 12 of 2003) requires the minimum wage, the working hours, the overtime, the leave, the safety, the social insurance. The contractor who violates the labor law (the underpayment, the overwork, the unsafe conditions, the unregistered workers) is the civil and the criminal liability.

## Recognizing When You're Approaching a Red Line

The discipline of the red lines includes the recognition of the approach — the early warning signs that the contractor is approaching a red line, before the crossing. The recognition is the operationalization of the *ittiba' al-dhan* principle (the avoidance of the suspicion — see Dilemma 5 in `13_CASES/ethical_dilemmas.md`).

### The internal signals

The internal signals are the spiritual and the psychological indicators that the contractor is approaching a red line. The Prophet ﷺ said, "The halal is clear and the haram is clear, and between them are matters that are doubtful, which many people do not know. Whoever guards against the doubtful has preserved his religion and his honor" [Sahih al-Bukhari 52, narrated by al-Nu'man ibn Bashir — Tier 1, classical]. The doubtful matter is the internal signal.

The internal signals:
- **The doubt (*shakk*)**: the contractor's hesitation about the action, the questioning of the permissibility. The doubt is the signal that the action is approaching the red line; the doubt requires the further inquiry (the *fatwa* of the qualified *mufti*) before the action.
- **The discomfort (*irtiya'*)**: the contractor's discomfort about the action, the sense that the action is wrong even if the justification is available. The discomfort is the signal of the conscience (*wajd*) that the *aqeedah* has trained; the discomfort requires the further reflection before the action.
- **The concealment (*khafiya*)**: the contractor's desire to conceal the action from the family, the friends, the *mufti*, the public. The desire to conceal is the signal that the action would not survive the disclosure; the action that would not survive the disclosure is the action approaching the red line.
- **The rationalization (*ta'wil*)**: the contractor's construction of the justification for the action that the contractor would not endorse in the calm state. The rationalization is the signal of the doubt that the rationalization is attempting to suppress; the rationalization requires the further inquiry before the action.

### The external signals

The external signals are the situational indicators that the contractor is approaching a red line.

The external signals:
- **The unusual request**: the client's request for the cash payment, the supplier's offer of the "special discount", the subcontractor's request for the undocumented work. The unusual request is the signal of the situation that is approaching the red line.
- **The urgency pressure**: the client's demand for the immediate decision, the supplier's "limited-time" offer, the partner's pressure for the immediate commitment. The urgency pressure is the manipulation tactic (see `04_PSYCHOLOGY/manipulation_patterns.md`) that is the signal of the situation that is approaching the red line; the urgency is the obstacle to the deliberation the red-line recognition requires.
- **The asymmetry of the information**: the contractor's knowledge of the material fact that the counterparty does not know (the defect the client does not know, the conflict of interest the partner does not know, the side arrangement the public does not know). The asymmetry is the signal of the situation that is approaching the red line; the asymmetry requires the disclosure before the action.
- **The precedent**: the contractor's awareness that the action would set the precedent for the future actions (the *rishwa* once paid is the *rishwa* expected; the *ghish* once committed is the *ghish* expected). The precedent is the signal of the action that, once taken, becomes the pattern; the pattern is the red line that the contractor crosses once and crosses repeatedly.

## Responding When You've Crossed a Red Line

The discipline of the red lines includes the response to the crossing — the repentance (*tawbah*), the restitution (*radd al-mazalim*), and the reform (*islah*) when the contractor has crossed the line. The response is the operationalization of the *rahma* (the mercy) of Allah that the *aqeedah* depends on; the response is the long-game discipline that the strategy depends on.

### The *tawbah* (the repentance)

The *tawbah* is the return to Allah after the sin. The Quran says, "O you who believe, repent to Allah with sincere repentance" [Quran 66:8 — al-Tahrim]. The conditions of the *tawbah* (per the classical *fiqh*): the immediate cessation of the sin, the sincere regret (*nadam*) for the sin, the firm resolve (*'azm*) not to repeat the sin, the restitution of the rights that the sin violated. The *tawbah* without the restitution is incomplete; the *tawbah* with the restitution is complete.

The contractor's *tawbah* for the *riba*: the immediate cessation of the *riba*-based financing, the regret for the *riba* the contractor has paid or received, the resolve not to engage in the *riba* again, the restitution (the return of the *riba* to the one who paid it, if possible; if not possible, the donation of the *riba* amount to the charity). The contractor's *tawbah* for the *ghish*: the immediate cessation of the *ghish*, the regret, the resolve, the restitution (the disclosure of the *ghish* to the client, the repair of the defect, the refund of the overcharge). The contractor's *tawbah* for the *rishwa*: the immediate cessation of the *rishwa*, the regret, the resolve, the restitution (the disclosure of the *rishwa* to the appropriate authority, the recovery of the *rishwa* if possible, the donation to the charity if not).

### The *radd al-mazalim* (the restitution)

The *radd al-mazalim* is the return of the rights that the sin violated. The Prophet ﷺ said, "Whoever has wronged his brother in his honor or anything else, let him seek his brother's pardon today, before there is no dinar and no dirham" [Sahih al-Bukhari 2449, narrated by Abu Hurayra — Tier 1, classical]. The restitution is the operationalization of the *tawbah*; the restitution is the action that the *tawbah* requires.

The contractor's restitution: the return of the *riba* to the one who paid it (if the *riba* was received) or the recovery of the *riba* from the one who received it (if the *riba* was paid); the disclosure of the *ghish* to the client and the repair of the defect or the refund of the overcharge; the disclosure of the *rishwa* to the appropriate authority; the payment of the unpaid wages to the workers; the payment of the unpaid invoices to the subcontractors; the disclosure of the undisclosed conflicts of interest to the partners; the repair of the harm to the public, the environment, the workers.

### The *islah* (the reform)

The *islah* is the reform of the conditions that produced the red-line crossing. The Quran says, "Indeed, Allah does not change the condition of a people until they change what is in themselves" [Quran 13:11 — al-Ra'd]. The *islah* is the operationalization of the firm resolve not to repeat the sin; the *islah* is the structural change that the recurrence of the sin requires.

The contractor's *islah*: the transition from the *riba*-based financing to the Islamic financing (the structural change that prevents the recurrence of the *riba*); the implementation of the quality inspection process (the structural change that prevents the recurrence of the *ghish*); the implementation of the anti-corruption policy (the structural change that prevents the recurrence of the *rishwa*); the implementation of the worker payment discipline (the structural change that prevents the recurrence of the unpaid wages); the implementation of the subcontractor payment discipline (the structural change that prevents the recurrence of the unpaid invoices); the implementation of the conflict-of-interest disclosure (the structural change that prevents the recurrence of the undisclosed conflicts); the implementation of the environmental and the safety standards (the structural change that prevents the recurrence of the harm).

## The Daily Discipline of the Red Lines

The red lines are not the theoretical framework; they are the daily discipline. The contractor's daily discipline includes:

- **The morning audit**: the brief review of the day's planned actions against the red lines. The audit takes 5 minutes; the audit identifies the actions that approach the red lines and the actions that require the further inquiry.
- **The decision audit**: the brief review of each major decision (the bid, the contract, the financing, the subcontractor engagement) against the red lines before the decision. The audit takes 5 minutes per decision; the audit identifies the red-line risks of the decision.
- **The evening audit**: the brief review of the day's actions against the red lines. The audit takes 5 minutes; the audit identifies the actions that approached the red lines (the *tawbah* for the actions that crossed), and the actions that require the restitution (the *radd al-mazalim* for the actions that violated the rights).
- **The weekly review**: the longer review of the week's patterns against the red lines. The review takes 30 minutes; the review identifies the patterns that approach the red lines and the structural changes (the *islah*) that the patterns require.

The discipline is the operationalization of the red lines; the discipline is what the contractor does, not what the contractor believes.
