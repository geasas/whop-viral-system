## The Premise

The honesty-in-deception question is the most contested question in the strategic ethics. The question is not whether the contractor may deceive (the *kidhb*, the lie, is prohibited by the *ijma'* of the *fiqh* schools); the question is whether the contractor may *conceal* the information that the contractor is not obligated to disclose, and whether the contractor may *misrepresent* the information that the contractor is obligated to disclose accurately. The distinction between the concealment and the misrepresentation is the line; the line is the operational discipline this file develops.

The Islamic framework distinguishes three categories: the *kidhb* (the lie, the affirmative false statement, prohibited in all but three exceptional cases), the *tawriya* (the ambiguous statement that is true in one sense and misleading in another, permitted in the negotiation and the dispute), and the *katm* (the concealment of the information the contractor is not obligated to disclose, permitted in the competition). The contractor's discipline is the recognition of the category the action falls into and the operationalization of the category's ruling.

The Western framework (the just-war tradition, the business-ethics literature) provides the parallel distinction: the *lie* (the affirmative false statement, prohibited), the *concealment* (the non-disclosure of the information not obligated to be disclosed, permitted), and the *misrepresentation* (the affirmative false statement that the duty of the disclosure requires, prohibited). The two frameworks are compatible; the Islamic framework is the *sharia* articulation; the Western framework is the secular articulation.

## The Islamic Framework

### The prohibition of *kidhb* (the lie)

The *kidhb* (the lie, the affirmative false statement) is prohibited in the Islamic *fiqh* with the *ijma'* of the schools. The Quran says, "O you who believe, fear Allah and be with those who are truthful" [Quran 9:119 — al-Tawba]; "And do not mix the truth with falsehood or conceal the truth while you know" [Quran 2:42 — al-Baqara]. The Prophet ﷺ said, "Beware of lying, for lying leads to wickedness, and wickedness leads to Hell. A man may tell lies until he is recorded with Allah as a liar" [Sahih al-Bukhari 6094, narrated by Abdullah ibn Mas'ud — Tier 1, classical]; "The signs of the hypocrite are three: when he speaks, he lies; when he promises, he breaks; and when he is entrusted, he betrays" [Sahih al-Bukhari 33, narrated by Abu Hurayra — Tier 1, classical].

The *kidhb*'s scope: the prohibition applies to the false statement of the fact (the false claim of the certification, the false statement of the capacity, the false claim of the past work); the prohibition applies to the false promise (the promise the contractor does not intend to keep); the prohibition applies to the false witness (the testimony to the false); the prohibition applies to the false oath (the oath on the false).

The *kidhb*'s exceptions: the classical *fiqh* recognizes three exceptional cases where the *kidhb* is permitted:
1. **The reconciliation between the spouses**: the Prophet ﷺ said, "I do not consider it permissible for a person to lie except in three cases: ... the reconciliation between the spouses" [Sunan Abi Dawud 4921, narrated by Asma bint Yazid — hasan, classified by al-Albani as sahih]. The lie that reconciles the spouses is permitted; the lie that furthers the discord is not.
2. **The war**: the *hadith* "War is deception" [Sahih al-Bukhari 3028, narrated by Jabir ibn Abdullah — Tier 1, classical] permits the *kidhb* in the war (the deception of the adversary).
3. **The protection of the life or the property**: the *fiqh* permits the lie that protects the life (the lie to the oppressor who seeks the innocent) or the property (the lie to the thief who seeks the wealth). The exception is the protection, not the deception for the gain.

The *kidhb*'s exceptions' limits: the exceptions are narrow; the contractor may not invoke the negotiation or the competition as the "war" that justifies the *kidhb*. The classical *fiqh* (Ibn Taymiyyah's *Majmu al-Fatawa*) distinguishes the *hiya* (the stratagem, permitted) from the *kidhb* (the lie, prohibited); the negotiation and the competition permit the *hiya*, not the *kidhb*.

### The permission of *tawriya* (the ambiguity)

The *tawriya* (the ambiguous statement that is true in one sense and misleading in another) is permitted in the Islamic *fiqh* with the conditions. The classical *fiqh* (al-Ghazali's *Ihya*, Ibn Qudama's *al-Mughni*) discusses the *tawriya* as the legitimate alternative to the *kidhb* in the cases where the contractor needs to mislead without lying.

The *tawriya*'s example: the contractor is asked by the competitor's representative whether the contractor is bidding for a particular project. The contractor answers, "I am not bidding for any project tomorrow" — meaning that the contractor is not bidding the *next day* (the contractor is bidding the day after). The statement is true in the literal sense (the contractor is not bidding tomorrow) but misleading in the implied sense (the competitor's representative understands the contractor is not bidding for the project at all). The *tawriya* is the true statement that the misleading implication does not convert into the *kidhb*.

The *tawriya*'s conditions:
- **The truth**: the statement must be true in the literal sense. The contractor who says "I am not bidding tomorrow" must not be bidding tomorrow (the literal sense); the misleading implication (the contractor is not bidding at all) is the implication the competitor's representative draws, not the statement the contractor makes.
- **The legitimate interest**: the *tawriya* must serve the legitimate interest (the protection of the trade secret, the avoidance of the competitor's exploitation, the privacy of the contractor's plans). The *tawriya* that serves the illegitimate interest (the deception of the client, the manipulation of the partner) is the *ghish*.
- **The non-disclosure of the material fact**: the *tawriya* must not be the non-disclosure of the material fact that the contractor is obligated to disclose (the defect, the conflict of interest, the litigation). The *tawriya* that conceals the material fact is the *ghish*.

The *tawriya*'s discipline: the *tawriya* is the legitimate tool the contractor may use in the negotiation and the competition; the *tawriya* is not the license to deceive the client or the partner. The discipline is the recognition of the legitimate interest (the trade secret, the privacy) and the material fact (the defect, the conflict) — the *tawriya* serves the legitimate interest; the *tawriya* does not conceal the material fact.

### The permission of *katm* (the concealment)

The *katm* (the concealment of the information the contractor is not obligated to disclose) is permitted in the Islamic *fiqh*. The *katm* is the legitimate non-disclosure; the *katm* is not the *ghish*.

The *katm*'s scope:
- **The trade secrets**: the floor price, the future plans, the team capacity, the subcontractor relationships — the concealment of these from the competitor is the *katm* the contractor may use.
- **The personal information**: the contractor's personal finances, the contractor's family situation, the contractor's religious practice — the concealment from the client, the partner, the public is the *katm*.
- **The immaterial information**: the contractor's past projects that are not relevant to the current bid, the contractor's past relationships that are not relevant to the current partnership — the concealment is the *katm*.

The *katm*'s limits: the *katm* does not include the concealment of the material fact that the contractor is obligated to disclose (the defect, the conflict of interest, the litigation, the financial condition). The concealment of the material fact is the *ghish*.

The *katm*'s discipline: the *katm* is the legitimate non-disclosure; the *katm* is not the silence that the contract or the *amanah* requires to break. The discipline is the recognition of the contractor's disclosure obligations (the contract, the law, the *amanah*) and the non-disclosure rights (the trade secrets, the personal information, the immaterial information) — the *katm* operates within the disclosure obligations; the *katm* does not violate them.

### The disputed cases

The disputed cases are the cases where the classical *fiqh* scholars differ on the application of the *kidhb*-vs-*tawriya*-vs-*katm* framework. The contractor's discipline is the awareness of the dispute and the conservative resolution.

The disputed cases:
- **The *tawriya* in the negotiation**: the *tawriya* that misleads the counterparty about the contractor's reservation price (the floor price) is permitted by some scholars (the *tawriya* serves the legitimate interest of the trade secret) and questioned by others (the *tawriya* in the negotiation is the *ghish* if the counterparty relies on the implication). The conservative resolution: the *tawriya* that protects the trade secret is permitted; the *tawriya* that misleads about the material fact is prohibited.
- **The *katm* of the competitor's information**: the *katm* of the information the contractor knows about the competitor (the competitor's financial difficulty, the competitor's team defection) is permitted by some scholars (the contractor has no duty to disclose the competitor's information) and questioned by others (the contractor's silence benefits from the competitor's harm). The conservative resolution: the *katm* of the competitor's information is permitted; the active use of the information (the exploitation of the competitor's difficulty, the recruitment of the competitor's team) is the separate question that the harm-minimization principle addresses.
- **The exaggeration in the advertising**: the *tawriya* or the *katm* in the advertising (the emphasis on the strengths, the de-emphasis of the weaknesses) is permitted by some scholars (the advertising is the representation, not the disclosure) and questioned by others (the advertising is the disclosure to the public that depends on the honest representation). The conservative resolution: the emphasis on the strengths is the *hiya*; the misrepresentation of the strengths (the false claim of the certification, the false claim of the past work) is the *ghish*; the concealment of the material weaknesses (the recent bankruptcy, the recent license suspension) is the *ghish*.

## The Western Framework

### The just-war tradition

The Western just-war tradition (Augustine, Aquinas, Grotius, Walzer) distinguishes the *jus ad bellum* (the justice of the war) from the *jus in bello* (the justice in the war). The *jus in bello* includes the *permissible deception* of the adversary — the concealment of the intentions, the misrepresentation of the strength, the surprise attack. The deception in the just war is permitted because the adversary has consented to the war (the implicit consent of the war); the deception in the unjust war is prohibited because the war itself is prohibited.

The just-war tradition's application to the competitive market: the competitive market is the analog of the just war (the competitors have consented to the competition); the deception in the competition (the concealment of the intentions, the misrepresentation of the strength) is permitted. The deception's limits: the deception does not include the harm to the non-combatants (the public, the third-parties); the deception does not include the breach of the trust (the contract, the partnership).

### The business-ethics literature

The Western business-ethics literature (Shell's *Bargaining for Advantage*, Lewicki's negotiation literature) distinguishes the *lie* (the affirmative false statement, prohibited) from the *concealment* (the non-disclosure of the information not obligated to be disclosed, permitted) and the *misrepresentation* (the affirmative false statement that the duty of the disclosure requires, prohibited). The distinction is the operational discipline the contractor uses.

The business-ethics literature's disclosure duties:
- **The contractual disclosure**: the disclosure of the material facts the contract requires (the scope, the price, the time, the quality).
- **The fiduciary disclosure**: the disclosure of the material facts the fiduciary relationship requires (the conflicts of interest, the side arrangements, the financial condition of the partnership).
- **The legal disclosure**: the disclosure of the material facts the law requires (the environmental impact, the safety hazards, the consumer-protection disclosures).
- **The ethical disclosure**: the disclosure of the material facts the ethical relationship requires (the defects, the risks, the limitations).

The business-ethics literature's non-disclosure rights:
- **The trade secrets**: the non-disclosure of the trade secrets (the pricing methodology, the team capacity, the future plans).
- **The privacy**: the non-disclosure of the personal information (the contractor's personal finances, the family situation, the religious practice).
- **The immaterial information**: the non-disclosure of the immaterial information (the past projects not relevant to the current bid, the past relationships not relevant to the current partnership).

## The Contractor's Specific Cases

### Case 1 — Concealing the floor price

The contractor's floor price (the minimum price the contractor would accept) is the trade secret the contractor is not obligated to disclose to the client. The concealment of the floor price is the *katm* the contractor may use in the negotiation.

The discipline:
- **The concealment is the *katm***: the contractor may conceal the floor price from the client (the contractor's first offer is above the floor price; the contractor's anchoring is the legitimate stratagem).
- **The *tawriya* is permitted**: the contractor may use the *tawriya* in the negotiation (the statement "I cannot go below X" where X is above the floor price and the statement is true in the sense that the contractor has decided not to go below X).
- **The *kidhb* is prohibited**: the contractor may not say "My cost is X" where X is above the actual cost; the false statement of the cost is the *kidhb*.
- **The disclosure of the cost-plus-margin is the contract**: when the contract is the cost-plus-margin type (the *muqawala muqawwama*, the cost-plus contract), the contractor is obligated to disclose the actual cost; the concealment of the actual cost in the cost-plus contract is the *ghish*.

### Case 2 — Revealing the defects

The contractor's defects (the known defects in the work) are the material facts the contractor is obligated to disclose to the client. The concealment of the defects is the *ghish* the contractor may not use (see Dilemma 6 in `13_CASES/ethical_dilemmas.md`).

The discipline:
- **The disclosure is the *amanah***: the contractor discloses the defects to the client (the location, the nature, the severity, the cause) before the client's final inspection and the final payment.
- **The repair is the *ghish* prohibition's operationalization**: the contractor repairs the defects at his own expense; the repaired work is warranted.
- **The non-disclosure of the cosmetic defect is the *ghish***: even the cosmetic defect (the visible imperfection that does not affect the function) must be disclosed; the *ghish* prohibition applies to the cosmetic defect as much as to the fire hazard.
- **The non-disclosure of the code violation is the *ghish***: the code violation (the deviation from the code that does not immediately produce the hazard) must be disclosed; the contractor's licensing may be at risk if the code violation is discovered by the inspection.
- **The non-disclosure of the fire hazard is the *ghish* and the criminal liability**: the fire hazard (the defect that may produce the fire, the injury, the death) must be disclosed; the concealment is the *ghish* and the criminal liability.

### Case 3 — Representing the capacity

The contractor's capacity (the crews, the equipment, the working capital) is the material fact the contractor is obligated to represent accurately in the bid. The misrepresentation of the capacity is the *ghish* the contractor may not use.

The discipline:
- **The accurate representation is the *amanah***: the contractor represents the capacity accurately in the bid (the crews the contractor has, the equipment the contractor has, the working capital the contractor has).
- **The concealment of the immaterial information is the *katm***: the contractor may conceal the immaterial information (the contractor's future plans, the contractor's other projects, the contractor's personal finances).
- **The concealment of the material capacity is the *ghish***: the contractor may not conceal the material capacity limitation (the contractor's crews are already committed to other projects, the contractor's working capital is insufficient for the project, the contractor's equipment is not the equipment required for the project).
- **The *tawriya* on the capacity is the borderline**: the contractor may use the *tawriya* on the capacity (the statement "we can do this project" where the contractor means "we can do this project with the additional crews we will hire") if the *tawriya* is true in the literal sense; the *tawriya* that conceals the material limitation (the contractor cannot do the project without the additional crews the contractor has not yet hired) is the borderline that the conservative resolution questions.

### Case 4 — The competitor's information

The contractor's knowledge of the competitor's information (the competitor's financial difficulty, the competitor's team defection, the competitor's project failure) is the information the contractor is not obligated to disclose to the client, the partner, or the public. The concealment of the competitor's information is the *katm*; the active use of the information is the separate question the harm-minimization principle addresses.

The discipline:
- **The concealment is the *katm***: the contractor may conceal the competitor's information from the client, the partner, the public.
- **The *kidhb* is prohibited**: the contractor may not make the false statement about the competitor (the false claim of the competitor's bankruptcy, the false claim of the competitor's license suspension). The false statement is the *ghish* regardless of the competitor's status.
- **The active use of the competitor's information**: the contractor's use of the competitor's information (the bid at the lower price when the contractor knows the competitor cannot match, the recruitment of the competitor's team when the contractor knows the competitor cannot retain) is the separate question that the harm-minimization principle addresses (see `14_ETHICS/harm_minimization.md`).

### Case 5 — The conflict of interest

The contractor's conflict of interest (the relationship with the supplier, the subcontractor, the consultant) is the material fact the contractor is obligated to disclose to the client. The concealment of the conflict of interest is the *ghish* the contractor may not use.

The discipline:
- **The disclosure is the *amanah***: the contractor discloses the conflict of interest (the relationship with the supplier, the subcontractor, the consultant) to the client before the bid or the contract.
- **The recusal is the operationalization**: the contractor recuses from the decisions where the conflict exists (the selection of the supplier, the subcontractor, the consultant); the client makes the decision or the independent third-party makes the decision.
- **The concealment is the *ghish***: the contractor may not conceal the conflict of interest even if the contractor's recommendation is the best for the client; the concealment is the *ghish* regardless of the recommendation's quality.

## The Discipline — Where the Line Is, How to Honor It

The discipline of the honesty-in-deception line is the daily practice the contractor develops. The discipline is the operationalization of the framework in the contractor's specific cases.

### The pre-action audit

The pre-action audit is the contractor's audit of the planned action against the honesty-in-deception framework. The audit identifies the category (the *kidhb*, the *tawriya*, the *katm*) the action falls into and the ruling (prohibited, permitted with conditions, permitted) the category requires.

The audit's questions:
1. **Is the action the *kidhb* (the affirmative false statement)?** If yes, the action is prohibited unless the action falls into the three exceptional cases (the reconciliation, the war, the protection of the life or the property). The negotiation and the competition do not qualify for the exception.
2. **Is the action the *tawriya* (the ambiguous statement that is true in one sense and misleading in another)?** If yes, the action is permitted with the conditions (the truth in the literal sense, the legitimate interest, the non-concealment of the material fact).
3. **Is the action the *katm* (the concealment of the information the contractor is not obligated to disclose)?** If yes, the action is permitted; the concealment of the trade secrets, the personal information, the immaterial information is the *katm*.
4. **Is the action the disclosure of the material fact?** If the action is the concealment of the material fact, the action is the *ghish*; the disclosure is the *amanah*.

### The disclosure obligations audit

The disclosure obligations audit is the contractor's audit of the planned action against the disclosure obligations. The audit identifies the material facts the contractor is obligated to disclose (the defects, the conflicts of interest, the capacity limitations, the financial condition in the partnership) and ensures the disclosure before the action.

The audit's questions:
1. **What is the material fact the contractor knows that the counterparty does not know?**
2. **Is the material fact the contractor obligated to disclose (the contract, the fiduciary, the legal, the ethical)?**
3. **Has the contractor disclosed the material fact before the action?**
4. **Has the disclosure been the honest disclosure (the truth, not the *tawriya*; the full disclosure, not the partial disclosure)?**

### The non-disclosure rights audit

The non-disclosure rights audit is the contractor's audit of the planned action against the non-disclosure rights. The audit identifies the information the contractor is not obligated to disclose (the trade secrets, the personal information, the immaterial information) and ensures the non-disclosure before the action.

The audit's questions:
1. **What is the information the contractor is not obligated to disclose?**
2. **Is the non-disclosure the *katm* (the legitimate non-disclosure) or the *ghish* (the concealment of the material fact)?**
3. **Has the contractor maintained the *katm* without crossing into the *ghish*?**

### The daily discipline

The daily discipline:
- **The pre-action audit**: the contractor's audit of the planned action against the honesty-in-deception framework before the action. The audit takes 5 minutes per major action.
- **The disclosure obligations audit**: the contractor's audit of the planned action against the disclosure obligations before the action.
- **The non-disclosure rights audit**: the contractor's audit of the planned action against the non-disclosure rights before the action.
- **The post-action audit**: the contractor's audit of the completed action against the framework. The audit identifies the *kidhb* (the *tawbah* for the lie), the *ghish* (the *radd al-mazalim* for the deception), the missed disclosure (the belated disclosure if the action is still reversible).
